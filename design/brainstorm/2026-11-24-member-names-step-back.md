# Brainstorm: member names, a step back (three new ways to name members)

Date: 2026-11-24
Asked by Cory: "i think the themes are maybe a little too constraining.... maybe the themes need to be a touch more abstract" (12:40pm Eastern); on Blue: "sports is interesting if we can make it work. Maybe a combo of 1 and 2. idk" (12:41pm); on the Red office names: "nope I don't like these still. we maybe need to zoom out and try a new direction." (12:44pm)
Skill read: bash .claude/hooks/brainstorm-read.sh at 12:58pm Eastern

What failed before, and why these are different shapes (design/pitch-ledger.md, Red names 1 to 5): (1) jobs inside a cult (Den Mother, Elders, Missionaries) "I dont like any of the names"; (2) everyday phrases for a kind of person (Social Butterfly, Picky Eaters) "focusing too much on the concept and not what the card actually does", "better as professions or archetypes of a concept (i.e. Wingman)"; (3) any profession, no theme (Temp and Warden kept, the rest "makes no sense"); (4) more professions, then "The colors should also be a little thematically linked"; (5) one setting per color, Red the office: "zoom out". So the three directions here keep archetypes whose role does the card (what Wingman, Temp and Warden did) and change only what ties a color together: (A) an abstract verb per color instead of a setting, (B) characters everyone already knows from fairy tales and old stories, (C) one shared world for the whole game, an old village, instead of one setting per color.
Same three cards in each direction so he can compare: Red Assimilators, Blue Thrift Hunters, Yellow Cosmopolitans. Names only; every rule unchanged.
Cut before sending: Collector for Brethren (the game already has Tax Collector, Toll Collectors, Duty Collector); Team Captain (puts sports in Red, muddling Blue's); Lifeguard (Comeback Coach uses his sports-plus-second-chances words); Tour Guide (too close to the live Tourist); Joseph for Cosmopolitans (Noah reads faster); a sins-per-color direction (gets dark fast, and two colors land on greed).

## Idea: Tailor

1. Printed text: unchanged, "Your grey members count as members of your biggest color." (Red). Replaces the name Assimilators. Direction 1 (one verb per color: Red matches).
2. Played turn: a name change only; in a 4-player round III the card plays exactly as Assimilators does today, and its gain against the best other space is unchanged.
3. Both sides: the card has the same choices as today; the name adds no choice and removes none, so nobody chooses differently.
4. Who feels it: the same players as today; what the name tells a player: a tailor fits the plain grey members into your main color's clothes, so they count as that color.
5. Not refused: not a live member name and not in design/cuts.md; a different shape from the five refused Red passes (see the top of this file): an archetype whose role does the card, tied to its color by direction 1.
6. Judged: recruit test: "You recruited a Tailor, and of course they dress your grey members in your biggest color." It reads in one pass, playful with a slight dark edge, and the name says what the card does.
7. Public and certain: the rule is unchanged, so nothing drawn from a pile, random or hidden is added.
8. Color interactions: none: a name change on a single-color Red card, no tie to another color.
9. Biggest case: unchanged rule; with 3 grey members in round III it adds 3 to your biggest color for the Red contest (4/6/10 followers), the same as Assimilators today.
