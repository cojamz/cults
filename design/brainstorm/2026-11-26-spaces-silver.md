## Idea: Make Haste

1. Printed text: "+1 purple per 2 minions placed this round, this one included. Your next turn comes right after this one." (fill-in numbers). Cards it touches: Hurriers "Whenever you take a consecutive turn, +2 followers.", Courier "Whenever you take a consecutive turn, +1 of each other color.", Keep Going (pays for turns in a row).
2. Played turn: round I, 4 players, 12 minions. Taken 4th: +2 purple and an extra turn right away, while good spaces remain. Taken 9th: +4 purple, but the quick turn lands on scraps, and on your last minion there is no next turn. Lie Abed gives +5 and the token. Gain: 2 to 6 purple plus a turn moved forward; the choice is early-and-small or late-and-big.
3. Both sides: no chooser; the decision is when to take it, and both early and late have a cost.
4. Who feels it: the player whose turn you jump ahead of (they lose the space you take on the quick turn); Purple rivals racing for the late placements.
5. Not refused: it keeps Cory's Make Haste and adds a count that grows late; not a smaller Flurry "+2 purple. Your next two turns come right after this one." (that moves two turns).
6. Judged: a timing gamble, Purple's own question (when do I act?). It is still small; flag it as the weaker of this batch.
7. Public and certain: minions on the wheel are counted by anyone.
8. Color interactions: none; reads all minions on the wheel.
9. Biggest case: round III, 20 minions, taken late: +10 purple and a consecutive turn (Hurriers +2, Courier +3), or Green copying it for +10 purple. Resources, not followers; under the line.
Intuitive: count the minions on the wheel, halve it, go again.
Fun: late is bigger, but the second turn gets worse.
Interacts: you take a turn ahead of rivals, and it reads the whole table.
Powerful: up to 10 purple and a turn.
Engine or counter: feeds Hurriers, Courier and Keep Going.
Its color: Purple bends turn order.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Salvage

1. Printed text: "Discard the whole shop and refill. Then recruit two members from the discard pile now. +5 blue." (fill-in). Cards it touches: Gravedigger "Whenever shop members go to the discard pile, +1 follower."; Rag-and-Bone Man; Gleaner "You may recruit from the discard pile as you would from the shop."; Market Sweeper "On Use, once per game: discard the whole shop and refill."
2. Played turn: round III, 4 players, 8 shop members. You discard all 8 (the good ones a rival was saving for included), refill, then recruit the two best of those 8 or anything older in the pile, paying as usual. Today's Salvage picks from leftovers only. Gain: two good members every time, plus a fresh shop the rivals didn't plan for.
3. Both sides: no chooser on the card; the only choice is which two members to take, always the best two.
4. Who feels it: everyone who planned a round III recruit from the row; it is gone, though the new row may be better.
5. Not refused: Clear Out "Discard 3 shop members and refill..." is round I and small; this is the burst. Not a pile draw: the refill is the normal shop refill and every pick is face up.
6. Judged: one move (wipe and pick) that keeps Blue's discard-pile burst and fixes the empty pile. Round III state: 8 shop members, a full row of choices.
7. Public and certain: the 8 discarded members are face up; the refill is the shop's normal refill.
8. Color interactions: none; the shop and discard pile are shared pieces.
9. Biggest case: round III, two 12-cost members (worth up to 3 each) for about 24 resources, plus Gravedigger's follower(s). Same size as today's Salvage at its best, now every game.
Intuitive: clear the shop, then shop the pile.
Fun: rivals lose the card they waited for.
Interacts: resets everyone's shop.
Powerful: the best two of 8 members.
Engine or counter: feeds Gravedigger and Gleaner.
Its color: Blue works the shop and the discard pile.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Pay Up Front

1. Printed text: "+2 white. Buy followers now, as well as your recruit this turn." (fill-in). Cards it touches: Spend Freely "+2 white. If you recruit a member this turn: +1 follower for every 2 resources you pay."; Redeem; Penitents "Whenever you buy followers, +2 white."; Penny-Pincher "Whenever you buy followers, +1 blue."
2. Played turn: round I, you hold 3 resources. Here: +2 white, buy 5 followers at once, and still recruit (a member, or more followers next turn). Put By gives +3 white. Gain: an extra purchase of followers this turn. Round III: buy 10 followers here and recruit a member too.
3. Both sides: no chooser on the card; you choose how many followers to buy, up to the card size.
4. Who feels it: rivals in Most followers turn order (fewest goes first next round); a reason to block it in round III. Mild.
5. Not refused: Pocket Money "+3 white. Buy followers now, as well as your recruit this turn." was pitched Nov 11 with no verdict, not refused; no member count in round I.
6. Judged: one rule bent (a second purchase), fits White's few-members plan (followers without members).
7. Public and certain: the follower cards are public and fixed.
8. Color interactions: none; it touches only the follower cards.
9. Biggest case: round III, 10 followers from the extra purchase (the follower card caps it at 10), Green copying it for another 10 with a big pile. At the line, not over; the center Redeem stays bigger.
Intuitive: buy followers twice this turn.
Fun: it lets a memberless White plan score early.
Interacts: a space to block in round III.
Powerful: up to 10 followers without spending the recruit.
Engine or counter: feeds Penitents and Penny-Pincher.
Its color: White cashes in past the limits.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Pay in Coin

1. Printed text: "Each rival +2 silver. This turn your silver pays for any color. +1 silver." (Silver space, fill-in). Cards it touches: Silver Tongues "Your silver pays for any color." (the same for one turn); Raise a Glass.
2. Played turn: round I, you hold 5 silver after Raise a Glass and want a 5-cost red member: today silver pays only the any part; here it pays all of it. The best other round I space gives 3 to 4 resources. Gain: a member you couldn't buy, and 6 silver handed to rivals (hurting their Fewest silver count).
3. Both sides: no chooser on the card; you decide whether to use the silver this turn or keep it, and both are live.
4. Who feels it: rivals get silver (a gift that costs them in the contest) every time someone takes it.
5. Not refused: Cory's own direction, Nov 26. Not a discount (the price is the same; only the colors change).
6. Judged: a special use of silver that one strategy (buy members) wants now; that is the shape Cory described.
7. Public and certain: every pile is public and nothing is drawn.
8. Color interactions: none; silver is Silver's own resource and it pays any color.
9. Biggest case: round III, 12 silver (after Bride and Groom doubles 6) buys a 12-cost member worth up to 3 followers, against a winner's 70 to 110; rivals receive 6 silver, a step toward losing Fewest silver (+10 in round III). Green copying it gives the copier the same for a turn. No follower burst.
Intuitive: silver works as any color this turn.
Fun: everyone gets silver, and you're the one who can spend it today.
Interacts: gifts silver to every rival.
Powerful: turns a dead pile into a member.
Engine or counter: engine with Bride and Groom and Raise a Glass.
Its color: Silver gives, and spends silver.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Buy Loyalty

1. Printed text: "Each rival +1 silver. Give a rival up to 5 silver and take that many of their followers." (Silver space, fill-in). Cards it touches: Cutthroats; Plunder "Steal 3 followers and 3 resources. +1 magenta."; Informer "Whenever a rival steals from you, they lose 1 follower." (paying is not stealing, so Informer stays quiet).
2. Played turn: round II, you hold 6 silver, the leader has 20 followers they bought. Pay them 5 silver, take 5 followers. You: +5 followers, −5 silver (good for Fewest silver). They: −5 followers, +5 silver (bad for Fewest silver; they can spend it later). Plunder takes 3 followers and 3 resources. Gain: about the same size, and the silver flow is the point.
3. Both sides: the rival has no choice; they are paid.
4. Who feels it: the leader, paid in silver that counts against them.
5. Not refused: Cory's direction; followers taken only from bought followers (verdict 40 holds).
6. Judged: a special silver use for the follower-buying plan; Silver's giving now has teeth.
7. Public and certain: bought followers and silver piles are public.
8. Color interactions: none; followers and silver are shared pieces.
9. Biggest case: round III, 5 followers from the leader and 5 silver dumped on them, Green copying it for 5 more. Well under 10.
Intuitive: buy a rival's followers with silver.
Fun: the leader is paid off in silver they don't want.
Interacts: moves followers and silver between two players.
Powerful: 5 followers and a contest swing.
Engine or counter: counters the follower leader.
Its color: Silver gives (here, pays) its way.
Verdict: send
