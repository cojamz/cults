# Brainstorm: Silver members, 6 open slots (redo with the confirmed principles)

Date: 2026-11-11
Asked by Cory: "then we need o generate new ideas here, given the updated actions and the color competition" (2:27pm); on Silver: "this is one where we probably need some new ideas" (2:29pm). Principles (confirmed 4:25pm): "complimentary to the color and any color would buy are different things", "ideally some of each. but not required." Each deck mixes On Recruit, activated, and 2 or 3 or more cards that score followers on their own (3:58pm); members memorable game to game; "jam people up" (Agents); nothing like the cloak (Caterer "Whenever you gift silver, +2 silver.").
Skill read: bash .claude/hooks/brainstorm-read.sh at 3:58pm Eastern (within 12 hours).

## Frame

- Contest: fewest silver held at round end (0 counts). Verb: give.
- Spaces: Toast "+4 silver. Each rival +2 silver. +1 silver." · Polish "+2 silver. Turn all your silver into one other color. +1 silver." · Party Gifts "Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank. +2 followers per silver given." · Flaunt "+1 of each other color per 3 silver you hold, up to 3 each. +1 silver." · Buyout "Recruit a rival's member, paying them its cost in silver. +1 silver." · Largesse "Give rivals 12 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +10 followers."
- The Silver player's round: take silver early (Toast), use it while holding it (Flaunt, Buyout, Polish), hand rivals silver (Toast, Party Gifts, Largesse), and end the round low. The pull: Flaunt pays for holding silver, the contest pays for holding none.
- What the spaces don't give it (the complementary test): ~~a way to turn its own leftover silver into followers~~ (wrong: silver buys followers 1 for 1 and pays for members like any color; Cory, Nov 11 7:08pm: "when you say shit like this it's genuinely baffling"); a payoff for rivals sitting on silver between gifts; protection from rivals handing silver back (Kingmakers in other hands, a rival's Toast).
- What other colors want at a Silver table (the cross-color test): a use for the silver they're handed, protection from it, or a payoff for being handed it.
- Members kept (Cory, 2:29pm): Kingmakers "Round End: give rivals up to 3 of your silver, split as you choose. +1 follower per silver given." · Patron Saints "Game End: +1 follower per silver member, anyone's." · Smooth Talkers "Your silver pays for any color." · Debutantes "On Use, once per game: double your silver. Each rival +2 silver."
- Deck mix today: On Recruit 0, activated 1 (Debutantes), scorers 2 (Patron Saints, Kingmakers), rule 1 (Smooth Talkers). The six slots need at least one On Recruit, one more activated, and two or more scorers.
- Real counts: a Silver player hands each rival about 2 to 5 silver a round (Toast 2 each, Party Gifts up to 3 total, Largesse 12 split in round III); rivals hold 0 to 6 silver at most round ends unless they spend it.

## Idea: Fairy Godmothers

1. Printed text: "On Recruit: give rivals any of your silver, split as you choose. +1 follower per 2 silver given." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Party Gifts (above, bank silver, +2 per), Kingmakers (above, your silver, up to 3, round end), Debutantes (above).
2. Played turn: round II, 4 players, you hold 7 silver after Toast and want the contest. Recruiting Fairy Godmothers: you give 4 to the rival tied with you for fewest and 3 to another, +3 followers, and you hold 0. The other member you could buy at that price, Kingmakers, gives at most 3 silver and 3 followers, and only at round end.
3. Both sides: you choose how to split; you load whoever is racing you for fewest silver. No dominated split.
4. Who feels it: the rivals handed silver lose ground in the contest at once (a sting); a non-Silver player who was handed silver can use it to clear it.
5. Not refused: not Caterer (gift pays silver); not Return the Favor (silver coming back); it gives your own silver, once, which no live card does.
6. Judged: complementary (turns Silver's leftover silver into followers, the gap above) and cross-color (anyone flooded with gifted silver clears it for followers). Silver's first On Recruit. Round I: a round II card by price; reads no members.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none named; silver is a resource every player can hold.
9. Biggest case: round III, Debutantes doubles 8 silver to 16, then recruit: 8 followers in one turn. Under 10. A winner's 70 to 110.
Verdict: cut (dominated: buying followers turns silver into followers 1 for 1, this pays 1 per 2; the gap it filled does not exist)

## Idea: Paupers

1. Printed text: "Round End: if you hold no silver, +3 followers." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: the contest (0 counts), Toast, Party Gifts, Largesse, Smooth Talkers (above).
2. Played turn: round II end, you were handed 2 silver by a rival's Toast; you spent it on a recruit (Smooth Talkers, or a silver member's colored part): +3 followers. Without it, spending the 2 silver only helped the contest.
3. Both sides: no choices on the card. The Silver player chooses whom to hand silver to: aiming at you stops it, at the cost of not aiming at the contest leader.
4. Who feels it: the Silver player, who now has a reason to load you; you, who must spend what you're handed.
5. Not refused: he disliked "held color" contests for most silver; this pays for holding none, the same direction as today's contest. Anchorites' shape ("Round End: if you have 3 or fewer members, +5 followers"), which he kept.
6. Judged: complementary (pays the Silver player for ending every round at 0, not only the winner) and cross-color (any player not using silver buys it). A scorer, his "members are victory conditions". Memorable: "she's a Pauper, don't give her silver… or do".
7. Public and certain: yes, everyone sees the silver you hold at round end.
8. Color interactions: none named; silver is a resource every player can hold.
9. Biggest case: 3 followers per round end, 9 a game over 3 rounds; a contest pays 4/6/10 and a winner ends near 70 to 110.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Chaperones

1. Printed text: "Round End: +1 follower per rival holding silver." (Claude's numbers; a Round Start version, "Round Start: +1 follower per rival holding silver.", passed the paper gate Nov 4 as Chaperone and was built in the idea pipeline, never shown to Cory). Cards it touches: Toast, Party Gifts, Largesse, Kingmakers (above).
2. Played turn: round II end, you Toasted (each rival +2) and gave a Party Gift; two rivals still hold silver: +2 followers. Round End rather than Round Start so it pays in round I too and reads the same moment as the contest.
3. Both sides: no choices on the card; rivals can spend their silver to deny it, which costs them a recruit's worth of choices.
4. Who feels it: rivals holding silver feed you; gives Silver a reason to spread gifts to all three.
5. Not refused: Gratitude ("+1 follower per silver your rivals hold") was refused for its size; this counts rivals, at most 3.
6. Judged: complementary (pays for the gifts Silver already makes) and cross-color in a Silver game (Silver's gifts reach every player, so a non-Silver player holding Chaperones gets paid by them). Scorer.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none named.
9. Biggest case: 3 per round end, 9 a game.
Verdict: cut (Cory, Nov 11 7:33pm: win more, "instead we need a member that benefits the opposite strategy")

## Idea: Loan Officers

1. Printed text: "On Use, once per round: give a rival up to 3 of your silver, then take as many of their resources, you choose which." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Polish (above, turns silver into one color), Help Yourself "Steal 1 resource per minion you've placed this round. +1 magenta.".
2. Played turn: round II, you hold 4 silver and need 3 red for a member; a rival holds 3 red. Use it: they get 3 silver, you get their 3 red and recruit. Polish would also turn your silver into red, but it takes a minion and a space.
3. Both sides: you choose the rival and the colors; the rival has no choice. The use always helps you; the cost is the sting you hand out.
4. Who feels it: the rival loses the colors they were saving and holds 3 more silver for the contest.
5. Not refused: Debt Collectors and Return the Favor pulled silver to you; this pushes it out. Not a member-taking card ("one spot taking rival members is enough").
6. Judged: complementary (turns silver into colors without a space, and loads a rival) and cross-color (anyone handed silver trades it for what they need). Activated.
7. Public and certain: yes, the silver and resources moved are all on the table.
8. Color interactions: Magenta also takes resources; this one pays for them. Names no color.
9. Biggest case: Cory's version (Nov 11, 7:33pm), all your silver: once per round with up to 5 silver, 5 resources a round, 15 a game; once per game with all of it, round III after Debutantes doubles 8 to 16, 16 resources if a rival holds that many (rivals hold 0 to 10 of other colors).
Verdict: send

## Idea: Gold Diggers

1. Printed text: "Whenever a rival gives you silver, +2 followers." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Toast, Party Gifts, Largesse, Kingmakers, Debutantes (above).
2. Played turn: round II, the Silver player takes Toast: each rival +2 silver; you take 2 silver and +2 followers. Without it, Toast's silver only hurts your contest count.
3. Both sides: the giver chooses whom on Party Gifts, Largesse and Kingmakers; with Gold Diggers out they aim elsewhere, which narrows their split. Toast and Debutantes give to every rival, so they can't avoid you.
4. Who feels it: the Silver player, whose gifts now pay you.
5. Not refused: Caterer (the giver's side, gift pays silver) he disliked; this is the receiver's side, paid in followers.
6. Judged: cross-color, not complementary (a counter in Silver's own deck, like Snitch in Magenta's, which he rated 9). Scorer. Memorable.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none named.
9. Biggest case: round III, Toast, Party Gifts, Largesse, Kingmakers and Debutantes all reach you: 5 gifts, 10 followers over the round, 2 per gift. Under the cap per turn.
Verdict: cut (Cory, Nov 11 7:33pm: "I don't love that")

7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none named.
9. Biggest case: no followers; it turns away up to 12 silver.
Verdict: cut (Cory, Nov 11 7:33pm: "weird and not good")
