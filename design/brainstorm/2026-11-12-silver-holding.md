# Brainstorm: a Silver member for the opposite strategy (holding silver)

Date: 2026-11-11 (7:40pm Eastern)
Asked by Cory (7:33pm): "instead we need a member that benefits the opposite strategy. You know what I mean? Like, that somebody might want even if they have silver or something like that. But not giving points based on silver?" Replaces Chaperones' slot. Paupers and Loan Officers kept (his calls).
Skill read: bash .claude/hooks/brainstorm-read.sh at 7:38pm Eastern.

## Frame

- Silver's contest pays the fewest silver held at round end. The other strategy holds silver: Flaunt "+1 of each other color per 3 silver you hold, up to 3 each. +1 silver." and Buyout "Recruit a rival's member, paying them its cost in silver. +1 silver." pay for holding it; any player handed silver by Toast "+4 silver. Each rival +2 silver. +1 silver." also holds it.
- Silver already spends like any resource (buys followers 1 for 1, pays for members), so "use your silver" is not a gap. What holding silver lacks: a reason to keep it past round end without losing the contest, and a payoff that is not followers per silver.
- Kept: Kingmakers, Patron Saints, Smooth Talkers "Your silver pays for any color.", Debutantes, Paupers "Round End: if you hold no silver, +3 followers.", Loan Officers (give a rival your silver, take as many of their resources; numbers open).
- Real counts: a holder ends a round with 4 to 10 silver; round III has 5 minions and a buy after each placement.

## Idea: Safekeepers

1. Printed text: "Round End, before scoring: put up to 5 of your silver on this card; it isn't yours until Round Start, when you take it back." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: the fewest-silver contest, Flaunt, Buyout, Toast (above), Paupers (above).
2. Played turn: round I end, you hold 6 silver and want Flaunt in round II. Put 5 on the card: you count 1 for the contest. Round II start, take it back: 6 silver, Flaunt gives +2 of each other color (6 resources). Without it you either count 6 at round end (last in the contest) or spend the silver in round I and lose the Flaunt.
3. Both sides: you choose how many to set aside, 0 to 5; more is never worse at round end, but it is a real number only at 5+ held. No rival choice.
4. Who feels it: Silver rivals racing for fewest silver now face a player who holds silver and still counts low; a Toast that loads you is cleared at round end.
5. Not refused: not a points-per-silver card (his 7:33pm limit); not the cloak "Whenever you gift silver, +2 silver."; not Gratitude's shape.
6. Judged: complementary (Silver can hold for Flaunt and Buyout and still contest, the pull the color has) and cross-color (anyone Toasted keeps their silver out of the count). Rule card, memorable ("she's hiding 5 silver"). Round I: a round II card by price.
7. Public and certain: yes, the silver sits on the card.
8. Color interactions: none named; silver is a resource every player can hold.
9. Biggest case: 5 silver out of the count per round, a contest swing of up to 5; no followers on its own. Contest pays 4/6/10.
Verdict: cut (Cory, 7:38pm: "so fucking complicated. Never say anything like that again.")

## Idea: Valued Clients

1. Printed text: "Members cost you 1 less per 4 silver you hold, up to 2 less." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Smooth Talkers (above), Flaunt, Toast (above), the contest.
2. Played turn: round II, you hold 8 silver from Toast and Flaunt's +1. A 6-cost member costs you 4, and you keep the 8 silver for the next recruit. The best other plan, spending the silver on the member, leaves you 2 silver and one recruit.
3. Both sides: holding 8 for the discount costs you the fewest-silver contest; spending it wins the contest and loses the discount. A real choice every round.
4. Who feels it: rivals racing you for face-up members (you buy more of them); Silver players whose gifts now make you cheaper.
5. Not refused: Big Spenders (pays silver to rivals on recruit) failed its set check Nov 4; this keeps the silver. Not points based on silver.
6. Judged: cross-color first (anyone handed silver wants it) and complementary to Silver's holding side (Flaunt, Buyout). Rule card. Round I: a round II card by price.
7. Public and certain: yes, the silver you hold is on the table and the discount is printed.
8. Color interactions: none named; any player holding silver uses it.
9. Biggest case: round III, hold 8: 2 off each of up to 5 recruits, 10 resources over the round, 2 per turn.
Verdict: cut (Cory, 7:41pm: "That's too good, but it's interesting")

## Idea: Tycoons

1. Printed text: "While you hold 5 or more silver, rivals can't take or discard your members." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Buyout "Recruit a rival's member, paying them its cost in silver. +1 silver.", Repossessors "On Use, once per game: discard a rival's member costing up to 8." (Cory's Nov 11 text).
2. Played turn: round II, you hold 6 silver and your best member is a 7-cost; a rival takes Buyout to recruit it. With Tycoons they can't, and they take Toast instead. Without it you lose the member and get 7 silver.
3. Both sides: you choose to stay at 5+ silver (losing the fewest-silver contest, 4/6/10) or spend below 5 and drop the protection. A real choice each round.
4. Who feels it: anyone holding Buyout or Repossessors, and the Magenta player if a member-taking card is out; it blocks them, the Agents shape Cory asked for ("jam people up").
5. Not refused: no ledger verdict; not points based on silver (his 7:33pm limit); not the cloak "Whenever you gift silver, +2 silver.".
6. Judged: the holding side of Silver (complementary to Flaunt and Buyout holders) and cross-color (any player with a member worth protecting). Rule card, memorable. Round I: a round II card by price.
7. Public and certain: yes, silver held is on the table and the rule is printed.
8. Color interactions: none named; it reads silver held and members taken by any card.
9. Biggest case: round III, it stops one Buyout of a 10-cost member, worth 10 resources plus that member's followers; holding 5 silver costs the 4/6/10 contest; no followers of its own.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Show-Offs

1. Printed text: "Round End: if you hold the most silver, +4 followers." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Paupers "Round End: if you hold no silver, +3 followers.", the fewest-silver contest, Toast, Flaunt (above).
2. Played turn: round II end, you hold 7 silver after Flaunt's +1 and Toast; the next most is 4: +4 followers, while you place last in the fewest-silver contest (0 for you). The other plan, spending down to 0, would win part of the contest instead.
3. Both sides: hold the most (Show-Offs) or hold the least (the contest, Paupers): you pick one each round. Rivals can hand you silver to help, or hand silver to another player to take the most from you.
4. Who feels it: the Silver player, whose gifts (Toast, Largesse "Give rivals 12 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +10 followers.") can make someone else hold the most.
5. Not refused: Cory said "not giving points based on silver? I don't know" (unsure, 7:33pm): this pays a fixed 4 for a position, not per silver. Flagged to him as the closest to that line.
6. Judged: the exact opposite of Paupers, cross-color (a Toasted player likes it). Scorer, memorable.
7. Public and certain: yes, everyone sees silver held.
8. Color interactions: none named; any player can hold silver.
9. Biggest case: 4 per round end, 12 a game over 3 rounds, against a contest's 4/6/10 and a winner's 70 to 110.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Fixers

1. Printed text: "Whenever a rival would steal from you, you may pay them 2 silver instead." (Claude's numbers). Cards it touches: Help Yourself "Steal 1 resource per minion you've placed this round. +1 magenta." and the other steal cards.
2. Played turn: round II, Magenta takes Help Yourself with 3 minions placed, about to steal 3 of your red you need for a member. Pay 2 silver instead: you keep the red, they get 2 silver (worse for them in the fewest-silver contest).
3. Both sides: you choose per steal: pay 2 silver or lose what they'd steal. Paying is better when the steal is 3+ or a color you need; worse when it is 1.
4. Who feels it: the Magenta player, whose steal turns into silver they didn't want.
5. Not refused: Cory asked Magenta for Bodyguards "defensive against getting stolen from, but doesn't block it outright"; this is Silver's version, paid in silver. Flag: it sits close to that Magenta rework.
6. Judged: cross-color (anyone at a Magenta table) and on Silver's verb (give). Rule card, memorable.
7. Public and certain: yes, the steal and the payment happen on the table.
8. Color interactions: Magenta's steal meets Silver's give; it names neither color.
9. Biggest case: Heist round III, a steal of 4 or more turned into 2 silver; saves about 4 resources a turn, 0 followers of its own.
Verdict: send

8. Color interactions: none named; any player holds silver.
9. Biggest case: round III, 12 against 0: a 12-silver swing and the 10-follower contest; no followers of its own.
Verdict: send
