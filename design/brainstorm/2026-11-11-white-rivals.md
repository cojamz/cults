## Idea: Bequest

1. Printed text: "Give a member to a rival for its cost in white. +1 white." (Cory's Bequest from Nov 8, "Give one of your members to a rival. Gain white equal to its cost, +3 followers. +1 white.", with the +3 followers dropped after his "white is a LOT of follower gain"; in Cards: Test today with the followers). Cards it touches: Estate Sale "Discard a member for its cost in white. +1 white."; Modest Means "+2 white. +1 white per rival with more members than you."; Cash In "Turn all your white into followers. +1 white."; Swindle (Magenta) "Steal a rival's member costing up to 7; give them a shop member of your choice in its place. +1 magenta."
2. Played turn: round II, 4 players; you hold 2 members (a 4-cost and a 6-cost), rivals hold 3, 2 and 4. Give the 4-cost to the rival with 2: +4 white and +1 white = 5. Estate Sale gives the same 5 white but the member goes to the discard pile. Gain over Estate Sale: 0 resources for you, but one rival now holds one more member: that rival drops in the Fewest members contest (you are now on 1 member, they are on 3), and next round your Modest Means counts one more bigger rival (+1 white).
3. Both sides: you choose the member and the rival. Member: the one whose ability you use least, weighed against white. Rival: give it to whoever ties you for fewest members (pushes them out of White's contest), or to whoever it helps least (not the Red player if it's red, not the Blue player who wants most members). The rival has no choice; nobody refuses a free member.
4. Who feels it: the receiver gets a gift (a member worth its followers plus its ability) and loses ground in Fewest members; the other rivals see one of them grow, a reason to take the space first when you hold a strong member. Worst case for a rival: a Blue or Red player handed a member that pushes them up in their contest is happy; a small-cult rival handed one is stung.
5. Not refused: Bequest is Cory's "one of the better ideas I've seen from this process… encourages member movement outside of red… makes sense for white" (Nov 8) → test set. Hand-Offs (twice the cost) was never pitched to him. Not Hand-Me-Downs (round III, up to 2 members, "figure out the right implementation", Nov 8; the Silver version on Nov 11 was "way too complicated"). Not Hand-Me-Down (shop member to a rival, "too similar", today).
6. Judged: one verb, give, done when taken; round II real state is 1 to 3 members, so it always has fuel when Estate Sale does. Intuitive: yes, the same shape as Estate Sale with a destination. Fun: a choice of whom to grow. Interacts: the receiver's contest and White's own. Powerful: same size as Estate Sale today. Engine: feeds Cash In and Modest Means. Its color: White gets smaller while others get bigger.
7. Public and certain: your members and every cult are face up; nothing from the pile, no randomness.
8. Color interactions: the shared moment is a member changing cults, which Red and Magenta also cause; names no color.
9. Biggest case: round III, give a 10-cost member: +11 white, then Cash In turns it into 11 followers on a later turn; Green copying Bequest needs its own member to give. Estate Sale reaches the same 11 today, so no new top; one turn's white is 11, but followers come only through Cash In on another placement. Against a winner's 70 to 110 and a contest's 4/6/10: the same size as live.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Clean House

1. Printed text: "Discard a member. Each rival discards a member of their choice. +2 white." (Cory's idea from Nov 8, 7:27pm: "you discard a member to make everybody else have to discard a member… a very destructive card"; the +2 white and name are Claude's fill-ins). Cards it touches: Farewell "Discard a member for its cost in followers. +1 white."; Estate Sale as above; Modest Means as above; Recluse (White member) "Round End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you."
2. Played turn: round II, 4 players; you hold 1 member (cost 3), rivals hold 3, 2 and 4. You discard your 3-cost; each rival discards their cheapest (about 2 to 4 cost, 1 or 2 followers and an ability each): 3 rivals lose about 5 followers and 3 abilities between them; you lose 1 or 2 followers and +2 white. Estate Sale gives 4 white and touches nobody. Gain over Estate Sale: 2 less white, and every rival loses a member.
3. Both sides: each rival picks which member to lose: always their cheapest or least-used one, so in effect it is "each rival discards their weakest member". You pick yours the same way. You have no reason not to take it when you hold a member you don't need and rivals hold good ones; with no members you can't take it usefully (you'd still get +2 white and rivals still discard: check this reading with Cory).
4. Who feels it: every rival, a sting; the player with the most members loses least in share; a rival with 1 member loses their only one. Reason to block: anyone holding one important member.
5. Not refused: Cory's own idea, on the ideas pile since Nov 8; no Clean House or "everybody discards" in the pitch ledger. Not Clean Slate (discard any number of yours, +3 followers each, pool only).
6. Judged: one sentence, done when taken; table state round II is 1 to 3 members each, so it always bites. Intuitive: yes. Fun: a big swing moment; risk of feeling bad (direct harm to everyone, not just the leader), which is the point of Cory's "very destructive". Interacts: every rival. Powerful: yes, by loss to others. Engine: counters big cults; feeds White's Fewest members only a little, since everyone drops by one.
7. Public and certain: every member is face up; each rival chooses in the open; no pile draws or randomness.
8. Color interactions: the shared moment is members leaving cults; names no color.
9. Biggest case: round III, rivals with 6 to 8 members each lose their cheapest (about 2 to 4 cost, 1 or 2 followers each, 3 to 6 followers across the table); you gain +2 white. Green copying it makes every rival (and you) discard again. No one gains 10+; the loss is spread, about 2 followers per rival per use.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Rival choice for a gift (cut)

1. Printed text: "+2 white. Each rival may give you 1 resource to gain 2 followers." Cards it touches: Party Gifts (Silver) "Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank. +2 followers per silver given."
2. Played turn: round I, 3 rivals each pay 1 for 2 followers: you +5 resources.
3. Both sides: 1 resource for 2 followers beats every follower card (1 for 1), so every rival always says yes: not a choice. At 1 follower each, nobody says yes.
4. Who feels it: everyone gains.
5. Not refused: Care Packages ("That's a lot of followers. I don't like this one.") is the same giving shape.
6. Judged: fails step 3.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none.
9. Biggest case: 6 followers to rivals and 5 resources to you every round.
Verdict: cut (no real choice; Care Packages' shape)

## Idea: Dues (cut)

1. Printed text: "Each rival with more members than you gives you 1 resource of their choice. +1 white."
2. Played turn: round II, 3 bigger rivals: +4.
3. Both sides: the rivals choose which resource, always their least needed.
4. Who feels it: every bigger rival, a small sting.
5. Not refused: same count as Modest Means ("too similar" risk) and a take from rivals is Magenta's verb.
6. Judged: doesn't grow by round (kill rule 5).
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none.
9. Biggest case: +4 in round III, flat.
Verdict: cut (Modest Means' count, Magenta's verb, flat)
