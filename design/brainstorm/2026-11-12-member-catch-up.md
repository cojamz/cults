# Brainstorm: catch-up when one cult is far ahead in members (Red and Yellow game)

Date: 2026-11-12
Asked by Cory: "im playing a yellow and red game, its very hard to catch up when somebody is way ahead in members"
Skill read: bash .claude/hooks/brainstorm-read.sh at 7:25pm Eastern

## What the games show

Red's contest, Most members of one color, and Yellow's, Most member colors, both pay the player with the most members. Yellow's Melting Pot ("For each color among your members: +1 follower and +1 of that color (grey: 1 of any color).") and Offering ("+5 yellow. Each rival discards 2 of each color among your members.") pay that same player again, and Red's Borrow, Late Shift and Initiation recruit for free. Hard-bot games from Nov 11 and 12 (21 four-player games with Red and Yellow): a player 3 or more members ahead after round II went on to win 9 of 17 games (53%; 25% is an even share). Across all 120 games that number is 39%. Small sample; it points the same way as Cory's game.

Catch-up already in a Red and Yellow game: Red's Promote and Demote (swap a member with another cult), grey Purifiers, and Consolation Prize ("Round End: +2 followers per contest you don't win."). White's Recluse ("Round End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you.") and Modest Means are not on this table.

## Idea: Mutineers

1. Printed text: "On Use, once per game: take a member costing up to 6 from a rival with more members than you." Grey member, round II deck, 6 any, worth 1 (Claude's fill-ins). Cards it touches: Swindle "Steal a rival's member costing up to 7; give them a shop member of your choice in its place. +1 magenta."; Demote "+3 red. Swap any member for one costing up to 3 less, from another cult or the shop."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round III, you hold 3 members, the leader holds 8 including one that costs 6. You place on Gather (+3 red) and use Mutineers: you take that 6-cost member, its followers and its effect, and the leader loses one member toward Red's and Yellow's contests. The best other use of the turn, Late Shift ("Recruit a shop member costing up to 2 per minion you've placed this round, this one included, for free."), gives a 6-cost member from the shop but takes nothing from the leader. Gain: the same member plus a 2-member swing between you and the leader in both contests.
3. Both sides: you choose the rival (only among those with more members) and the member. The rival has no choice. Taking from the leader is always best when the leader is the only one ahead; with two rivals ahead, you choose the one whose contest lead you can break, a real choice.
4. Who feels it: the leader loses a member they paid for (a sting, up to 6 resources and its followers), and the gap in Red's and Yellow's contests closes by 2. Players behind the leader also gain, since the leader's contest totals drop. The leader has a reason to block nothing (it's a member, not a space), but a reason to fear buying past the holder.
5. Not refused: pitch-ledger Nov 9 had Foreclose (put a leader's member in the shop) and Purifiers (discard; Cory Nov 12: "purifiers might be eh"); Gift Swappers ("crazy"). Taking one member from a player ahead of you, once, is a different shape from everyone discarding or everyone passing; verdicts.md has no no on a once-a-game take.
6. Judged: one trigger, one plain effect; reads right the first time. Feels powerful (a free 6-cost member). Interacts directly with the leader. Counters the member engine Cory named. Round I: it's a round II card, so it never asks a round I table to hold members. Meets the bar on its own.
7. Public and certain: every member and every count is face up; no pile draws.
8. Color interactions: none named; grey, so it shows up in any game. It meets Red (Promote and Demote move members too) through members leaving cults, the moment Martyrs and Leavetakers pay on.
9. Biggest case: round III, right after the leader recruited a 6-cost member: you take it, about 3 followers for you plus a 2-member swing in two contests worth 10 each. Red's contest winner changing is the largest swing: 10 followers. One use per game. Against a winner's 70 to 110, that's a comeback, not a blowout, and it can't repeat.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Hopefuls

1. Printed text: "Round Start: if a rival has 3 or more members than you, recruit a shop member costing up to 5 for free." Grey member, 5 any, worth 1 (Claude's fill-ins). Cards it touches: Late Shift "Recruit a shop member costing up to 2 per minion you've placed this round, this one included, for free. +1 red."; Borrow "Recruit the cheapest shop member, for free. It returns to the shop at round end, before scoring. +1 red."
2. Played turn: 4 players, start of round II, you hold Hopefuls and 1 other member, the leader holds 5. You recruit a 5-cost member for free before anyone places, the same as one Late Shift on your third minion, without spending a minion. Gain: one free member a round while you're 3 behind, about 5 resources.
3. Both sides: you choose which shop member; no choice for rivals. Taking the member that best helps your contest is always right, which is a real choice among the shop.
4. Who feels it: a gift to the player behind; the leader sees the best cheap shop member gone at Round Start. No sting.
5. Not refused: no ledger line on a free recruit gated by a member gap. Late Bloomers (a bot-test card) pays on fewest followers, not members. Different piece: the shop, not followers.
6. Judged: one trigger, one effect, plain. Powerful enough to feel (a free member twice). Counters the member lead by building yours, without hurting anyone, which Cory has liked ("Never make it feel BAD"). Weaker as interaction: the leader does nothing about it.
7. Public and certain: counts are public; the shop is face up.
8. Color interactions: none named; grey. It meets Red and Yellow through recruiting, which both colors pay for.
9. Biggest case: bought in round I, behind by 3 at the start of rounds II and III: 2 free members of up to 5 each, about 10 resources over the game, or 4 to 6 followers counting their worth. Under one contest's 10.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Two contests at most

1. Printed text (rule): "Each player can win at most 2 contests a round. A third goes to the next player in it." (Claude's fill-in.) Touches every contest, here Red's "Most members of one color" and Yellow's "Most member colors".
2. Played turn: 4 players, round III end, the leader is first in Red's, Yellow's and Blue's contests (10 each, 30 total). Under the rule they take 20 and the second player in Blue's takes 10. Gain for the second player: 10 followers; the leader's lead shrinks by 20.
3. Both sides: which 2 the leader keeps: the rule needs an order (cloak order, Cory Nov 4), so no choice; if the leader chose, they'd keep the biggest, which are all equal.
4. Who feels it: the leader (a sting of 10 in round III), the runner-up (a gift). Felt every round by the leader.
5. Not refused: no ledger line on a contest cap. Different from Consolation Prize, which pays the loser on a card.
6. Judged: a rule, not a card; easy to say, but it punishes winning a contest, which Cory has called core ("they're kind of core to… what's getting rewarded from your color"). It makes a player stop contesting a third color, which reads as a penalty for doing well. Fails "feels powerful" and "never make it feel bad" for the leader.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: touches every color equally; no shared moment.
9. Biggest case: round III, a 10-point contest moves from leader to runner-up: a 20-point swing between them.
Verdict: cut (penalizes winning contests, which Cory calls the core of each color)

## Idea: Fewest members places first

1. Printed text (rule): "From round II, fewest members goes first." (now fewest followers).
2. Played turn: in Red and Yellow games the member leader is usually the follower leader too, so the order rarely changes.
3. Both sides: no choices.
4. Who feels it: almost nobody; the order mostly stays.
5. Not refused: no ledger line.
6. Judged: changes little at the table.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none.
9. Biggest case: one better first pick a round; under a contest's 4/6/10.
Verdict: cut (changes almost nothing in these games)
