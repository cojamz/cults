## Frame
- Contest: most members. His sentence: "get through the whole deck, buy from anywhere" (deck, discard pile, a rival, always paid), plus its own engine.
- What the game already has a lot of (live board, all colors): taking or swapping rivals' members (Red Promote/Demote/Swindler/Rabble-Rousers/Pied Piper, Magenta Sleight of Hand/Poach/Changeling/Briber/Press Gang, Silver Hire Away, Yellow Exchanger, Blue Go-Between); free recruits (Red Borrow, Call to Arms, Induct, Governess; Yellow Benefactor); discarding your own members (White Bid Farewell, Executioner, Martyrs, Evictors; Blue Purifiers); copying (Green); resources (Magenta, Silver, Yellow).
- What is rare: anything that pays for where a member came from; any space that reaches more than one source at once; anything that pays a rival for selling (only Go-Between, Hire Away). The discard pile is public and fills every round (the old shop row goes there between rounds, plus White's and Blue's discards), so it is the public half of "the whole deck". The draw pile is face down, so "see the deck" can't be a card under the public-information rule.
- Complement: White wants fewer members (sells or sheds them); Blue wants more (buys them). Buying from a rival pays the seller, so a White player is glad to be bought from.
- Band: round II 4 to 6 or a member changing hands (Promote "+3 red. Swap any member for one costing up to 3 more, from another cult or the shop."); round III a burst (Poach "Recruit a rival's member you choose, for free. +1 magenta.", Induct "Recruit a shop member, for free. +1 red.").

## Idea: Buy Anywhere

1. Printed text: "Recruit a member now from the shop, the discard pile or a rival's cult, paying its cost (to that rival, if it's theirs). +3 blue." (round II space; +3 is a fill-in.) Cards it touches: Bring Back "Recruit a member from the discard pile now. +3 blue."; Hire Away "Recruit a rival's member, paying them its cost in silver. +1 silver."; Go-Between "On Use, once per round: recruit a rival's member, paying its cost to that rival."; Scavengers "Whenever you recruit from the discard pile, +2 followers."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round II, you hold 9 resources and 2 members. The discard pile holds round I's old row (6 members costing 2 to 6); a rival holds a 7-cost member. Here: +3 blue (12), recruit the best of all three places (say the rival's 7-cost member), then your normal recruit from the shop (5 left: a 4-cost member). Two members this turn. Best other space, Promote: +3 red and a swap up one step, no extra member. Gain: one extra member of your pick (worth 1 to 2 followers, its ability, +1 toward Blue's most-members count) and wider choice than Bring Back, whose pile can be empty of anything good.
3. Both sides: you choose the place. Shop vs pile vs rival: the shop and pile take members nobody holds; a rival's member also takes it away from them, but pays them. No choice is always best: it depends on what's in each place that turn. No rival choice on the card.
4. Who feels it: the rival bought from loses a member and its ability but gains its full cost in resources (7 resources is 7 followers through follower cards later, or a recruit). A White player (fewest members) is helped. Everyone else has a reason to block the space in round II.
5. Not refused: pitch-ledger Blue lines: Gleaner "recruit from the discard pile as you would from the shop" (cut Nov 25) was an always-on member; Go-Between's "whenever" version was "way too good", once per round built. This is a space, taken once by one player per round. Verdict 25 refused "buy a rival's member by paying its cost" as a Red x Blue tie; this is pitched as Blue's own verb (his Nov 26 "buying from a rival… fits blues idea of buy from anywhere"), not as a tie.
6. Judged: Intuitive: one verb, three named places. Fun: you look at three places and pick, the planner's turn he described. Interacts: takes from rivals with payment; competes with Silver's Hire Away and Red's swaps for rivals' members. Powerful: an extra member of your choosing in round II. Engine or counter: feeds Scavengers, Blue's contest, Spread the Word. Its color: Blue's "buy from anywhere" in one card. Clears the bar on its own; round II tables hold 1 to 3 members so the rival option is real.
7. Public and certain: the shop, the discard pile and every cult are face up. No pile draw.
8. Color interactions: White sheds members, Blue buys them (shared moment: a member leaving a cult; Chapman "Whenever one of your members leaves your cult, +2 followers." and Mourners "+2 white" pay the seller). In a game without White it still works on any rival.
9. Biggest case: round III, after Dusk Buyer and Homecomers filled your pile options, with Scavengers: +3 blue, one member (worth up to 3) and +2 followers = about 5 followers' worth plus an ability, against a winner's 70 to 110 and a contest's 4/6/10. Green Replay All copying it gives a second recruit, still paid for, so capped by resources (round III players hold about 10 to 20). Under 10.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Scour the Village

1. Printed text: "Recruit one member each from the shop, the discard pile and a rival's cult. Pay the rival for theirs. +5 blue." (round III space; +5 is a fill-in.) Cards it touches: Salvage "Recruit two members from the discard pile now. +7 blue."; Poach "Recruit a rival's member you choose, for free. +1 magenta."; Induct "Recruit a shop member, for free. +1 red."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round III, you hold 14 resources. +5 blue = 19. Shop 6-cost, pile 5-cost, a rival's 7-cost: 18 paid, three members, then your normal recruit with 1 left (a 1-follower card). Best other space, Poach: one rival's member free (worth about 7 resources) and your recruit with 14. Gain: about 2 more members than Poach at full price; Poach saves resources, this spends them. Real choice by how rich you are.
3. Both sides: each place is a may in practice (you pay for each), so a poor player takes one or two. Which places: each once, so you can't take three from the richest source; that is the "anywhere" point. No rival choice.
4. Who feels it: one rival loses a member and is paid. Blue's most-members contest (+10 in round III) swings by up to 3 members. Others want to block the center.
5. Not refused: Salvage's rework "Discard the whole shop and refill. Then recruit two members from the discard pile now. +5 blue." was "no." (Nov 26): that was a shop wipe plus pile recruits; this has no wipe and spreads over three places. Not Poach (free) or Induct (free).
6. Judged: Intuitive: one line, three places, each once. Fun: the round III burst for a planner who set up all three places. Interacts: rival bought from; contest swing others see coming. Powerful: three recruits in round III, where the recruit is the scarce thing. Engine or counter: feeds Scavengers (up to +4), Blue's contest, Blue Bloods. Its color: buy from anywhere as the finale. On its own it clears the bar.
7. Public and certain: all three places face up.
8. Color interactions: same shared moment as Buy Anywhere (a member leaving a cult pays Chapman, Mourners, Martyrs if discarded later). Works with any rivals.
9. Biggest case: round III with 20 resources, Scavengers held, Green Replay All copying it: three members (up to about 6 followers of worth) + 4 followers from Scavengers + a +10 contest swing; the copy is capped by what's left (about 2 resources). About 10 to 14 followers' worth counting the contest, on round III's center, which the rule allows (10+ is cut only outside round III's center). Salvage's numbers today are weaker (7 fewer wins in 100).
Verdict: send

## Idea: Scavengers (change)

1. Printed text: "Whenever you recruit a member from the discard pile or a rival's cult, +2 followers." (was "Whenever you recruit from the discard pile, +2 followers.") Cards it touches: Go-Between, Buy Secondhand "Recruit a member from the discard pile now, paying 2 less. +3 blue.", Homecomers "On Recruit: +3 blue, then recruit from the discard pile.", Dusk Buyer "Round End: +3 blue, then you may recruit from the discard pile."
2. Played turn: round II, you hold Scavengers and Go-Between. Use Go-Between on a rival's 6-cost member: +2 followers today, where the live card pays nothing. Best other member in Blue's engine slot, Gravedigger "Whenever shop members go to the discard pile, +1 follower.", costs 10.
3. Both sides: no choices on the card.
4. Who feels it: nobody directly; rivals see Blue reaching into cults more often, so it is a reason to hold Warden "Your other members can't be swapped." (it doesn't stop buying) or to block Blue's spaces.
5. Not refused: Scavengers (Thrift Hunters) was his "pretty good… I do like Thrift Hunters"; this widens it to his "buy from anywhere". Not a refused shape.
6. Judged: Intuitive: one trigger, one gain. Fun: makes every off-shop buy a small payday. Interacts: pays for buying from rivals. Powerful: steady. Engine or counter: this is the engine that ties Blue's two sources together. Its color: rewards buying anywhere but the shop.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none new; single-color payoff.
9. Biggest case: round III, with Scour the Village (2 off-shop recruits, +4), Go-Between (+2), Dusk Buyer (+2) = about +8 in a round, at most +4 in one turn. Under 10.
Verdict: send

## Idea: See the deck (cut)

1. Printed text: "Round Start: discard shop members until one of a color you name shows." Cards it touches: Clear Out, Culler.
2. Played turn: round II, reveals 1 to 6 cards, random.
3. Both sides: you name a color; no rival choice.
4. Who feels it: rivals lose shop members at random.
5. Not refused: shape close to pile draws he has refused many times.
6. Judged: random, fails the bar.
7. Public and certain: fails, the cards come off a face-down pile in random order.
8. Color interactions: none.
9. Biggest case: 6 shop members discarded, 6 blue to a Blue Cloak holder (verdict: "+6 resources for the blue robe player? come on").
Verdict: cut (random draws from the face-down pile; "the whole deck" can only mean the public discard pile under his rule)
