## Idea: Houseguest

1. Printed text: "Take a rival's member costing up to 7 until round end; it counts for you in this round's contests and you may use its abilities, then it goes back. Give its owner 3 silver from the bank. +1 silver." (cap 7 and 3 silver are Claude's fill-ins). Cards it touches: Borrow: "Recruit the cheapest shop member, for free. Discard it at round end, before scoring. +1 red."; Buyout: "Recruit a rival's member, paying them its cost in silver. +1 silver."; Guest of Honor: "On Use, once per round: give a rival one of your members. +4 followers."
2. Played turn: round II, 4 players. The Red player leads Most members of one color 3 red to your 2 red, and holds a 7-cost red member. You take it: you lead 3 to 2 at round end and win Red's 6, they lose it, and they hold 3 more silver (Fewest silver pays 6, so they must spend it or fall back there). Best other round II space: Toast "+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III." gives +6 silver. Gain: a 6-point contest swing plus one ability use, against 6 silver that counts against you in Fewest silver.
3. Both sides: you choose the rival and the member; the rival has no choice. The member always comes back, so the owner never loses it for good.
4. Who feels it: the owner (a sting: they lose that member for this round's contests and abilities, softened by 3 silver that also hurts their Fewest silver); any player racing that contest. Reason to block: hold the space if you lead a contest by one member.
5. Not refused: pitch-ledger Silver lines and verdicts.md checked; nothing borrows a rival's member for a round. Borrow (Red) takes from the shop and keeps nothing; Buyout keeps the member for good and costs your silver; Swindle and Poach take for good.
6. Judged: one verb (borrow a member), on Silver's theme of hosting (a guest comes to stay, the host is thanked with silver), and member movement Cory liked on Buyout. Round II real table: rivals hold 2 to 7 members, so a target exists; with the cap at 7 most round II members qualify. Feels powerful when it flips a contest, weak when no contest is close: flag that. Tracking: the card sits in front of you with a token until round end.
7. Public and certain: every member, its cost and every contest count is face up; nothing is drawn or hidden.
8. Color interactions: the shared moment is a member changing cults for a while, which Red (Promote, Borrow), Magenta and Blue also cause; the card names none of them.
9. Biggest case: round III, Green copies it with Cover "Copy an action with a rival's minion on it. +1 green.": two players each take a 7-cost member for the round. Each swings at most one contest (10 in round III) plus one ability (Guest of Honor's +4 followers is the largest On Use in Silver at 7 or less), so at most about 14 for one player and only when a contest is within one member; usually 0 to 6. Against a winner's 70 to 110.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Pawn

1. Printed text: "Gain silver equal to one of your members' cost. At round end, before scoring, pay that much silver back, or that member goes to the shop. +1 silver." (Claude's text). Cards it touches: Estate Sale: "Discard one of your members. Gain its cost in white. +1 white."; Polish: "+3 silver. Then turn all your silver into one other color."; Buyout: "Recruit a rival's member, paying them its cost in silver. +1 silver."
2. Played turn: round II, 4 players, you hold Debt Collectors (cost 9). You gain 9 silver (plus 1). Next turn you take Buyout and recruit a rival's 9-cost member with that silver; at round end you can't repay, so Debt Collectors goes to the shop, where anyone can recruit it in round III. Best other round II space: Toast's +6 silver. Gain: 10 silver now (3 more than Toast's 6 plus 1), and a real choice at round end.
3. Both sides: only you choose, at round end: repay (keep the member, and end near 0 silver, good for Fewest silver) or forfeit (keep the silver's value, lose the member to the shop). Each is right in different spots: repay when the member is worth more than the silver; forfeit when you already spent it on something better. Neither option always wins.
4. Who feels it: rivals, when a forfeited member lands in the shop for them to recruit (a gift to whoever moves first in round III); a rival whose member you buy with the pawned silver. A weaker sting than Houseguest: say so.
5. Not refused: no loan or pawn in the pitch ledger or verdicts.md. Closest live card is Estate Sale (White); different because you keep the member if you repay, and a forfeited member goes to the shop, not the discard pile.
6. Judged: one verb (borrow silver against a member), on the silver-as-money side of Silver, and it ties to Fewest silver: repaying drains you to 0 at round end. Round II real table: everyone holds 2 to 7 members, so it always works. Weaker than Houseguest on "who feels it"; flag it as the weaker of the two.
7. Public and certain: the pawned member is marked face up with a token; the amount owed is its printed cost.
8. Color interactions: none: a single-color card about Silver's own pile and its contest.
9. Biggest case: round III, you pawn an 11-cost member for 11 silver plus 1, then Polish turns 12 + 3 = 15 silver into 15 of one color, bought as 15 followers (one 10 and one 5 card), and you lose the member (worth 2) before scoring: about +13 across two spaces. Green copying Pawn takes silver against the copier's own member. In band against a winner's 70 to 110.
Verdict: send
