# Brainstorm: grey members, unique and interactive like Ghost

Date: 2026-11-25
Asked by Cory: "we need to aim for kind of unique but interactive members that tie to their theme. like ghost is super unique. a lot of the greys really. Think of other examples along these lines to show me that you understand the principle or the quality that I'm talking about."
Skill read: bash .claude/hooks/brainstorm-read.sh at 8:50pm Eastern

The quality: Plague Bearer (his Ghost) "Worth 0 members. Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers." breaks one rule everyone assumes (owning a member is good), so every swap, gift and steal at the table becomes a way to hurt someone with it. Lord of the Manor "You go first every round." breaks turn order; Spoilsport "Round End: choose a contest; nobody wins it this round." breaks contests; Jester "Counts as a member of every color." breaks colors. One short line, one basic rule bent, and the bend changes what the other players do; the name says the bend.

Real game numbers used below: a 4-player winner ends near 70 to 110 followers; contests pay 4/6/10 in rounds I/II/III.

## Idea: Hanger-On

1. Printed text: "Worth −4 followers. Round Start: it joins the cult with the most followers (on a tie it stays)." 5 any (Claude's fill-in, Ghost's price). Compared with Plague Bearer: "Worth 0 members. Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round II, P3 is 12 followers behind the leader P1. P3 recruits Hanger-On for 5 (P3 −4 for now). Round III starts: it joins P1, so P1 −4 and P3 +4 back: an 8-follower swing between them for 5 resources. The best other buy for 5 is the 5-follower card, +5 to P3 only; here P3 closes the gap by 8 instead of 5.
3. Both sides: no choices on the card; it moves by itself. The leader can't refuse it. Buying it while leading only hurts yourself, so only a trailing player buys it.
4. Who feels it: the leader, a visible sting at round start; everyone watches the follower counts in round II to see who will catch it. Gift to the trailing buyer.
5. Not refused: pitch-ledger "Hanger-On +1 any when a rival recruits a 6+" (Oct 28) was a different card; verdicts.md 40 forbids subtracting followers a table can't take away: this is a member's worth, like Plague Bearer's −5, which Cory kept. Harm points only at the leader (design principles).
6. Judged: on its own: intuitive (a hanger-on attaches to whoever is doing best), fun (a hot seat everyone watches), interacts with the whole table; a first-time player reads it once.
7. Public and certain: follower counts are public; no pile draws, nothing hidden or random.
8. Color interactions: none: a grey card; it reads only follower counts.
9. Biggest case: bought in round II, it moves once at round III start: −4 to the leader, +4 back to the buyer, an 8-follower swing at most, against a winner's 70 to 110 and a contest's 10. Round I: nobody can afford it.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Brownie

1. Printed text: "Round End: +3 followers. Then, if you have the most followers, it goes back to the shop." 5 any (Claude's fill-in). Compared with Comforter: "Round End: +2 followers per contest you don't win."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round II, P4 in last recruits Brownie for 5: +3 at round II end and stays (not the leader); +3 more at round III end: 6 followers for 5, against the 5-follower card's 5. If P1 (leading) buys it: +3, then it goes back to the shop for anyone.
3. Both sides: no choices on the card. The leader may still buy it for one +3 and to keep it from a rival, then hands it to the shop.
4. Who feels it: a gift to whoever is behind; the leader feels it leave; the shop gets it back for the next player.
5. Not refused: not a win-more card (it leaves the leader); no running discounts, tokens or pile draws; Comforter is the closest live card and pays per lost contest, a different trigger.
6. Judged: intuitive (folklore: a brownie leaves a house that gets proud), fun (a member that won't stay with the rich), catch-up; reads once.
7. Public and certain: follower counts and the shop are public; nothing hidden or random.
8. Color interactions: none: a grey card that reads only follower counts.
9. Biggest case: bought in round II by a trailing player: +3 at round II end and +3 at round III end, 6 followers in all, against a winner's 70 to 110 and a contest's 10.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Hag

1. Printed text: "On Use, once per round: a rival's member does nothing this round." 6 any (Claude's fill-in). Turned sideways on the table, as used-up members are.
2. Played turn: 4 players, round III, P1 holds Trumpeters "Whenever you win a contest, +2 followers." and Lord of the Manor "You go first every round."; P2 uses Hag on Trumpeters before round end: P1 loses +2 per contest won, about 4 to 6 followers. Best other use of the turn's buy is a 5-follower card; Hag takes 4 to 6 from the leader instead.
3. Both sides: you choose the member: always the rival's best engine, which is a real read of the table (round-end payers vs. On Use members).
4. Who feels it: the rival whose member is turned off, one round at a time; everyone with a strong member watches the Hag holder.
5. Not refused: no ledger entry turns a member off; it does not make a rival act on your turn; no token (sideways turning already exists for used members).
6. Judged: intuitive (a hag curses someone's helper), fun, aims at the strongest cult; reads once.
7. Public and certain: the turned member is public; no randomness.
8. Color interactions: none: works on any member of any color.
9. Biggest case: round III, turning off the strongest round-end member: Trumpeters at 3 contests won, 6 followers, or Comforter's up to 8; under a contest's 10, against a winner's 70 to 110.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Lord of Misrule

1. Printed text: "Round End: choose a contest. The lowest count wins it this round." 8 any (Claude's fill-in, Spoilsport's price). Compared with Spoilsport: "Round End: choose a contest; nobody wins it this round."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round II end, Most gold: P1 6, P2 4, P3 2, P4 0. The holder P4 flips it: P4 wins 6 followers instead of P1. Spoilsport would only stop P1 and give P4 nothing.
3. Both sides: the holder chooses the contest; they pick one where they are lowest, or where a rival leads by a lot. A real choice each round between helping yourself and stopping the leader.
4. Who feels it: the leader of the chosen contest loses the prize; whoever has least of it wins; everyone feels it at each round end.
5. Not refused: verdicts.md 30 (no cards built on who leads a contest) is about reading the leader as a condition; this changes the scoring rule and works on any counts, ties shared as usual. Same family as Spoilsport, which Cory likes ("I like spoilsport").
6. Judged: intuitive (the Lord of Misrule turns the village upside down for a day), fun, a big visible moment; reads once.
7. Public and certain: counts are public; no randomness.
8. Color interactions: none: it works on any contest.
9. Biggest case: round III end, one contest's 10 moves to the lowest player: at most 10 followers to the holder, the same as one contest, against a winner's 70 to 110.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Scarecrow (cut)

1. Printed text: "Rivals can't place on a space next to your minions."
2. Played turn: round I, 4 players, 12 spaces and 12 minions: every round-I space fills, so blocked spaces leave rivals with nowhere to place.
3. Both sides: no choices.
4. Who feels it: everyone, too hard; the draft breaks.
5. Not refused: not in the ledger, but misreads a piece of the game (verdicts.md 1).
6. Judged: breaks round I.
7. Public and certain: yes, the wheel is public.
8. Color interactions: none.
9. Biggest case: round III with 5 minions each it closes up to 10 spaces: the wheel can run out of spaces.
Verdict: cut (every round-I space fills; blocking breaks placement)

## Idea: Changeling swap (cut)

1. Printed text: "Round Start: swap this with a rival's member of the same cost."
2. Played turn: the same shape as Swindler "swap yours for a rival's dearer one each round".
3. Both sides: the holder picks the rival and the member.
4. Who feels it: the rival, as Swindler already does.
5. Not refused: Swindler is live and Changeling is a live name: same shape counts as the same idea.
6. Judged: not new.
7. Public and certain: yes.
8. Color interactions: none.
9. Biggest case: one member a round, about 2 to 3 followers.
Verdict: cut (same shape as Swindler)

## Idea: Waylayer

1. Printed text: "Whenever a rival places a minion next to one of yours, steal 1 resource from them." Magenta, 3 magenta + 4 any (Claude's fill-in). Compared with Cutpurse: "Whenever you recruit, steal 1 resource."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round II, 20 spaces, 4 minions each; your 4 minions border up to 8 spaces. Rivals take about 3 of those over the round: +3 magenta, against Cutpurse's 1 per recruit (about 4 a round with 4 recruits, but nobody feels it). Here the table chooses: take the good space beside you and pay 1, or go elsewhere.
3. Both sides: the rival chooses where to place: paying 1 for the better space is a real choice; you choose to sit beside the spaces rivals want.
4. Who feels it: every rival who places beside you, a small sting they choose to pay; it makes where you place matter twice.
5. Not refused: not a cut steal shape (Cory Nov 10 "enough steals" was a round I space, not a member); nobody acts on your turn: the rival is placing on their own turn and the steal happens there with no choice by them.
6. Judged: intuitive (a waylayer robs whoever passes by; Highwayman is a live name, so not that), fun, interacts through the wheel; gives resources as Cory asked; reads once.
7. Public and certain: minions on the wheel are public; nothing hidden or random.
8. Color interactions: none: works with any colors on the wheel.
9. Biggest case: round III, 5 minions each, up to 10 bordering spaces and 15 rival minions: about 6 resources, 2 to 3 followers' worth, against a winner's 70 to 110 and a contest's 10.
Verdict: send

## Idea: Tinker

1. Printed text: "Round End: +2 followers per color of space your minions are on." 3 any + 4 any as a grey (7 any, Claude's fill-in). Compared with Juggler: "On Use, once per game: +3 followers per turn in a row you've taken just before this one."
2. Played turn: 4 players, round II, 4 minions: on four different colors +8 at round end, on two colors +4. The pull: the fourth minion goes to a weaker space of a new color (+2 from Tinker) instead of the best space of a color you already hold.
3. Both sides: no choices on the card; every placement is a real choice between the best space and a new color.
4. Who feels it: rivals, as you spread out and take one space in every color, sometimes the one they wanted; you feel the pull on each placement.
5. Not refused: verdicts 41 (a count that collapses as the table fills) does not apply: your own minions, always placeable; scoring reads visible state (minions on the wheel at round end), not events.
6. Judged: unique, simple, a scoring condition that steers placement without naming an action; a tinker goes everywhere in the village.
7. Public and certain: minions on the wheel are public; nothing hidden or random.
8. Color interactions: none: it reads the four colors on any wheel.
9. Biggest case: round III, 5 minions on 4 colors: +8 a round end, 8 + 8 = 16 over rounds II and III, against a winner's 70 to 110 and a contest's 10; never 10+ in one turn.
Verdict: send
