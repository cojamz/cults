# Calibration run 3: version 2 on 17 held-out calls (Nov 7 10:02pm Eastern)

Plan fixed before reading: run3-plan.md. Method as the readers saw it: run3-method-as-given.md. Three fresh readers, each saw only the method, the rulebook and holdout-calls-blind.md.

**Panel (majority first choice) matched Cory's pick on 8 of 17 calls (47%; 95% range about 26% to 69%).**
- Chance (picking at random among each call's options): about 32%.
- Counting his back-pocket likes as picks: 9 of 17 (53%).
- Each reader alone: 8, 8 and 7 of 17.
- Bar was about 4 in 5. Not met.

Readers agreed with each other far more than in runs 1 and 2: all three gave the same first choice on 10 of 17 calls. Five of those 10 unanimous calls were wrong. So the misses are not noise; the method points the wrong way in specific places.

## Right (8): C03 Silver members (Kingmakers / Ballroom), C05 Red starter (Drifter), C07 guest members (Champion / Big Spender), C08 Yellow round I (keep both), C09 White round I, C13 Magenta round I (Inside Job), C14 Blue round III (Spree), C16 Purple member (Night Manager).

## Wrong (9), and what each says
1. Kept the old card too rarely. C02 Silver round III: all three picked Grand Gift "Give a rival a shop member you choose. Gain followers equal to its cost." over keeping High Tide; Cory: "these are bad except high tide". C06 Green fuel space: all three picked "do its On Recruit twice" over keeping Build Up; Cory kept Build Up. C15 Purple: all three picked Runner "Whenever you take two turns in a row, recruit once more on the second." over Courier; Cory: Runner "No", keep Courier. The method says the current card stays unless the new one is clearly better; readers still swapped. Fix: make the current card the default answer, and require a named, larger moment to replace it.
2. Top case missed a "too strong" card. C12 White members: all three picked "Whenever you recruit followers, +1 follower per white member you have."; Cory: "so strong… What the fuck is this?". Fix: the top-case check must play the card's best round III turn with numbers, not describe it.
3. A hard rule was misread. C01 Silver round II: readers ruled out Sweet Nothings ("Give rivals up to 3 of your silver, split as you choose") as "a choice on another's turn", but the owner makes the choice on their own turn. Cory picked it. Fix: the rule says who chooses and when, with an example of each side.
4. No "none of these" answer. C04 Green round II: Cory refused the whole list; every reader picked one. Fix: "none of these" is always an option, and weak-only lists get it.
5. No context the readers could see. C11 Yellow round III was Cory's own idea read back to him. C10 Yellow member split three ways (one reader found his pick). C17 Blue member went to a back-pocket card he liked.

## What this means
Version 2 is a better way to explain a card than version 1, and readers now agree with each other, but on calls it has never seen it finds Cory's pick about half the time. It can't stand in for his taste. Two honest uses: fix the four points above and test again on a new set of unseen calls (this set is now used up), or use it only to sort and explain, with Cory making every pick.
