# Members as piles: does counterplay go up?

Prices are today's (Party Crasher and Anarchist were 4 and 7 when the games were played). Bot games on the 10-card deck, 4 players, tables of 4 colors drawn from the redesigned colors without Blue. Each game was played three times on the same seed: once with today's shop, once with Grey piles, once with All piles. 200 games each with Normal bots, 28 each with Hard bots (Hard is slow; Very Hard was not run).

## What each version is (Claude's build, for you to change)

- **Grey piles**: five grey members are out every game as face-up piles of 3 copies. Anyone can recruit the top copy, and the next one turns up. The other grey members are not in the game. The colored members are the normal shuffled deck and row, as today. In these games, piles of round II and III members opened in their round; since your call at 3:32pm every pile is open from round I.
- **All piles**: no deck. Ten piles a game, drawn at random: 2 from each color on the table (one round I member, one later) and 2 from the grey five. 4 copies each in these games; 10 copies since your call at 3:31pm.
- **The grey five** (my pick from today's 10 grey members, the ones that reach rivals; your call):
  - **Party Crasher** (5): "On Use, once per game: your next minion can be placed on a space a rival already took."
  - **Anarchist** (6): "On Use, once per round: swap any 2 members, in any cult or the shop, that cost within 2 of each other."
  - **Con Men** (7): "Counts as 2 members. On Recruit: steal 5 resources."
  - **Old Maid** (5): "Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers."
  - **Line Cutters** (2): "You go first every round."
- Two copies of the same member never swap with each other (it would change nothing except setting off "joins your cult" again, so Old Maid could be fired at a rival for free). This is the one rule I added.

## Results

**Players hit each other much more often with piles.** With today's shop, a game has about 18 turns (Hard) or 20 turns (Normal) where a player's move costs a rival resources, members or followers. Grey piles: about 31 (Hard) and 33 (Normal). All piles: about 25 (both).

**Almost all of that is Con Men.** With three Con Men out from round II, every player buys one and steals 5 from the others: about 56 resources stolen a game against about 30 today. When I swapped Con Men out of the grey five for **Tiebreaker** ("You win every contest tie you're in.") and replayed the 200 Normal games, stealing fell to about 26 a game, a little under today. What stayed up without Con Men: followers lost to Old Maid (about 6 a game against 3.6 today) and players placing on a rival's taken space with Party Crasher (about 2 a game against 0.6).

**The hits don't go to the leader any more than today.** In every version, about a third of the hits land on whoever leads in followers at that moment. Piles make the table rougher but don't make players gang up on the leader.

**Whether the round II leader goes on to win is mixed.** Hard bots: 54% today, 32% in both piles versions. Normal bots: 31% today, 35% Grey piles, 27% All piles. 28 Hard games is a small sample, so this is the number to watch in your own games, not a finding.

**Winning with 5 or fewer members** (the bar is at least 20%): today 7% (Hard), 10% (Normal). Grey piles 7% and 5%. All piles 18% and 14%, the closest of the three.

**All piles: players stack copies.** A player held two or more copies of about 10 members a game. When Con Men was one of the ten piles, it was again the member winners stacked most. Top scores drop by about 8 followers in both piles versions, because more gets stolen.

## A played turn (Normal bots, Grey piles; Silver, White, Yellow and Purple on the table)

Round II. The shop shows the round II piles for the first time, Con Men among them.

- P3 recruits Con Men for 7 and steals 5: 1 purple from P1, 2 white and 1 silver from P2, 1 yellow from P4. The next copy turns up.
- P1 recruits Con Men and steals 5, 4 of them from P3. Later in the round P1 buys the third copy and steals 5 more.
- P3 takes **Farewell** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain followers equal to its cost. +1 white."): casts out Con Men for 7 followers. The copy goes back on its pile, so P3 recruits it again for 7 silver and steals 5 again.
- P1 does the same with **Estate Sale** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain white equal to its cost. +1 white."): 7 white for casting Con Men out, then buys it back and steals 5 (4 silver from P3). In round III P1 repeats it with Farewell for 7 followers and another 5 stolen.

What this shows: with piles, a member you cast out comes straight back to be bought again, so casting out and rebuying Con Men is a repeatable steal. Today a cast-out member goes to the bottom of a 40-card deck and is gone for the game.

## Self-copies checked (tests in the game)

- One cult with two **Line Cutters** goes first; two cults with one each keep their usual order.
- Two tied players who both hold **Tiebreaker** share the tie as usual; one cult holding two still wins its ties.
- A second **Party Crasher** used while the first is waiting is handed back unused.
- Two copies never swap; an **Old Maid** copy can still be pushed onto a rival for a different member.
- Whole bot games with every member as a pile of 3 play to the end with every card accounted for.
