# "By color" contests against the printed contests

Setup: deck 10, the same bots and code for both runs (0f94cfcd). Each run was 400 four-player, 100 three-player and 100 two-player games with Normal bots, plus 120 four-player games with Hard bots. The numbers are in numbers.md and the six game logs are in packet-*/.

The By color contests in this run:
- Red: the priciest member.
- Blue: the most members.
- White: the fewest members (at least 1).
- Magenta: the most resources, all colors together.
- Yellow: the most full sets (one of every color in play).
- Green: the most minions on Green spaces.
- Silver: the most silver.
- Purple: whoever holds the Goes Last token. This is the older rule. The game now gives it to whoever placed the round's last minion, so this run doesn't tell us anything about Purple.

## What held up (a critic, then a skeptic)
1. **No color takes over.** Red is the strongest main color under both settings: +18 points over an even share with By color, +12 with the printed contests. The gap between the two is within noise. Blue is the one color the change clearly moves, from -5 to +8 with Normal bots, but Hard bots show it flat (+10 to +12).
2. **Players don't focus on fewer colors.** A cult holds 2.7 colors with 57% of its members in its biggest color under both settings, with Normal and Hard bots alike.
3. **Winners recruit more.** A winner averages 8.1 member cards with By color against 7.1 with the printed contests. Winners with 5 or fewer member cards fall from 32% to 25%, still above the 20% bar. This is a table-wide shift and can't be pinned on one contest.
4. **Green's contest is usually shared.** 6 of the 9 Green round-ends in the logs were split between 2 or 3 players. Nobody won Green in all three rounds in 198 games (4% did with the printed contests).
5. **White's "fewest members" often goes to the same player every round.** One player takes it all three rounds in 16% of games, against 7% printed. That player wins 37% of games (an even share is 25%).
6. **Blue's contest swings games with Normal bots.** One player takes all three Blue contests in 16% of games and wins 88% of those. Hard bots don't show it (the sweeper won 2 of about 6 games).
7. **Feel.** A round-III comeback (the winner 10+ followers behind going into round III) happens in 18% of games against 13% printed. Gifts and late hits on the leaders rise slightly.

## Dropped by the skeptic
- Magenta paying the thief twice: one game, and the contest wasn't bigger than that game's margin.
- Yellow ties: one game, and its sweep rate didn't change.
- Purple fought over: the rule tested is no longer the game's rule.
