# Color cost test: 1 of every member's "any" cost moved to its own color

Claude, Nov 2. Bot games on the 10-card deck, 4 players, random tables of the 2.0 colors, the same games played both ways (Normal bots 320 games each, Hard bots 48 each; games with a buy-followers-only seat left out). Nothing changed in the real game.

Rough: today's bots play from pre-written choices that underplay Yellow, Silver and Magenta cards. These get re-run once the new bots land.

What changed, today → with the color cost (Normal bots; Hard bots in brackets):
- Member cards at game end: 7.2 → 6.7 (7.0 → 6.4). Members got harder to buy, so cults are a bit smaller.
- Winners with 5 or fewer members: 7% → 13% of games (6% → 12%). Closer to your 20% bar.
- Colors in a cult: 3.0 → 2.85 (2.9 → 2.7). Slightly narrower.
- A cult's biggest color share of its cards: 40% → 40% (40% → 41%), winners the same. Going deep in one color still doesn't win more than spreading out.
- Followers: about 72 both ways. Fewer come from members (15.5 → 14.4), more from follower buys and abilities (34.8 → 35.8): resources a player can't spend on a member go to followers instead.
- Main color win share (even is 25%): Red's lead shrinks (41% → 30%); Purple rises (26% → 46%, from 57 and 50 seats). Hard bots' color numbers are too few seats to read.

What it means at the table: when you're short a member's color, you buy followers with what you have instead. The 1-for-1 follower buy absorbs the change, which is why cults shrink but don't focus.
