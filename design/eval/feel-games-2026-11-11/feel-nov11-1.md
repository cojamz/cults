Fun in the round diaries: round I average 3.75 (Ben 3, the rest 4), round II average 4.0 (Ava 5, Dee 3), round III average 4.0 (Ben 5, Cal 3).

Where the play backs the score:
- **Dynamic 5** is earned. Every diary in every round names a rival's move that changed the plan, and members kept moving: Heist Crew and Dabblers each changed hands three times, and five more members were pulled off or pushed onto a rival.
- **Satisfying choices 4** fits rounds II and III (33 of 36 placements tagged hard) but not round I, where half the placements were obvious and the last one was forced. Ben said it himself: "round I was thinner."

Where the tags run hotter than the play:
- **Round III's 19 of 20 "hard"** is probably high. Ava's diary counts 1 of her 5 as "close to obvious" and Ben's counts Stash as "fairly obvious", though both tagged hard. Cal tagged all 5 hard but wrote "the dilemma was mostly about guessing Ben's Spoilsport target", so several of his hard turns were one guess repeated, not five different dilemmas.
- **Cal's Modest Means**: tagged hard all three rounds, but in the postgame he wrote "I took it every round without thinking." His reasoning at the time does weigh it against Help Yourself each round, but the answer was the same space three times. Treat it as a habit for the one player with no members, not a dilemma.
- **Ben's recruits**: his choice score of 4 is about his placements. His recruits weren't choices: he bought single followers turn after turn because the White Cloak paid him 2 white each time.

Impact 3 from Cal and Dee holds up. Cal: "my turns were steady +4 to +11 but the big swings… were other people's." Dee's swings were real (Poach, Scouts) but undone within a lap.

## 4. Spaces

Verdicts are from this one game.

### White
- **Modest Means**: taken all 3 rounds, always by Cal, who had no members and so got +5 every time. Nobody else wanted it ("with members it's a weak +2-3"). The decision it gave Cal was when to take Help Yourself instead, not whether to take this. *Always taken* (by the one player it fits).
- **Splurge**: Ava in round I (the forced last pick, +3 followers off Counterfeiters) and round II (+10 followers off Double Agent, the biggest single space payout of the game). Empty in round III, when it drops back to 1 follower per 2 resources. Players loved round II ("the only space that felt like a jackpot", Ben) and called it a sideline otherwise. *Working*, but the excitement lives in round II only.
- **Nest Egg**: Cal in round I (tagged obvious, "flat +3"), empty in round II, Ava's last placement in round III (to afford Old Guard). Rated 1-2 by everyone. *Never wanted*: taken only as filler or when it was what was left.
- **Farewell**: Cal in round II (undoing Ben's gift, tagged obvious), Ben in round III (cashing out Repossessors for 8). Both were answers to something a rival or the player had just done, and both swung a contest or the lead. *Working.*
- **Write-Off**: Ben in round II as an attack (his favorite move), Cal in round III purely as a block, which he regretted ("Write-Off on my first placement for nothing, just to block gifts"). Giving a member away as a weapon was a surprise to Cal at first. *Working*: the attack use is a highlight; the block use was a wasted minion this time.
- **Cash In**: never taken in its one round. Cal: "with buy-followers at 1:1 it only saves a recruit." Dee: "white piles never got big enough by round III." It pays what buying followers already pays, and a member (Cashier) did the same for free. *Never wanted.* The kind of change that might help: give it something buying followers can't do.

### Blue
- **Test Drive**: Cal in rounds I and II, both times on Accomplice for a steal of 3; empty in round III when nothing in the row was worth it. Ben: "later it had little worth using." Strong when the row has a good On Recruit card, nothing when it doesn't. *Working*, swingy with the shop.
- **Out of Style**: first pick of the game (Dee, tagged obvious, about 7 resources with his Blue Cloak), Dee again in round II, Ben's last placement in round III (its +4 won him the most-resources contest by one). Without the Blue Cloak players called it "a small mixed gain"; with it Dee called the pair "maybe too strong". *Working*; watch the Blue Cloak pairing.
- **Markdown**: taken every round (Ben for Anchorites at 4, Ava for Trendsetters, Ben for Repossessors at 6). Each take was a hard call that depended on what sat in the discard pile, and it set up the game-deciding play. Ben and Ava were both unsure whether it used up their normal recruit. *Working*, one of the best spaces in the game.
- **Recruit Twice**: Ava in round II (also to deny Dee the last blue recruit space), Dee in round III (Martyrs from a pile he'd just filled with Scouts). Ava: "a race for it each round"; Ben: "used it… without much thought." *Working.*
- **Word of Mouth**: Dee in round II, Ava in round III, +5 and +6 followers. Weighed often as the safe option ("the safe pick I almost always weighed", Ava). Nothing for small cults. *Working*: quiet and reliable.
- **Spree**: one take, Ava in round III, and she regretted it. +7 blue only covered one 8-cost member, so "two discard recruits" played as one. Dee: "the pay-full-price rule made it weaker than it reads." Rated 2-3 by all. *Feel-bad*: it promises more than it pays. Rests on one take.

### Magenta
- **Help Yourself**: taken every round (Dee, Cal, Dee). Its steal grows with how many minions you've placed, so when to take it was a real decision, and Cal took it in round II only to keep Dee from a steal of 4 on the last turn. With Desperado it stole 4. *Working*, one of the best decision spaces.
- **Stash**: Ben in rounds I and III, empty in round II. Everyone rated it 1-2 as "plain +3", but twice it was the top-up that held Ben's resource lead. *Never wanted* for itself; useful only for contest math.
- **Catch Up**: Ben in round I (tagged obvious, +5), Ava in rounds II and III (+6 each). "Made being broke fine" (Ben). *Working.*
- **Heist**: first pick of round II (Dee), second pick of round III (Ben, right after Poach). Both times aimed at the leader, Ava, for 4 followers and 4 resources. The thinking around it was real (Heist vs Melting Pot, Heist vs a Write-Off trick), but it never lasted past the second pick. Ava: "it hit me twice and it's a feel-bad but a real swing." *Working*, close to *always taken*.
- **Swindle**: Ben in round II (took Dabblers, handed Dee Extortionist), Ava in round III (took Line Cutters, handed Dee the Ghost for −5). Highest-rated space in the game; three of four called its turns the table moments. It was also the sharpest feel-bad: Dee lost a 3-member card and 5 followers in one placement. *Working*; the Ghost-as-replacement pairing is where the feel-bad comes from.
- **Poach**: one take, Dee's first pick of round III, taking Heist Crew back. Dee's best moment; Ben: "big but only one player gets it." *Working*; one take.

### Yellow
- **Harvest**: taken every round (Ava, Dee, Dee), each time for a color-specific buy (Last-Minute Shopper, Desperado). Rated 2-3: "nobody's first choice." Dee's last Harvest was his regret: 3 resources but he could only buy 1 follower with one recruit. *Working* as a quiet utility.
- **Survey**: taken every round (Dee, Ben, Cal). Pays more when one color is crowded, and Ben took it once just to keep Cal from a resource jump. *Working.*
- **New Blood**: one take, Ava in round I, where it paid +5 with her Yellow Cloak. Empty in rounds II and III. Everyone: "only good for Ava with her Yellow Cloak." *Never wanted* after round I.
- **Housewarming**: empty in round II (Cal weighed it, but a free member would have cost him the fewest-members contest), Cal in round III for a free Cashier that cashed 5 white. "Mostly there was no cheap new-color member to take." *Too few games to say*: great when the shop has a target, nothing when it doesn't.
- **Melting Pot**: Ben in round II, Dee in round III, both tagged obvious (+4 followers and resources for 4 colors). Dee tried to save it for later in round II and lost it to Ben. Dee noticed that Dabblers counted as 5 colors here but as 1 in the colors contest. *Working*; for a wide cult it's an easy yes rather than a dilemma.
- **Makeover**: one take, Cal in round III, mostly for the +4. "The recolor rarely mattered." Rated 2 by all. *Never wanted* for what makes it different; a weak center space in this game.

## 5. Members players called out

- **Spoilsport** (8 any, round end: cancel a contest): decided the game by cancelling Cal's +10. Ava, Cal and Ben all named it, or the fight over it, as the thing that decided the game; Ava and Cal named it the first thing to change ("one 8-cost card decided the game"). Ben: "worth far more than 8 in round III." From the receiving end, Cal: "one card wiping a whole round of play feels bad." *Underpriced* in round III, and *feel-bad* for whoever it targets. The kind of change: limit what it can cancel or when.
- **Repossessors** (put a rival's member costing up to 8 back in the shop): the counter to Spoilsport, then 8 followers at Farewell. Everyone rated it fun 5. *Working*: a real answer card.
- **Anchorites** (+5 at round end with 3 or fewer members): 15 followers over the game for 4 resources off Markdown, and it carried the winner's small-cult plan. Ben: "maybe too much for its price." *Underpriced.*
- **White Cloak** (not a member, a starting boon: +2 white per follower buy): it made "buy 1 follower" the right recruit nearly every turn for the winner. Ben: "turned my recruits into a no-brainer… I'd change the White Cloak first." *Always taken*: a recruit with no decision in it.
- **Blue Cloak** with Out of Style and Scouts: Dee's Scouts wiped an 8-card shop for 8 resources, "a 7-cost, 2-member body was effectively free." Flagged as maybe too strong by Dee.
- **Heist Crew** (worth 4, counts as 4 members): changed hands three times. Fought over, but Cal: "the ping-pong got silly." *Working*, though it's the clearest case of members feeling temporary.
- **Ghost** (−5 followers when it joins you): nobody would buy it; it exists as a Swindle payload. Dee's −5 was the game's sharpest feel-bad. Unanimous fun 4-5 from the players who weren't Dee.
- **Double Agent**: Ava's best turn. Dee: "brutal." *Working.*
- **Last-Minute Shopper** (round end: +3 blue, then recruit): tied two round III contests for Ava after Dee could no longer answer. Cal was surprised you can recruit after the round's last placement. *Working*; slightly *unclear*.
- **Members that count as 3 or 4** (Line Cutters 4, Window Dressers 5, Trendsetters, Alchemists 7, Martyrs): Ava: they "dominate the Blue and White contests for their price." Line Cutters at 4 for 3 followers and 3 members was called "a bargain" by three players. *Underpriced* as a group.
- **Dabblers** (counts as 2 wild members): passed around as a weapon. Three players were unclear how "wild members" count (1 color in the colors contest, 5 on Melting Pot). *Unclear.*
- **Counterfeiters** (turn a rival's resources into one color): used every round, and players said it never changed a contest or a purchase. Ava regretted using it. *Overpriced*: nothing happened at the table when it fired.
- **Never bought, full-set and small-trigger cards** (Jack-of-All-Trades, Seamstress, Matchmaker, Duty Collector, Critic, Old Money): "full sets never came up." *Never wanted.* Cal: "a lot of the shop is never bought." In this game 21 of the 54 members that showed were ever recruited, and several were sent to the discard pile by shop-clearing before anyone could buy them.

## 6. The game as a whole

- **Best round: II.** 14 of 16 placements hard, Ava's fun 5, Ben: "This was the good round." The white gift and discard spaces (Write-Off, Farewell) and the magenta member spaces (Heist, Swindle) opened, and members started moving.
- **Weakest round for choices: I.** Half the placements were obvious, Dee's three were all obvious, and with 12 minions for 12 spaces the last placement was forced. Ben's fun was 3: "the contests I could reach were all taken by others and I mostly bought followers." Fun was still fine overall (average 3.75), mostly from the steal war.
- **Round III as a payoff: split.** Ben (small cult and cloak paid off) and Dee ("a bigger, different round") said yes. Ava and Cal said no. Ava: "round III was won by take-that cards (Poach, Heist, Repossessors, Spoilsport), not by what I built." Cal: "round III was mostly round I with +10 contests; my zero-member plan never built into anything, it just repeated." The game was decided by a member that only appeared at round III's deal, not by anything set up earlier on the wheel.
- **Comeback:** the round II leader (Ava, 41) did not win; Ben, 4 behind her, did. Dee climbed from 20 to 45 but finished last. Dee: "nobody behind had a real path to first."
- **Clarity surprises** (from the players' own words): whether a discard-pile recruit (Markdown, Spree) uses your normal recruit; how grey and wild members count in the colors contest; that Last-Minute Shopper recruits after the round's last placement; that one recruit buys only one batch of followers.

## Five findings that matter most (ranked)

1. **The member-swapping spaces are why players want to play again, and also why building felt pointless.** Swindle, Write-Off, Heist and Poach scored highest and produced every highlight, and every diary named a rival's move that changed its plan. But Heist Crew and Dabblers each changed hands three times and five more members were moved by rivals; Dee lost three in one round ("rolling a boulder up a hill") and Cal said "building anything feels pointless." The sharpest feel-bad was Swindle handing over the Ghost for −5 along with a stolen 3-member card. The kind of change that might help: fewer ways for the same card to bounce back and forth, or a softer worst case for the victim of a swap. One game.
2. **One shop member, Spoilsport, decided the game, ahead of any wheel space.** Cancelling a round III +10 was worth more than any engine, and the fight over it (Spree to grab it, Repossessors to take it back) took over round III. Two of the four named it the first thing they'd change, and the player it hit called it a feel-bad. The kind of change: limit what it can cancel in round III.
3. **White's round III space didn't work.** Cash In was never taken, because it pays what buying followers already pays and a member did the same thing for free. Nest Egg was filler and Modest Means belonged to the one player with no members. White's interesting spaces are Write-Off and Farewell. The kind of change: give Cash In something buying can't do.
4. **Round I is thin.** 12 minions fill all 12 spaces, so the last placement has no choice. Half the round's placements were tagged obvious, and the three blue spaces went in the first three picks (Out of Style with the Blue Cloak was an obvious first pick). The kind of change: more spaces than round I minions, or a less automatic blue opening.
5. **A few spaces promise more than they pay, and some counting rules surprised players.** Spree ("two recruits" that played as one at full price), New Blood (only the Yellow Cloak player could use it, and only in round I) and Makeover (its rearrange never mattered) were rated 2 to 3 by everyone. Clear scores of 3 came from discard-pile recruits and the normal recruit, and from how grey and wild members count in the colors contest. On the member side, the White Cloak turned the winner's recruits into automatic single-follower buys, and Anchorites (+15 for 4 resources) carried the win. All from one game.
