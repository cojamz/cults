# Feel report: two games, Nov 11 (batch 2)

**Game A** (Red, Green, Purple, Silver): Dee 121, Ava 80, Ben 78, Cal 74.
**Game B** (Yellow, White, Red, Purple): Ben 69, Ava 58, Cal 56, Dee 53.

Four experienced players in each game. Each one marked every placement as hard (two or more options were close), obvious (one was clearly best) or stuck (every option was poor), wrote a diary after each round, and scored the game at the end. Red and Purple appeared in both games. Green and Silver appeared only in Game A, Yellow and White only in Game B, so anything said about those four colors rests on one game.

---

## 1. The counts

### How players marked their own placements

Counts are hard / obvious / stuck.
- Round I (12 minions): Game A 4 / 7 / 1. Game B 4 / 7 / 1.
- Round II (16 minions): Game A 9 / 6 / 1. Game B 7 / 9 / 0.
- Round III (20 minions, plus the extra ones from Overtime): Game A 15 / 7 / 1. Game B 15 / 6 / 0.

Hard turns go from one in three in round I to about two in three in round III, in both games.

By player:
- Game A, round I: each player had exactly 1 hard placement. Ava's third minion was stuck. Round III: Dee had 5 hard and 1 stuck. Everyone else had 3 or 4 hard.
- Game B, round I: Ben (3 obvious) and Dee (2 obvious, 1 stuck) had no hard turns at all. Ben had 0 hard in round I and 2 in round II, then 4 of 5 hard in round III once members started moving.

### How full the wheel got
- Round I: all 12 open spaces were taken in both games, so the last minion of the round always took whatever was left. Every stuck turn in round I was a last minion: Ava's Right Away in Game A, Dee's Borrow in Game B.
- Round II: 16 of 18 spaces taken. Left empty in Game A: Crunch Time, Toast, Flaunt, Buyout. Left empty in Game B: Right Away, Crunch Time, Borrow, New Blood.
- Round III: Game A left only Last Call empty, and only because Protesters blocked it. Game B left Borrow, New Blood and **Cash In** empty.

### Did a rival's move change their plan?
In the diary question "did a rival's move change your plan", 22 of 24 diaries said yes and named the move. The two exceptions were both in round I of Game B: Ben ("nothing else a rival did changed my plan") and Ava (she said Ben's lead pushed her toward members, but nobody took a space she wanted).

### Copies (Game A only)
- Party Gifts was copied 4 times in rounds I and II, by Echo, Cover and Reprise, for +6 followers each time.
- In round III, Largesse was copied once (+10). Greatest Hits on Red was copied twice, and each copy also did Initiation and Promote again. Overtime was copied twice by members (Fan Club, Buskers).

## 2. The scores checked against the play

- **Dynamic** (did rivals change your plans) was scored 5 by 7 of 8 players. The diaries back this up (22 of 24). This score is trustworthy.
- **Satisfying choices** was scored 4 by most players. That holds for rounds II and III but not round I, where only a third of turns were hard. Ben in Game B gave 3 and was honest about why: "rounds I and II were almost all obvious white-space-and-buy turns." He marked 2 of his 4 round II turns hard, but his diary says "the white spaces still decide themselves for me." I trust the diary.
- **Fun** averaged about 4. The winners gave 5 (Dee in Game A) and 4. Players who were hit hard gave 3: Cal in Game A ("having my last three set-ups cancelled with no answer felt bad"), and Ben in round I of Game B while he was leading ("every turn was the same decision").
- **Impactful** shows the real split. In Game A, the winner gave 5 and the other three gave 2 or 3, all for the same reason: "most of them only cancelled a rival's move" (Ava). Their big turns moved points among the three chasers, not toward catching the leader.
- **Comeback** was scored 2 or 3 by everyone. The play matches: in both games, the player leading after round I won.
- **Clear** was scored 2 to 4. Every low score names a specific misread (see section 6).
- Cal in Game A marked two turns "obvious" that turned out to be traps. Overtime was copied twice to take the Goes Last token from him, and his On a Roll lost the token the same way. An "obvious" mark from him does not always mean the turn was simple.

## 3. The moments

**Felt great**
- Game A, Ben, round III: "every turn was a real decision, members flew around between cults, and the last three placements decided second place, which I'll tell people about tomorrow, even though I lost it."
- Game A, Ava, round II: "Late Shift/Anarchist was hard and the most interesting turn of the game." She took a free Anarchist and swapped back the Dabblers that Dee had just taken.
- Game B, Ava, round II: "the Ghost passing around and the Red tie fight felt like a real table, everyone reacting to everyone."
- Game A, Dee, round I: "holding the end of the round and cleaning up two contests felt great, though Sleep In meaning I just waited felt passive in the middle."

**Felt bad**
- Game A, Ava, round I: "Right Away did literally nothing since I had no turn after it."
- Game B, Dee, round I: "Holding the token meant my last two minions waited until every good space was gone, so the token's price is that you get the leftovers."
- Game A, Cal, round II: "watching Dee buy 20 followers off purple while my plays got copied or reversed was a feel-bad."
- Game B, Ben, round I, while leading: "the plan worked and I'm leading, but every turn was the same decision."

**A race for one space**
- Game A, Ben, round II: "Party Gifts was clearly the space to race for: it got copied three times this round and four times total."
- Game B, Cal: Late Shift made "the round turn into a race to place your third minion first."
- Game A, round III: Greatest Hits. Dee took it, then Ava (Reprise into Echo) and Cal (Cover) copied it in the same lap.

**A plan changed by a rival**
- Game A, Cal, round III: "three of my set-ups were undone in the last six turns by copy and cancel cards and I couldn't answer any of them."
- Game B, Dee, round III: "Ava's Overtime took the token and sent my 4 minions back into normal order, Ben's Demote swapped my Courier for Scouts." She ended up "just buying 5 followers."

**Swings**
- Game A, Dee, round III: Buskers copied Cal's Overtime to take back the Goes Last token. She called it "a 26-point swing from one member use."
- Game B, Ben, round III: his Spoilsport cancelled the Went last contest, which cost Ava 12 (10 plus Cheerleaders 2). That was his winning margin.
- Game B, Ava, round II: Demote swapped Cal's Deprogrammers (worth 4) for the Ghost (−5), so Cal lost 9 in one move. Cal passed the Ghost to Ben with Write-Off on his next turn.

## 4. Spaces, one line each

### Red (both games)
- **Borrow**: In Game A it was taken every round, each time to borrow a member with an On Use payout before it left at round end (Guest of Honor twice, Patrons once): "a neat trick." In Game B it was taken once, as a stuck last space, and was empty in rounds II and III: "the leftover space nobody wants" (Dee). *Never wanted* on its own. It only works when the cheapest shop member has a once-a-round or once-a-game use.
- **Late Shift**: Taken every round in both games, nearly always on a player's third or fourth minion. Players raced to it because a free 6-cost member on a third minion beats everything else in round I. *Working*: when to take it is a real timing decision.
- **Gather**: Game A: taken every round as a fallback for red, never paid its +4 followers, and was a stuck pick once ("nothing open changed any contest"). Game B: paid +4 twice in rounds II and III (Ava with Foster Parents, Cal) and was marked hard. *Working* from round II when you already own a member to match. Plain filler in round I.
- **Promote**: Taken in rounds II and III of both games, nearly always marked hard. All 8 players rated it 4 or 5. It carried every Ghost move and the Spoilsport grab. *Working*: the space players liked most.
- **Demote**: Same takes and same marks as Promote. Ava's Ghost-for-Deprogrammers swap (−9 to Cal) and Ben taking it "mainly as a block" both came from here. *Working*.
- **Initiation**: The first placement of round III in both games (Ben, Cal). Marked hard both times, but Cal said "the first player in round III always takes it." In Game A its real effect came from Greatest Hits copying it 3 times. *Always taken*, first.

### Green (Game A only)
- **Build Up**: Taken every round, mostly to win the green contest. "Scaled to +5 or +6 green late with no decision behind it" (Cal). *Working* as the contest space. It is weak as a choice.
- **Echo**: Taken every round, mostly right after Party Gifts or Largesse. Players called these copies their best turns, but the copy is obvious once the big space has just been played. *Working*, though it is what makes Party Gifts too strong (see Silver).
- **Reprise**: Taken every round. It built the round III chain, Reprise into Echo into Greatest Hits. "Fun but hard to read" (Ben). *Working*.
- **Crowd**: Taken in rounds II and III for +3 to +5 followers. "Fine filler." *Working*, low excitement.
- **Cover**: Taken in rounds II and III, both times to copy the best space of the round (Party Gifts, Greatest Hits). *Working*, but it makes the same space pay twice.
- **Greatest Hits**: Round III, taken once, copied twice. All four players named it the moment of round III. *Working*, too few games to judge how big it is in general.

### Purple (both games)
- **Sleep In**: Taken every round in both games. Each time a player took it to grab the Goes Last token early, a rival took the token straight back with On a Roll: Cal (A, round II), Cal (B, round I), Ben (B, rounds II and III). Three of them listed it as their regret. Ben: "If I had taken Modest Means first and On a Roll second… Dee couldn't have gone back to the token." *Working* as a resource space. As a token grab, it is a trap whenever another token space is still open.
- **On a Roll**: Taken every round in both games, and it decided Went last in most rounds: "whoever moved second in the token fight won it" (Ben, B). *Working*, and it decides who goes last more than any other space.
- **Right Away**: Taken in 5 of 6 rounds, mostly as filler or a forced last space. Ava's round I turn was stuck: "did nothing since I had no turn after it." Scored 1 or 2 by most players. *Feel-bad* on a last minion. Otherwise it only matters when paired with Lookouts or Hot Streak.
- **Last Call**: Taken early in rounds II and III (Dee). "Huge first pick (+8 purple, +4 followers) but the token rarely survives." *Working*.
- **Crunch Time**: Round III only. Taken twice, once as filler that "did nothing" (Cal, A) and once by Dee to set up Lookouts bonuses (B). Scored 1 or 2. *Never wanted*.
- **Overtime**: Round III, taken once in each game. Copied twice in Game A to steal the token, and it flipped Went last in Game B. "The best purple space." *Working*.

### Silver (Game A only)
- **Toast**: Taken in rounds I and III, both times by Dee late in the round: she gave everyone 2 silver, then spent her own to finish alone on Fewest silver. "A quiet killer" (Cal). *Working*, but it goes to whoever places last, and that was the same player who held Went last.
- **Polish**: Taken every round to empty a player's silver. "A fix for other people's gifts." *Working* as an answer to gifts, though it is rarely the space a player is aiming for.
- **Party Gifts**: Taken every round and copied 4 times in rounds I and II. Strategic scores of 2 or 3 from every player meant "always take it." Ben, who opened with it, listed it as his regret because Cal copied it immediately and the copy put silver back on him. *Always taken*. Its +6 is too easy to copy (see the top findings).
- **Flaunt**: Taken once (round III, last lap), by Dee to set up a Kingmakers gift at round end. Everyone scored it 1: "nobody held enough silver for it to pay." *Never wanted*. It pays you for holding silver, and holding silver loses Silver's own contest.
- **Buyout**: Taken once, as the stuck last space: "can't afford anything, it just gives 1 silver." *Never wanted*, for the same reason as Flaunt.
- **Largesse**: Round III, taken as the second placement (marked obvious) and copied once by Echo (+10 again). The 12 silver it hands out went straight into rivals' follower buys (Ben turned 10 of it into 10 followers). *Always taken*. It is big, but it also gives a lot back to the table.

### Yellow (Game B only)
- **Harvest**: Taken every round as filler. "Fine filler, nobody fought over it." *Working* as filler. It is never a decision.
- **Survey**: Taken every round as filler. Its +3 to +6 depends on where other players crowd. *Working* as filler.
- **New Blood**: Taken once (round I, Cal, who held the Yellow Cloak), then left empty in rounds II and III. "Only Cal with the Yellow Cloak made it work." *Never wanted* without the cloak.
- **Makeover**: Taken in rounds II and III, once to block it and once to pay a member's colored cost. "Fixes Counterfeiters." *Working*.
- **Melting Pot**: Taken in rounds II and III. Once it was obvious last-minion filler. Once (round III) it was hard, worth +4 to +5 followers. *Working*, quiet.
- **Offering**: Taken once in round III. Dee timed it before her rivals' last buys and made three players discard 9 resources: "felt great." *Working*. Too few games to judge its size.

### White (Game B only)
- **Modest Means**: Taken by Ben every round. With 0 members he got 4 or 5 white each time. He scored its strategic value 1: "always 4 or 5 white for me with zero members, so I always took it." *Always taken* by the zero-member player. It was part of the winning loop.
- **Splurge**: Taken every round. In round II, Dee bought a 7-cost member and got +7 followers. For Ben it was "just 2 white." *Working*: timing it with a big recruit is a real decision.
- **Nest Egg**: Taken every round as filler. "Plain +3, nobody thought about it." Strategic scores of 1 or 2. *Working* as filler. It is never a decision.
- **Farewell**: Taken in rounds II and III. Martyrs gave +10 and Con Men gave +9 (+11 with Brokers). Players called it "the best space in the game" (Ava). *Working*.
- **Write-Off**: Taken in rounds II and III, both times to pass the Ghost on: a 10-point swing between two players. "The most fun space on the board" (Ben). Cal: "maybe too good." *Working*. Watch how it pairs with the Ghost.
- **Cash In**: Never taken. "Buying followers does the same job" (Dee). With the White Cloak, white was always spent on followers as it came in. *Never wanted*.

## 5. Members players called out

- **Ghost** (both games): Every player's highlight. It moved 4 to 5 times per game, −5 each time, carried by Promote, Demote, Write-Off, Anarchist, Patrons and Guest of Honor. Cal (B) warned it is "too swingy": whoever held a swap at the right moment won a 10-point swing. *Working* as fun. It moves a lot of points around but never once hit the leader of Game A.
- **Night Managers** (A): It paid Dee +5, +6 and +6, about 17 followers for 6 resources. Ben and Cal both called it too cheap, and Dee herself said "possibly too good." *Underpriced* when one player holds Went last every round. In Game B it went unbought ("the last-minion fight is too contested to plan around"). Rests on one game.
- **Timekeeper, Picket Line, Closer** (A): The rest of Dee's purple pile. Each was "quietly" good: +6 at game end, a purple every time someone took a Purple space, +4 purple. Together with the Purple Cloak they funded buys of 10 followers every round. *Working* alone. Their combined effect is the problem.
- **Guest of Honor** (A): "+4 a round by giving away a Ghost or a borrowed member; the best fun-per-cost card." *Working*. Watch it paired with Borrow, which lets a player borrow a member, give it away, and gain 4.
- **Patrons** (A): Borrowed for free, then used to give the Ghost to Dee: +5 for Cal, −5 for Dee. *Working* (a trick players enjoyed).
- **Spoilsport** (both games): It changed hands 3 times in each game and decided a +10 contest in round III both times (Red in A, Went last in B, which won Ben the game). Fun for whoever held it, and a feel-bad for the player cancelled: "cancelled the Red contest I had just tied" (Cal, A). *Working*. It is a feel-bad for the victim, who has no answer that late.
- **Fan Club and Buskers** (A): Each copied Cal's Overtime to take the token, so the token went to Ben and then Dee. "I didn't see it coming" (Cal). *Working*. These are the "copy cards I couldn't answer."
- **Kingmakers** (A): A silver gift at round end, after everyone's last move, that took Fewest silver away from Ben. "Nasty and easy to miss." *Working*, though it gives one more last-word tool to the player who also places last.
- **Anarchist** (both games): Same-cost swaps undid Dee's Demote (A) and got around Cal's Loyalists (B). *Working*.
- **Martyrs** (B): Free from Late Shift, then +10 at Farewell. "The best card I owned." *Working*.
- **Con Men** (B): Free from Initiation. It stole 5 resources, then was given up at Farewell for 9. *Working*.
- **Dabblers and Doppelganger** (A): They decided the Red contest and were swapped 4 to 6 times, but three players said they could not tell how wild members count. *Unclear*.
- **Deprogrammers** (both games): "Worth 4 for 5 is the best rate in the shop," which made it a swap target. Cal (A) bought it thinking "counts as 4 members" helped the Red contest. It doesn't: "each card counts 1." *Unclear*.
- **Loyalists** (B): Ava did not expect it to block her own swaps. Cal did not expect it could itself be swapped away, which cost him the Red contest. *Unclear*.
- **Panel** (both games): Cal (B) called it "the engine I liked most." In Game A, Ben "didn't expect it to swap any cult's members." *Working*, somewhat unclear.
- **Counterfeiters** (B): "Annoying in a good way," though it "rarely stopped a buy." *Working*.
- **Sleepyheads, Lookouts** (both games): Small, reliable +3 and +2 payouts on token grabs and back-to-back turns. *Working*.
- **Cheerleaders** (B): Ava regretted it ("I'd rather have bought 5 followers"). *Overpriced*.
- **Courier, Hot Streak, Latecomers** (both games): Courier was swapped away before it ever paid. Hot Streak paid +3 once for 7 resources. Latecomers gave Dee nothing because she already held the token. *Overpriced*.
- **Mob, Tribute Band, Clan, Extra Hand** (both games): 10 to 16 cost and never bought. *Overpriced*.
- **Debt Collectors, Debutantes, Ballroom Dancers** (A): They pay you for holding silver, which loses Silver's own contest. *Never wanted*.
- **Copyist, Agents** (A): Never bought, even with copying everywhere. Players were surprised. Agents "would have stopped the Party Gifts copies." *Never wanted*. Too few games to say why.
- **Sore Loser** (B): Never triggered, because a tied contest counts as a win and Cal always tied something. *Never wanted*.
- **Jack-of-All-Trades, Seamstress** (B): Nothing else on that table counted full sets, so both were blank cards. *Never wanted* in this mix of colors.
- **Big Tent, Barker** (B): Each paid a little, but players misread how Big Tent counts a member worth 3, and when Barker's extra buy happens. *Unclear*.
- **Protesters** (A): Dee blocked Last Call with it and then could not use Last Call herself. *Unclear*.

## 6. The game as a whole

**Best round: III, in both games.** Two in three turns were hard, members changed hands every lap, and contests were worth 10. Ben (A): "every turn was a real decision." Ben (B): "I'd play again for round III alone."

**Worst round: I.** One in three turns were hard. All 12 spaces fill, so the last minion takes a leftover (Right Away or Borrow). In Game B, the White Cloak let Ben take a white space and buy 5 followers on every turn. Dee (B): "my last two turns had no choice at all."

**Round III as a payoff of earlier choices: mostly no, except for the winners.** The early choices that paid off in round III were the winners' loops (Night Managers bought in round I, the White Cloak with zero members). For everyone else, round III was a new fight with new cards:
- Ben (B): "the cards that set it up mostly arrived in round III's shop rather than building from my early choices."
- Ava (B): "my early Red engine got taken away by swaps instead of paying off."
- Dee (B): "it felt like round I with bigger numbers."

**Feel-bads in the last turns of round III.** Copy cards (Fan Club, Buskers), Spoilsport and Toast all act late in the round and leave the victim no answer. These moves were exciting for whoever made them and frustrating for whoever they hit (Cal, A: "I couldn't answer any of them").

**Misreads that changed purchases:**
- "Counts as N members" against the Red contest, which counts each card once (Cal, Ava, Dee in A).
- Grey not counting as a member color for Yellow (Ava, Cal in B).
- Loyalists protecting everything except itself (Cal, B).
- Re-taking the Goes Last token you already hold gives no cloak bonus (Dee, B).

## Five findings that matter most

**1. Both winners won the same way. They held one contest every round that rivals could not reach, and turned a pile of one color into followers, 1 for 1, on almost every turn.**
- In Game A, Dee won Went last in all three rounds. Night Managers paid her 5 to 6 followers each round, and her purple pile bought 10 followers at a time.
- In Game B, Ben stayed at or near 0 members, so he won Fewest members every round (+4, +6, +10). The White Cloak gave back 2 white on every follower buy.
- Swaps did not reach either winner. Ava (B): "the swaps never touched him because he had nothing worth taking." Cal (B): "nothing in the game punishes holding zero members."
- In both games the round I leader won, and every player scored comeback 2 or 3.
- In Game A, the same player who placed last also won Fewest silver twice, with a late Toast and Kingmakers' round-end gift. That gave one player two contests from one seat.
- Kinds of change that might help: make these contests easier to fight over, make the payouts that stack on them smaller (Night Managers, the White Cloak refund on every buy), or stop the last player in the round from also deciding the silver contest.
- Each case rests on one game. Purple lost in Game B, where rivals stole the token four times.

**2. Swapping members is what players will come back for.**
- Promote, Demote, Write-Off, Anarchist, Guest of Honor and the Ghost were named in every player's final note in both games.
- Promote and Demote scored 4 or 5 from all 8 players.
- The catch: these swings mostly moved points among the three players chasing the leader. Ava (A): "most of them only cancelled a rival's move."
- The Ghost's −5 can come back around several times a game. One player called that too swingy.

**3. Party Gifts and the green copy spaces made one space pay +6 to two or three players a round.**
- It was copied 4 times in two rounds and was always the first or second pick. The player who opened with it regretted it.
- Players marked the copies obvious, so no real choice was involved.
- Kinds of change that might help: make Party Gifts less valuable to copy, or make the copy spaces cost something when they copy a gift.
- Rests on one game, since Green and Silver were only at one table.

**4. Some spaces nobody wanted, and round I leaves the last placer with leftovers.**
- **Flaunt** and **Buyout** pay you for holding silver, which loses Silver's own contest. Everyone scored them 1.
- **Cash In** was never taken.
- **Right Away** and **Crunch Time** do nothing on a player's last minion.
- **Borrow** only matters with a member you can use and give away the same turn.
- In round I, every space fills. The last minion, often the Went last player's, takes whatever is left, and every stuck turn in round I happened that way.

**5. Card wording misled players into buys they regretted.**
- "Counts as N members" (Deprogrammers, Dabblers, Doppelganger) against the Red contest's "each card counts 1."
- Grey not counting as a color for Yellow.
- Loyalists being swappable itself.
- In each case a player paid for a member to win a contest and then found the member didn't count, or lost the member to a swap.
- Kinds of change that might help: wording that settles these cases on the card itself, so a table reads them the same way the first time.
