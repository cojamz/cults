# Silver, review 2 (Nov 8)

Silver's contest is now **Fewest silver** (0 counts; +4 / +6 / +10, ties split rounded up). Silver you gain now pulls against the contest, silver you hand rivals is a sting as well as a gift, and silver you can get rid of is worth more than before.

**The color in one line.** Silver gives rivals gifts that pay you back (rulebook). Under the new contest, every gift also costs the receiver the contest.

**A great Silver game.** In round I you take Toast for fuel and spend it down by round end, and Party Gifts hands each rival 1 silver so they can't sit on 0. You win the round I contest alone and take +6 followers on top. In round II you keep pushing silver onto rivals and keep yours near 0. Rivals have to spend theirs before round end, and Smooth Talkers or Polish helps you spend your own. In round III Largesse drops 12 silver on the table for +10 followers and the +10 contest. Rivals push back by spending silver fast and by taking Silver spaces first.

**A fresh start, if Silver had nothing.** A big fuel space that also gifts (Toast). A gift space that pays followers (Party Gifts). A space that pays you for what rivals hold (High Tide). A way to get your own silver out (Smooth Talkers). Members that score off silver on the table rather than in your own pile. Polish, Smooth Talkers, Party Gifts, Toast, Largesse and Kingmakers already fit this. The two round II spaces and the gaps in the members don't.

## Assessment

**Spaces**
- **Toast** "+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III." **Keep.** Cory: "I like, it's good." Under Fewest silver it's now a fuel space with a real cost: you have to spend the +3/+6/+9 before round end, while every rival ends up off 0.
- **Polish** "Turn 2 of your silver into 4 of any colors." **Keep** (Cory, Nov 8: "okay in this case"). It's Silver's plainest way to get rid of silver, so it matters more now. Flag: on your first turn of round I it does nothing unless you hold the cloak's 1 silver plus 1 more, so it only works after Toast.
- **Party Gifts** "Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver you give." **Keep** (Cory: "fine as is"). Flag, one line: with the flip, "up to" is no longer a choice, since giving 3 always beats giving fewer, and in round I it also tends to win you the contest. Example: rivals at 1 silver each and you at 0 gives you +6 followers and +4 from the contest. Cory said "maybe it makes that fun". Watch it when people play.
- **Sweet Nothings** "+3 silver. Give rivals up to 3 of your silver, split as you choose: +2 of any colors each." **Replace** (Cory: "weird"). Silver comes in and goes straight back out, so in practice the card reads "rivals +3 silver, you +6 of any colors". With the flip, "up to" isn't a choice any more either, and it trades 1 silver for 2 colors, the same rate as Polish.
- **Sweet Talk** "+3 silver. Then turn as many of your silver as you want into other colors." **Replace or keep, Cory's call** (Cory: "too close to yellow"). Under the new contest it's Silver's best way to get down to 0, but it's a currency converter, which is Yellow's job. Nothing below beats it yet (see the third pick).
- **Largesse** "Give rivals 12 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +10 followers." **Keep** (Cory: star). With the flip it's +10 followers plus a strong grip on the +10 round III contest.

**Members**
- **Godparent** "On Use, once per game: give one of your members to a rival. Gain followers equal to its cost." **Moves to White** (not this review).
- **Guest of Honor** "On Use, once per round: give a rival one of your members. +4 followers." **Moves to White** (not this review).
- **Caterer** "Whenever you gift silver, +2 silver." **Cut** (Cory). Under the new contest its +2 silver works against you.
- **Debutantes** "On Use, once per game: double your silver. Each rival +2 silver." **Cut** (Cory: "straight up shouldn't be in the deck").
- **Debt Collectors** "On Use, once per round: each rival gives you 2 silver, if they have it." **Cut** (Cory: "counter to the competition").
- **Toastmaster** "Whenever you take a Silver space, +1 follower." **Keep.** It's quiet, Cory said "maybe a nice extra way to get points", and the flip doesn't change it. It's the weakest member left, but it's the cheap one and does no harm.
- **High Society** "Whenever a rival takes a Silver space, +1 follower." **Keep.** It fires on anyone's move without anyone stopping to decide, which Cory likes. The flip doesn't change it.
- **Smooth Talkers** "Your silver pays for any color." **Keep** (star). It's now Silver's best way to get rid of silver.
- **Ballroom Dancers** "On Use, once per game: +2 followers per minion on Silver spaces." **Keep.** Top case: round III with all 6 Silver spaces filled pays +12 once. That's about one Largesse, so it isn't broken. Cory's "a lot of points really easily" is about numbers, and the numbers are his call. If he wants it smaller, the obvious change is +1 per minion.
- **Kingmakers** "Round End: give a rival 3 silver from the bank. +3 followers." **Fix: +3 becomes +2 followers** (Cory: "maybe just a little too many followers"; the +2 is Claude's fill-in). Round-end abilities happen before contests are scored, so after the flip Kingmakers also pushes one rival off 0 every round. That makes it stronger than when he marked it.
- **Patron Saints** "Game End: +1 follower per silver member, anyone's." **Keep** (Cory: star, "a little too strong, a piece everyone wants"). With four silver members gone it now counts fewer cards, so it shrinks unless the open slots are filled with silver members.

**The cloak**
- **Silver Cloak** (now Caterer's text) "Whenever you gift silver, +2 silver." **Replace** (Cory: no followers; "a little different from silver somehow"). Every time it fires it now costs its holder in the contest.

**Open member slots after the cuts and moves:** five (Caterer, Debutantes, Debt Collectors, Godparent, Guest of Honor). Six members remain.

## Picks

1. **Silver Cloak.** Cory asked for a new text. The current one fights the new contest every time it fires.
2. **Sweet Nothings' slot (round II).** Cory marked it weak ("weird"). Under the flip it collapses to one option (always give 3) and repeats Polish's rate.
3. **Sweet Talk's slot (round II).** Cory marked it weak ("too close to yellow"). Both round II spaces turn your silver into other colors, which is one shape twice in the same round.
4. **A member for the open slots (Debutantes' price band, 8 to 11).** Cory cut Debutantes and Debt Collectors and called the pitched replacement "a little awkward". Five slots are open, and Patron Saints shrinks with every one of them left empty.

## Replacements

### Pick 1. Silver Cloak

A cloak is a boon all game plus 1 silver, picked at setup by any player. It should be weaker than a member and differ from Silver's members. Other cloaks pay a little of their color when their color's verb happens: Red Cloak "Whenever you recruit a member, +1 red.", Magenta Cloak "Whenever you steal, +1 magenta.", White Cloak "Whenever you buy followers, +2 white."

**Survivor A. Silver Cloak: "Whenever you gift silver, +2 of any colors."**
- **Printed text.** The trigger stays the same (Toast, Party Gifts, Largesse and Kingmakers all count, and so do copies of them, per Cory, Nov 6). The payoff is now colors you choose instead of silver.
- **One played turn.** Round I, 4 players, you hold the cloak. Turn 1: **Party Gifts** ("Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver you give."). You give each rival 1 silver and take +6 followers, and the cloak adds +2 of any colors, say 2 blue for a member. The old cloak here gave +2 silver, which you then had to spend before round end or lose the contest. The best other space, **Toast** ("+3 silver in round I…"), gives +3 silver that has the same problem. Gain over the old cloak: the same 2 resources, now free of the contest.
- **Top case.** Round III, built around gifts: Toast, Party Gifts and Largesse, plus Kingmakers at round end, is 4 fires, so +8 resources that round and about +16 to +20 over the game. Compare the Red Cloak, about +12 to +15 red over a game, and the White Cloak, +2 white per follower buy. It's in their range, not double.
- **Choices.** None. It fires and pays.
- **Who feels it.** The holder mostly, as a quiet engine. Rivals feel it only through gifts the holder is happier to make.
- **Six principles.** Intuitive: one line with the old trigger. Fun: makes every gift a little sweeter, without anyone stopping to decide. Interacts: through gifts to rivals, which now cost them the contest. Powerful: modest, as a cloak should be. Engine: feeds recruits from gifting. Its color: pays in the colors you need rather than more silver, so it works with the new contest instead of against it.
- **Not refused.** Cory's no was to followers on the cloak. This pays resources, as every other cloak does.
- **Against the current card.** Clearly better under the new contest. Every fire of the current text costs its holder 2 silver of contest standing.
- **Risk, one line.** "A little different from silver" may mean the trigger shouldn't be gifting either. If so, Survivor B is the one.

**Survivor B. Silver Cloak: "Whenever any player gifts silver, +1 of any color."**
- **One played turn.** Round I, 4 players, you're the Red player and took the Silver Cloak at setup. The Silver player takes **Toast** (rivals +1 silver) and then **Party Gifts**. You get +1 of any color each time, +2 by your second turn, on top of whatever your own space gave. With the old cloak in your hands those gifts gave you nothing.
- **Top case.** The Silver player gifts about 8 to 12 times a game, so +8 to +12 resources for the holder, whoever that is. If the Silver player holds it themselves, it's half of A.
- **Choices.** None. Nobody decides anything on another player's turn.
- **Who feels it.** A non-Silver holder gets paid for the Silver player's turns, so this cloak goes to whoever doesn't play Silver. It's "different from silver" by who wants it.
- **Six principles.** Intuitive: yes. Fun: small, a rider on someone else's turn. Interacts: fires on anyone's move, the shape Cory praised on the White Cloak (Nov 7: "i like the artifacts linking to any move, not just your own"). Powerful: weak, as cloaks are. Engine: no, a steady trickle. Its color: new, Silver's gifts paying a bystander.
- **Not refused.** No similar card in the pitch log.
- **Against the current card.** Better under the new contest. It's quieter than A.

**Cut**
- "Silver gifted to you comes as any colors you choose." This is a shield against Silver, the same corner-to-hide shape as Lie Low ("too frustrating for opponents, no counter"), and its best moment is that nothing happens.
- "Whenever a rival recruits paying silver, +1 of any color." It fires on most rival recruits now that everyone dumps silver, and it's a lot to watch. That shape is better on a member (Socialites, below).
- "Round End: give a rival 1 of your silver." No payoff, and it's close to the line Cory just called awkward.
- "Round End: if you hold no silver, +2 of any colors." It pays for winning the contest you already won, the overlap Cory flagged on Recluse.
- "Your silver counts as 2 when you buy followers." The rate is wrong: 5 silver buys the 10-follower card, far too strong.
- "Whenever you take a Silver space, each rival +1 silver." The holder gets nothing they can feel, and rivals get the resources.
- "Whenever a rival gives you silver, you may pass it to another rival." You'd decide on someone else's turn.
- "Whenever you turn silver into other colors, +1 follower." Followers on a cloak again, and it only works with Polish.

### Pick 2. Sweet Nothings' slot (round II)

The bar for round II is a space that changes what someone owns or how much they recruit, about 4 to 6. Beside it sit **Sweet Talk** ("+3 silver. Then turn as many of your silver as you want into other colors.") and, open all game, **Party Gifts** ("…+2 followers per silver you give.").

**Survivor. High Tide, back in round II: "Each rival +1 silver. Then gain 1 follower per silver the rival with the most silver holds."**
- **Printed text.** This is Cory's own starred round III card from Oct 22 to Oct 31. He replaced it with Largesse, his own idea, and never refused it. He said: "then you try to get others more silver, and you can pay yours down. I like it quite a bit." Under Fewest silver, "pay yours down" is now the contest itself.
- **One played turn.** Round II, 4 players. You're Silver at 0 silver. Rival B holds 4 silver (from your round I gifts and this round's Toast), C holds 2, D holds 1. **High Tide**: each rival +1, so B is at 5 and you take +5 followers. All three rivals now hold silver and you're at 0, so you win the round II contest alone, +6. Sweet Nothings here would give you +6 of any colors and rivals +3 silver. The other best choice, Party Gifts if it's open, gives +6 followers and rivals +3 silver. High Tide matches it in followers and grows with what rivals hoard.
- **Top case.** Round III (round II spaces stay open). You take **Largesse** first and put 12 silver on one rival, then High Tide, which reads 13 or more for +13 to +15 followers. The best single round III space, Largesse, gives +10, so this is under twice the best alternative. And the rival you loaded spends 10 of it on the 10-follower card on their next turn, so the case limits itself unless the two turns come back to back.
- **Choices.** None for you. The richest rival's choice comes on their own turn: spend silver now (good for their contest, and it starves your High Tide) or hold it for something bigger. Both sides are real.
- **Who feels it.** The rival with the most silver. They become your bank, so they spend down or take High Tide first to deny it. That's a reason to block.
- **Six principles.** Intuitive: two short sentences, one visible count. Fun: the richest rival squirms. Interacts: it reads what rivals hold, the implicit kind of interaction. Powerful: +4 to +7 in round II, more in round III. Engine: Toast, Party Gifts and Kingmakers load rivals, and High Tide cashes it. Its color: "flip what a resource means", now also the contest.
- **Not refused.** It was approved. Silver Lining ("Each rival +2 silver. Then +2 followers per rival holding more silver than you.") was refused Oct 31 and counts rivals, not the richest pile, so it's a different shape.
- **Against the current card.** Sweet Nothings' best turn is +6 of any colors with rivals +3 silver. High Tide's best ordinary turn is +5 followers with rivals +3 silver and an edge in the contest. Followers beat resources, and the moment (the hoarder pays you) is one Sweet Nothings never makes.
- **Risk, one line.** Cory called it "way too strong" once before (Oct 28) before keeping it. In round II the numbers are smaller than they were in round III.

**Cut**
- Sweet Nothings without its "+3 silver" ("Give rivals up to 3 of your silver… +2 of any colors each.") is the same rate as Polish, a near copy in the next round.
- "Give all your silver to rivals, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver given." This is Pay It Forward's shape, refused Oct 31.
- "Turn up to 5 of your silver into followers." This is White's verb (turning what you have into followers), and it skips the follower cards.
- "Raise each rival's silver to match yours." It hands the table far more than you get, the shape that flopped (Fundraisers, Evictors).
- "Each rival +2 silver. Then +1 follower per rival holding more silver than you." This is Silver Lining, refused.
- "Recruit a rival's member, paying its cost in silver to them." This is Buyout's shape, refused Oct 31.
- "Take 1 resource you choose from each rival; each then gains 2 silver." Taking is Magenta's steal, and it was passed over Nov 6.
- "Each rival +2 silver. Gain 1 of any color per silver rivals hold." This is High Tide paying a pile change instead of followers, smaller and flatter.
- "Recruit a shop member costing 5 or less for free. Each rival +2 silver." This is Red's Initiation with a bonus stuck on.

### Pick 3. Sweet Talk's slot (round II)

**None passes.** Every new way to get rid of your own silver was already Yellow's (convert), White's (cash in), Blue's (an extra recruit), a refused Pay It Forward, or a tiny change. The tiny change was "+3 silver. This turn, your silver pays for any color." It's Sweet Talk with different words.

Candidates tried:
- "+3 silver. This turn, your silver pays for any color." Tiny change.
- "Give rivals any of your silver, split as you choose. Recruit a shop member costing up to the silver given, for free." Member-cost math and an extra recruit.
- "Recruit a shop member, paying its whole cost in silver; that silver goes to rivals, split as you choose." An extra recruit, Blue's.
- "Give rivals up to 6 of your silver. +1 follower per silver." Pay It Forward's shape.
- "Swap all your silver with a rival's silver." You lose your own currency, and it's close to the refused pile swap.
- "Each rival +2 silver. You go first next round." Purple's.
- "Give each rival 2 silver; each gives you 1 follower." Pass the Plate, refused.
- "Give the player with the most followers 4 silver." A sting on the leader, which readers overrate, and it's small.

**The question for Cory.** Polish and Smooth Talkers already turn your silver into other colors. Should Sweet Talk's slot (a) stay a way to get rid of your own silver (keep Sweet Talk), or (b) become a second space that pays you for silver rivals hold, beside High Tide? The default is (a), keep Sweet Talk, because it's the current card.

### Pick 4. A member for the open slots (8 to 11)

**Survivor A. Socialites: "Whenever a rival recruits paying silver, +1 follower."** (4 silver + 3 any, worth 2; this is its bot-test price, Claude's fill-in.)
- **Printed text.** Cory liked it Nov 4 ("hit on something… for silver"). It was pulled from the live game only because it took High Society's slot against his call. It needs its own slot, and this is one.
- **One played turn.** Round II, 4 players. You own Socialites. B, C and D each hold 2 to 5 silver from your Toast and Party Gifts. To win Fewest silver, each wants to spend it, so each recruit they make with silver pays you +1. That's about 3 fires this round, +3. Their other options are to pay with other colors and keep the silver, which loses them the contest, or to dump it all in one big buy, which is 1 fire instead of several. The other member you could buy here, **Kingmakers**, pays +3 a round.
- **Top case.** Round III after Largesse put 12 silver on rivals: if they pay silver on every recruit, that's up to 15 fires. They won't. Bunching it into one 10-follower buy is one fire, so the realistic figure is +4 to +6. Kingmakers over the game is +9, Ballroom Dancers +12 once. It's in range.
- **Choices.** The rival chooses on their own turn how to pay. Spending silver pays you +1 and protects their contest. Holding it denies you +1 and costs them the contest. Both sides are real.
- **Who feels it.** Every rival with silver, every round. It ties straight to the contest. And it doesn't go useless when you're winning: rivals still have to spend silver for their own contest.
- **Six principles.** Intuitive: one sentence. Fun: rivals feel taxed for doing what the contest asks. Interacts: it fires on rivals' moves without anyone stopping to decide. Powerful: steady, about +9 a game. Engine: your gifts become your income. Its color: a new way in, since Silver profits when the silver it gave away gets spent.
- **Risk, one line.** It fires fairly often, and the owner has to watch each rival's payment. That's close to Cory's "annoying to remember" (said of Shoplifter, on your own recruits).

**Survivor B. Silver Barons: "Round End: +1 follower per 3 silver all players hold together."** (4 silver + 4 any, worth 2; Claude's fill-in.)
- **Printed text.** Cory, Nov 5: "i also like barons, that should be short list."
- **One played turn.** End of round II, 4 players. You at 0, B 4, C 3, D 2 is 9 silver on the table, so +3 followers before contests. End of round III, after Largesse, the table holds 15 to 20, so +5 or +6. Round I is about +1 or +2. That's about +10 a game. Kingmakers is +9 a game.
- **Top case.** You take Toast in round III (+9) and keep it, so the table holds about 30 and Barons pays +10. But you lose the +10 contest. Your own silver pays the same as rivals', so holding it trades the contest for Barons. That's a real choice, not a broken case.
- **Choices.** Yours: hold your silver (more Barons) or spend it down (the contest). Rivals spend theirs down for their own contest anyway, which works against you, so your gifts and their spending pull against each other.
- **Who feels it.** Mostly quiet. Rivals see the count and spend down a little sooner.
- **Six principles.** Intuitive: one count, at round end. Fun: modest, more of a plan than a moment. Interacts: it reads the whole table's silver, the communal angle Cory asked for Nov 5. Powerful: steady. Engine: every gift card you own feeds it. Its color: new, since it scores the table rather than your pile.
- **Not refused.** Short-listed by Cory.

**Cut**
- Debutantes reworded, "Round End: each rival takes 1 of your silver. +1 follower for each." Same shape as Kingmakers, beside it in the same deck, and Hostesses (the same shape) was cut.
- Bartenders, "Rivals may pay with your silver. +1 follower per silver they use." Rivals refuse to feed the leader, so it goes useless when you're winning.
- "Round End: +1 follower per rival holding more silver than you." A second copy of the contest's own reward (Recluse: "too overlapping with the competition").
- "Rivals' silver counts double in the silver contest." It bends the contest's own count, the same overlap.
- "On Use, once per game: give rivals all your silver, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver given." Pay It Forward's shape.
- "Whenever you're given silver, +1 follower." It almost never fires for the Silver player who buys it.
- "On Recruit: give rivals 6 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +1 follower for each." It would be the fourth gift-for-followers card (Party Gifts, Largesse, Kingmakers), with nothing new.
- "Whenever you gift silver, +1 follower" (Gift Wrapper). A near copy of Toastmaster, since almost every Silver space gifts.
- "On Use, once per round: give a rival 2 of your silver and take 1 resource you choose from them." Taking is Magenta's steal.

**Still open after these:** three member slots. If Cory picks both survivors, three more are needed. Patron Saints counts silver members, so whatever fills them should be silver.
