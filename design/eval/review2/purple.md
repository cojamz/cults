# Purple, review 2 (Nov 8)

Purple in one line, in Cory's words: "battling for a token… you kind of want the last turn token." The contest pays whoever placed the round's last minion (+4 / +6 / +10), so the Goes Last token is now worth the prize, and every Purple card is judged by how it plays in that fight.

A great game with Purple: in round I you take **Sleep In** for the token and the +4 contest, or use **Right Away** to land two turns in a row on a space you need. In round II the token changes hands between Sleep In and **Last Call**, and **Crunch Time** sets up **Hot Streak** or **Lookouts**. In round III **Overtime** grabs the token, **Closer** does the last space again, and the +10 contest decides close games. Rivals answer by taking a token space back or by taking the space a Closer owner would repeat.

A fresh start would have: spaces that take the token, spaces that move your turns up, a space that pays for waiting; members that pay when you take the token, pay when you go last, take the token another way, and pay for turns in a row. Today's cards outside that picture: **Bouncers** and **Gossip** (skips), **Picket Line** (Purple spaces taken), **Timekeeper** (purple members at game end), **Courier** (resources). That is where I looked first.

One rule fact that matters for this review: a skipped turn is placed at the end of the round. When nobody holds the Goes Last token, the skipped player can end up placing the round's last minion, and wins the Purple contest.

## Assessment

### Spaces
- **Sleep In** "+4 purple in round I, +5 in round II, +6 in round III. Take the Goes Last token: your next minion is placed after everyone else's." Keep. It is the main way into the token fight, and the only one in round I. With the new contest it pays +4 / +6 / +10 on top of its purple. Star.
- **Hold Up** "+2 purple in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. Whoever's turn is next skips it, even yours." Keep. Cory chose this shape. The new contest gives it a second use: when nobody holds the token, the skipped turn goes to the end of the round, so a player at the turn of the snake can skip their own turn and place last. Words fix: the rulebook says a skipped turn is "placed later in the same round" and should say "at the end of the round", or a table can't play this right. Fine.
- **Right Away** (approved, coming) "+1 purple. Your next turn comes right after this one, and that space gives +2 more of its color in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III." Keep. Cory: "I like Right Away a lot." Star.
- **Last Call** "+1 purple. +1 follower and +1 purple per minion you have left. Take the Goes Last token: your other minions are placed after everyone else's." Keep. You give up where your minions go for followers you can count, and you now also get the contest. Star.
- **Crunch Time** "+2 purple. Your next two turns come right after this one." Keep. Cory: "great", keep as is. Star.
- **Overtime** "+4 purple. +1 minion this round. Take the Goes Last token: that minion is placed after everyone else's." Keep. Cory: "Overtime is good. I like it." It is round III's way to take the token, worth +10 with the contest. Star.

### Members
- **Courier** "Whenever you take a consecutive turn, +1 of each other color." Keep. Cory: "I actually kind of like the old courier." It pays in resources, so it differs from Lookouts, which pays followers on the same trigger. Fine.
- **Bouncers** "On Recruit: the next two rival turns are skipped." Replace. A skip only moves a turn later in the same round. With the new contest, when nobody holds the token, both skipped turns move to the end of the round, so the second rival places the last minion and wins Purple's contest (+6 in round II). The one sting this card has can hand a rival the prize. It never touches the token. Weak.
- **Gossip** "Whenever a player's turn is skipped, +1 follower." Keep. Cory asked for this card. Words fix: in the game, taking the token with Sleep In or Last Call counts as a skipped turn, but neither card's text says so. A table won't know to pay Gossip, so a token space's text or Gossip's text has to say it. If Bouncers goes, Gossip still has Hold Up and the token spaces to pay off. Fine.
- **Timekeeper** "Game End: +1 follower per purple member you have." Keep. Cory asked for a go-deep card for each color, and it has beaten three replacements in play. A quiet card that pays its owner is fine. Fine.
- **Lookouts** "Whenever you take a consecutive turn, +2 followers." Keep. Cory added it at +2. Fine.
- **Sleepyheads** "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +3 followers." Keep. Cory: "maybe even more." It pays you for taking the token, which is exactly what the contest wants. Star.
- **Night Managers** "Round End: if you placed the round's last minion, +1 follower per purple member you have." Keep, but watch it. Cory made it count as 2 members on Nov 6. It now pays at the same moment as the contest, so it adds to the contest winner's prize. Its top case: round III with 3 other purple members (5 counting itself as 2) gives +5 on top of the +10 contest. That is big but not broken. Fine.
- **Closer** (approved, coming) "When you place the round's last minion, do that space's action again." Keep. Cory: "really strong", "quite expensive" (his price, not set yet). It pays at the same moment as the contest but in a different thing, a second use of the space. Star.
- **Hot Streak** "On Use, once per game: +3 followers per turn in a row you've taken just before this one." Keep. Cory: "I like hot streak." Star.
- **Picket Line** "Whenever a rival takes a Purple space, +1 purple." Fix. Cory's taste: "+1 resource" triggers are boring. Its bot trial called it "quiet at the table (4 to 10 purple in drips)". But the trigger is good: the three token spaces are Purple spaces, so every rival who fights for the token pays you, and it fires on rivals' moves with no decision for anyone. The fix is the payout (below). Weak as printed.
- **Pocket Watch** (the Purple cloak) "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +3 purple." Fix. Cory: "could be interesting when we make the Goes Last token better. problem is going last is secretly kind of good." The contest has now made the token better, so the cloak stacks more onto a move that already pays. It has the same trigger as Sleepyheads (cloaks should differ from the color's members), and it paid the least of all the cloaks (about 6 purple per game against a 10 to 12 target; one bot count, evidence only). Weak.

## Picks
1. **Bouncers**: the weakest card in the deck under the new contest. Its only sting can hand a rival the round's last minion, and it never touches the token.
2. **Picket Line**: Cory's standing note that "+1 resource" triggers are boring. The trigger fits the token fight, so option A fixes the payout. Option B puts a second member that gives you the token (Cory, Nov 8: "maybe a couple members") in this place instead.
3. **Pocket Watch**: Cory's own note on this card. Since the contest, going last already pays, and the cloak has the same trigger as Sleepyheads.

## Replacements

### Pick 1: Bouncers' place

**Latecomers** (price 7 as a player sees it, Claude's fill-in) "On Recruit: take the Goes Last token: your next minion is placed after everyone else's."

- Printed text and what it touches. **Sleep In** "+4 purple in round I, +5 in round II, +6 in round III. Take the Goes Last token: your next minion is placed after everyone else's." **Last Call** "+1 purple. +1 follower and +1 purple per minion you have left. Take the Goes Last token: your other minions are placed after everyone else's." **Overtime** "+4 purple. +1 minion this round. Take the Goes Last token: that minion is placed after everyone else's." **Sleepyheads** "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +3 followers." **Closer** "When you place the round's last minion, do that space's action again." Purple's contest: whoever placed the round's last minion, +4 / +6 / +10.
- One played turn, round II, 4 players (4 minions each). Ana took **Last Call** with 3 minions left: +4 purple, +3 followers, and her other minions wait for last. On Ben's third turn, Latecomers is in the shop. He places, then recruits it for 7. He takes the token. Ana's waiting minions go back to normal turns, and Ben's fourth minion is placed after everyone else's: he wins the contest, +6, and the member is worth 1, so +7 for 7 resources. His best other buy that turn is the 5-follower card, +5 for 5 resources. In round III the same buy is worth +10 from the contest, with +3 from Sleepyheads if he has it, against at most 10 followers bought 1 for 1. Ana keeps what Last Call paid her and loses the +6.
- Current card's best turn for comparison: Bouncers bought in round II pushes two rivals' turns later. If nobody holds the token, the second of them places last and wins +6. With the token held, it is a short delay that nobody counts. Latecomers makes the bigger and clearer moment: a token snatch in the shop that the whole table watches, where Bouncers' best turn is a delay that can backfire.
- Top case: round III, the owner also has Sleepyheads and Closer and recruits Latecomers with their fourth minion. That pays +10 for the contest, +3 from Sleepyheads, one more use of the last space taken, and the member's worth. It is the combo the color is built for, and each other piece cost its own recruit. Latecomers alone is worth about +11 for 7 resources and a round III recruit, under twice what the follower cards pay. It isn't safe: a rival still holding a minion can take **Overtime** or **Sleep In**, if one is open, and take the token back.
- Both sides of every choice: the card has no choice. The timing is the owner's decision: buy it too early and rivals still have token spaces open to take it back; buy it on your last turn and it does nothing, because you have no minion left to place last. Both mistakes are real, so the timing is a real decision.
- Who feels it: the token holder (a sting: they lose the contest and their minions go back to normal turns), and every player watching the shop, who has a reason to buy it first to block it.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence, done the moment it's recruited, and the token moves on the table. Fun: a snatch in the shop that everyone sees. Interacts: with all three token spaces and with whoever holds the token. Feels powerful: yes, it takes the contest. Engine or counter: engine with Sleepyheads, Closer and Night Managers, and a counter to Last Call and Overtime holders. A new way into its color: today the token is taken only on the board; this is the first way to take it through the shop.
- Hard rules: no new token; works without any other color; nobody decides on another player's turn; nothing to remember.
- Not already refused: Go-Getter ("On Use, once per round: take the Goes Last token.") and Stragglers (the same, as a new member) were pitched Nov 7 with no verdict yet, and nothing like them was turned down. Latecomers fires once and only when you recruit it, so rivals can see it coming in the shop.

### Pick 2: Picket Line (fix)

**Picket Line** (same price, 6) "Whenever a rival takes a Purple space, +1 follower."

- Printed text and what it touches: the six Purple spaces above. Three of them take the token.
- One played turn, round III, 4 players. Cal owns Picket Line. Ana takes **Overtime**, Ben takes **Sleep In** to take the token back, Dee takes **Hold Up**. That is +3 followers for Cal; the printed card gives +3 purple. Over a game, rivals take about 9 Purple spaces (all 3 in round I, about 4 of 5 in round II and 5 of 6 in round III, most of them by rivals), so the fix pays about 7 to 9 followers from round II on, against about the same in purple today. Purple only becomes followers by spending a recruit on a follower card, so the fix saves the owner recruits, which are scarce in round III.
- Best other member at this price: **Timekeeper** "Game End: +1 follower per purple member you have." pays about 3 to 5.
- Top case: bought in round I, it pays about 9 followers plus its worth, for 6 resources. That is under twice what the follower cards pay.
- Both sides: no choice. Who feels it: everyone who fights for the token pays the owner a follower, so the fight has a small cost.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence, and you see the space taken. Fun: quiet but steady. Interacts: with the token fight and every rival's Purple turn, with no decision on their turn (Cory likes triggers on any player's move: "i like the artifacts linking to any move"). Powerful: modest. Engine: pays more the harder the table fights for the token. Its color: being paid for the token fight you aren't in.
- Honest note: the moment stays quiet. This is a payout fix in the way Cory often fixes cards himself, not a bigger moment. The current card is the default if Cory prefers it.
- Not refused: it is the live card with followers in place of purple.

### Pick 2, option B: a second member that gives you the token (Cory, Nov 8: "there should definitely be a purple that gives the go last token. maybe a couple members.")

**Night Porters** (price 8 as a player sees it, Claude's fill-in) "Whenever you recruit another purple member, take the Goes Last token: your next minion is placed after everyone else's."

- Printed text and what it touches: Latecomers, the three token spaces, Sleepyheads and Closer, all quoted under Pick 1. The contest is whoever placed the round's last minion, +4 / +6 / +10.
- One played turn, round III, 4 players (5 minions each). Ben owns Night Porters. Ana took **Overtime** earlier, so she holds the token and her extra minion waits. On his fourth turn Ben places, then recruits **Lookouts** ("Whenever you take a consecutive turn, +2 followers.") for 7. He takes the token, Ana's waiting minion goes back to a normal turn, and Ben's fifth minion is placed last: +10. He would have bought Lookouts anyway, so the token is pure gain. His best other choice was to buy Lookouts and leave Ana the +10. Ana can only take the token back with an open **Sleep In** (+6 purple) or **Last Call**, and that costs her a minion.
- Next to Latecomers: Latecomers gives the token once, when you recruit it. Night Porters gives it every time you recruit another purple member. They pay at different times and at different sizes, so they are siblings, not twins. Held together, the token can come from the shop twice.
- Top case: bought in round II, then a purple member recruited near the end of round II and of round III: +6 and +10 for the contests, plus +3 each from Sleepyheads. That is about 22, for 8 resources plus purchases the owner made anyway, which is over twice what the follower cards pay. But each take can be answered: a rival with a minion left takes a token space and the token back. If play shows it never gets answered, the fix is "once per round".
- Both sides: no printed choice. The owner chooses when to recruit purple: on a late turn while still holding a minion. Rivals choose whether to buy the purple members in the shop to deny it.
- Who feels it: the token holder (a sting), and the table, which now watches purple members in the shop.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence, done when you recruit, and the token moves on the table. Fun: the shop becomes part of the token fight. Interacts: with the token holder and the shop row. Powerful: yes. Engine: Purple's members feed the contest, and with Sleepyheads each recruit pays. Its color: going deep in purple pays off in the token fight.
- Not refused: no member that takes the token on a recruit has been pitched before.

### Pick 3: Pocket Watch (the cloak, fix)

**Pocket Watch** "Whenever any player takes the Goes Last token, +2 purple."

- Printed text and what it touches: the three token spaces, Sleepyheads "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +3 followers.", and the other live cloaks: Magenta "Whenever you steal, +1 magenta.", Silver "Whenever you gift silver, +3 silver.", White "Whenever you buy followers, +2 white.".
- One played turn, round II, 4 players. Dee holds Pocket Watch. Ana takes **Sleep In**, then Ben takes **Last Call** and the token from her. Dee took neither: +4 purple. The printed cloak gives Dee 0 that round. If Dee takes the token herself, she gets +2 instead of +3.
- Over a game the token is taken about 4 to 6 times, so the fix pays 8 to 12 purple, inside Cory's 10 to 12 target, against about 6 today.
- Cory's note: going last already pays. The fixed cloak stops adding to your own take (+2 in place of +3) and pays you for the fight around you. It now differs from Sleepyheads in who sets it off (any player, not only you) and in what it pays (purple, not followers). It stays weaker than the members.
- Both sides: no choice. Who feels it: mainly the owner; rivals see that each take pays the cloak. As with the White cloak, that is enough for a cloak.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence. Fun: small, steady, and tied to the color's main fight. Interacts: with every token take. Powerful: no, on purpose. Engine: purple toward Purple's members. Its color: the token.
- Not refused: the Purple cloak options on Nov 1 were "+2 purple per skipped turn" (picked, then replaced by the token version), "+1 purple and +1 follower", and "+1 of the resource you have least of". A take by any player was never pitched.

### Cut, and why
- On Use, once per round: take the Goes Last token (Go-Getter, Stragglers). In round III it is a +10 on demand that nobody can answer once the token spaces are gone, about 20 followers a game from one member. Too strong.
- "Round Start: if you have the fewest followers (ties count), take the Goes Last token: your last minion is placed after everyone else's." (Late Risers, pitched Nov 7, no verdict.) It goes useless once you're winning, which Cory's standing taste rules out.
- "Round Start: take the Goes Last token." In round III, unless a rival spends a minion on a token space, it is +10 for doing nothing, and it turns every round into the table chasing one player. Too strong for an ordinary turn.
- On Use, once per game: take the Goes Last token. A twin of Latecomers that is harder to answer, because it doesn't show up in the shop first.
- "Your Goes Last token can't be taken." It ends the token fight Cory calls "maybe best competition" and locks the contest every round. Too strong, and it kills the fun.
- "When you take the Goes Last token, you may give it to a rival instead," and Courier handing the token on. Giving the token gives a rival the contest, so nobody would choose it.
- "You count as having placed each round's last minion" (split the contest). It halves the prize for whoever wins the fight, which flattens the tension Cory likes.
- "Whenever a rival takes the Goes Last token from you, +3 followers." The token resets each round and is taken from a holder only 1 or 2 times a game, so it does almost nothing.
- "Whenever a rival takes the Goes Last token, +2 followers." The same trigger and payout as Sleepyheads, from the other side: too close to a card in the same color.
- "Whenever you place a minion once everyone else is out of minions, +2 followers." This is Holdouts, which Cory refused ("I also don't like holdouts").
- "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +1 minion this round." This is Night Owls again, which Cory retired ("weird").
- "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, recruit once more." Extra recruits are Blue's job.
- "Whenever you take the Goes Last token from a rival, steal 2 purple." Stealing is Magenta's job.
- "Whenever a rival places the round's last minion, +3 followers." It pays you for losing and goes useless when you win.
- Cloak "Whenever your turn is skipped, +2 purple." Cory already moved the cloak away from this.
- Cloak "Whenever a player takes two turns in a row, +1 purple." It fires all the time (the turn of the snake alone does it twice a round), and that trigger already belongs to Courier, Lookouts and Hot Streak.
- Cloak "Whenever you place the round's last minion, +3 purple." This is the old Closer, and it adds more to going last, which is the problem Cory named.
