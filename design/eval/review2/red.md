# Red review 2 (Nov 8)

**Red's story.** Red organizes members: it moves them between cults and the shop, and it gets them free. Its contest is now **Most members of one color** (grey counts as a color, each card counts 1), so Red wins by reshaping its color groups and breaking up rivals' groups. Cory: "Red should basically be organizing members. like moving them around. maybe just rewarded for that and nothing else."

**A great Red game.** Round I: a cheap member or two (Seat Filler, Foster Parents, Loyalists), fuel from Gather. Round II: Promote and Demote move members toward one color, and Body Snatchers takes a rival's member at round start. Round III: Initiation brings in a free big member of your main color, Panel makes one more swap, and Patriarch and the contest pay at the end. Rivals answer with Loyalists, by taking your members back, or by grabbing the color you need first.

Prices below are what a player pays (the deck file's numbers). Round I members cost 6 or less.

## Assessment (every card)

Spaces
- **Tryout** (round I) "Use the On Recruit effect of a shop member; it stays in the shop. +2 red." **Keep for now.** Cory marked it fine, but it "overlaps with green": it borrows a card's ability, which is close to Green's copying. It's the next Red card to replace after the four below.
- **Gather** (round I) "+2 red in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III." **Keep.** Every color has one fuel space, and this one is tied to recruiting. "Not the most interesting" is a quiet card, not a weak one.
- **Walk-In** (round I) "Recruit the top member of the pile. Each rival +1 red. +1 red." **Replace** (Cory: "I don't want randomness involved"). Pick 1.
- **Promote** (round II) "Swap any member with one in another cult or the shop costing up to 3 more. +3 red." **Keep** (star). It's Red's best tool for building one color group.
- **Demote** (round II) "Swap any member with one in another cult or the shop costing up to 3 less. +3 red." **Keep** (star). It now also takes a cheap member of the color you need from a rival's group.
- **Initiation** (round III) "Recruit a member in the shop costing 10 or less, for free. +1 red." **Keep** (star). Cory said "might need a little balancing on the price cap". Leave the cap until human games show a problem; no bot games for this.

Members
- **Body Snatchers** (7) "Round Start: swap one of your members with any rival's member." **Keep.** Cory's new text from tonight. Top case: at the starts of rounds II and III, swap your cheapest (5) for a rival's priciest (12 or more), twice a game. That's strong, and Cory called it "maybe a little too good, but let's try it". Loyalists is the answer to it. One loose end: the decision log says its price stayed at 6, but the deck shows 7.
- **Seat Filler** (5) "Round Start: you may give this to the shop and recruit a shop member costing up to 3 more, for free." **Fix the wording to his note** ("needs to say discard"): "Round Start: you may discard this and recruit a shop member costing up to 3 more, for free." Today it goes face up into the shop, so this changes where it ends up: the next player can no longer pick it up from the row.
- **Mentor** (11) "On Recruit: recruit the cheapest member in the shop." **Replace** (Cory: "too hard to build around"; he has also said he doesn't like "recruit the cheapest"). Pick 3.
- **Proselytizers** (10) "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower." **Keep.** Followers, simple, and it pays off Red's many free recruits.
- **Mob** (14) "Round End: +2 followers per rival with fewer red members than you." **Replace** (Cory: "don't like this"). It also counts red members, which the contest no longer does. Pick 2.
- **Recruiter** (8) "Whenever you recruit a member, +1 red." **Keep** (Cory: "yes"). Note: the Red Cloak has this exact text, and cloaks should differ from their color's members. If anything changes, change the cloak, not this member. That's outside this page.
- **Patriarch** (10) "Game End: +1 follower per 2 members you have." **Keep** (star).
- **Foster Parents** (6) "Whenever a member joins your cult from a rival's, +2 followers." **Keep** (star). Under the new contest, taking a rival's member of your color grows your group and shrinks theirs, so it now touches colors.
- **Deprogrammers** (5) "Whenever you take a rival's member out of their cult, they lose 1 follower." **Replace** (Cory: "I don't like it"). Pick 4.
- **Panel** (9) "On Use, once per round: promote or demote another of your members." **Keep** (star).
- **Loyalists** (5) "Your other members can't be swapped." **Keep** (star). With Body Snatchers now reaching any member, this matters more.

## Picks

1. **Walk-In's place** (round I space). It's out, and its slot must still work in round I, when most players hold 0 or 1 members and few resources.
2. **Mob's place** (top-price member). Cory marked it weak, and it counts something the contest no longer counts.
3. **Mentor's place** (pricey member). Marked weak: "too hard to build around".
4. **Deprogrammers' place** (cheap member). Marked weak: "I don't like it".

Body Snatchers was also marked weak, but Cory has already rewritten it. Tryout comes next if Cory wants a fifth.

## Replacements

### Pick 1: Walk-In's place (round I space)

The current card's best turn: round I, 4 players, your first minion. You take the top card of the pile, which in round I costs 6 or less, and hope it fits. Each rival gets +1 red. What players would miss: a free member in round I, when resources are scarce. What Cory dislikes is only the luck. Starting from "keep it" (fix 1 of the judging steps), the fix is to let the player choose.

**1A. Walk-In** "Recruit a shop member costing 6 or less, for free. Each rival +1 red. +1 red." [default]
- Printed beside: **Initiation** "Recruit a member in the shop costing 10 or less, for free. +1 red." **Gather** "+2 red in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower in round I…"
- Played, round I, 4 players. Ana goes first, holding 1 red from her cloak and nothing else. Her best other space is Gather: +2 red, but with 3 resources she can't buy a member, so she gets no follower. On Walk-In she picks any card in the row of 6 for free, say a 5-cost blue member to start a blue group, plus 1 red. Each rival gets +1 red. Difference: about a 5-cost member, against 2 red.
- Round III: a member costing 6 or less is small next to Initiation's 10, so it never becomes a second Initiation. A copy recruits another one, as the old Walk-In did.
- Choices: Ana picks which card. She takes the card that starts her color group, or the one a rival needs. Both are real picks.
- Who feels it: every rival gets +1 red; the player who wanted that card loses it and has a reason to take this space first.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence, the same words as Initiation. Fun: the first real pick of the game, everyone watching which color you start. Interacts: the gift to rivals, and the card you deny them. Powerful: a free member in the round when nobody can afford one. Engine: it starts the color group the contest pays for. Its color: Red gets members free (not new, but this is the current card without the luck).
- Not refused: Cory never objected to the free member, only the randomness. **Risk, said plainly:** this is a smaller Initiation in round I, and pitches with that shape have been refused before. The old Walk-In lived beside Initiation without complaint because it came with the pile's luck and the gift to rivals. If Cory sees it as "a smaller Initiation", 1B is the different version.

**1B. Walk-In** "Recruit a member costing 6 or less from the shop or the discard pile, for free. Each rival +1 red. +1 red."
- Played, round I: the discard pile is still empty, so it plays exactly like 1A.
- Played, round II, 4 players. Ben holds 2 green and 1 grey, and Cleo leads Red's contest with 3 blue. The discard pile is face up with last round's 6-card row, including a 5-cost green that Blue's **Out of Style** ("Send every shop member of one color to the discard pile…") threw out. Ben takes it free and ties Cleo at 3. Against Gather's +3 red, he gains a member of the exact color he needs and a share of the +6 prize.
- Round III: the discard pile holds two old rows (13 or more cards), so the pick gets wider each round without any number changing. The cap of 6 keeps it below Initiation.
- Choices: which card, and from which place. Shop or discard pile is a real choice, because taking from the shop also denies rivals.
- Who feels it: the White player who discarded that member, or the Blue player who sent it away, sees it come back to work for you. Every rival gets the +1 red.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence; the discard pile is face up already. Fun: "I'll take that one back." Interacts: it feeds on what White discards and Blue sends away, but the old row goes to the discard pile every round, so it works without either color. Powerful: a free pick from a bigger choice than the shop. Engine: it fixes your color group for the contest. Its color: new for Red, organizing the members other players threw away.
- Not refused: no recruit from the discard pile has been pitched to Cory. Blue's **Dumpster Dive** ("Look at the top 3 members of the discard pile and recruit one…") was a trial card in Blue and isn't live. Flag: Cory may say the discard pile is Blue's.

### Pick 2: Mob's place (top-price member)

The current card's best turn: round III end, 4 players. You hold more red members than all three rivals, +6 followers. Cory doesn't like it. It isn't the default, because he has said he dislikes it.

**2A. Exchange Students** (12, Claude's fill-in) "On Use, once per game: give a rival all your other members of one color, and take as many of theirs as you gave, your pick."
- Played, round III, 4 players, your third turn. Ana leads Red's contest with 4 blue. Ben has 3 green. You have 3 grey (5, 6 and 7 cost, worth 1 each), Exchange Students, and 1 red member. You give Ana your 3 grey and take her 3 priciest blue (10, 9 and 8 cost, worth 3, 2 and 2).
  - Now you have 3 blue and Ana has 3 grey and 1 blue, so you, Ana and Ben tie at 3 of one color. Each tied leader gets half the +10, rounded up: +5.
  - Ana drops from a likely +10 to +5, and the swap costs her 4 followers of member worth. You gain 4 worth and +5 from the contest.
  - Against the best round III spaces: Initiation is a free member costing 10 or less, worth 2 or 3 followers, and buying followers turns 12 resources into 12. Exchange Students swings about 9 followers your way and 9 off Ana, once a game, for 12. That's big, but within about twice the best alternative.
- Member count never changes (you give as many as you take), so Blue's Most members contest isn't touched. It only reshapes colors.
- Choices: you pick which of your colors to give and which of theirs to take. Giving away your second-biggest group can hand the rival a new group. Giving your biggest group wrecks yours. So the choice of color is real every time.
- Who feels it: the rival hit loses their best members and maybe the contest. Everyone with a big group has a reason to buy **Loyalists** ("Your other members can't be swapped."), which blocks it.
- Top case: the most it can do is swap 4 cheap cards for a rival's 4 best. That needs the rival to hold 4 or more swappable members, and it happens once.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence, no costs to compare. Fun: the biggest single reshuffle in the game, with a clear victim. Interacts: Red's contest, Loyalists, any rival's groups. Powerful: whole groups change hands. Engine or counter: it breaks the contest leader's group, or finishes yours. Its color: organizing members as whole groups, at a bigger size than Body Snatchers' one-for-one.
- Not refused: the refused Silver "swap your silver for a rival's whole pile" was too strong because 1 could take 10. Here the counts are equal, so that case can't happen. Body Snatchers is the one-card sibling at a different size, the kind Cory has said yes to (Drifter beside Promote).

### Pick 3: Mentor's place (pricey member)

The current card's best turn: round III, you recruit Mentor (11) and the cheapest shop member comes along free, usually a 5 or 6 you didn't choose. What players would miss: the second member. What Cory dislikes: you can't plan around which card comes.

**3A. Long-Lost Cousins** (13, Claude's fill-in) "On Recruit: recruit a member of the color you have the most of, from the shop or the discard pile, for free."
- Played, round III, 4 players. You hold 3 yellow members and Cleo holds 4 green. You buy Long-Lost Cousins for 13 and take the 10-cost yellow from the shop free. Now you have 4 yellow, tied with Cleo for the +10 (5 each).
  - Mentor would have given you a 5-cost grey.
  - Against spending 13 on 13 followers: you get two members (worth 2 and 3 followers), the yellow card's ability, and +5 from the contest. That's about the same, but it builds toward your plan.
- Top case: a 14-cost member of your color is free with a 13-cost card. That's two big members for 13, with the contest on top. It's strong, but it's one card, once, in round III, and that's why it's priced at 13.
- Choices: which member of that color, and from which place. With a tie for your biggest color, you choose which group to grow. That's a real choice when the contest is close.
- The discard pile keeps it from ever being dead: the old rows are there even when Blue's Out of Style has cleared your color from the shop.
- Who feels it: the rival you're racing for the most members of one color. A card leaving the shop also stings whoever wanted it.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence; "the color you have the most of" is what the rim counts. Fun: you plan for it, and it lands as a free big card. Interacts: Red's contest, and what White and Blue throw away. Powerful: a free member with no cost cap. Engine: built around your color group, which is what Cory asked Mentor to be. Its color: Red recruits free.
- Not refused: Godfather ("recruit a shop member costing up to the number of members you have") was cost math, and Cory said "absolutely not". This has no cost math. **Flag:** if Cory takes 1B, the two share the discard-pile reach. Pick one of them for it; drop "or the discard pile" from this one and it still works most games.

### Pick 4: Deprogrammers' place (cheap member)

The current card's best turn: round II, you Demote and take a rival's 9-cost member, and they lose 1 follower. That's a small sting, and Cory doesn't like it. Not the default.

**4A. Concerned Parents** (5) "Whenever a rival takes one of your members, +3 followers."
- Played, round III, 4 players. Ben's **Body Snatchers** ("Round Start: swap one of your members with any rival's member.") swaps his 5-cost for your 10-cost. You get +3 followers and his 5-cost. Later Cleo takes **Promote** ("Swap any member with one in another cult or the shop costing up to 3 more. +3 red.") and also looks at your cult, but taking from you now gives you +3, so she takes from Ana instead.
  - Against Deprogrammers' 1 follower off a rival: this pays 3 to its owner, and it changes where rivals aim.
- Top case: two Red players and a Magenta player take from you 4 times in a game: +12. Each take also costs you a member (or trades one), and the takers can simply go elsewhere, so it limits itself.
- Choices: nobody decides anything on another player's turn. The trigger just pays. The taker weighs the member against giving you +3.
- Who feels it: every player with a swap or a steal. It's a reason to leave you alone, or a price they pay.
- Six principles. Intuitive: one sentence, the same words as Turncoats used. Fun: the taker pauses; the owner smiles when robbed. Interacts: Body Snatchers, Promote, Demote, Panel, and any rival's take, from any color. It never names one. Powerful: 3 followers for something rivals do anyway. Counter: Loyalists blocks the take; this lets it happen and charges for it. It's the other way to defend. Its color: members moving, seen from the side they leave. It's also where Red meets Cory's "when a member leaves your cult" for White, without depending on White.
- Not refused: Deprogrammers was the taker's-side sting. This pays the member's old owner. Turncoats (free recruit when taken) was cut below because its owner picks a card on someone else's turn. This needs no pick. **Flag:** a lab check once cut a "+3 when one of yours leaves" as a mirror of Foster Parents. That was for Proselytizers' place and an engine role. Here it's a counter, and it fires only when a rival takes the member, not when you give one away.

### Cut, and why

Walk-In's place
- "Recruit a shop member costing up to 4 in round I, 6 in II, 8 in III, for free": a smaller Initiation that grows into a second one in round III.
- "Recruit a shop member of a color one of your members has, for free": in round I most players have no members, so it does nothing (the same failure as tonight's refused round I swap).
- "Each player, you first, recruits a shop member costing 4 or less for free": rivals pick on your turn, and it hands them as much as you get.
- "+2 red. This turn your red can pay any member's colored part": small, and it's Yellow's job (Interpreters).
- "Recruit a member of one color; send the rest of that color in the shop to the discard pile": clearing a color from the shop is Blue's Out of Style.
- "Recruit a shop member, paying followers instead": you start with 1 follower, so it's dead in round I.
- "Choose a shop member; only you can recruit it this round": access without power, refused for Blue.
- "Recruit from a rival's cult, paying them its cost": nobody holds members in round I.

Mob's place
- "Game End: +2 followers per member of your biggest color": pays the contest's winner a second time, sits next to Patriarch, and scores on a count Cory has turned down.
- "Round End: +1 follower per member in your biggest color group": the same, every round.
- "On Use, once per game: swap all your members of one color for all of a rival's of that color": 1 for 5 is the refused "my one silver for all your gold".
- "Round End, before contests: swap one of your members with any rival's member": a near twin of Body Snatchers at the next moment.
- "Round End: swap any two rivals' members": Promote and Demote lost that power on Nov 1, and it makes a kingmaker.
- "Your members count as one color you choose": the Dabblers shape Cory called an auto-win.
- "Your swaps ignore cost limits": a member that upgrades shared spaces, refused Nov 3.

Mentor's place
- "On Recruit: swap any number of your members for shop members of one color": too big, and fixing it needs cost math.
- "On Recruit: take one member from each rival, give each one of yours": Tithe Takers' shape, and it overlaps Exchange Students.
- "On Recruit: recruit a shop member costing up to the number of members you have": Godfather, refused.
- "Game End: +3 followers per color you have 3 or more members of": scoring on variety, Yellow's ground.
- "Whenever you recruit a member of your biggest color, +2 followers": Proselytizers with a condition stuck on.

Deprogrammers' place
- **Stagehands** "Whenever you swap a member, +1 follower": it's Cory's own sentence ("rewarded for that and nothing else"), but it overlaps Foster Parents and Proselytizers, and he said it about the cloak. Better raised as the cloak's text (outside this page).
- **Turncoats** "Whenever a rival takes one of your members into their cult, recruit a shop member costing up to its cost, for free": its owner picks a card during a rival's turn.
- "Whenever one of your members goes to a rival's cult, +2 followers": fires on your own swaps too, so it overlaps Foster Parents.
- **Exit Counselor** "Whenever you give one of your members away, use its On Recruit": re-using an ability is Tryout's overlap with Green again.
- "On Use, once per round: a member becomes a color you choose": a table needs a token to track it.
- "This counts as a member of your biggest color": a twin of grey Dabblers.
- "On Use, once per game: swap this with any rival's member": a 5-cost that takes a 14-cost, over twice the best alternative.
- "Rivals can't take your members of your biggest color": a narrower Loyalists.
- "Round End: you may swap one of your members with a shop member costing the same or less": a weaker Panel.
