# Grey (shared) members: review 2 (Nov 8)

Judged with the card-judging steps (version 2 plus fixes), the taste guide, Cory's marks, and tonight's contests. Prices are as a player sees them. New prices and names are Claude's fill-ins.

## Grey's story

**One line**: grey members are rule-benders any cult can use in any game. They bend the pieces every game shares (minions, turn order, how members count, how you pay, contests) and never do one color's job.

**A great game with grey**: in round I a cheap grey (Line Cutters, Dabblers) gives you a head start on turn order or member count. In round II a mid grey changes how you play: Alchemists lets any resources pay for any member, Con Men raids the table. In round III the big greys (Extra Hand, Spoilsport) set up one swing turn that rivals can see coming and try to buy first. Rivals answer by buying the grey before you do. Every cult at the table wants grey members, so they're the cards people fight over in the shop.

**Contests that touch grey** (tonight's): Red's Most members of one color counts grey as a color. Blue's Most members counts Dabblers as 2 and Line Cutters as 3. White's Fewest members counts Ghost as 0. Yellow's Most member colors counts grey as one color.

**Fresh start**: about 4 kinds of grey. One cheap member that bends turn order. One that changes how members count. One that changes how you pay. One table-wide disruptor that counters an engine. Today's deck has all four. It also has two cards that do the same job (Party Crasher and Extra Hand both bend minion placement) and two that are really contest cards (Sore Loser, Cheerleaders).

## Assessment (every member in the deck)

- **Party Crasher** (7): "On Use, once per game: your next minion can be placed on a space a rival already took." **Replace.** Cory: "objectively very good… a little boring… cheap investment for a huge swing", and "we probably don't need this and party crashers". Its best turn: in round III you take Initiation ("Recruit a member in the shop costing 8 or less, for free. +1 red.") after a rival already took it, which is a free member worth up to 8 for a 7-cost card. That's the same kind of moment as Extra Hand (a bonus placement). Extra Hand is starred, so Party Crasher is the one you'd miss least.
- **Alchemists** (7): "All your resources count as any when you recruit a member." **Keep** (star). This is how you pay across colors, and nothing else does it.
- **Extra Hand** (10): "On Use, once per game: +1 minion this round." **Keep** (star, "a good one to fight over"). Its top case is +1 minion in round III, a sixth placement, worth about one round III space (~8 to 10), which is under twice the alternative.
- **Line Cutters** (4): "You go first every round." (In the game it's also worth 3 followers and counts as 3 members, though the deck line doesn't show it.) **Keep.** Cory: "plays weak… but it does feel elemental and is nice as a cheap one." From round II the fewest-followers player goes first anyway, so the turn-order part fades. What carries it now is counting as 3 members for Blue's Most members, and that's a quiet job, not a weak one. Three earlier tries to replace it found nothing better (ledger, Nov 4). One possible number change, Cory's call: show "counts as 3" on the card, since a table can't know it otherwise.
- **Ghost** (5): "Worth 0 members. Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers." **Fix.** Cory: "I really like this. maybe… slightly redesign so that it doesn't require something." Today nobody recruits it on purpose. It only stings once a Red swap, a White gift or Anarchist moves it, so it depends on other cards. Fix below.
- **Dabblers** (5): "Counts as 2 wild members." **Keep** (star). It beat its single version in blind tests, and tonight it feeds three contests (Blue's member count, Red's one color, Yellow's colors).
- **Cheerleaders** (7): "Whenever you win a contest, +2 followers." **Keep for now; hold for the contests color.** Cory: "better in a dedicated competition color." It's quiet, not broken, and the current card stays until that color exists. When it does, move this card there.
- **Anarchist** (8): "On Use, once per round: swap any 2 members that cost the same, in any cult or the shop." **Fix.** Cory: "maybe too good. needs redesign", but also "member movement is kind of the only counter some time". Top case: in rounds II and III you swap your weakest member for a rival's best member of the same cost, every round, for one card. That's a member upgrade each round on top of your recruit, and it does Red's job (Body Snatchers: "Round Start: swap one of your members with any rival's member."). Fix below.
- **Spoilsport** (8): "Round End: choose a contest; nobody wins it this round." **Keep** (star, "really interesting strategically"). It's contest-flavored, but it bends every contest at once, which suits grey. It stays.
- **Con Men** (9): "On Recruit: steal 5 resources." **Keep** (star, "fun when it jumps around cults"). It's close to Magenta's job, but Cory starred it. Note it for later, no action now.
- **Sore Loser** (7): "Round End: if you win no contest, steal 5 followers from the rival who wins the most." **Replace in grey; hold the card for the contests color.** Cory: "confusing. should be contest color. needs redesign." It has two conditions and a choice among tied leaders, so it's muddy (taste pattern 7). Since Cory has already said he dislikes it, keeping it isn't an option.

Grey cards outside the deck that Cory marked: Coach, Big Spender and Cheerleader were already cut in review 1. Launderers ("too directly yellow") stays out. Tiebreaker ("You win every contest tie you're in.") is held for the contests color, as Cory said. None needs work here.

## Picks

1. **Anarchist: fix.** Cory's own "needs redesign". Its top case is a free member upgrade every round, and it does Red's job.
2. **Ghost: fix.** Cory's own "doesn't require something". Today it does nothing unless another card moves it.
3. **Party Crasher: replace.** Cory's "a little boring" and "we probably don't need this and party crashers". It overlaps Extra Hand, which he starred. His Nov 8 "there should definitely be a neutral that refreshes shop" fills this slot first.
4. **Sore Loser: replace its grey slot.** Cory's "confusing… should be contest color. needs redesign". The card itself waits for the contests color.

Cards he starred (Alchemists, Extra Hand, Dabblers, Spoilsport, Con Men) aren't touched. Line Cutters stays: he wavered on it, and earlier replacement tries found nothing better.

## Replacements and fixes

### Pick 1. Anarchist (fix)

**Survivor A (recommended). Anarchist, 8: "On Use, once per round: swap any 2 members that cost the same, but not your own."**
- Printed text: two words changed from today's card. You can still swap a rival's member with the shop or with another rival's member. You just can't pull one into your own cult.
- One played turn: round III, 4 players. Ana leads by 9 and is set up with Extra Hand (10, "On Use, once per game: +1 minion this round.") still unused. The shop shows a 10-cost member. On your turn you swap Ana's Extra Hand into the shop and the shop card into her cult. Ana loses her sixth minion: about one round III space, ~8 to 10 followers' worth. You gain nothing directly. Your best other choice is to recruit a 7-cost member for yourself (about 7 followers' worth). Against the leader the swap moves the gap by about the same amount, and it also leaves Extra Hand in the shop for anyone to buy, you included, with your next recruit.
- Today's card in the same turn: you swap your 10-cost member for Ana's Extra Hand and use it yourself, so +1 minion for you and −1 for her. That's twice the swing every round. So the fix shrinks the top case. Is the moment still big? Yes: breaking the leader's engine is the counter Cory said grey needs.
- Both sides: only the owner chooses, on their own turn. Nobody else decides anything.
- Who feels it: two rivals, every round. The leader has a reason to buy Anarchist first, or to avoid relying on one key member.
- Six principles: intuitive (one sentence, today's words plus "but not your own"); fun (a sting someone notices); interacts with every color, since any member can move; feels powerful (it wrecks a plan); counters engines and doesn't build its owner's; a new way in, because grey becomes the table's disruptor instead of a second Red. Not refused: same card, smaller. The refused movers (Usurper, Exile, Royalties, Picketers) all moved members into or out of your own cult or added conditions.
- Risk in one line: at 2 players it needs the shop to have a member of the same cost, which is usually true from round II.

**Survivor B. Anarchist, 8: "On Use, once per game: swap any 2 members that cost the same, in any cult or the shop."**
- Today's card, once per game instead of once per round. Same round III turn: you take Ana's Extra Hand for your 10-cost member, once. The top case drops from every round to one turn, but it's still a profit for you.
- It keeps today's moment and cuts its repeats. It still does Red's job (Body Snatchers), which is why A is recommended.

Cut: "swap one of your members with a shop member of the same cost" (Red's Seat Filler and Promote job); "only rivals' members, not the shop" (dies at 2 to 3 players).

### Pick 2. Ghost (fix)

**Survivor (my reading of "doesn't require something": it shouldn't need another card to move it). Ghost, 5: "Worth −5 followers and 0 members. On Recruit: give this member to a rival."**
- Printed text: the −5 is printed beside the member mark, the way other members show their worth, so a table tracks it with no trigger to remember. Whoever holds Ghost at game end loses 5.
- One played turn: round II, 4 players. Ana leads Bo by 6. You pay 5 any for Ghost and give it to Ana. She's −5 at game end. Your best other choice is buying 5 followers with the same 5 resources. Against Ana it's the same 5-point swing. Against Bo and Cy it's 5 worse. So you take it when one rival is the threat. The extra pull: it costs nothing to keep in your cult, it takes no member slot in Blue's or White's contests, and Ana now has to spend a Red swap, a White discard or an Anarchist use to pass it on, and whoever ends up with it groans.
- Both sides: the owner picks the rival on their own turn. Nobody decides anything else.
- Who feels it: the rival who gets it (a sting), and anyone it could be passed to (a reason to keep ways to pass it).
- Six principles: intuitive (one sentence); fun (hot potato, Cory's original "Old Maid"); interacts with Red, White and Anarchist moves without needing any of them; feels powerful as a targeted −5; counters the leader; a new way in (grey's only card that hurts by being held). Not refused: it's Cory's own card with a new way in. He set −5 and price 5 (Nov 1, Nov 6). Both kept.
- Risk in one line: a once-only 5-point sting for a recruit's worth can look flat next to buying 5 followers, so Cory may want −6.

Cut: "On Recruit: give this to a rival; they −5 followers" (loses the hot potato: once it lands, nobody minds holding it); keeping "whenever it joins your cult, −5" plus On Recruit (you'd lose 5 when you recruit it).

### Picks 3 and 4. Two open grey slots (Party Crasher's, Sore Loser's)

Seventeen candidates, one list for both slots. Cory, Nov 8 (relayed): "there should definitely be a neutral that refreshes shop." So one slot goes to a shop refresher first.

**Survivor 0 (Party Crasher's slot, first choice). Scouts, 5, worth 2 members: "On Use, once per round: send any shop members to the discard pile, then refill."**
- Printed text: this is the grey card that was live until Nov 3. It left only to make room for Spoilsport (Claude's call), and Cory had said "I like scout, why cut? … make it more members maybe". It's worth 2, as he asked. Blue cards for comparison: Window Dressers "On Recruit: send 3 shop members to the discard pile, then refill."; Trendsetters "Whenever you recruit a member, send 1 shop member to the discard pile, then refill."; Bury "Send a shop member to the discard pile, then refill. Gain blue equal to half its cost, rounded down. +1 blue."; Restockers (out of the 10 deck) "On Use, once per game: send the whole shop to the discard pile, then refill."
- One played turn: round II, 4 players. The shop is 7 cards, and none fits your colors. Ana has saved 9 for Con Men ("On Recruit: steal 5 resources.") on her next turn. You use Scouts, send Con Men and three cards you don't want to the discard pile, and refill 4. Now you recruit from a fresh row, and Ana's plan is gone. Your best other choice without it: buy followers with what you hold, or recruit a member you don't want. The gain is a better recruit every round, plus breaking a rival's saved-up buy.
- Against Party Crasher's best turn (a free round III member worth up to 8, once): Scouts makes a smaller moment every round and a distinct one, since the shop belongs to everyone and it fills the gap Cory named.
- Both sides: only the owner chooses (which cards go), on their own turn.
- Who feels it: anyone saving for a shop card, so a reason to buy Scouts first or to recruit sooner.
- Six principles: intuitive (Blue's own words); fun (a sting and a fresh row); interacts with every color's shop plans; feels powerful as a once-per-round control; counters a saving plan; a new way into grey (the only grey that touches the shop).
- Doing Blue's job: it does what Blue's shop cards do, once a round, for anyone. It doesn't do Blue's other parts (extra recruits, gaining blue per card sent, a look at the pile), so Blue still owns turning the shop into gains. If Cory feels it outdoes Blue, the smaller version is "On Use, once per round: send up to 2 shop members to the discard pile, then refill."
- Not refused: Cory's own liked card, and its earlier removal wasn't his call.
- Cut shop refreshers: "On Use, once per game: send the whole shop to the discard pile, then refill." (that's Restockers, a Blue card); "On Recruit: send the whole shop to the discard pile, then refill." (Restockers' twin, one shot); "Round Start: refresh the shop" (the row already refreshes between rounds, so it does nothing).

**Survivor 1 (Party Crasher's slot). Masqueraders, 6: "On Recruit: swap cloaks with a rival."**
- Printed text: cloaks today, for reference. White "Whenever you buy followers, +2 white." Red "Whenever you recruit a member, +1 red." Purple "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +3 purple." Magenta "Whenever you steal, +1 magenta." Green "Whenever you copy an action or a member, +2 of its color." Yellow "Whenever you recruit a member of a color none of your members have, +2 yellow." Blue "Whenever you discard a member, +1 of its color." Silver (being redone).
- One played turn: round II, 4 players. Ana has bought followers every turn with the White Cloak (+2 white each time). You hold the Purple Cloak and never get the Goes Last token. You recruit Masqueraders for 6 and swap. For the rest of the game you get +2 white on each follower buy (about 3 a round, so ~+12 white over rounds II and III), and Ana is stuck with a cloak her plan never uses. Your best other choice is 6 followers. Party Crasher's best turn, for comparison: a round III free member worth up to 8, once. This one is smaller but more distinct. It's the first card that touches cloaks, and a rival loses the boon they picked at setup.
- Both sides: the owner picks the rival and swaps on their own turn.
- Who feels it: the rival whose plan leaned on their cloak, so the cloak boon is no longer safe.
- Six principles: intuitive; fun (a sting); interacts with every color through its cloak; feels powerful moderately (cloaks are kept weaker on purpose, so the swap is mid-sized); counters a cloak engine; a new way in (no card touches cloaks today).
- Not refused, with one flag: Heist "Take a rival's cloak until the round ends. +1 magenta." lost a paper ranking on Nov 4 ("small trigger, rarely a big moment"). Cory never saw it. This one is for good, not one round, and it's a member, not a space. Still, it's the nearest shape on record.

**Survivor 2 (Party Crasher's slot). Superfans, 7: "Game End: +2 followers per grey member, anyone's."**
- One played turn (top case): round III, 4 players. At game end the table holds Dabblers, Con Men, Alchemists, Ghost, Spoilsport and Superfans: 6 grey members, so +12 followers. A typical table has 3 or 4, so +6 to +8. Your best other choice at 7 any is 7 followers now. The top case is under twice the alternative, so it doesn't break the game.
- Both sides: no choice. It pays on anyone's grey recruit, which is taste pattern 3, a payoff that fires on anyone's move.
- Who feels it: the owner, quietly (pattern 6). Rivals see that buying a grey also pays you, a mild reason to think twice.
- Six principles: intuitive; fun mostly for the owner; interacts with every cult's buys; powerful in the top case; builds a small engine around the shared cards; a new way in (grey has no scorer since HR left the deck).
- Not refused. Same shape as Patron Saints ("Game End: +1 follower per silver member, anyone's."), which Cory said yes to. It pays in another color at a different size (pattern 9's "sibling" yes).
- Risk in one line: it may read as quiet "small ball", so Masqueraders comes first.

**Survivor 3 (Sore Loser's slot). Bulk Buyers, 7: "Whenever you buy 10 followers at once, +5 followers."**
- Why this slot: Cory, Nov 7: "there was kind of a strategy to hold resources and buy followers. I dont want to lose that entirely." Tonight's contests are almost all about members. This keeps the hold-and-cash plan alive in every game, and it's grey because buying followers is a move every color has.
- One played turn: round III, 4 players. You've held resources through round II (which also helps you in Magenta's Most resources). You buy 10 followers for 10 resources and get 15. Your best other choice: the same 10 resources buy a 10-cost member, or 10 followers without this card. Top case: two 10-buys in round III plus one in round II is +15 over the game, for a 7-cost card. The best round III spaces give about 8 to 10. That's under twice, so not broken, and getting 30 resources means skipping most recruits.
- Both sides: no choice. The tension is real, though: holding 10 makes you Magenta's steal target, and holding silver costs you Silver's Fewest silver.
- Who feels it: Magenta players (a fat target), Silver's contest, and the owner when the cash-in lands.
- Six principles: intuitive (one sentence, "buy followers" is Cory's word); fun (the big cash-in turn); interacts with Magenta's steals and contest and Silver's contest; feels powerful (+5 in one buy); builds the hold-and-cash engine; a new way into grey (how you score).
- Not refused. "Bulk Buyer" appeared in an Oct 24 list with no verdict.
- Risk in one line: buying followers is close to White's job ("turns what you have into followers", and the White Cloak pays on buys), so Cory may call it "too white".

**Cut, and why**
- Dabbler-like "counts as a member of the color you choose": twin of Dabblers, and the every-color version already lost to Dabblers.
- "Counts as 3 members, worth 0 followers": twin of Dabblers and Line Cutters' count.
- "On Use, once per game: your next minion may go on a space that opens next round": same shape and problem as Party Crasher (a once-only access to a space, a cheap huge swing).
- "Your members can't be stolen, swapped or given away by rivals": twin of Loyalists (Red) and Bodyguards.
- "Round End: choose a rival; they win no contest this round": twin of Spoilsport, and a contest card.
- "On Use, once per game: you count as the leader of a contest you choose this round": strong and clean, but a contest card. Hold it for the contests color.
- "Whenever a member leaves a rival's cult, +2 followers": needs Red, White or Anarchist to ever fire, so it breaks the needs-another-color rule.
- "On Recruit: steal 5 followers from the rival with the most followers": a leader sting (overrated, per the taste guide), Magenta's job, a twin of Con Men in followers.
- "Recruit from the discard pile": Blue's shop job, and the pile's order is muddy.
- "Game End: each resource you hold is worth 1 follower" (Miser): a weaker twin of Bulk Buyers, since buying is already 1 for 1.
- "On Use: trade places in turn order with a rival": Purple's job.
- "Round Start: if you have the fewest followers, +1 minion": Stand-In, lost to Extra Hand twice, and Purple's job.

## Summary

1. Anarchist fix: "On Use, once per round: swap any 2 members that cost the same, but not your own." (recommended) or once per game.
2. Ghost fix: "Worth −5 followers and 0 members. On Recruit: give this member to a rival."
3. Party Crasher's slot: Scouts back (Cory's shop-refresh ask) "On Use, once per round: send any shop members to the discard pile, then refill." (5, worth 2). Other options: Masqueraders "On Recruit: swap cloaks with a rival." (6), or Superfans "Game End: +2 followers per grey member, anyone's." (7).
4. Sore Loser's slot (Sore Loser held for the contests color): Bulk Buyers "Whenever you buy 10 followers at once, +5 followers." (7), flagged as possibly too white.
5. Keep: Alchemists, Extra Hand, Dabblers, Spoilsport, Con Men, Line Cutters. Cheerleaders stays until the contests color exists.
