## META notes

- g1 Ava: The contests are the heart of the game and the color tricks around them (Dilute, Hold Up, Trade Seeds, Pocket Watch) are fun and tense. What isn't working is that buy-10 makes most members a worse deal than cashing out, so round III turns into everyone dumping their piles to zero, and then whoever places last (Last Call, or Line Cutters/snake ends) takes all four +10 contests for almost nothing. Promote and Demote offering rival-to-rival and wide cost gaps, combined with Recruiter and Deprogrammers, was the biggest feel-bad of the night. First change: make Promote/Demote only swap your own member within the printed cost range, then look at Last Call in round III.
- g1 Ben: The core of the game works: the hold-versus-spend tension on contests is real and interesting in rounds I and II, and the colors feel different. In round III a recruit is worth 10 followers and the contests are worth 10, so whoever places last with the most placements wins the contests, and Last Call hands that seat to one player. The biggest swings came from flat spaces (Party Gifts, Largesse, Walk-In) and the Red swaps, not from engines. First fix: limit Promote and Demote to swapping your own member, as printed, then look at Last Call in round III.
- g1 Cal: The contest prizes and buying 10s dwarf most members by round III; the game came down to who held the last placements, and Last Call let one player go last four times and sweep 40 followers. The gift spaces (Party Gifts, Largesse, Philanthropists) are fun, but they pay the giver a lot, and the silver has to land on whoever isn't fighting the silver leader. Red's swap spaces are the most interesting decisions on the wheel, but the options list went far beyond the printed 'costing up to 2 more/less' and offered rival-to-rival swaps. First change: cap Last Call (for example 'skip your next two turns' instead of 'until everyone else is out') so the +10 contests can't all be swept by one closing run.
- g1 Dee: The contests are the heart of the game and the moment-to-moment reads (Dilute, Toast, Sleep In to move your last minion) are genuinely good. What isn't working: round III is decided by turn order, not by what you built: Last Call hands one player four closing placements against +10 contests, and buying followers 10 at a time makes recruits the only limit, so engines matter less than tempo. Promote and Demote offering rival-to-rival swaps (with Recruiter paying) created the runaway leader and should be fixed first; after that I'd cap or rethink Last Call in round III.
- g2 Ava: The contest race plus theft makes every round a real fight over who holds what at the bell, and Lie Low, Shake Down and Farewell create sharp, readable decisions. What isn't working is that the copy spaces (Echo, Encore, Greatest Hits, Reprise) make On Recruit steals like Con Men and Accomplice repeat over and over, so a player with no resources and no members ends up with nothing to do and nothing to lose, as Ben did. Buying followers only in 1/5/10 packs also strands leftovers on everyone's last turn. The first thing I'd change is to stop copy effects from replaying a rival's On Recruit steals (or cap Encore and Greatest Hits to non-steal effects).
- g2 Ben: The contest economy and the color-by-color holding tension are working, and conversion spaces like Exchange make last turns interesting. What isn't working is that theft is so dense (Help Yourself, Crime Wave, Shake Down, Poach, plus every copy space repeating Con Men and Accomplice) that a player who falls behind round one gets stripped every turn and has no way back; being broke also makes Shake Down a free member. The one thing I'd change first: stop On Recruit steals (Con Men, Accomplice) being copyable by Encore, Echo and Greatest Hits, or make Reprise unable to repeat Shake Down and Poach.

In round III, buying followers beats nearly every member, so the shop goes quiet when it should be peaking. First thing I'd change: stop Reprise and Cover from repeating Poach, and make Encore/Greatest Hits unable to copy steal On Recruits like Con Men.

Lie Low in round III is the one real shield, and it decided this game. First thing I'd change: stop On Recruit copies (Encore, Tryout, Greatest Hits) from hitting steal cards like Con Men and Grifter, or cap what a copy can steal.
