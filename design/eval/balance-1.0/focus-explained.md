# The three places to focus, in plain words

## 1. Green: what a copy can repeat

**Your chain question first.** The game already has that rule: a copy can chain into another copy, but it can never go back to an action already used in the same chain. Echo can copy a Reprise, and that Reprise can copy something else, but nothing in that chain can copy Echo or that Reprise again. The tests for it pass.

**What the AI players actually complained about.** Not Echo itself. Echo ("Copy the previous action. +1 green.") was the Green space they liked most. The complaint was one specific space getting repeated:

- **Largesse** ("Rivals gain up to 10 silver, split as you choose. +1 follower for each.") is worth 10 followers. In one game's round III, four players got it: Ava took it, Cal used Echo right after to get 10 more, Ava used Reprise ("Copy an action with your minion. +1 green.") on her own Largesse for 10 more, and Ben used Party Crasher ("On Use, once per game: your next minion can be placed on a space a rival already took.") to join it for 10 more. That's 40 followers from one space in one round; a contest prize is 10. All four players in that game, and all four in another game, said this was the first thing they'd change.

**Repeating On Recruits.** You may be right that this one is fine. What the games showed:
- **Greatest Hits** ("Copy the On Recruit of each of your members, in any order. +1 green.") won one game by 41 points, with a cult full of Mentor & Protégés ("On Recruit: recruit the cheapest member in the shop."). In another game it gave all four players nothing. The bots win more often when they take it, at every difficulty. So it swings hard rather than being broken every game.
- **Encore** ("Copy the On Recruit of a rival's member. +1 green.") on a rival's **Con Men** ("Counts as 2 members. On Recruit: steal 5 resources.") is a 5-resource steal. Players in five games called that the annoying one, because the same victim got hit twice.

**What a change could look like** (your call, these are directions): leave Echo, Reprise and the On Recruit copies alone, and only stop a copy from paying the followers on Largesse and Party Gifts ("Rivals gain up to 3 silver, split as you choose. +2 followers for each."). Or decide Largesse is fine and the copying is the fun, and change nothing.

## 2. The last few turns of round III

**What happens.** In 5 of the 12 AI games, the winner was decided by the very last placements of the game, by a player acting after everyone else or on a space someone already took. Two cards do this:

- **Last Call** ("+1 purple. +1 follower and +1 purple per minion you have left. Skip your turns until everyone else is out of minions."). In round III the player who takes it places all their remaining minions after everyone else is done. They can see every pile, so they know exactly which contests they can win and by how much. In one game Ava did this and won all four round III contests by one each. The AI players rated Last Call the best space in the game, and the bots win more when they take it at every difficulty.
- **Party Crasher** (text above). In one game Ben used it on the last placement to join Cal's **Makeover** ("+4 yellow, then rearrange your resources into any mix of colors."). He rearranged 19 resources so he had the most red, the most yellow and the most blue, and won all three contests: 30 followers in one turn.

**Why it matters.** The players said it makes holding resources for contests pointless in round III: whoever goes last wins them anyway, so buying followers is safer.

**What a change could look like:** keep Last Call's idea (give up turns now, act last later) but in round III let rivals still place after the caller, or pay less per minion left. For Party Crasher, don't let it join a round III center space.

## 3. White: casting out a member you already used

**What happens.** **Heiresses** ("On Use, once per game: +1 white per member you have.") gave Ben 7 white. Then he used **Estate Sale** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain white equal to its cost. +1 white.") on her and got her full cost, 9 white, even though her once-per-game ability was already spent. 16 white from one member. **Farewell** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain followers equal to its cost. +1 white.") works the same way for followers. All three players in that game named it first.

**Money Changers** ("Whenever any player buys followers, +1 white.") kept Ben ahead in white all game in that same game, but the bots say it's only strong with 4 players and weak with 2 or 3, so it's a watch, not a change.

**What a change could look like:** a member whose once-per-game ability is used pays less when cast out. This fits your Oct 31 note that White needs a revisit.
