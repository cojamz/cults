# Skeptic pass on the balance-1.0 verdicts (deck 10)

Claude, 2026-11-01. I tried to knock down every NUMBER and REWORK verdict in the four color reviews and meta.md (40 in all, counting meta's "copies versus steals" as one). 12 survive, 22 drop to watch or need a human table, and 6 are killed. Nothing was run; I read the reports, choice logs, feel probe, expert games, decisions.md, verdicts.md and the bot code.

## Five things that knock down more than one verdict

1. **For members, the "two choice-log levels" are really one instrument.** Hard and Very Hard only play out the options Normal's quick score already rates within 4 of its best (thinker.js, `window ?? 4`), and each rollout stops at the end of the round and is then scored by Normal's static value. A member with "contender" under about 10% (Courier 9%, Gossip 6%, Tastemaker 6%, Patroness 4%, Wallflower 1%, Hangers-On 12%) was never really weighed by the lookahead. DEAD on those members is Normal's opinion, and a trickle that pays over several rounds is exactly what a round-end rollout can't see. So for members, the choice log counts as backing only together with experts or a played turn.
2. **Artifact lifts are zero-sum.** Each seat drafts one artifact. At Hard, Money Changers (+18, 61 owners) and Recruiter (+21, 56 owners, inflated by the swap bug) take about 2,270 owner-points that come out of the other six. Spread back evenly, that's roughly +6 each at Hard and +5 each at Normal. After that correction Bargain Bin, Guest Book, Caterer and Fence sit at about even at Normal. Only Impressionist stays clearly low (about −9). Fix Money Changers and the swap bug before pricing any other artifact.
3. **The swap bug taints every Red owner.** Red main-color lift is Normal +8, Hard +16, Very Hard 0. Red members are owned mostly by Red players, so a Red member's lift carries the color's bug-fed edge.
4. **Cory has ruled on several of these.** decisions.md: Early Birds blocks your own skips (Oct 22), Line Cutters "does what it says" even when a swap hands it to the leader (Nov 1, 1:12am), Window Shopper stays "for real games to judge" (Oct 30), Stash's cut, Encore's rework on his backlog, the Caterer, Fence and Impressionist tests queued (Nov 1, 10:00am), and Harmonist "needs to be more expensive" (Oct 31).
5. **Two instrument claims in the reviews are wrong.** The red reviewer says "bots never use Hot Streak", and the silver reviewer reads Polisher's On Use as DEAD. leads.md checked 40 Normal games: Hot Streak was used 3 of 9 times and Polisher 11 of 77. The choice log just can't see uses.

## SURVIVES (ranked by how much a player feels it at the table)

1. **Last Call** ("+1 purple. +1 follower and +1 purple per minion you have left. Skip your turns until everyone else is out of minions."). **REWORK, round III only:** keep the trade Cory loved, but shorten the round III block so the other players still have placements after it (for example "skip your next two turns", g1 Cal's idea). Backing: STRONG at all three bot levels (Normal +6, Hard +16, Very Hard +14; winners take it about 2x as often at Hard and Very Hard). The choice log tags it MISJUDGED, and the lookahead likes it more than Normal does (under 74, over 59), so better play makes it stronger. Experts 5.0 / 4.3, with 7 of 8 saying round III is too strong. g1 is played out in the transcript: Ava won all four +10 contests by one each (6-5, 5-3, 4-3, 3-2), 40 followers, after taking Last Call first in round III. **High.** One caveat: g1's table was already split by the swap bug, and meta.md points out that round III buy-10 cash-outs are what make the contests cheap. A contest-side fix could do the same job.
2. **Copies repeat steals:** **Encore** ("Copy the On Recruit of a rival's member. +1 green."), **Greatest Hits** ("Copy the On Recruit of each of your members, in any order. +1 green.") and **Echo** ("Copy the previous action. +1 green.") all replay **Con Men** ("Counts as 2 members. On Recruit: steal 5 resources."). **REWORK:** a copy doesn't repeat a steal from an On Recruit, or a copied steal must hit a different rival. Backing: three AI games (g2 and g3, where all 8 experts named it first; the Nov 1 AI playtest, won by 52 with the victim left on 0 members), plus the probe (copy spaces 8.0-8.5 per placement in round III against 5.4 on average). Bots don't aim several copies at one victim, so their flat Green number (Very Hard −15) isn't evidence against it. **Medium.** Every copier only gains about what **Cover** ("Copy an action with no minion on it. +1 green.") gives (about 6 against 7.0): the harm is the pile-on of one victim, and all the witnesses are AI players, not people. Encore itself is already on your list.
3. **Money Changers** (White's artifact, "Whenever any player buys followers, +1 white."). **NUMBER, fewer fires:** rivals' follower buys only, or only buys of 5 or more. Both still fire on other players' moves, which keeps your Nov 1 direction for artifacts. Backing: Normal +17 (313 owners), Hard +18 (61), Very Hard +20 (22), 3 players +9, 2 players +8. It grows with skill, and it's the biggest artifact edge in the game. Experts 4.5 / 4.0, including "I can't realistically take white off him" (g4 Dee). White sweeps are 15% at Hard, and in the Nov 1 screen playtest the winner held Most white all three rounds in 3 of 7 White games. Played turn: about 10 follower buys in round III give its owner about +10 white without placing, against **Allowance**'s +4 for a minion. **High.**
4. **Party Crasher** ("On Use, once per game: your next minion can be placed on a space a rival already took."). **NUMBER: price 4 to 6.** Backing: Hard +13 STRONG (77 owners), and it grows with skill (Normal +2). Two expert games show the swing: g4 Ben joined **Largesse** ("Rivals gain up to 10 silver, split as you choose. +1 follower for each.") for about +10 and a silver contest; g1 Dee did the same. **Medium.** You made it cheaper on Oct 28 when it became once per game. A price won't change how the victim feels about the last-turn join, so if a table still complains, the center-space limit is next.
5. **Splurge** ("This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower for every 2 resources you pay for it. +1 white."). **NUMBER:** per 2 in round I, per 1 from round II. Backing: experts 1.4 / 1.6 (8 raters, 6 call it a leftover), plus a played turn (round II, a 6-cost buy: Splurge 3 + 1 = 4 against **Allowance** "+2/+3/+4 white; if you recruit a member, +1/+2/+3 follower" = 5). The choice log (Hard DEAD, Very Hard FILLER) is Normal-filtered, so it doesn't count on its own. **Medium.** Risk: at per 1 in round III, a 12-cost member returns 12 followers on top of itself (+6 over buy-10). Check that against "absurd best case" (verdicts #13), or keep round III at per 2.
6. **Crunch Time** ("+2 purple. Your next two turns come right after this one."). **NUMBER: +2 to +4 purple.** Backing: experts 1.0 / 1.0 (8 raters; nobody took it in either Purple game), the probe (2.1-2.3, the lowest round II space), Hard DEAD and Very Hard FILLER. Played turn (round II): **Sleep In** ("+4 purple (+5 in round II, +6 in round III). Skip your next turn.") gives +5 and moves you later; Crunch Time gives +2 and spends your minions early. **Medium.**
7. **Recruit Twice** ("Recruit twice this turn. +3 blue."). **REWORK:** pay for actually using the second recruit on members (followers or blue per member recruited, never a discount). Backing: Normal −5 ±2, 3 players −5, 2 players −4, Hard −7 ±4. It was measured at the +3 blue you picked on Nov 1, so that number didn't fix it (Very Hard +2 on 38 games is the one dissent). Experts 2.9 / 2.9: "mostly read as +3 blue", "dull but correct". **Medium.** You picked a number over a rework once already, and this is the evidence that it didn't work.
8. **Right Away** ("+1 purple (+2 in round II, +3 in round III). Backing: experts 1.4 / 1.4 (8 raters) and a played turn (round II: 1 purple behind Hold Up, nothing a rival feels).
