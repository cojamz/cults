# Buying followers: what the games showed (Nov 1, 10-card deck)

Today: pay 1, 5 or 10 resources of any color for that many followers. Each buy uses one of your recruits for the turn.

## Bot games (about 1,600 four-player games, plus 2 and 3 players)
- One seat per test game only ever buys followers, never members. That seat wins 22% of games against the easiest bots (an even share is 25%), 8% against Hard and 3% against Very Hard. So buying followers alone is a fallback, not a winning plan: the better the table plays, the more members matter.
- At 2 players it wins 43% (even is 50%): close to even. At 3 players, 33% (even).
- Small cults rarely win: the winner had 5 or fewer members in 8% of games against the easiest bots, 9% against Hard, 15% against Very Hard. Your bar is at least 20%. It gets closer as the bots get better, so part of the gap is the easier bots over-buying members.
- Winners usually hold 13 to 15 members.

## AI players (12 games, 40 players)
- Liked: the 1-for-1 rate makes counting easy, and it plays well with the contests (several named "holding a color versus cashing it in" as the core decision).
- About 10 of 40 said that by round III, buying 10 for 10 beats most members on followers, so the shop goes quiet when it should peak; members are bought for their abilities only.
- Some said holding resources for a contest feels like a trap when you can cash in 1-for-1 anytime, mostly when someone else gets to place last (Last Call, Makeover). One player in g10: "Holding resources for contests looks like a trap when you can buy followers at 1:1 any time."
- Players in 3 of 12 games disliked the 1/5/10 packs stranding odd leftovers on the last turn (g2: "I'm throwing away 2 white here"). Only a 1-pack buy spends them, and each buy uses a recruit.
- Money Changers ("Whenever any player buys followers, +1 white.") felt like a tax on everyone's buys in two games.

## How it reads
The shop is doing its job as the safe line: it never wins alone against good play, and it makes the contests a real choice. The two complaints are round III (members lose to a 10-pack) and leftovers on the last turn. The first ties into Last Call: once one player can't take every contest by going last, holding resources is worth more and cashing in less.
