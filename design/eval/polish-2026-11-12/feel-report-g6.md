# Feel report: game 6 (polish batch, Nov 12)

One game, four players: Ava, Ben, Cal, Dee. Colors on the wheel: Red, Purple, Blue, White.
Result: **Dee 119**, Ava 89, Cal 77, Ben 61.
Everything below rests on this one game. Where it says "never", it means never in this game.

---

By player, whole game (hard / obvious / stuck):
- **Dee** (winner): 11 / 1 / 0. Every round II and III placement was hard.

- **Purple**: Sleep In every round (Ben, Cal, Ben). On a Roll every round (Cal, Ben, Cal). Right Away every round (Cal, Cal, Ben). Last Call II (Ben), III (Cal). Crunch Time III only (Cal); empty all of round II. Overtime never taken: Ben blocked it with Protesters all of round III.

### Diaries where a rival's move changed the plan
11 of 12. The one exception was Ava in round I: "Not much... I just ran my own plan."
But the changes stayed in two separate pairs. Ben and Cal fought over the Goes Last token every round. Dee and Ben fought over Red's contest. Cal in round III: "Dee and Ava I never touched and they never touched me."

### Contests
- **Most members (Blue)**: Dee in all three rounds (+4, +6, +10).
- **Most of one color (Red)**: Ben in round I, then Dee in rounds II and III (+6, +10).
- **Went last (Purple)**: Cal, then Ben, then Cal.
- **Fewest members (White)**: Cal in all three rounds. Nobody else tried for it after round I.
- **Ava won no contest.** Her Consolation Prize paid her +5 in rounds II and III.

## 2. Scores against the play

- **Ben** rated "satisfying choices" 4 and said "mostly real decisions". His own tags say fewer than half his placements were hard (5 of 13), and 2 were stuck. Trust the tags: his choices were thin, especially in round I and his final turn.
- **Dee** called Initiation "no real decision" in his ratings but tagged it hard when he placed it. What he weighed was which free member to take. Taking Initiation itself was automatic for whoever went first in round III.
- **Ava** rated "dynamic" 3. That fits: she said she ran her own plan, and the one rival move that touched her was Ben's gift.
- **Fun ratings** (4, 3, 3, 4) match the diaries. The two players who fought the token war (Ben, Cal) gave 3 and both said a runaway leader "took the air out" / "took the shine off". The two who ran engines (Dee, Ava) gave 4.
- **Comeback** was rated 1 or 2 by all four, including the winner. That is the strongest agreement in the game.

## 3. The moments

**Felt great**
- Dee, round II, Promote: "the Promote reaching into a rival's cult and using his own gift against him is a moment I'd tell someone about." Ben had just given Dee Courier with Write-Off. Dee used Promote to swap that same Courier back for Ben's Dabblers, which took Red's +6 from Ben, and Deprogrammers cost Ben 1 more follower.
- Cal, round III: "the token duel with Ben was a real back-and-forth with discard-pile reads, the best part of the game." Cal placed Crunch Time, then Recruit Twice (taking Last Callers out of the discard pile so Ben couldn't buy it), then On a Roll.
- Ben, round III: "Spree on my second minion pulling Latecomers and Timekeeper out of the discard... my favourite turn of the game."
- Ava, round III: "Patrons gave itself to Dee (+7) and Guest of Honor gave itself to Dee (+4), dropping me back to 3 members so Anchorites paid +5. That was +16 off one placement." Also round II: "Splurge into Consolation Prize for +7 was the most satisfying turn I had."

**Felt bad**
- Cal, round I: "Wait, my Seat Filler pick through Test Drive didn't actually give me Deprogrammers." The game itself asked him to pick a member costing up to 8 for free. He picked Deprogrammers and nothing happened. His first turn of the game gave him 2 resources.
- Cal, round II: "Huh, the token doesn't hold the minion you're about to place. Only the later ones. Expensive lesson." He paid 7 for Last Callers, used it on his final minion, and Ben still went last (+6 to Ben, plus Night Managers +3).
- Ben, round III: "Right Away pulled my Latecomers minion forward, so I handed Cal the last placement myself. And my own protest sign is sitting on Overtime." This cost him the +10 he had built the whole round toward.
- Ben, round I: "Dee ran away with a free-member chain I couldn't touch."
- Dee, round III, as the receiver of gifts: "Rivals giving me members late (Protesters, Patrons, Guest of Honor) only padded my lead, which felt backwards."

**Races**
- Borrow in round I. Cal: "Borrow was the race: Borrow into Guest of Honor gift was +4 for one space and Dee got there."
- Farewell in rounds II and III. Cal took it in round II on his own Last Callers, the turn before Dee planned to use it on Martyrs.
- The Goes Last token, every round, between Ben and Cal. The token changed hands six or seven times.

**Swings**
- Dee's round I: Borrow into Guest of Honor (given to Ben for +4), Late Shift into a free Line Cutters (worth 3, counts as 3, goes first every round), then Deprogrammers bought for 5 (worth 4, counts as 4). That put him at 8 members after round I, and both member contests followed for the rest of the game.
- Dee's two Promotes: round II flipped Red (+6), round III took Ben's Night Managers out right after Ben tied Red at 5 to 5.

## 4. Spaces

Each line: how often it was taken and when, whether taking it was a real decision, what players said, and a verdict.

### Red
- **Borrow** (Recruit the cheapest shop member, for free. Discard it at round end, before scoring.) Taken in round I only. With Guest of Honor in the shop, it was the best opener at the table, and three players called it a lot for round I. In rounds II and III the cheapest member was junk or the Ghost, so nobody wanted it. Verdict: **working** in round I, **never wanted** later. If the cheapest member is a card you can give away for followers, Borrow is close to free followers.
- **Late Shift** (Recruit a shop member costing up to 2 per minion you've placed this round, for free.) Taken every round, twice by Dee. Players thought hard about which minion to put on it. The round I free Line Cutters was what started Dee's lead. Verdict: **working**. It is the best Red space, and in round I it hands out a member that is far too good for its price (see Line Cutters below).
- **Gather** (+3 red; +4 followers if you recruit a member of a color you already have.) Taken every round, always as plain resources. The +4 never paid in this game. Ben tried to trigger it with Dabblers, and wild did not count. Verdict: **working as filler**. Its bonus is invisible.
- **Promote** (Swap any member for one costing up to 3 more, from another cult or the shop.) Dee took it in rounds II and III, and both times it decided Red's contest. The best-rated space in the game: Dee 5/5/5, Ava and Cal 4. Ben rated "fun 2": being on the receiving end feels bad, but that is the sting it should have. Verdict: **working**.
- **Demote** (Swap any member for one costing up to 3 less.) Blocked by Protesters in round II. Taken once in round III by Ava, who took it for the red and so Cal couldn't swap her Anchorites for the Ghost, then chose not to swap. Its threat mattered more than any use. Verdict: **too few games to say**.
- **Initiation** (Recruit a shop member costing 10 or less, for free.) Taken first placement of round III by Dee, who goes first every round. Nobody else got near it. Verdict: **always taken** by whoever places first in round III. This game it was the leader, because Line Cutters let him go first.

### Purple
- **Sleep In** (+4/+5/+6 purple, take the Goes Last token.) Taken every round, and it opened each token fight. Ben and Cal tagged it hard. Verdict: **working**.
- **On a Roll** (+2 purple per consecutive turn, take the Goes Last token.) Taken every round, as the cheap way to take the token back. Cal's best placement was On a Roll as his third turn in a row. Verdict: **working**.
- **Last Call** (+1 purple, +1 follower and +1 purple per minion you have left, take the token.) Taken as the first placement of rounds II and III. Strong when you place it early, and players knew it. Verdict: **working**, close to obvious as a first placement.
- **Crunch Time** (+2 purple, your next two turns come right after this one.) Empty all of round II. In round III Cal used it for a three-turn run that took the token, and both rivals praised that turn. Verdict: **too few games to say**. It looks weak until someone sees a combo.
- **Right Away** (+2/3/4 purple, your next turn comes right after this one.) In round I it was the last open space and gave Cal nothing beyond the purple. In round III it pulled Ben's held-back minion forward and gave away his last placement, which he had planned around all round. Verdict: **unclear**. Players did not see that it uses the minion the token was holding back.
- **Overtime** (+4 purple, +1 minion this round, take the token.) Never taken. Ben blocked it to protect his token, then needed it himself. Verdict: **too few games to say**. It only appears in round III, and this time a block hid it.

### Blue
- **Test Drive** (Discard a shop member, use its effect now.) Cal's round I use on Seat Filler: the game offered him a free member up to 8, he chose Deprogrammers, and he got nothing. Ben's round II use on Extra Hand gave him an extra minion and was praised. Verdict: **unclear**. The Seat Filler case looks like the game asking a question and then not acting on the answer, so it needs checking before it is judged as a design.
- **Out of Style** (Discard 3 shop members and refill, +1 of each one's color.) Taken every round as a few mixed resources. Once Cal used it to clear the Ghost and Spoilsport out of the shop. Verdict: **working as filler**.
- **Markdown** (Recruit from the discard pile, paying 2 less, +3 blue.) Taken every round, a real decision each time: Seat Filler, Last-Minute Shopper and Loyalists came through it. Ava and Dee rated it 4. Verdict: **working**.
- **Recruit Twice** (Recruit a member from the discard pile now, +5 blue.) Taken in rounds II and III. Players liked "the discard pile is a second shop". Dee: "'Twice' in the name confused me since it recruits once." Verdict: **working**, with a name that misleads.
- **Word of Mouth** (+1 follower per 2 members you have.) Taken only by Dee, for +6 and +9. Every other player said it pays only the player with the most members. Ava: "rewards the player already winning Blue, no decision in it." Cal: "pure snowball." Dee knew nobody else wanted it, so he could put it off safely. Verdict: **feel-bad**. It pays the member leader a third time, on top of two contests.
- **Spree** (Recruit two members from the discard pile, +7 blue.) Taken once in round III by Ben, and it was his favourite turn: pulling Latecomers took the token back. Verdict: **working** (one use).

### White
- **Modest Means** (+2 white, +1 per rival with more members than you.) Ava took it in all three rounds. Nobody else wanted it, and players rated it 2 to 3: "small and uncontested." Verdict: **always taken** by the small-cult player, with no decision.
- **Splurge** (+2 white; if you recruit a member this turn, +1 follower per 2 resources paid, or per 1 in round II.) Taken every round. Ava's round II Splurge into Consolation Prize (+7) was her best turn. Verdict: **working**. Round II's bigger rate is the moment it shines.
- **Nest Egg** (+3 white.) Taken every round, always called filler (ratings 1 to 2). Verdict: **working as filler**.
- **Farewell** (Discard a member for its cost in followers.) Taken in rounds II and III, the race space of those rounds. Cal turned his Last Callers into +7. Dee turned Martyrs into +10 and +4 white. Verdict: **working**.
- **Write-Off** (Give a member to a rival for its cost in white.) Ben took it in both rounds and gave both members to Dee. He picked Dee because Dee was "out of reach" or "he leads Blue regardless", so the gifts went to the player whose contest they fed. Ava said the threat of being given a member "shaped my whole round III". Verdict: **working** as a threat. **Feel-bad** for the table, because the easy target is always the leader.
- **Cash In** (Turn all your white into followers.) Never taken. Every player said buying 5 or 10 followers does the same job, and the White Cloak made it worse. Verdict: **never wanted**.

## 5. Members players called out

- **Line Cutters** (4 any, worth 3, counts as 3: you go first every round.) **Underpriced.** Dee got it free from Late Shift in round I. It went first every round, which cancels the rule that the player with fewest followers goes first. In round III it gave Dee Initiation. Ben: "too much, and it shuts off the only catch-up rule." Cal: "too cheap."
- **Deprogrammers** (2 red + 3 any, worth 4, counts as 4: rivals lose 1 follower when you take their member.) **Underpriced.** Cal: "the best price in the shop." It won Dee's first Most members contest by itself, and it made every Promote cost the target 1 more follower.
- **Guest of Honor** (On Use, once per round: give a rival one of your members, +4 followers.) **Working.** It went Dee to Ben to Ava to Dee, +4 each time, and Ben used it to switch off Ava's Anchorites. Ava rated it 5/5. Taken free from Borrow in round I, it is +4 for one space, which three players called a lot.
- **Seat Filler** (Round Start: put it in the shop and recruit a member costing up to 3 more, for free.) **Working**, strong. Dee turned it into Alchemists in round II and Minimalists in round III. Through Test Drive it did nothing (see Test Drive above).
- **Anchorites** (Round End: +5 followers if you have 3 or fewer members.) **Working.** It paid Ava in rounds I and III. Guarding it against gifts was "the whole game's puzzle" for her, and it is the clearest reason gifts felt like interaction.
- **Consolation Prize** (Round End: if you win no contest, +5 followers.) **Working.** It paid Ava +5 twice. Players called it a fair floor for someone outside the contests.
- **Last-Minute Shopper** (Round End: +3 blue, then you may recruit.) **Working.** It gave Ava about 6 followers a round, and players called it quiet.
- **Martyrs** (Whenever discarded, +4 white and +4 followers.) **Working.** Martyrs into Farewell paid +10 and was "the cleanest combo I saw".
- **Minimalists** (Game End: +2 followers per member you have costing 6 or more.) Dee got it free from Seat Filler and it paid +10. It was one more card scoring off a big pile of members.
- **Loyalists** (Your other members can't be swapped.) **Working**, but it shut off the only counterplay to Dee's pile right when Ben wanted to swap back.
- **Dabblers** (Counts as 2 wild members.) **Unclear.** It counted as 2 in Red's contest, which says "each card counts 1", and it did not trigger Gather. Ben and Dee were both surprised. It changed hands twice.
- **Last Callers** (On Use, once per round: take the Goes Last token.) **Unclear.** Using it before your final minion does nothing, which cost Cal +6 in round II.
- **Latecomers** (On Recruit: take the token, your next minion goes after everyone else's.) **Unclear** next to Right Away (see Right Away above).
- **Protesters** (On Use, once per round: block an open space.) **Working.** Ben's blocks shaped both rounds he used it. In round III he blocked the space he later needed himself.
- **Night Managers, Timekeeper, Courier, Gossip** (Ben's purple pile.) **Working**, in small amounts: +3, +5, a few resources, +1.
- **Spoilsport** (Round End: choose a contest, nobody wins it.) It was the only card at the table that could take 10 from Dee, and Cal discarded it with Out of Style before anyone could buy it. One game is not enough to judge it.
- **Members that came out after the last recruit** (Cheerleaders, Brokers, Lookouts in round III): all three players called these "arrived too late". The shop refills on the final turns hand out cards nobody can use.
- **Never bought** (Mentor, Clan, Panel, Con Men, Patroness, Snobs, Thrift Hunters, Swap Shoppers, Mannequin, Scouts, Anarchist, Hot Streak, Purifiers, Downsizers, Foster Parents, Picket Line): **too few games to say**. Mentor and Clan were called too expensive. Snobs and Mannequin need blue members nobody had.

## 6. The game as a whole

- **Worst round for choices: round I.** Half the placements were obvious and one was stuck. Dee's free-member chain set the order of the whole game. Three of four round I fun ratings were 3.
- **Best round for choices: round III** for Dee and Ava (every placement hard). It was also the best round for interaction, between Ben and Cal over the token and between Dee and Ben over Red.
- **Best round for fun: round II.** Diaries rated it 5, 4, 3 and 4, with the Promote swap, the gift that switched off Anchorites, and the token stolen back and forth.
- **Did round III feel like a payoff?** For the two engine players, yes. Dee: "round I free members fed Word of Mouth and Minimalists, so round III really was bigger." Ava: Anchorites and Shopper "paid all game". For the two contest fighters, no. Ben and Cal both rated the arc 2: "round III was the same plan with bigger numbers." The leader after round II (Dee, 50) won by 30, and every player rated comeback 1 or 2.

**Why the leader ran away.** A big pile of members paid four ways at once: Blue's Most members contest, Red's Most of one color contest (grey members count), Word of Mouth, and Minimalists. Together that was about 61 of Dee's 119. The table's only tools against a big pile (Write-Off, Guest of Honor, Patrons) give a member away, so each one made his pile bigger. Line Cutters stopped him from ever being last in turn order. Promote and Demote could take a member, but Loyalists closed that off in round III.

---

## Five findings that matter most, ranked

1. **A big member pile pays four times, and nothing at the table shrinks it.** Most members, Most of one color, Word of Mouth and Minimalists all paid Dee for the same 19 members (about 61 followers). Every way to hurt a rival's pile (Write-Off, Guest of Honor, Patrons) adds a member to that pile. All four players rated comeback 1 or 2. What might help: make Word of Mouth stop stacking on Blue's contest, or give the table one way to take members out of a cult rather than only into it.
2. **Round I hands out members that are far too good, and the first player to take them wins.** Borrow into Guest of Honor (+4 for one space), Late Shift into a free Line Cutters (worth 3, counts as 3, first every round) and Deprogrammers for 5 (worth 4, counts as 4) put Dee at 8 members after round I. Round I had the most obvious choices of the game. What might help: price Line Cutters and Deprogrammers like the members they beat, or stop Line Cutters from overriding fewest-followers-goes-first.
3. **The Goes Last token fight is the best thing in the game, but three timing rules caught the players fighting over it.** Ben and Cal both called it the best part. Each lost a contest to a misread: Last Callers on your final minion does nothing, Right Away pulls forward the minion Latecomers was holding back, and the token never holds the minion you are placing now. What might help: say on Right Away, Last Callers and Latecomers which minion moves.
4. **Test Drive on Seat Filler asked Cal to pick a free member and then gave him nothing.** The game offered "recruit a shop member costing up to 8", he chose Deprogrammers, and it stayed in the shop. This needs checking as a game fault before it is judged as a design question. Dabblers counting as 2 in a contest that says "each card counts 1" also surprised two players.
5. **Some White spaces are never a decision.** Cash In was never taken, because buying 5 or 10 followers does the same job. Modest Means went to the smallest cult every round and nobody else wanted it. Nest Egg was filler. The good White moments were Farewell, Splurge in round II and Write-Off as a threat. What might help: give Cash In something buying followers can't do, so it competes with a 10-follower buy.
