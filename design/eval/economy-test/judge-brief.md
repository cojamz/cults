# Economy test: the feel judges' brief (Nov 7)

You read one or two whole games of Cults, written out decision by decision for one seat (every option they had, what
they picked, and every rival turn in between). The games were played by strong bots. The game's economy (member prices,
what spaces and cloaks give, follower buys, contest prizes) may differ from game to game; you are not told how, and you
must not guess the settings. Judge only how the game would feel to four friends at a table.

Read design/rulebook.md first (the rules). Then read the game(s).

## Cory's own words on how the game should feel (the bar)
- "still fun and dynamic but you don't have to track like 10 member cards across each player" (Nov 7)
- "a lot of members have so much text. Its just too much mental load to track so many effects." (Nov 6)
- "every turn is biggest turn/number, and you never quite feel the payoff (except for sometimes)" (Nov 2, the flaw to fix)
- "i'd rather things be more expensive" (Nov 5), then "I can already tell stuff is probably a bit too expensive" (Nov 5)
- "i like somebody being able to get it round 1" (Nov 5, on a member)
- Contests are core: "what do you want to be doing and what's getting rewarded from your color" (Nov 7)
- "more consideration and skill should increase win rate drastically" (Nov 5)
- Steals piling on one player: "this feels bad" (Nov 1). "getting a minion is so strong, really feels bad at 4 player table" (Nov 7)
- "1 for 1" follower buys are essential (Nov 7).

## What to judge (no targets: describe what you see, then score)
For each, one or two plain sentences that point at a real turn ("Round II, Ava's 3rd turn: ..."), then 1 to 5.
- A. Tracking: could you follow every cult at the table? How many member cards and effects pile up, and when does it get hard?
- B. Recruits: does a member feel like an event you planned for, or a routine buy every turn?
- C. Choices: on the seat's turns, were there two or more options a thoughtful player would weigh? Count turns that were obvious because the player could afford nothing, or anything.
- D. Resources: do resources feel scarce and worth fighting for (steals, gifts, contests on held colors), or do they pile up?
- E. Paths: did different plans compete (many members, follower buys, holding colors for contests)?
- F. The race: was it close late, could the player behind catch up, was there a lead change?
- G. Size: did the game still feel big and each color's tricks happen (free recruits, steals, copies, extra turns)?

Then:
- **The best moment** and **the worst moment** of the game, one line each, with the turn.
- **Overall**: would these four friends want to play this economy again? 1 to 5, and the one reason that decides it.

Plain words, no jargon, no storytelling. Do not invent rules; if a line in the write-up is unclear, say so.
