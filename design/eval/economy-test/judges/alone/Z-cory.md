# Z, judged as Cory

A. Tracking: 2/5
Game 1 gets too heavy to follow by round III. Cory ends with 12 member cards (Mentor, Scouts, Trendsetters, Power Shoppers, Godparent, Con Men, Sore Loser, Heist Crew, Loyalists, Cheerleaders, Kingmakers, Critic), and Trendsetters plus Power Shoppers fire on every recruit. In game 2, round III, Ava's turn 8, one Demote and one Anarchist move Panel, Night Managers and Latecomers between Ben and Cal, so nobody at the table could say who holds what. Game 3 is easy to follow only because Ben and Cal end with 1 member each.

B. Recruits: 2/5
In game 1, round III, recruiting is routine. Cory's turn 9 brings in Con Men by Poach and then buys Sore Loser, and turn 10 brings in Heist Crew free and then buys Loyalists: two members a turn without a plan. In game 3 members barely matter. Ben recruits only twice and sells Agents to Estate Sale for white, and Cory sends Con Men (9 followers) and Smooth Talkers (12) to Farewell for followers. The planned recruit is rare. Game 2, round II, Ava's turn 4, where she saved up for Cheerleaders, is one of the few.

C. Choices: 3/5
Most turns list 10 to 25 spaces, but a lot of them are obvious. Game 1, round I, Cory's turn 4 has 2 options (Gather or nothing, then pass). Game 2, round I, Ava's turn 3 ends in a 1-follower buy because she can afford nothing else. In game 3, round II, Ben's turn 6 says "couldn't afford anything". In round III of every game the buy is almost always the largest follower buy, 10 resources for 13 followers. The real weighing happens in round II.

D. Resources: 2/5
Resources pile up in round III and get dumped into follower buys. In game 1, round III, Cal holds 20 silver at Cory's turn 11. In game 3, round III, Ava holds 18 silver and Cory's Makeover turns his whole pile into 22 yellow. Toast gives +9. Steals do happen, but they pile onto the same players: in game 1, Con Men changes hands five times and steals 5 resources each time, and Cal is hit by Heist three times in round III.

E. Paths: 3/5
Different plans can win or nearly win. In game 1 Ben (3 members) ties Cory (12 members) at 93. In game 3 Cal (1 member) ties Ava (10 members) at 111. But in round III every seat ends up on the same plan, big follower buys every turn, so the paths meet rather than compete.

F. The race: 4/5
All three games end within 2 to 8 points, and the lead changes. In game 1 Ava leads with 82 late in round III, and Cory wins on the member tiebreak at 93. The bad part is how game 2 is decided: at round end, Cal's Sore Loser steals 5 followers from Ava, and that one card turns her win (87 to 81) into Cal's win (86 to 82).

G. Size: 4/5
Every color's tricks show up: free recruits (Walk-In, Seat Filler, Initiation, Mentor), steals (Con Men, Heist, Help Yourself), copies (game 3: Echo copies Cover, which copies Makeover), extra turns and skips (game 2: Hold Up, Right Away, Sleep In). The game feels big, if anything a bit too busy.

Best moment: Game 1, round II, Cory's turn 5. He uses Demote to swap his Snobs for Ava's Mentor, Mentor brings Scouts in free, and he buys Con Men to steal 5. It was planned, it was punchy, and the table felt it.
Worst moment: Game 1, Con Men passes Cory → Ben → Ava → Cory → Cal, stealing 5 each time. The same few players keep getting hit by one card they can't keep up with.

Overall: 2/5
What decides it: round III turns into "biggest number every turn", with piles of 10 to 20 resources dumped into 10-for-13 follower buys and a dozen members to track, which is the exact flaw I want fixed.
