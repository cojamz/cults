# Z, judged by the veteran worker-placement player

A. Tracking: 2/5
Game 1 gets out of hand by round III. Cory ends with 24 members, and Con Men changes hands three times (Cory buys it in round II turn 5, Ben's Promote takes it, Ava's Demote takes it in round III, Cory's Poach takes it back on turn 9), alongside Anarchist and Shake Down swaps. Game 2 is similar (Ava at 16 members, and Ava's round III turn 8 Demote and Anarchist reshuffle three cults). Game 3 is easy to follow, since Cal and Ben each sit on 1 member.

B. Recruits: 2/5
Members show up too often for free to feel like events: Walk-In, Seat Filler, Mentor and Initiation hand them out, and Trendsetters plus Power Shoppers turn Cory's round III turns into a members conveyor (game 1, turns 9 and 10). The one recruit that felt planned was Cory's 9-cost Con Men in game 1, round II turn 5, paid for by a Demote swap that brought Mentor and a free Scouts along.

C. Choices: 3/5
Most placements offered 10 to 25 spaces, and rounds I and II had real trade-offs. But the buy step was often a non-choice. In game 3, Ben's round I turn 4 had 2 spaces to pick from, his buys on turns 3, 5 and 8 were "1 follower or pass", and on turn 6 he "couldn't afford anything". In round III almost every buy is the obvious "pay 10, get 13" (Ben turns 11 and 12, Cory game 1 turn 11).

D. Resources: 2/5
Round I is tight: Cory in game 1 holds 0 to 1 resources on most turns, and the early steals sting. By round III the resources pile up. Cal holds 20 silver in game 1 (turn 11), Cory's Makeover in game 3 turns his whole pile into 22 yellow twice, and Toast pays +9 to its taker and 3 to everyone else. Once everyone can afford everything, stealing and holding colors for contests stop mattering.

E. Paths: 4/5
Different plans really did compete. In game 3, Cal tied for first at 111 with 1 member against Ava's 10, and in game 1 Cory's 24 members tied Ben's 3 at 93. Contests on held colors (most resources, fewest silver) and follower buys both paid. The weak spot is that a big follower buy sits at the end of nearly every plan.

F. The race: 4/5
All three finishes were very close: 93-93-92-91, 86-82-81-79, and 111-111-105-103. There were real lead changes. In game 2, Ava sat at 47 against 76 on her round III turn 12 and finished second at 82, and Cal's Sore Loser took 5 followers from her at the end. The catch is that two of the three games came down to the member-count tiebreak, which feels more like the scoring evening everyone out than like anyone outplaying the table.

G. Size: 4/5
Every color's tricks happened. Steals and Heists, Echo and Cover copying the best space (game 3, round II), skipped turns and extra turns, and a running fight over the Goes Last token in game 2. Blocking mattered: Cal's Sleep In in game 2, round II, took the Goes Last token and sent Cory's 3 waiting minions home.

Best moment: Game 2, round II: Cal's Sleep In takes the Goes Last token from Cory, and Cory's 3 minions waiting on it go back to his hand. That is a real block that cost Cory.
Worst moment: Game 1, round III, after Cory's turn 11: Cal's Spree buys 13 followers twice, then Ben buys 13 and 6, and Ava buys 13 twice. That stretch was turning a pile of resources into followers, with no tension.

Overall: 3/5
Rounds I and II are tight and full of blocking, but round III loses all scarcity: resources flood and every turn becomes "pay 10, get 13", so the close finishes come from the scoring evening out, not from hard trade-offs.
