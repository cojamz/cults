# Q, judged by the veteran (worker-placement player)

A. Tracking: 2/5
Game 1 is hard to follow: by round III Cory holds 12 members, and Con Men went from Cory to Ben (round II, Ben's Promote), then to Ava (round III, Ava's Demote), then back to Cory (round III, Cory's turn 9, Poach), with Anarchist and Godparent moving cards too. Game 3 is the other extreme and easy to follow (Ben and Cal end with 1 member each), so how hard it is depends on how much swapping the table does.

B. Recruits: 3/5
Some recruits were planned events: game 1, Cory's turn 8 (Recruit Twice into Power Shoppers) set up turn 9, where Poach plus Sore Loser made a second member and +4 followers. But in round III members mostly stop: game 3, Ben's last four turns buy only followers, and game 1 Ben wins a share of first with 2 member cards, so a member is often a side buy.

C. Choices: 3/5
Placement usually had 8 to 24 open spaces and several real trade-offs (game 2, Ava's turn 11, Overtime taking the Goes Last token from Cory). But the buy step was often obvious: in round III the "pay 10 get 13" buy was the clear pick whenever someone held 10 (game 1 Cory turn 11; game 2 Ava turn 13; game 3 Ben turns 11 and 12). Few placements were forced: game 1 Cory turn 4 had 2 spaces, game 3 Ben turn 4 had 2, and game 3 Ben turn 6 could afford nothing.

D. Resources: 2/5
Round I is tight (game 1, Cory buys single followers on turns 2 and 7), but round III floods. Toast pays +9 to +11, Largesse hands out 12 silver, and Makeover turned Cory's whole supply into 22 yellow twice in one round (game 3, round III), so everyone buys 13 followers once or twice a turn. Steals happen (Con Men 5, Heist Crew 3 in game 1), but at those amounts a steal stops mattering by round III.

E. Paths: 3/5
Different plans did finish close: in game 1, Cory (12 member cards) tied Ben (2) at 93; in game 3, Ava (many members) tied Cal (1 member) at 111. But by round III every plan comes down to the same thing, turning resources into followers, so the paths only differ in the early rounds.

F. The race: 4/5
The finishes were very close (game 1: 93-93-92-91; game 3: 111-111-105-103; game 2: 86-82-81-79), and the lead changed: in game 1 Cory was last with 12 at the start of round II and won the tie-break, and Ava's 82 lead in round III got passed. The catch is that round III is worth more than 60 followers per player, so the first two rounds barely set the result.

G. Size: 4/5
Each color's tricks showed up: free recruits (Seat Filler, Mentor, Walk-In, Initiation), copy chains (game 3, Ben's Reprise copied Cover, which copied Sweet Nothings), extra and skipped turns (Crunch Time, Hold Up, Overtime), and steals of members (Poach, Shake Down). A few did nothing: in game 2, round I, Cory's Right Away had no minion left to move, and in round II Ben's Crunch Time did nothing.

Best moment: Game 1, round III, Cory's turn 9: Poach takes Ava's Con Men, which steals 5 blue from her, then Sore Loser comes in as his second member that turn for +4 from Power Shoppers, a combo he set up on turn 8.
Worst moment: Game 3, round III: Cory makes everything yellow twice with Makeover and buys 13 followers each time, while Ben's last four turns are just turning resources into followers; nobody is fighting for anything.

Overall: 3/5
What decides it is that the economy is tight in round I and loose in round III, where the "pay 10 get 13" follower buy turns the end game into a race to cash out instead of a fight over spaces and members, even though the scores stay close.
