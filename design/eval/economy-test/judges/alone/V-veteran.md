# V, veteran judge

A. Tracking: 4/5
Member cards stay few: across the three games nobody holds more than seven names, and in game 3 Ava and Ben hold none at all by the end. The hard spot is member churn in game 1 round III. Dabblers goes Ava to Cory (Kidnapper, Cory's turn 9), then Cory to Ava (Ava's Poach and Kidnapper), then out to the shop (Cory's Anarchist, turn 10), then to Ben, then to Cory (Tryout copying Kidnapper, turn 12), all in one round.

B. Recruits: 2/5
Members are a side dish. Ava wins game 3 with 0 members, and Ben's only paid recruit, Fan Club (round II, turn 7), gets sold to Estate Sale for 10 white three turns later. Most members arrive free off the top of the deck (Walk-In, Initiation, Mentor, Housewarming), not as buys anyone saved up for. Cory's Scouts (game 1, round II, turn 5, 9 any) is the rare planned buy.

C. Choices: 3/5
The placement step usually has a real choice. The recruit step mostly doesn't: from round II on, the seat almost always buys the biggest follower bundle it can pay for (game 3, Ben's turns 10 to 13 in round III: buy 13, buy 13, buy 13, buy 6). About a quarter of the seat's turns are obvious either way. Game 1, Cory's turn 4 (round I) had two spaces and nothing worth buying. Game 2, Ava's turns 3 and 7 were take +2 yellow and pass or buy 1.

D. Resources: 2/5
Resources never pile up, but only because every one turns straight into followers at 1 for 1 or better (5 for 6, 10 for 13). Holding colors for a contest is almost never worth the points. The bank pours silver around: Largesse hands rivals 12 (game 1, Cory's turn 11; game 3, Ben's turn 9), and Toast in round III gives everyone +3. "Fewest silver" ends in a four-way split often (game 1 round II, game 3 rounds I and III), which shows nobody fought over it. Steals happen (Con Men took 5 blue from Ben, game 1 round II, Cory's turn 7) but just move points around.

E. Paths: 3/5
Some different plans finished near each other. Game 1: Cory went for most members and took 87, Ava stole members and took 98, and Cal bought followers with 2 members and took 78. Game 2: Ben won 102 off the purple Goes Last token. In game 3 the 0-member and 5-member cults tied at 108. Underneath, every plan ends with the same move each turn, buy 13 followers, so the paths feel more like different ways to get resources than different ways to win.

F. The race: 4/5
The finishes are close: 102, 100, 93, 86 in game 2, and 111, 108, 108, 102 in game 3. The lead changed late. In game 2, Ava led 87 to 60 at her round III turn 12 and lost to Ben, who took four big follower buys plus Patron Saints +9. Game 3 had Cory ahead at 86 with one minion each left. Game 1 is the weak one: Ava led from mid round II to the end.

G. Size: 4/5
The colors' tricks fire and feel big. Cal's Spree (game 1), Spoilsport cancelling the Purple contest Ben led (game 2, round II end), Sleep In taking the Goes Last token and sending Cal's 3 minions back (game 2, round II), Makeover turning 27 resources green (game 3, Ben's turn 11), Godparent for +10. Scores over 100 make every number feel inflated, though.

Best moment: Game 2, round II: Ben's Sleep In takes the Goes Last token from Cal and sends Cal's 3 waiting minions back to his hand. A real block that cost a rival his plan.
Worst moment: Game 3, round III, Ben's turns 10 to 13: sell his only member, turn everything green, then buy 13, 13 and 6 followers. Four turns of turning resources into points with nothing to weigh.

Overall: 3/5
Close finishes and big tricks keep the table interested, but the follower buy at 1 for 1 or better turns every resource into points the turn you get it. So members, contests and saving up rarely compete with "take the biggest pile and buy 13", and a worker-placement crowd will feel there are no hard trade-offs.
