# P15, casual judge (friend at game night)

**A. Tracking: second, clearly.** In the first game Cory ends with 19 members and Ava with 16. Con Men changes hands five or six times through Promote and Demote swaps (Round II: Cal, then Ava, then Cal; Round III: Cory, then Cal, then Cory through Double Agent), and I'd lose track of who holds it. In the second game Cal ends with 2 members and Ben with 4. The only steady passing around is Dabblers and Kidnapper, so I could follow every cult.

**B. Recruits: second, slightly.** In the first game Cory recruits on almost every turn (Round III turns 10, 11, 13 and 14 each buy a member), so it starts to feel routine. In the second game a bought member is a real event: Scouts on Round II turn 5, Alchemists on turn 7. Most of his other members come free from Walk-In and Initiation.

**C. Choices: first, slightly.** In the second game Cory has several dead turns: Round II turn 8 is Stash and pass, every member was "short 10 any", and Round III turn 12 is pass. In the first game his Round II and III turns usually have two or three real buys to weigh, for example Spoilsport against Power Shoppers on turn 14.

**D. Resources: second, slightly.** In the second game the Round II shop is out of reach. Grabbing 5 blue from Ben with Con Men on turn 7 matters, and holding colors for contests matters. In the first game resources get stolen and refilled so often (Con Men steals 5, five times over) that no single pile feels precious. In the second game, though, Round III resources all pour into 13-follower buys.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** In the second game Cal goes from 31 to 78 with only 2 members by buying followers (13 at a time), while Cory goes wide on members and contests. Those are two plans you can actually see racing. In the first game the member pile mostly decides it, and Ben's late follower buys get close but fall short.

**F. The race: first, clearly.** The first game ends 63, 61, 57, 48. Ben leads at 56 going into Cory's last turn, and Cory takes it with Spoilsport plus the most-members contest. In the second game Ava leads from Round II turn 6 (27 to 12) to the end and wins 98 to 87, and Ben finishes far behind on 50.

**G. Size: first, clearly.** The first game has every color's tricks: Mentor chaining two free recruits on Round II turn 5, Extra Hand giving an extra minion, Spree, Double Agent, Spoilsport cancelling a contest, Body Snatchers. The second game has some (Largesse, Heist Crew, Anarchist, Kidnapper), but Round III turns into everyone buying 13 followers over and over.

**Best moment (first game):** Round II turn 5. Tryout uses Mentor's free recruit, Cory then buys Mentor itself, and that brings Window Shopper along free: three members in one turn.

**Worst moment (second game):** Round III, Ben's and Ava's last turns. They go "bought 13 followers, bought 13 followers" back to back. It works, but it's just counting.

**Overall: first, slightly.** The first game is a mess to track. Still, it stays close to the very last turn and something surprising happens every round. In the second game the cheap bulk follower buys make Round III a counting exercise, and the winner was clear an hour earlier.

What the economy changed at the table: with pricier members and bonus followers for buying in bulk, people held fewer members, which made the table easier to follow. The game also got flatter: the late rounds became "save up and buy 13 followers," and the early leader was never caught.
