# P5, judged by the veteran (worker-placement regular)

**A. Tracking: second, clearly.** In the first game the table recruited 36 times, and by Round III Cory had 15 members with Courier, Lookouts, Ballroom Dancers, Timekeeper and Sore Loser all firing in one stretch (Move 203 onward). In the second game there were 22 recruits, Ben sat on 3 members all game, and Round III was mostly follower buys, which anyone can follow.

**B. Recruits: second, slightly.** In the second game Ava saved up for each member (Toastmaster off Toast on turn 2, Spoilsport for 9 on turn 4, Cheerleaders for 8 on turn 6), and each one changed what she did next. In the first game members came at her in chains (Tryout into Mentor into a free cheapest member on turn 5, then Anarchist, Godparent, Caravan, Hot Streak), so they felt routine.

**C. Choices: first, slightly.** In the first game more of Ava's buy steps had two real options, member or followers (turns 5, 10 and 11). In the second, turns 3, 5 and 10 were "buy 1 follower or pass", and turns 11 and 12 were an obvious buy of 10. Both games had dead passes (first: turns 2, 4 and 8).

**D. Resources: second, slightly.** In the second game Rounds I and II were tight: Ava held 1 or 2 resources at the start of most turns and spent nearly all her silver on Toastmaster and Cheerleaders. Round III flooded in both games, though. In the second, Makeover gave 12 purple and Toast gave 9 silver, and from then on every seat bought 10 followers almost every turn.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** In the second game the plans really split: Ben went for follower buys on 3 members, Cal built 12 members, and Ava played the contests (Spoilsport, Largesse, Cheerleaders). In the first game all four seats played members plus followers, and only Ben's purple went-last engine stood apart.

**F. The race: second, slightly.** The first game was close at the top (93 to 90), but Cory led from Round III on, and Ben only closed the gap with three buys of 10 and Night Managers' +8 on the final turns. In the second game Ava was 22 behind Cory (58 to 80) going into her turn 12. The 10 followers she bought there, plus Spoilsport cancelling Cal's Yellow contest and Cheerleaders paying 6, flipped the lead on the last move. That is real catch-up, but one card did a lot of the work.

**G. Size: first, clearly.** The first game had member swaps (Demote, Promote, Anarchist twice), Con Men stealing 5, Godparent handing over Dyer for +9, and Ballroom Dancers for +8. Every color's tricks showed up. In the second game the tricks were few: Counterfeiters flipping Ava's 11 resources, Largesse, Spoilsport twice, and little else.

**Best moment:** second game, Ava's turn 12. She cancels the Yellow contest with Spoilsport and wins from 22 behind.
**Worst moment:** second game, Round III. Twelve straight turns of "take a space, buy 10 followers" while 8 members sit unbought in the shop.

**Overall: second, slightly.** The tighter first two rounds and the split plans give each placement more weight, but Round III goes flat in both, and the second loses a lot of the first game's sense of a big, busy table.

What the economy changed at the table: members cost about one more resource, so people bought fewer of them and planned for each one. One player could win on follower buys alone. The board got easier to read but smaller, and late resources still piled up into buys of 10.
