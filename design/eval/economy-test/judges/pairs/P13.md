# P13, judged as Cory

**A. Tracking: first, clearly.** In the first game nobody holds more than four member cards. Cory ends with Scouts, Window Dressers, Alchemists, Anarchist and Dabblers, and Cal holds only Snobs. In the second game, by round III turn 12, Cory holds 8 cards, Ava 7 and Cal 5. Con Men changes owner six times (Cal, then Ava, Cal, Ava, Cory, Cal, Cory), and each move fires a 5-resource steal. That is exactly the mental load I don't want.

**B. Recruits: first, slightly.** In the first game Cory can't afford a member in round I. His Scouts buy in round II (9 of anything) is a planned event, and so is Alchemists. In the second game Cory gets Line Cutters in round I, which I like, but by round III he recruits almost every turn (Extra Hand, Trendsetters, Double Agent, Spoilsport). The Mentor chain in round II turn 5 brings in three members on one turn, which is fun once but turns recruiting into routine.

**C. Choices: second, slightly.** In the first game several Cory turns are obvious leftovers. In round I turns 2 and 3 he can only buy 1 follower, and in round II turn 8 he takes Stash and passes. In the second game more turns weigh a member against a follower buy: Body Snatchers against Mannequin, and Scouts against Sore Loser. The last turn (Tryout, then Spoilsport to cancel Cal's Magenta win) is a real decision that decides the game.

**D. Resources: same.** Both games feel scarce, and steals pile onto someone. In the first game Ava's Poach takes Cory's Kidnapper and then Dabblers, and Con Men takes 5 of Ben's blue. In the second game Cory loses 5 red to Con Men, then 3 red to Accomplice and 2 more to Second-Story Man in the same round. The pain moves around but doesn't get smaller.

**E. Paths: first, slightly.** In the first game Cal stays at 1 or 2 members all game and reaches 78 on follower buys. Ava wins on steals plus buys, and Cory plays the members-and-contests plan, so three plans compete. In the second game Ben's buys plus Patron Saints come close, but Cal's plan with few members falls apart (48).

**F. The race: second, clearly.** In the first game Ava leads from round II on and wins 98 to 87. In the second game the lead moves from Ava to Ben and then to Cory, and Cory wins 63 to 61 on the very last placement with Spoilsport.

**G. Size: same.** Both games have the colors' tricks: Largesse +10, Kidnapper and Con Men in the first; Mentor's free recruits, Extra Hand, Double Agent and Spoilsport in the second. The first game's bigger numbers (98 points, 10-for-13 follower buys) feel huge, while the second game has more tricks firing at smaller sizes.

**Best moment:** second game, round III, Cory's last turn: Spoilsport cancels Magenta and he wins by 2.
**Worst moment:** second game, round III: Con Men bounces between cults again and again, each time with a 5-resource steal.

**Overall: first, slightly.** The second game's finish is great, and its 1-for-1 follower buys are what I asked for. But by round III no one at the table could track 30 member cards with steals bouncing between them, and that matters most to me.

What the economy changed at the table: in the second game members were cheaper and follower buys gave no bonus. Everyone bought far more members, so cults got crowded and swaps kept happening, and scores stayed low and close. In the first game members were pricier and big follower buys gave extra followers. Cults stayed small, scores ran high, and an early leader could pull away.
