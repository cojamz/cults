# P11, judged by the veteran (worker-placement regular)

Same deal, same seat (Ava, Silver Cloak), first player in round I.

**A. Tracking: second, clearly.** In the first game Cory ends with 10 named members and Cal with 10 (16 counted), and round III has Counterfeiters, Anarchist, Duty Collector, Courier, Lookouts and Ballroom Dancers all going off in the same stretch (Cory's two turns before Ava's turn 10 alone fire four member effects). In the second game Cory and Ben end on 4 to 5 named members, and at Ava's turn 8 the whole table holds 18 named cards against 22 in the first game.

**B. Recruits: second, clearly.** In the first game Ben and Cal recruit on almost every round II turn (Cal takes Scouts, Tourist, Debutantes and the swapped-in Con Men in one round), so members feel like a routine buy. In the second game Ava's round I Toast into Toastmaster, and her round II Survey for exactly the 7 she needed for Alchemists, feel planned. Ben sits on 1 to 3 members through round II and "Cal couldn't afford anything" after Party Gifts.

**C. Choices: second, slightly.** In the second game's round I and II the turns are real trade-offs on +1 and +2 spaces. Ava's turn 4 (Promote, take Deprogrammers, pass) gives up a buy for position. In the first game, Ava's round III turns 9 to 12 could afford most of the shop each time. Both games have a few dead turns: first game turns 2 and 4 are a pass, second game turns 3 and 9 are a pass. In round III of both games, buying 10 followers is the obvious buy (first game turn 10; second game turns 8 and 11).

**D. Resources: second, clearly.** In round I of the second game Tryout and Gather give +1 and Hold Up gives +1, so every point counts and Hold Up on Cal stings. In the first game Cal's Makeover turns 17 resources red and buys Proselytizers outright. Ben ends round III with 18 resources and buys 10 followers three turns running. Both games still pile up in round III.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** In the second game Cory wins with 8 members on contests (Went last +10) and Big Tent and Patron Saints, ahead of Ava's 14 and Cal's 12 members, so wide recruiting is not the only road. In the first game the winner also has the most members bar Cal (15), and member count tracks the standings more closely.

**F. The race: first, slightly.** Both games have a late lead change. In the first game Ben leads from round I, 41 to Cory's 29 at the start of round III, and loses 93 to 90, with all four players within 21. In the second game Ben is 66 to Cory's 54 on the last turns and Cory takes it 91 to 86 on Went last and his end-game members, but Ava and Cal finish 25 to 31 behind.

**G. Size: first, clearly.** The first game is full of big moments: Ava's Godparent hands Dyer to Cory for +9, Ava's Counterfeiters turn Ben's 18 resources to silver, Cal swaps Con Men and steals 5 a second time, Ballroom Dancers gives +8, and Makeover makes 17 red. The second game still has Seamstress, Debt Collectors and an 11-purple Makeover, but fewer and smaller.

**Best moment.** First game: Ava's turn 9, Godparent gives Dyer to Cory for +9 followers. Second game: Cory overtakes Ben on the last turn of round III with Went last.

**Worst moment.** First game: before Ava's turn 6, Cory's Con Men steals 5, then Cal swaps that Con Men away from Cory and steals 5 red back from him. Second game: Ava's round I turn 3, +1 purple and pass.

**Overall: second, slightly.** The second economy makes the early placements and member buys into real trade-offs, and the table stays readable. It loses some of the first game's big turns, and round III still turns into buying 10 followers in both games.

What the economy changed at the table: spaces paid less early in the second game, so members came slower and meant more, cults stayed small enough to follow, and contests mattered more than member count. The flashy round III swings got smaller, and the spread between first and last grew.
