# P12, judged by the casual friend

A. Tracking: first, slightly. In the first game Cory only ever had 2 or 3 member cards (Deprogrammers, then Cheerleaders and Snobs, later Kingmakers and Anarchist), so his side was easy to read, though Ava still had 10 cards by the last turn. In the second game Cory had 9 cards with round-start effects (Window Shopper, Body Snatchers, Kidnapper), and Con Men changed hands about six times (Cal to Ava to Cal to Ava, then Cory in Round III turn 9, Cal on the next turn, Cory again on turn 13). I would lose track of who has it.

B. Recruits: second, clearly. In the first game Cory got no member in all of Round I and only recruited twice the whole game (Cheerleaders in Round II turn 8, Kingmakers in Round III turn 11). In the second he bought Line Cutters on Round I turn 4, and on Round II turn 5 a single turn got him Loyalists, Mentor and Window Shopper. That turn felt like a real event, not a routine buy.

C. Choices: second, clearly. In the first game about 7 of Cory's 13 turns ended in "pass" or "couldn't afford anything": all of Round I, and turns 5 and 6 in Round II. In the second game Round I was still thin, but from Round II on he usually had 4 to 8 members he could afford (Round III turns 9, 10, 12 and 13), plus the choice of buying 5 or 10 followers.

D. Resources: second, slightly. In the first game Cory "holds nothing" at the start of six turns, and every steal took his last cube. That feels broke, not tense. In the second he got up to 9 silver and 13 red, and the steals still hurt (Cal's Con Men took 5 red right after he spent on Trendsetters). Fighting over a pile is more fun than having nothing to fight over.

E. Paths: same. Both games had a few-members plan against a many-members plan. In the first, Cory went with 5 members and the gift spaces (Party Gifts, Largesse) and finished 3rd with 59. In the second, Ben went with 8 members and big follower buys and came 2nd, 2 behind Cory's 19 members.

F. The race: second, slightly. Both finished close, and both had a late lead change: in the first game Ben passed Ava by 1 only through Snobs (+9) at game end. In the second, Cory was 17 behind Ben (39 to 56) on his last turn and won 63 to 61 on round-end contests. That shows the player behind can catch up, though Ben might feel robbed.

G. Size: second, clearly. On Cory's own seat the second game had a free recruit chain (Mentor), a stolen member (Kidnapper), Extra Hand's extra minion, Double Agent grabbing Con Men, and Spoilsport. The first game's Cory mostly took gift spaces and passed.

Unclear in the write-up: in both games the Spoilsport line does not match the contest it names. In the first, "nobody wins the Blue contest" turns into "Most members: spoiled" at round end. In the second, Cory picked "Magenta (Cal leads)", but Most resources was spoiled and Cory won Most members.

Overall: second, clearly. Four friends would want to replay the second economy, because the player in the seat got to recruit and do things nearly every turn instead of passing broke through Round I. That was worth having more cards to keep track of.

What the economy changed at the table: spaces paid out more (Peek, Gather, Savings and Toast each gave about 1 more), so players held real piles and could afford members and big follower buys most turns, at the cost of more cards and more swapping to follow.
