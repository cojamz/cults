# P2, judged by the veteran (seasoned worker-placement player)

Same deal, same seat (Ava, Silver Cloak). The visible economy difference: in the second game a 5-resource follower buy gives 6 and a 10-resource buy gives 13; in the first, every follower buy is one for one.

**A. Tracking: second, slightly.**
First: by Round III, Ava's turn 10, Cory has 10 named members and Cal has 10 (16 member count), and Cal, Ben and Cory all have end-of-game effects stacking up (Patriarch, Big Tent, Timekeeper, Night Managers +8). Second: at the same point Cal wins holding only 5 to 7 members and Cory 6, so there are fewer cards to read across the table, although Ava's own 7 still take some reading.

**B. Recruits: same.**
In both games Ava's members come about one per round as planned engine pieces: Deprogrammers paired with Anarchist and Godparent in the first, Cheerleaders, Anarchist and Alchemists in the second. Rivals in both buy something nearly every turn they can. In the second, Round III Move 159 (Dyer against 13 followers) makes a member cost something you can see, but that happens once.

**C. Choices: same.**
Both games have the same thin spots: in Round I Ava passes after Polish (first, turn 2), or the 1-for-1 buy is the only option (both). In Round III, turns with a long list of places and a real follower-or-member choice come at about the same rate in both, for example first Move 244 (Hot Streak or more followers) and second Move 203 (1 follower kept back against the 6-for-5 buy).

**D. Resources: second, slightly.**
First: resources pile up late. In Round III Ben holds 18 when Ava turns them all silver on Move 248, and Cal's Makeover turns 17 into red. Second: almost everyone ends each turn near empty, because the bonus on follower buys pulls resources off the table fast. That is tighter, but it is tight because players spend, not because resources are hard to get.

**E. Paths: second, clearly.**
Second: Cal wins with 6 members, mostly from three 13-for-10 follower buys in Round III. Ava finishes 4 behind with 16 members and wins two contests. Two real plans compete to the last turn. First: the top three all have 12 to 16 members and buy followers on top. Ava's 11-member plan with Godparent and Anarchist swaps never gets close.

**F. The race: second, clearly.**
Second: the final scores are 86, 82, 81 and 79. Cory leads 74 to 61 at Ava's turn 12, Cal passes him on the last turns, and Cal's Sore Loser takes 5 from Ava at round end, which settles first place. First: 93, 90, 76 and 72. Cory and Ben were close at the top, but Ava trails from Round II on and is never in it.

**G. Size: first, slightly.**
First has more big plays: Ballroom Dancers +8, Night Managers +8 for the last minion, Largesse +10, Makeover turning 17 resources red, and Ava's Godparent giving away Dyer for +9. Second has Overtime, Guest of Honor and Sore Loser, but more of its turns are a 13-follower buy rather than a color's trick.

**Best moment:** second, Round III final turn (Move 221): Ava's 13-follower buy plus three contest wins put her in the running, then Sore Loser decides it.
**Worst moment:** first, Round III Move 248: Ava, last by 9, spends her turn turning Ben's 18 resources silver, which has almost no effect on her own race.

**Overall: second, clearly.** I'd play the second economy again (4 of 5). The first gets 3. The deciding reason: in the second, a small cult of follower buyers and a wide cult of members both stayed within reach until the final turn.

What the economy changed at the table: the bonus on big follower buys made buying followers a real rival to recruiting members. It drained everyone's resources each turn and packed the four scores within 7, at the cost of a few more turns that were just "spend everything on followers."
