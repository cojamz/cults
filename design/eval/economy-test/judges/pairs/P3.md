# P3, casual judge (friend at game night)

Same deal, same seat (Cory, Red Cloak). First game ended Cory 63, Ben 61, Ava 57, Cal 48. Second game ended Cory 93, Ben 93, Ava 92, Cal 91.

**A. Tracking: second, slightly.** In the first game, round II alone has Con Men going Cal to Ava to Cal in a few turns, and round III Cory's opening turn is a Body Snatchers swap, a Kidnapper steal, then a Promote swap for Con Men. I'd have lost track of who owns what. The second game still has swaps (Con Men goes Cory to Ben to Ava to Cory, Dabblers changes hands four times around the round II/III break), but most turns are "took a space, bought followers", which is easier to follow.

**B. Recruits: second, slightly.** In the first game Cory gets members in chains (round II turn 5: Tryout uses Mentor for a free Loyalists, then buys Mentor, which brings a free Window Shopper), and by round III picking up members feels routine. In the second, Cory has one member until round II and then sets up moments: Recruit Twice with Power Shoppers (round II turn 8), and Poach taking Ava's Con Men (round III turn 9).

**C. Choices: first, slightly.** Both games have filler turns where Cory buys 1 follower or passes (first: round I turn 2, round II turn 6; second: round I turn 3, round II turn 7). In the second game's round III, almost every rival turn is "buy 13 followers" (Cal does it twice off Spree, Ava twice in a row), so the right buy is obvious. The first game's round III turns are more varied: Extra Hand, Double Agent, Spoilsport.

**D. Resources: same.** In both, steals keep hands small (Con Men takes 5 at a time, and in the first game Cory loses 5 red to it in round III), but one player still hoards: Cal holds 8 to 9 silver in the first game and 20 silver in the second. Neither game feels much tighter than the other.

**E. Paths: second, clearly.** In the second game Ben ties for first with 3 members, all from buying followers and Heist, while Cory has 24 members, so you can see two different ways to win. In the first, the top three all got there by mixing members and contests.

**F. The race: second, slightly.** Both games end close and Cory comes from behind both times. First: Cory is 4th with 39 against Ben's 56 on his last turn and wins on round III contests. Second: Cory is 62 against Ava's 82 two turns from the end, and the game finishes 93-93-92-91, which would have the whole table yelling. The write-up doesn't say how the 93-93 tie was broken.

**G. Size: same.** Both games feel big, with free recruits, steals, extra recruits from Spree and Recruit Twice, and swaps. The second has bigger numbers (13-follower buys, a 93-point finish), and the first has more odd tricks (Extra Hand, Double Agent, Spoilsport).

**Best moment:** second game, round III turn 9: Cory's Poach takes Ava's Con Men, which steals 5 of her blue, and the next recruit sets off Power Shoppers.
**Worst moment:** first game, round II: Con Men is swapped back and forth three times in one round, and each swap steals 5 again, mostly from the same players.
Unclear line: in the first game, Cory's Spoilsport picks "Magenta (Cal leads)", but the round-end line says "Most resources" was the spoiled contest.

**Overall: second, slightly.** I'd want to play the second one again: fewer cards changing hands to keep track of, a near four-way tie at the end, and one player winning with almost no members. The downside is that its last round turns into everyone buying followers.

What the economy changed at the table: the second economy gives extra followers on bigger follower buys (pay 5 get 6, pay 10 get 13) and makes members a bit pricier early, so members come later and mean more, buying followers becomes a real way to win, and the final scores pile up much higher and end very close.
