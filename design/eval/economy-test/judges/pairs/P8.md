# P8, judged by the veteran (worker-placement player)

**A. Tracking: first, slightly.** In the first game, cults stay small. At the end of round III, Ava's last turn, the four players hold 7, 7, 5 and 7 named members. In the second game, members pile up: by round III, Ava's turn 10, Cory has 10 named members and Cal has 10. On top of that, Anarchist, Promote, Godparent and Guest of Honor keep moving cards between cults (round III, Ava's turns 8 and 9), so the table has more to follow.

**B. Recruits: first, slightly.** In the first game, a member costs a real setup. Round I, Ava's turn 2: she takes Toast to get 7 silver for Toastmaster. Round III, turn 11: she turns 22 resources into yellow with Makeover to afford Seamstress. In the second game, members are cheap and come almost every turn. Deprogrammers costs 5 in round I, and Cal ends with 16 members. The one recruit that clearly felt planned was Godparent in round III, turn 9: she recruits it and gives Dyer away for +9.

**C. Choices: second, slightly.** In the first game, many turns came down to "take the biggest pile, buy followers one for one." Round I, turn 3 (Hold Up, then buy 5) and round III, turn 8 (Walk-In, then buy 5) are examples. In the second game, Ava's turns had more to weigh. Round II, turn 6: she has to pick whom to hit with Counterfeiters. Round II, turn 7: she has to pick which Anarchist swap pays off with Deprogrammers. Round III, turn 12: she turns all of Ben's 18 resources into silver just before the fewest-silver contest is scored. In both games, only one or two turns had nothing affordable (second game, round I, turn 2, a pass).

**D. Resources: second, clearly.** In the first game, resources flood in late: Toast gives +12, Debutantes doubles Ava to 32 silver in round III, turn 10, and Makeover lands 22. Ten-follower buys happen almost every rival turn. In the second game, spaces give a point or two less. Ava often starts her turn holding nothing (round II, turn 6; round III, turn 8), so Con Men's steals and the Counterfeiters swaps actually hurt.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** In the first game, Ben won by buying followers and taking the went-last contest. Ava's wide spread of members ran last by 45. In the second game, Cory's 15-member plan with contest bonuses and Timekeeper beat Ben's purple plan of buying followers, 93 to 90, with Cal's many members close behind. The plans really competed.

**F. The race: second, clearly.** The first game ran away. Ben goes from 53 to 113 across round III and wins by 14, with last place 45 behind. The second game stays tight. Ben leads most of round II and round III, then Cory passes him at the end, 93 to 90, and last place is 21 behind. That is a real lead change.

**G. Size: first, slightly.** The first game has bigger blowout turns: Largesse +10, then Debutantes doubling to 32 silver (round III, turns 9 and 10), Makeover's 22, Patron Saints +15 and Ballroom Dancers +8. The second game still has its color tricks (Counterfeiters on 18 resources, Night Managers +8, Godparent +9, Ballroom Dancers +8), but at a smaller scale.

**Best moment:** second game, round III, Ava's turn 12. Counterfeiters turns Ben's 18 resources into silver right before the fewest-silver contest is scored.
**Worst moment:** first game, round III. Ben buys 10 followers three times and runs away with it, and no one can catch him.

**Overall: second, clearly (4 vs 2.5).** What decides it: the economy is tight enough that every buy costs you something, and the end game stays close.

What the economy changed at the table: cheaper members with smaller gains from each space meant fewer huge piles and fewer runaway follower buys. Cults got wider and swapped members more, but the game stayed close to the last turn.
