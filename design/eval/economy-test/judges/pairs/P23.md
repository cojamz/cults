# P23 — veteran judge (Ben's seat, seed 23)

Final scores. First game: Cory 102, Cal 99, Ava 93, Ben 87. Second game: Ava 110, Ben 99, Cory 83, Cal 81.

**A. Tracking: second, clearly.** In the first game, by round III Ben holds 10 members and Cory holds 9, and many of their cards act every round: Kingmakers at each round end, Cheerleaders, Debt Collectors taking silver from everyone, Counterfeiters recoloring Ava's whole stock, Spoilsport, and Guest of Honor handing members away. In the second game Cory and Ava end round II with 0 members, and only Ben and Cal (6 and 7 members) carry effects, most of them quiet ones (Dabblers, Line Cutters, Night Traders).

**B. Recruits: second, slightly.** In the first game Ben recruits on almost every turn (Agents, Kingmakers, Fan Club, Stunt Double, Con Men, Old Money, Dyer, Spoilsport), so a member is the routine way to spend. In the second game recruits are fewer and come after saving up. In round II Ben gets Night Traders free through Cover into Housewarming, then passes twice. In round III he builds toward Extra Hand, Song Request and Echo. Both games also use members as cast-out fodder: in the second, Ava recruits Con Men and Fan Club only to sell them at Estate Sale.

**C. Choices: first, slightly.** In the first game every Ben turn had a real option. The round II Fan Club chain (copy Reprise, then Echo, then Sweet Nothings again) is a combo he had to plan for. In the second game round II has two flat turns where he can afford nothing useful: Sample with nothing to copy gives +1 green, then he buys 1 follower, and Allowance takes 4 white, then he passes. The round III Song Request into Echo into Sweet Talk turn was rich, though.

**D. Resources: second, slightly.** The second game is tighter. Ben ends several round II turns holding nothing, and the price of Extra Hand forces a pass. In the first game Ben sits on 17 silver in round II, and both games end with big Makeover piles (17 yellow in the first; 29 green and 27 yellow in the second). The first game has more fighting over resources: Con Men is copied twice by Sample and Echo, and Debt Collectors pulls silver from the table. But two of those steals land on Ben, which feels like piling on.

**E. Paths: first, slightly.** In the first game the top three ran different plans: Cory with 9 members, Cal with 1 member and big follower buys, Ava buying followers through the White Cloak. Each stayed within 9 followers of the lead. In the second game Ava's no-member plan of follower buys plus Largesse ran away with it. Ben's member-color contest plan (+6 and +10 in rounds II and III, plus Big Tent) came second, and the other two were well behind.

**F. The race: first, clearly.** In the first game Ava leads rounds I and II (46 to 36 at the end of round II), Cal passes her mid round III (89 to 73), and Cory wins at the final scoring, 102 to 99, with contest shares and Cheerleaders. That is three leaders and a finish decided at the end. In the second game Ava leads from mid round II (37 to 15 against Ben) and is never caught. Ben closes from 95-70 to 110-99 only through the end-of-game bonuses.

**G. Size: first, slightly.** Both games show free recruits, copies, steals and an extra minion. The first adds more color tricks that land: Housewarming free recruits three times, Con Men stealing three times, Debt Collectors, Counterfeiters, Spoilsport cancelling a contest, and Guest of Honor giving members to Ava. The second relies more on the same play repeated: Largesse is fired three times in round III (Cal by Cover, Ava by Echo copying Cover, then Ben), each for +10.

**Overall: first, slightly.** The first game has the close, swinging finish and more choices on each turn, which outweighs its heavier card load. The second is cleaner and leaner, but it is decided by mid round II.

What the economy changed at the table: in the second game members cost a little less but spaces give less (Build Up +3 instead of +4, Housewarming up to 5 instead of 7), so players ran dry more often and two of them skipped members entirely. Buying followers with Largesse then beat building a cult. The first game's richer spaces let everyone keep buying members, so it was busier to track but stayed close to the end.
