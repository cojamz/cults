# P20, judged by the veteran (worker-placement regular)

Same deal, same seat (Ben, Green Cloak). First game: Ben wins 102, Cal 97, Cory 95, Ava 94. Second game: Ava wins 110 with no members, Ben 99, Cory 83, Cal 81.

**A. Tracking: first, slightly.** In the first game nobody holds more than 4 member cards until late round III (Ben ends on Jack-of-All-Trades and Guest of Honor, Cal on Downsizers and Tourist), so the table stays easy to read. In the second, Ben builds to Agents, Dabblers, Night Traders, Extra Hand, Song Request, Big Tent and Old Money by round III turn 14, and Cal carries Line Cutters, Stunt Double and Alchemists. Still manageable, because Cory and Ava hold none.

**B. Recruits: second, slightly.** In the second game Ben buys Extra Hand in round II turn 5 and holds it to round III turn 9, then the same turn takes Song Request, chains it into Echo and a second Sweet Talk, and later adds Big Tent and Old Money for the end. That is a plan. In the first, his round III Fan Club buy is mostly a way to copy Largesse, and he gives Fan Club to Cory right away through Guest of Honor for +4.

**C. Choices: second, slightly.** In the second, round I turn 4 (Dabblers vs Cashier vs Martyrs vs Jack) and round III turn 10 (Big Tent vs Seamstress vs Sore Loser vs Spoilsport) are real weighing turns. In the first, about four of Ben's turns are a pass or a bare follower buy because nothing in the shop fits (round I turn 3, round II turn 8), and most round III turns come down to "buy 10 followers". The second has those turns too (round II turns 6 and 7), only fewer.

**D. Resources: same.** Both are tight in round I and pile up in round III: in the first Ava sits on 19 yellow after Makeover, in the second Cal sits on 29 green and Ava on 27 yellow. Silver floods both games through Largesse and Toast. Neither game makes a held color worth fighting over late.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** The second game has a real split: Ben with 9 members against Ava and Cory with none, buying followers and recruiting members only to cash them back in (Ava's round II Estate Sale and Echo turns on Recluse, Con Men and Fan Club). In the first, everyone sits at 2 to 3 members and turns resources into followers, so the plans look alike. But in the second the no-member plan won by 11, which says the member path costs too much.

**F. The race: first, clearly.** The first game ends with 8 points between first and last. The lead goes from Cory (round II) to Cory and Cal level on 77 (round III turn 12) to Ben, who passes them on his last two turns with Fan Club copying Largesse and Sample copying Tourist. In the second, Ava jumps to 60 at the start of round III on two Largesse copies and is never caught (75, then 95). The only late question is second place.

**G. Size: second, slightly.** The second game shows more of each color's tricks: Con Men's steal, Stunt Double's copy, Extra Hand's extra minion, a Song Request into Echo into Sweet Talk chain, Night Traders' swaps, and two Makeovers. The first has the copy tricks too, but most of them land on the same Largesse.

**Best moment:** First game, round III turns 12 and 13. Ben, 18 behind, recruits Fan Club to copy Cory's Largesse, gives Fan Club away for +4, then uses Sample to copy Tourist and wins by 5.
**Worst moment:** Second game, round III opening. Cal copies Largesse through Cover, then Ava copies Cal's Cover through Echo for a second Largesse. That is +20 followers between them before Ben places a minion, and the race is effectively over.

**Overall: first, slightly.** The second economy gives better individual turns and buys, but its ending runs away and the winner never recruits a member. The first stays close to the last placement, and that is what brings a worker-placement table back. In both games one silver space, Largesse (+10 followers for giving rivals 12 silver), gets copied over and over. Either economy needs that fixed.

What the economy changed at the table: members were about 1 cheaper in the second game, so Ben bought more of them and had more real choices. But members still didn't pay as well as buying followers, so the player who skipped members ran away with it. In the first, pricier members pushed everyone onto the same follower buys, and the game stayed close.
