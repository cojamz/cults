# P7, judged as Cory

**A. Tracking: first, slightly.** In the first game Cory ends with 6 cards in front of him. Round III is busy (Con Men goes Cal to Ben to Cal to Cory, and Dabblers changes hands four times), but most of his turns are a plain steal or a follower buy. In the second game the start of round III is three things happening at once before Cory places anything: Window Shopper throws out 2, Body Snatchers swaps Loyalists for Kidnapper, then Kidnapper takes Deprogrammers. Con Men changes owner six times. Cory finishes with 9 cards and Ava with 8 (Mentor, Kidnapper, Body Snatchers, Snitch...). That is the "10 member cards across each player" problem.

**B. Recruits: first, clearly.** First game, Round I turn 3: Cory uses Peek to put Line Cutters on top, then buys it. That was planned. Patron Saints at the end of Round II was saved up for over two turns. Second game, Round II turn 5: Tryout copies Mentor and gets Loyalists free, then the real Mentor brings Window Shopper free. That's four members in one turn, and members keep spilling in after that (19 by the end). They feel like a routine flood, not events.

**C. Choices: same.** Both games have a few dead turns early on. First game: Ava "couldn't afford anything" in Round I, and Cory's last Round I placement had only 2 options. Second game: Cory passes on Round I turn 3, Ben can't afford anything, and turn 4 has 2 options. In round III both seats get real trade-offs: Kingmakers against Snitch in the first, Extra Hand and Double Agent in the second.

**D. Resources: second, slightly.** The second game is tighter: Gather gives +2 in Round I, and Cory often holds 1 to 4 resources. Shake Down became a real call: Ava paid 5 to keep Loyalists, where in the first game she simply couldn't pay 7 and lost Dabblers. The first game's piles got big (Ben with 10 red, Sweet Talk turning 14 silver one at a time). Against that, the second game's steals pile onto Cory in Round III: Con Men takes 5 red, then Accomplice 3, Help Yourself 1 and Second-Story Man 2. That feels bad.

**E. Paths: same.** In both games, one player won mostly by buying followers and another by piling up members. First game: Ben wins with 14 members, Cal reaches 67 with 5 members, and Cory wins on Patron Saints and Patriarch. Second game: Ben reaches 61 with 8 members on Largesse and buys of 10, and Cory wins on the +10 for most members.

**F. The race: first, slightly.** First game, Round III turn 12: all four sit at 35 to 50. Cory is last and wins 78-77 on his game-end members, which feels earned and public. The second game is also a comeback (Cory 39 against Ben 56 on his last turn, winning 63-61), but Spoilsport cancelling a contest decides it. That feels like a gotcha. One unclear line: Spoilsport names "Magenta (Cal leads)", yet the contest that went to nobody was "Most resources".

**G. Size: second, slightly.** More color tricks happened at Cory's seat in the second game: free recruits from Mentor, Extra Hand's extra minion, Double Agent's swap, Body Snatchers. The first game's size was mostly big numbers: Largesse for +10 and buys of 10 followers.

**Best moment:** first game, Round I turn 3, Peek setting up Line Cutters and buying it the same turn. **Worst moment:** second game, Round III start, a three-step chain of swaps before anyone places a minion.

**Overall: first, slightly.** It has fewer cards to watch, each member lands as a planned buy, and it ends in a one-point race decided by things everyone could see. The second economy is tighter on resources but drowns the table in members.

What the economy changed at the table: in the second game members cost less and spaces gave less, so resources got tight but members poured in. More cards changed hands and seats got crowded. In the first game members cost more, so fewer cards came out, every recruit counted, and most of the spending went to followers.
