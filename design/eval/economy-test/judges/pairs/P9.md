# P9: casual judge (a friend at game night)

**A. Tracking: second, clearly.** In the first game the cards never stop moving. Con Men changes hands about six times (Cal recruits it in Round II, then Ava takes it, Cal takes it back, Cory takes it in Round III, Cal takes it again, Cory gets it back with Double Agent). By Cory's last turn he holds 9 member cards and Ava holds 8, so I would lose track of who has what. In the second game people hold fewer cards (Cory has 5 to 6, Cal has 2). There are still swaps (Dabblers and Con Men move around in Rounds II and III), but I could keep up with them.

**B. Recruits: second, slightly.** In the first game Cory recruits a member almost every turn, and in Round II one Tryout plus Mentor brings in three members on a single turn. That is fun once, but after that recruiting is routine. In the second game Patron Saints (Round II, 10 silver) and Patriarch (Round III, 12 resources) cost real saving up, so they feel like moments Cory planned for.

**C. Choices: first, slightly.** In the second game Cory has more turns where the only possible recruit is followers, or a pass: Round II turns 5 to 7 offer only "buy 1 follower" or "pass" because every member is out of reach. In the first game he usually has two or three members he could afford and has to weigh them (Round III turn 10: Extra Hand, Spoilsport or Anarchist).

**D. Resources: second, slightly.** In the second game you hear "couldn't afford anything" (Ava in Round I), and Ava's Shake Down makes Cory pick between paying 9 and losing Window Shopper. A steal hurts there. In the first game, resources flow so freely that Cory spends 9 silver on Extra Hand without much thought.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** In the second game Cal wins on bought followers with only 5 members, Ben wins on member count with 14, and Cory mixes end-of-game bonus cards with a big follower buy. All three plans finish within 11 followers of each other. In the first game Ben's mostly-followers plan and Cory's lots-of-members plan also compete, so the difference is small.

**F. The race: second, clearly.** Before the last scoring of the second game, Cal leads 57 to 54 to 54 to 52, and the final is 78, 77, 67, 67, decided by end-of-game bonuses. Everyone was still in it. The first game is also close at the top (63 to 61), but Cal ends far behind on 48. Cory's win there came from blocking the Magenta contest with Spoilsport, which might feel cheap to the others.

**G. Size: first, clearly.** The first game is packed with big moments: the Mentor chain, Scouts clearing 7 cards from the shop, Double Agent stealing Con Men back, Extra Hand. The second game still has some (Cory takes Con Men on Tryout through Kidnapper, Recruit Twice brings +15 followers), but far fewer.

**Best moment (second game):** Round III, Cory's turn 13: Recruit Twice turns his saved resources into +15 followers to catch the leaders, and he wins by 1.
**Worst moment (first game):** Round II, Ava's and Cal's turns: Con Men is grabbed back and forth, and each grab steals 5 more resources, until nobody can say who holds what.

**Overall: second, slightly.** I would rather play the second one again. I could follow it, and all four of us had a shot at the end. The first was bigger and wilder, but it buried me in cards.

What the economy changed at the table: higher prices meant fewer members and less card-swapping, so the game was easier to follow and closer at the end, but there were fewer flashy combo turns and more turns where all you could buy was followers.
