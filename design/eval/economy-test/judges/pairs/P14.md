# P14, judged by the veteran (worker-placement tension)

Same deal, Ava's seat. First game: pricier members, follower buys pay a bonus (5 for 6, 10 for 13). Second game: cheaper members, follower buys one for one.

**A. Tracking: first, clearly.** In the first game, Cory ends with 7 member cards and Cal with 6, and members rarely move after Round II. In the second, Cory and Cal each hold 10 by Round III, and cards keep changing hands: Ava's Anarchist swaps Scouts for Lookouts (R III turn 8), her Promote moves Ben's Timekeeper, Ben's Demote takes Cheerleaders, and Guest of Honor and Godparent pass cards around. That is hard to follow at a table. (Write-up note: in the first game, Round III says "Cal's Spoilsport: nobody wins the Red contest", but the scoring line pays Red and spoils the Silver contest instead. I can't tell which happened.)

**B. Recruits: first, slightly.** In the first game Ava saves silver for Toastmaster (R I turn 2, all 7 she had) and spends 10 on Spoilsport (R II turn 6). Each buy cost her a turn of saving. In the second, Deprogrammers costs 5 on R I turn 3 and Anarchist and Hot Streak come cheaply, so members feel like routine buys. Godparent (R III turn 9) is the one recruit that feels planned.

**C. Choices: second, clearly.** In the first game's Round III, Ava's turns 9 to 12 nearly all end the same way: "pay 10 get 13", the obvious best buy. Three of her four buys there are that. In the second, the same turns weigh real options against each other: Godparent plus giving Dyer away, Counterfeiters on whom, Hot Streak after Right Away, or followers. Only R I turn 2 and R II turn 6 look forced.

**D. Resources: second, clearly.** In the first game's Round III, resources flood the table: Largesse hands out 12 silver, Toast gives +11, and Ben buys 13 followers three times and Cal twice. Nothing feels scarce. In the second, resources are fought over. Con Men steals twice in Round II. Ava's Counterfeiters turns all 18 of Ben's resources to silver (R III turn 12) to push him off the "fewest silver" lead, and turns Cal's 7 to silver in Round II. That is the resource fight I want.

**E. Paths: second, slightly.** In the first game, follower buying beats members outright: Ben wins with 7 members, Ava places second with 8, and the 13- and 14-member cults finish last. In the second, Cory wins with 15 members and Ben, two points behind, has 12. Members, contests and follower buys all count, though everyone still buys followers late.

**F. The race: first, slightly.** In the first game Ava leads at 87 in mid Round III. Ben passes her with three 13-follower buys and Patron Saints, and the top three finish 102/100/93. In the second, Cory pulls away in Round III (63 to Ava's 43 at turn 10). Ben closes to 90 against Cory's 93 only on round-end bonuses, and Ava falls from first to last.

**G. Size: second, slightly.** Both games have big moments. The first has Largesse +10, Godparent +10, Ballroom Dancers +8 and two Spoilsports. The second packs more color tricks into Ava's own turns (Counterfeiters twice, Anarchist twice, Promote, Godparent, Right Away into Hot Streak), plus Cal's Makeover into 17 red and Con Men stealing twice.

**Overall: second, slightly.** The first game's finish is closer, but its Round III is a follower-buying flood with little to weigh. The second keeps every placement a trade-off, and its steals and color changes give a reason to watch rivals.

What the economy changed at the table: cheaper members and one-for-one follower buys stopped the late flood of bonus follower buys. Resources stayed tight enough to fight over and turns became real choices, but players collected more cards and swapped them more, which made the table harder to follow.
