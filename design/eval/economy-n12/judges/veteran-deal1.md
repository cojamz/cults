# Veteran judge, deal 1 (same deal, Cory's seat, four economies)

Judged as a seasoned worker-placement player: is every placement and buy a trade-off, do plans compete, does blocking matter, does the end stay close, is there a reason to plan ahead.

## Game K (Cal wins 81; Ben 58, Cory 54, Ava 54)

- **A. Tracking: 1.** By round III three cults hold 11 to 15 members, and the members keep changing hands: Body Snatchers swaps at every round start, Swindle, Poach, Promote, Demote, Double Agent, Gift Swappers and Foster Parents all move cards between cults (Round III, Cory's turn 10 into Ava's turn: Ghost, Snitch, Foster Parents and Hustlers all change owners in one stretch). Nobody at a real table follows this.
- **B. Recruits: 2.** Members are bought almost every turn by someone (26 paid buys, 11 free). Cory's Round II turn 7 chain (Repeat Customers pays for itself with +3 blue, then pulls Con Men from the discard pile, which steals 5) is fun, but it is cheap stacking, not a buy he saved for.
- **C. Choices: 3.** Cory nearly always had several live options, often too many to weigh well; three shop members were affordable at the start of Round II turn 7 and four at Round III turn 8.
- **D. Resources: 2.** Resources move freely and are rarely short. Cory's Largesse (Round III turn 9) handed Cal 7 silver, and two turns later Cal turned it into 10 followers. Giving was cheaper for him than it should have been.
- **E. Paths: 3.** Contests and member count drive everything; buying followers is a side dish (only two 10-follower buys in the game).
- **F. The race: 3.** The lead changed (Ava ahead after round II, Ben ahead mid round III, Cal ahead at the end), but Cal jumped from 50 to 81 at the last round end by taking two round-III contests plus Cheerleaders, a 23-follower gap nobody saw coming in the scores.
- **G. Size: 5.** Every color's tricks fired over and over: free recruits, steals, swaps, gifts, chain recruits.
- **Best moment:** Round II turn 7, Cory's Repeat Customers into Con Men, stealing 5 magenta from Ava.
- **Worst moment:** Round III turn 9, Cory's Largesse giving Cal 7 silver that became Cal's winning 10-follower buy.
- **Overall: 2.** Big and lively, but too loose and too busy to plan; the winner is decided by round-end swings, not by a tight squeeze.

## Game P (Ava wins 59; Ben 45, Cory 42, Cal 42)

- **A. Tracking: 4.** Most cults stay at 2 to 9 members; only Ava reaches 13. Cory sat on 2 members from Round II turn 6 to Round III turn 8, which is easy to follow.
- **B. Recruits: 4.** A member is an event: Cory's Cheerleaders (Round III turn 8, 9 resources, right after Poach took Ben's Con Men and stole 5) was a real planned buy.
- **C. Choices: 2.** Cory recruited nothing on 6 of his 12 turns, and only once could he afford a shop member at the start of a turn. Round III turn 10, Recruit Twice gave +5 blue and "can't pay for any member in the discard pile", then he passed: a dead turn.
- **D. Resources: 4.** Resources are scarce and worth fighting over: Round III turn 9 Heist took 4 followers from the leader, Ava. It sometimes goes past tight into stuck.
- **E. Paths: 3.** Cory's plan became buying 5 followers at a time; Ava pulled ahead on members. Few other plans were open.
- **F. The race: 2.** Ava led from the end of round II (36 against 26) and won by 14; there was no late lead change, and the others finished in a 42 to 45 pile.
- **G. Size: 2.** Scores of 42 to 59 feel small; Ava's Spoilsport cancelled the Magenta contest in both round II and round III; Extra Hand costs 12, and Recruit Twice and Markdown had nothing to buy.
- **Best moment:** Round III turn 8, Poach takes Ben's Con Men, the steal pays for Cheerleaders.
- **Worst moment:** Round III turn 10, Recruit Twice with nothing to recruit, then a pass.
- **Overall: 2.** Tight, but too many turns do nothing, and the leader after round II is never caught.

## Game R (Ava wins 76; Ben 73, Cory 61, Cal 60)

- **A. Tracking: 3.** Ben climbs to 15 to 17 members and Ava to 13, which is heavy late, but Cory at 7 and Cal at 9 to 10 are readable.
- **B. Recruits: 4.** Cory's members came from deliberate spaces (Borrow for Window Shopper in Round II turn 6, Late Shift for Snobs in turn 7, Poach in Round III turn 8), and he saved up for a 10-follower buy the same turn. Buys felt planned.
- **C. Choices: 4.** Cory weighed saving against spending on most turns: he passed six times to hold resources for the Most resources contest, then cashed in with Flaunt and a 5-follower buy in Round III turn 10.
- **D. Resources: 4.** Holding resources mattered: Cory kept 9 at the end to take the round-III Most resources contest for 10, while steals (Heist in Round II turn 5) cost rivals real stock.
- **E. Paths: 4.** Three different plans competed: Cory with 7 members, resources held and follower buys (61); Ben with a big member count (73); Ava with members of one color plus Cheerleaders (76).
- **F. The race: 5.** Round III opened at 30, 33, 29 and 24; Cory led at 41 after his turn 8; Ava beat Ben by only 3 at the end.
- **G. Size: 4.** Free recruits, heists, swaps and Kidnappers at round end all happened; scores of 60 to 76 feel like a full game.
- **Best moment:** Round III turn 8, Poach brings Mentor and a free Brethren, then 10 resources become 10 followers.
- **Worst moment:** Round III round end, Cal's Kidnappers takes Cory's Mentor after he had no move left to answer it.
- **Overall: 4.** Every buy is a trade-off, three plans stay alive, and the finish comes down to 3 followers.

## Game T (Ava wins 62; Cal 58, Cory 40, Ben 29)

- **A. Tracking: 3.** Cal never goes past 4 members and Ben stays at 4 to 9; Ava and Cory reach 12, with swaps (Demote, Anarchist, Hustlers) adding some load.
- **B. Recruits: 3.** The big buys were planned (Cheerleaders in Round III turn 8, after Demote took Ava's Con Men and stole 5), but most spending went on single followers: 20 one-follower buys across the table.
- **C. Choices: 3.** Cory never had a shop member he could afford at the start of a turn, so many buy steps were "spend the 1 left over on 1 follower" (Round II turns 4, 5 and 6). Placements were still real choices.
- **D. Resources: 4.** Resources were scarce and fought over: Cory held nothing at the start of Round II turns 5 and 7, and the steals in Round III (Help Yourself, Con Men, Cat Burglars) hit real stock.
- **E. Paths: 5.** Cal nearly won with 4 members by buying 10 followers and taking the Most resources contest; Ava won on members and one-color groups. That is two clearly different plans racing.
- **F. The race: 3.** The top was close (62 against 58), and the lead changed from Cory in round II to Cal late in round III to Ava at the end, but Ben finished at 29 and was out of it by mid round II.
- **G. Size: 3.** Con Men, Anarchist and Word of Mouth (+6) gave real moments, but Spoilsport cancelled the Silver contest in round II and round III.
- **Best moment:** Round III turn 11, Cory recruits Anarchist and swaps his Accomplice for Ben's Deprogrammers.
- **Worst moment:** Round II turn 7, Party Gifts for 6 followers, then "couldn't afford anything".
- **Overall: 3.** Real competing plans and a close top two, but too many buy steps where a player can only spend 1 for 1 follower.

## Ranking (best to worst for four friends at the table)

1. **R**: the closest finish (3 followers) with three different plans still alive at the end, and buys you had to save for.
2. **T**: a 4-member cult nearly beat a 12-member cult, so the paths were real, but one player was left far behind.
3. **K**: big and full of tricks, but too many members changing hands to follow and a 23-follower blowout at the last round end.
4. **P**: too many turns where nothing can be bought, and the leader after round II was never caught.

## What the economy did at the table

- **P:** What the economy did at the table: members were almost never affordable, players passed or bought small follower lots instead, resources ran short to the point of dead turns, and the leader pulled away early.
- **R:** What the economy did at the table: members were affordable now and then (often through free-recruit spaces), resources were worth saving and holding, buys felt planned, and the race stayed close to the end.
- **T:** What the economy did at the table: shop members were never affordable at the start of a turn, so leftovers went on single followers, resources stayed scarce, big buys were saved for, and the top two finished close while one player fell far behind.
