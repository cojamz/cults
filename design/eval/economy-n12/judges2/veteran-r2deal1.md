# Veteran judge, deal 1 round 2 (same deal and seat, three price settings)

Judging as a seasoned worker-placement player: is every placement and buy a trade-off, do plans compete, does blocking matter, does the end stay close, is planning ahead rewarded.

## Game F (Cory 65, Cal 64, Ava 63, Ben 48)

- **A. Tracking: 3.** Cory never holds more than 5 cards, but Ava and Ben both reach 11 counted members in round III, and Con Men changes hands four times in round III (Cory recruits it at turn 8, Ben poaches it, Cal promotes it away, Ben demotes it back). Following who holds what takes work.
- **B. Recruits: 4.** Cory pays for only two members all game: Cheerleaders (8, round II turn 4, after Swindle took Dabblers) and Window Shopper (8, round II turn 7, after Polish turned 3 silver into blue). Each needed a setup turn first. His free ones (Deprogrammers off Late Shift, Con Men off Initiation) took a space someone else could have blocked.
- **C. Choices: 4.** Most turns weigh two or three real lines, for example round III turn 9: Largesse (+10 followers, but feeds 12 silver to rivals) against Catch Up for 6 magenta. Only round I turn 1 (nothing affordable) and the round II leftover 1-follower buys were obvious.
- **D. Resources: 4.** Cory's hand empties again and again: 0 after Con Men was stolen back (round III turn 9), "couldn't afford anything" after Largesse. Steals swing every round, and each round's Magenta contest goes to a different player (Cory, Ava, Cal).
- **E. Paths: 4.** Three plans finish within 2 followers: Cal on steals and follower buys with 4 members, Ava wide at 11 members, Cory on contests plus Cheerleaders and big follower buys. Ben's member pile falls behind.
- **F. Race: 5.** Cal leads from round I to the last turn (34 to 19 entering round III). Cory passes him only with the 10-follower buy on his final turn (round III turn 12) and two Cheerleaders points at the end. The finish is 65 / 64 / 63.
- **G. Size: 4.** Every color's tricks fire: free recruits (Late Shift, Initiation, Seat Filler via Test Drive), Heist twice, Swindle, Promote and Demote swaps, Largesse, Toast. Plenty happens, and it is busy.
- **Overall: 4.** The tightest race of the three: buys cost a setup turn, the hand gets emptied often, and three plans finish within two followers.

## Game J (Ava 76, Ben 73, Cory 61, Cal 60)

- **A. Tracking: 2.** Ben ends at 15 cards counting 17 and Ava at 16 counted. Round III adds Gift Swappers and Kidnappers moving cards between all four cults at once on the last turn. The table is hard to follow.
- **B. Recruits: 2.** Members are routine for the others: Ava and Ben buy or take one almost every turn (round I: Ben buys Dabblers and Kingmakers back to back, Ava gets Line Cutters free then buys Deprogrammers on the same turn). Cory's only paid member is Paupers in round I. After that he takes free ones (Window Shopper, Snobs, Mentor plus Brethren) and passes with resources in hand.
- **C. Choices: 3.** Round II turns 4, 6 and 7 Cory passes holding 6 to 12 resources because nothing in the shop is worth it. Those passes are a choice, but a flat one. Round III gives real choices (Poach on Cal's Mentor, then a 10-follower buy).
- **D. Resources: 2.** Resources pile up. Cory carries 14 into round II's end and wins Most resources by holding still. Ava then buys 10 followers twice in round III. Steals happen but don't hurt, because everyone is flush.
- **E. Paths: 3.** The two big member engines take first and second. Cory's small cult (6 members at the end) and Cal's steals fall 12 to 16 behind. The cheap shop makes the wide path the easy one.
- **F. Race: 4.** There are lead changes: Ava leads, then Ben 48 at round III turn 11, then Ava's 10-blue follower buy and Cheerleaders swap. The final gap is 3 between the top two, but the bottom two are well back.
- **G. Size: 4.** Lots fires: Con Men passed around three times, Scouts emptying the shop twice, Gift Swappers, Kidnappers, Purifiers making Ava discard. Big, but noisy.
- **Overall: 3.** Big and lively, but cheap members and piled-up resources make most buys automatic, and the wide-cult players run away from the rest.

## Game W (Ava 62, Cal 58, Cory 40, Ben 29)

- **A. Tracking: 4.** Cults stay small: Ava 5 to 8 cards, Cal 4, Ben 3 to 6. Cory's counted members (11 to 12) come mostly from Line Cutters and Deprogrammers being worth 3 and 4. Con Men still passes around in round III.
- **B. Recruits: 4.** Each paid recruit is a payoff. Line Cutters takes Cory's whole hand in round I turn 2. Snitch (7) needs Recruit Twice's 5 blue in round II. Cheerleaders (8) comes after the Con Men steal at round III turn 8. Anarchist (9) takes 6 silver plus a 4-resource Help Yourself steal at turn 11.
- **C. Choices: 3.** Many of Cory's buy steps are just "pass or 1 follower" because he holds 0 to 3 resources (round II turns 5 and 7, round III turn 9: "couldn't afford anything" after Party Gifts). Placements still carry weight, but the buy is often decided for him.
- **D. Resources: 5.** Scarce all game: Cory starts round II with 3 resources and has nothing at round II turn 5. Each steal (Con Men, Heist, Cat Burglars) costs the target their next buy. Snitch costs followers to steal from its holder.
- **E. Paths: 3.** Cal wins second with 4 members on steals and follower buys. Ava wins on contests (and Spoilsport cancels Silver twice). Cory's early member lead fades. Ben (29) never gets going after round I.
- **F. Race: 2.** Ava and Cal finish 4 apart, but Cory and Ben end 22 and 33 behind. Ben is out of it from round II (9 followers against Ava's 23). Cory leads at the start of round III (30) and then drifts to 40.
- **G. Size: 3.** The tricks still happen (Swindle, Promote and Demote swaps, Largesse, Buyout taking Deprogrammers, Double Agent), but players have fewer resources to set them up. Repeat Customers and Recruit Twice find nothing they can pay for.
- **Overall: 3.** The tightest economy, where every member is earned, but two of four players are out of the race by mid round III, and a table feels that.

## Rank, best to worst for four friends at the table

1. **F**: the only game where every player still had a chance going into the last turns, and members cost a setup turn without stalling anyone.
2. **J**: close at the top and full of action, but cheap members make buys automatic and turn the table into card-tracking.
3. **W**: buys feel earned, but the score spread (62 to 29) leaves two players with nothing to play for in round III.

## What the prices did at the table

- **F**, what the prices did at the table: a member was in reach about every second or third turn after a setup turn. Leftovers went to 1-follower buys instead of piling up. Buys were planned (Polish into blue for Window Shopper), and the race stayed close to the final turn.
- **J**, what the prices did at the table: members were in reach almost every turn, so the wide cults bought constantly. Resources piled up (14 held at the end of round II, 10-follower buys twice). Buys felt routine, and the race was close only between the top two.
- **W**, what the prices did at the table: members were in reach rarely, often once a round, and hands were near empty most turns. Every buy was planned around a big gain turn, but players who fell behind could not catch up, and the race split into two pairs.
