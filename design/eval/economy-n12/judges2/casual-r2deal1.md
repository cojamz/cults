# Casual judge, round 2 deal 1 (games F, J, W)

Judged as a friend who plays a few times a month: can I follow the table, do I sit around, are my turns exciting, would I know why I won or lost, would I come back next week.

## Game F (Cory wins 65, Cal 64, Ava 63, Ben 48)

- A. Tracking: 2. Members move all the time: in round II Ava's Promote takes Cory's Deprogrammers on the opening turn, and Cory's stolen Dabblers is swapped away by Ben's Demote one turn later. In round III, Con Men changes owner four times (Cory recruits it, Ben's Poach takes it, Cal's Promote takes it, Ben's Demote takes it back), stealing 5 each time. A casual player loses track of who owns what.
- B. Recruits: 3. Cory's Cheerleaders (round II, turn 4, 8 resources) and Window Shopper (turn 7) were saved up for and felt like events. Most other members came free (Late Shift, Borrow) or were swapped in.
- C. Choices: 3. Round II turns 5 and 6 were "Stash, buy 1 follower" and "Gather, buy 1 follower", small turns with nothing better to do. Round III turn 9 (Largesse for +10 followers) and the end-game 10-follower buy were real decisions.
- D. Resources: 3. Cory rarely held more than 9 and twice had nothing ("couldn't afford anything" after Largesse). Steals hurt: Ben's Con Men took 5 from him in round III. But some players reach round end with 0 to 2 resources, so they never feel rich.
- E. Paths: 4. Cal won follower buys and steals with only 4 to 5 members; Ava and Ben built 11 members each. Cory went through gifts and follower buys. All of these finished within 2 followers of each other except Ben.
- F. The race: 5. Cal led by 19 in round II (34 against 15). Cory caught up with Largesse (+10) and a 10-follower buy on his last turn, and the final score was 65, 64, 63. Everyone but Ben had a chance on the last turn.
- G. Size: 4. Free recruits, steals of followers (Heist), swaps, copies through Test Drive and Largesse's 12 gifts all happened. The table felt busy.
- Best moment: round III, Cory's last turn: 10 followers bought to take the win by 1.
- Worst moment: round III, Con Men passing through four hands in one round, each time stealing 5.
- Overall: 4. A finish this close would make everyone want a rematch, even if the member swaps were hard to follow.

## Game J (Ava wins 76, Ben 73, Cory 61, Cal 60)

- A. Tracking: 2. Ava reaches 16 members and Ben 17 (counting Mannequin). Round III ends with Gift Swappers moving four members between seats and Cal's Kidnappers taking Mentor, so it is very hard to keep up with what each cult owns.
- B. Recruits: 2. Members felt routine for Ava and Ben, who picked up one almost every turn, many free (Late Shift, Borrow, Mentor bringing a second one). Cory had a single member (Paupers) from round I until late in round II. His Snobs, recruited free, was taken by Ben's Body Snatchers at the start of round III.
- C. Choices: 2. Cory recruited nothing on turns 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11, often while holding resources. Many turns came down to taking resources and passing.
- D. Resources: 2. Cory ended round II holding 14 resources and won "most resources" with them, but bought nothing with them. Meanwhile the big cults spent everything each turn. Resources piled up for one player and meant little for the others.
- E. Paths: 3. The two players with the most members took first and second. Cal tried steals and follower buys and finished last. Holding resources gave Cory contest points but not a win.
- F. The race: 3. Ava and Ben were close at the end (Ben led 48 to 47 at one point in round III), but Cory and Cal finished about 15 behind.
- G. Size: 4. Lots happened: Con Men changed hands three times in round III's opening, plus Scouts clearing the shop twice, Largesse, Gift Swappers and Kidnappers.
- Best moment: round II, Cory's Heist turn, taking 4 followers and 4 resources in one go after sitting out.
- Worst moment: round II, Cory sitting with 1 member while Ava and Ben pass 9 to 14 members.
- Overall: 2. Watching two people stack 15 or more members while you have 1 to 4 is no fun, and the board is too full to read.

## Game W (Ava wins 62, Cal 58, Cory 40, Ben 29)

- A. Tracking: 3. Cults stay smaller (Cal 4 members all game, Ben 4 to 6, Ava 5 to 12), so it is easier to see what each player has. Swaps still pile up in round III: Cory's Deprogrammers is bought away by Ben's Buyout, then taken by Ava's Double Agent.
- B. Recruits: 4. Cory paid 5 for Line Cutters in round I, turn 2, a planned buy that let him pick first every round. Cheerleaders (round III, turn 8) and Anarchist (turn 11) were saved for too. Members felt like events, not every-turn buys.
- C. Choices: 3. Most of Cory's turns had a real trade-off (Swindle for Dabblers, Recruit Twice for Snitch, Hustlers to give Cal his silver). But round II, turn 7 ended with "couldn't afford anything", and several turns were take-resources-buy-1-follower.
- D. Resources: 4. Resources were tight. Cory held 0 to 6 most of the game, and Con Men, Heist, Cat Burglars and Help Yourself steals each cost someone something they needed.
- E. Paths: 3. Ava won with 12 members, and Cal came second with only 4, through steals and follower buys. Cory's many-members plan led for two rounds and then faded.
- F. The race: 2. First and second were 4 apart, and the lead changed (Cory first after round II, then Cal, then Ava). But Cory finished 22 behind and Ben 33 behind, and Ben had no chance from round II on.
- G. Size: 4. Free recruits, follower steals, swaps and Largesse all happened. Spoilsport cancelling the silver contest twice was a big, visible swing.
- Best moment: round I, turn 2, Cory saving for Line Cutters and opening the next two rounds by picking first.
- Worst moment: round III, Cory losing Con Men to Cal's Poach, followers to Ava's Heist and Deprogrammers twice, all in one round.
- Overall: 3. Buys feel planned and the table is readable, but two of four friends are out of it well before the end.

## Ranking, best to worst for four friends at the table

1. **F**: three players finished within 2 followers, and the last turn decided it.
2. **W**: members feel worth saving for and you can follow the table, but half the table is far behind at the end.
3. **J**: two players build huge cults you can't track while the others sit with almost nothing to recruit.

## What the prices did at the table

- F: What the prices did at the table: a member was buyable now and then after a turn of saving, resources rarely piled up (players often hit 0), buys like Cheerleaders felt planned, and the race stayed close to the last turn.
- J: What the prices did at the table: members were buyable for the big cults almost every turn, so two players stacked 15 or more. Resources piled up for the player who wasn't buying, buys felt routine, and the race was close only between the top two.
- W: What the prices did at the table: a member took a turn or two of saving and was often out of reach (one "couldn't afford anything"), resources stayed scarce, buys felt planned, and the top two were close while the bottom two fell far behind.
