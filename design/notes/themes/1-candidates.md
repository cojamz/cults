# New themes, each checked against every element at once

Circus is dropped (Cory, Nov 9: "I don't think it's going to be a fit"). Lesson from 12 circus passes: test the whole picture together, and make resources, minions and actions physical. A resource can be stolen, given, piled up and turned into score. A minion is a person who blocks a spot. An action is what that person does there.

Hard checks every theme must pass: stealing (Magenta), gifts that pay you back (Silver), copying a space (Green), bending turn order (Purple), hoarding that is counted (Gold), members costing partly their own color, rings opening toward the center, followers as score.

## 1. Machine politics (a Tammany-style city, 1880s)
| Element | Is | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Leader | A ward boss | Rival bosses share one city. |
| Followers | Voters | Real machines ran on loyal voters. A member counts 1 because his family votes with him. |
| Minions | Ward heelers, your men on the street | One man works a corner, so a rival can't. |
| Colors | The city's power blocs: the Docks, the Police, the Saloons, the Press, the Rails… | Each bloc has its own men, its own goods and its own way of operating. |
| Resources | Each bloc's goods: crates off the docks, whiskey, coal, newspapers | You can steal them, hand them out, stockpile them, and trade them for votes. Machines literally handed out coal and turkeys for votes. |
| Members | Men who join your machine: a police captain, a dock foreman | A dock foreman joins the boss who controls dock crates (his cost is partly dock goods). |
| Actions | What your heeler does at a spot | Police **Hold Up** ("+2 purple in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. The next turn is skipped, even if it's yours.") = arresting a rival's man. Stealing = hijacking a cart. Gifts = favors that buy loyalty. Copying = stealing a rival's playbook. |
| Rounds | Three campaigns (primary, council, mayor) | Prizes grow. |
| Rings | Outer wards, then downtown, then City Hall at the center for the final campaign | The center opens last. |
| Contests | Each bloc backs one boss (most crates = the docks back you) | Physical and countable. |
| Winning | Mayor | |
Weak spot: copying is the vaguest fit.

## 2. Rival gods (minor gods fighting for worship)
| Element | Is | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Leader | A young god | Rivals share one world. |
| Followers | Worshippers | This keeps today's word. |
| Minions | Your priests | A priest tends a shrine, so a rival can't use it. |
| Colors | Domains: sea, harvest, war, tricks, time, the sun… | Each domain has its own mortals, offerings and style. |
| Resources | Offerings: grain, pearls, wine, gold | Stolen (the trickster), given (a generous god), hoarded, and turned into worshippers through miracles. |
| Members | Heroes, prophets and saints who serve you | A sea hero serves the god rich in pearls. |
| Actions | A miracle at a shrine | The time god skips turns, the trickster steals, the echo god repeats another miracle. Copy and turn order both fit naturally here. |
| Rounds | Three ages | |
| Rings | The mortal lands, then the sky, then the throne of the heavens at the center | |
| Contests | Each domain's people worship whoever is strongest in it | |
| Winning | King of the gods | |
Weak spot: it's close to Cults, so it changes less.
