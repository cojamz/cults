# White (trading) redesign, Nov 7

Brief: design/notes/redesigns/BRIEF.md. Live cards checked Nov 7 with `live-board.mjs trading --members`. All prices and numbers are Claude's fill-ins.

## The story, and the tension the coordinator named

Cory, Nov 7: "White is all about like fewer followers… inherent synergy with like on recruits… or on use… you use stuff and then you get rid of it… with cards from… other colors… White needs to be thought about more deeply." Oct 15, when he picked the contest: "White is about when to buy followers; sell before the round ends to dip under the table, buy back after."

What the game counts: your followers are 1 + the followers you've gained + what your members are worth. White's contest goes to the fewest followers at round end (+4 / +6 / +10, ties split), and from round II the fewest followers also goes first. So a White player who casts out members drops their worth, and a White player who holds white instead of followers stays low.

The tension: five of White's six spaces pay followers (Allowance's recruit bonus, Splurge, Exchange, Farewell, Cash In). Taking them usually loses White's own contest. Some of that is a good choice (round III: Cash In for 12 now, or stay lowest for the +10). The part that isn't a choice is round I and II paying followers for recruiting (Allowance's bonus, Splurge), and that White has no way to hold white past the last contest except at half rate (Misers), and no way to dip under the table on purpose, which is exactly Cory's Oct 15 picture.

So the plan for the weak slots: pay white for staying low, let a White player turn followers into something on purpose (members, or white), and let held white score after the last contest at full rate. Kept as they are: Allowance and Exchange (Cory's numbers, Nov 6), Farewell and Estate Sale (Cory kept both, Nov 4 and Nov 7), Cash In and Cashier (Cory praised), Heiresses (protected), Barker (his ask), Magpie ("no I like hoarder!"), Recluse, Downsizers, Undertaker, Martyrs, Money Changers.

## Slots chosen (the weak ones)

1. Splurge (round I space): cost math, a near twin of Allowance's recruit bonus, and it pays followers for recruiting, against White's contest.
2. Brokers (member): "+1 follower" stuck on other cards; counts as 3, which fights Recluse and the contest.
3. Misers (member): half the rate of Cash In for the same white; never the right play.
4. Old Money (member): the exact card Cory turned down Oct 22; rewards keeping members White means to cast out.

Not chosen: Cash In vs Cashier and Farewell vs Estate Sale overlap, but Cory praised or kept all four. Magpie does Yellow's job but Cory kept it by name.

## Slot 1. Splurge (round I space)

Current: "This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower for every 2 resources you pay for it (every 1 in round II). +2 white."
Current best turn: round II, recruit a 9-cost member, +9 followers and +2 white. That puts you up 9 and usually costs you White's +6 and first pick in round III. Round I with a 5-cost member: +2 followers, +2 white.
What a player would miss: a big follower turn in round II. Nothing about it is White's own.

### Option A. Pocket Money
Prints: "+1 white per rival with at least as many followers as you (+2 each in round II, +3 in round III). +1 white."
- Played, round I, 4 players, first turn: everyone has 1 follower, so all 3 rivals count: +4 white. Allowance beside it: +3 white (+1 follower if you recruit). Gain: +1 white and no follower that lifts you.
- Played, round II: Ann has 10 followers; rivals 13, 15, 18. Pocket Money: +7 white. Splurge instead with a 9-cost member: +9 followers, +2 white, Ann goes to 20 and loses White's +6 and first pick in round III. Pocket Money keeps her lowest: +7 white for Cash In, +6 contest, first in round III (first at Cash In).
- Biggest case: round III, lowest of 4: +10 white. Cash In beside it is 12 to 15 followers; Initiation a free member up to 8. Not broken.
- Choice: none on the card; the choice is the whole round before it (buy followers now, or stay low and get paid for it).
- Who feels it: the table sees the lowest player banking white; rivals who want White's contest have to stay low too.
- Ties through: followers and turn order (fewest goes first), every color's follower gains.
- Green copying it: a copier gets white per rival at or above them; fine.
- Ledger: Oct 22, "+1 white × round per rival with no white" drew "eh" and "they lack an elegance and a connection to the core theme of the color". Same shape (white per rival meeting a condition), but this one reads White's own contest, which was the missing link. Built Nov 4 as Pocket Money and preferred 12 to 0 by blind judges over Allowance; never shown to Cory. "Turn up to 2 × round into white", "+1 white per member (at most 3)" and "white equal to cheapest shop member" ("uhg") are different pieces.
- Intuitive: one count everyone can see on the follower track.
- Fun: staying low stops feeling like losing; the leader can see the lowest player getting paid.
- Interacts: reads every rival's followers, from any color.
- Powerful: +4 on turn one, +10 in round III.
- Engine or counter: feeds Cash In, Cashier and Misers; quietly a catch-up.
- Its color: a new way in: White paid for being small, not for recruiting.
- Risk: still a white formula, the kind Cory has called small ball.

### Option B. Keep Splurge
Cory took it Nov 1 and buffed it Nov 6. Its round II turn is big, but it pulls a White player out of White's contest.

### Cut
- Low Profile ("+2/+3/+4 white, doubled if you have the fewest followers"): shown Nov 5; Cory chose the plain +1 white buff instead.
- Sell Off ("Cast out one of your members costing 4 or less. +4 white."): same card as Downsizers on a space.
- Cast out one of your members, then do its On Recruit again: Green's Greatest Hits does that.
- Pawnshop (followers into 2 white each): round I empty (few gained followers), lost its blind test, and 2 for 1 then Cash In doubles.
- Recruit from the discard pile: Blue's job (the shop and the pile).
- Each rival +1 follower, you +white: hands rivals as much as you get (flopped shape; pitched Oct 22).
- Lose followers down to the fewest any rival has: empty in round I, and a space that charges you.

**Pick: Pocket Money.** Why: pays White for staying low in white, the reverse of Splurge's followers that cost White its contest.

## Slot 2. Brokers (member)

Current: [3 white + 4 any, worth 3, counts as 3] "Whenever a White action gives you followers, +1 follower."
Current best turn: Exchange, Farewell and Cash In across a game, +3 followers. Its worth 3 lifts you 3 toward losing White's contest.
What a player would miss: a worth-3 body. Little else.

### Option A. Spendthrifts
Prints: [4 white + 4 any, worth 1] "You may pay for members with followers, 1 follower for each resource."
(The followers your members are worth can't be spent, as with steals.)
- Played, round II, 4 players: Ann has 14 followers; rivals 15, 17, 20. She holds 6 white and 2 red, no magenta. Accomplice is in the shop ([2 magenta + 3 any] "On Recruit: steal 3 resources."). She pays 5 followers: steals 3, drops to 10 (+1 for Accomplice's worth), lowest, wins White's +6 and goes first in round III, still holding 8 resources plus the 3 stolen. Without Spendthrifts her best is buying 8 followers to reach 22 and losing the contest. Difference: about +5, a rival down 3 resources, and the contest moves to her.
- Played, round III: Ann at 30 (20 gained); rivals 28, 32, 35. She pays 10 followers for Kidnapper ([6 magenta + 4 any] "On Recruit and at Round Start: steal a rival's cheapest member."), takes a rival's member, drops to about 22, lowest: +10 contest. But it's round III: the resources she kept only turn back into followers by raising her count again before round end, or at game end through Misers. A real choice.
- Both sides of the choice, each recruit: pay in followers (score down now, resources kept, contest and turn order up) or in resources (score kept). Neither always wins: it turns on how close the contest is and what the member does.
- Who feels it: whoever was counting on White's contest; the rival robbed by the On Recruit; everyone watching the shop, since White can now buy any color's member.
- Biggest case: two dips that each win White's contest, +6 and +10, against spending about 15 followers on members whose worth and abilities come back. Recluse's top is 18 a game. Not broken.
- Ties through: the shop, members of every color (their On Recruit), followers, contest, turn order.
- Ledger: Cory's own idea, Oct 27, "a follower-spending color… pay members with followers… folded into White" (no verdict yet). Not refused. Not "follower recruit has no limit" (that misread the follower cards; this leaves them alone).
- Intuitive: your score becomes money, one for one.
- Fun: the lowest-followers race stops being predictable.
- Interacts: any color's On Recruit, any color's member in the shop.
- Powerful: bends the rule everyone feels (only resources pay).
- Engine or counter: engine with Farewell, Estate Sale and Downsizers (buy, use, cast out); with Cash In (white into followers into members).
- Its color: fewer followers on purpose, spent on other colors' members, Cory's Nov 7 picture.
- Risk: paying a colored part with followers lets White buy colors it doesn't hold.

### Option B. Pallbearers
Prints: [3 white + 4 any, worth 1] "Whenever you cast out one of your members, first do its On Use, even if it's used up."
- Played, round II: Ann used Heist Crew ([6 magenta + 3 any, worth 4, counts as 4] "On Use, once per game: steal half of a rival's biggest pile, rounded down.") in round I. Round II, Estate Sale casts it out: first it steals half of a rival's biggest pile again (about 5), then +10 white (cost 9, +1). Estate Sale alone: +10 white. Difference: the second steal, about +5, and a rival stung twice.
- Biggest case: round III, Heiresses used (+7 white), then Downsizers casts her out: she fires again (+6), Downsizers +4, Cash In for about 20. A three-card combo, near twice Cash In. Strong, not broken alone.
- Choice: which member to cast out, and when. Yours, on your turn.
- Who feels it: whoever the used member hits again.
- Ties through: members of any color with an On Use, the discard pile.
- Ledger: not pitched before.
- Intuitive: one sentence, a last use on the way out.
- Fun: the second Heist Crew steal.
- Interacts: any color's On Use members.
- Powerful: a once-per-game card twice.
- Engine or counter: engine with Farewell, Estate Sale, Downsizers, Undertaker.
- Its color: "use stuff and then get rid of it", literally.
- Risk: close to Green's doing members' abilities again ("synergize, but not overlap").

### Option C. Keep Brokers

### Cut
- Mourners ("Whenever any player casts out a member, +1 white."): only White casts out, so it's Undertaker paid in white.
- "Whenever a member goes to the discard pile, +1 white": the row clearing alone pays 6 to 8 a round, and Blue's cloak already pays on discards.
- Pilgrims ("On Recruit: +4 white. Round End: cast this member out."): a flat one-color gain ("those type of resource gain members can basically all go").

**Pick: Spendthrifts.** Why: Cory's own idea; fewer followers on purpose, spent on any color's members to use and cast out.

## Slot 3. Misers (member)

Current: [4 white + 5 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "Game End: +1 follower per 2 white you hold."
Current best turn: game end with 10 white, +5. Cash In or follower cards turn the same 10 into 10.
What a player would miss: nothing; it's never better than spending.

### Option A. Misers at full rate
Prints: [4 white + 5 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "Game End: +1 follower per white you hold."
- Played, round III, 4 players: Ann holds 12 white, 24 followers; rivals 26, 30, 33. Without Misers she takes Cash In: +12, now 36, and the +10 goes to the player at 26. With Misers she skips Cash In, places elsewhere (say Initiation for a free member), stays lowest, takes the +10, then +12 at game end. Difference: +10, plus her round III minion freed for another space, and the rival at 26 loses the +10.
- Biggest case: Heiresses (+8), Estate Sale and Pocket Money feed 18 white: +18 at game end. That's white she'd have cashed anyway; the gain over Cash In is the last contest. Not broken.
- Choice: hold white for game end (contest, but Magenta can steal it, and it doesn't help your turn order) or cash in now. Both sides live.
- Who feels it: rivals racing for the last contest; Magenta players see a pile to steal.
- Ties through: the contest, turn order, the white every White card makes.
- Ledger: same idea as live Misers at a new rate; a number fix (Cory sets numbers). Built Nov 4 and preferred 4 to 0 by blind judges. Overlaps Cash In and Cashier in payoff, but at a different moment (after the last contest).
- Intuitive: one line, one count.
- Fun: the lowest player sits on a pile everyone can see.
- Interacts: White's contest, Magenta's steals without naming them.
- Powerful: 12 or more followers from one card.
- Engine or counter: payoff for Heiresses, Downsizers, Estate Sale, Pocket Money.
- Its color: hold white, stay low, cash in after the last count: the first White card that solves the tension.
- Risk: its worth 2 lifts you toward losing the contest; worth 1 fits better.

### Option B. Keep Misers

### Cut
- "Round Start: +1 white per 3 white you hold." (interest): lost its blind test 0 to 4.
- Late Bloomers ("Round End: if you have the fewest followers, +3 white."): fired once in 6 games.
- "Game End: +3 followers per rival with more members than you": Recluse at game end.

**Pick: Misers at full rate.** Why: same card, real rate; lets White hold white through the last contest, which nothing else does.

## Slot 4. Old Money (member)

Current: [2 white + 6 any, worth 1] "Game End: +1 follower per white member you have."
Current best turn: game end with 5 White members, +5. Cory turned down this card Oct 22 ("didn't really line up with the theme"), and it rewards keeping members White means to cast out.

### Option A. Penitents
Prints: [3 white + 3 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per round: turn up to 5 of your followers into white, 1 for 1."
(The followers your members are worth can't be turned in.)
- Played, round I, 4 players, last turn: Ann has 6 followers (3 gained); lowest rival 5. She turns 3 followers into 3 white: down to 3, lowest, +4 contest, and first in round II. Her 3 white buys the 3 followers back next round (1 for 1 on the follower cards) or waits for Cash In. Without it: no contest, the rival at 5 takes +4. Difference: +4 and first pick.
- Played, round III: Ann at 27, lowest rival 24. She turns 5 into white: 22, lowest, +10. The 5 white turns back at game end with Misers, or she buys them back before round end and gives up the contest. A real choice.
- Biggest case: wins White's contest every round: +4 +6 +10 = 20 a game, plus first pick in rounds II and III. Recluse's top is 18. With Misers at full rate the dip costs nothing at all, which is the strongest pairing; flag.
- Choice: dip or not, and how far. Yours, on your turn. Rivals can't drop their own followers, so a dip mostly holds; a rival stealing your followers lowers you further, and a rival gaining nothing all round can still tie you.
- Who feels it: whoever was lowest and expected the contest (a sting); turn order changes for everyone.
- Ties through: followers, the contest, turn order, the follower cards in the shop.
- Ledger: Cory's own Oct 15 picture ("sell before the round ends to dip under the table, buy back after"). Pawnshop (a round I space, followers into 2 white each) lost a blind test; this is a member, 1 for 1, never shown to Cory.
- Intuitive: one sentence.
- Fun: a last-turn dip the table can see coming but can't stop.
- Interacts: every rival's follower count.
- Powerful: turns a lost contest into a won one.
- Engine or counter: with Misers and Cash In; a counter to a rival who plays low.
- Its color: fewer followers on purpose, White's contest made into a decision.
- Risk: with Misers at full rate it wins the contest every round.

### Option B. Keep Old Money

### Cut
- Under-this-card scoring of cast-out members: cast-out members must go to the discard pile.
- "Game End: +1 follower per member in the discard pile": about +20 for doing nothing; the row clear fills it.
- "When this member leaves, recruit a shop member up to its cost for free": Red's job.
- "Round Start: +3 white if you have fewer members than every rival": Recluse paid in white; a flat gain.

**Pick: Penitents.** Why: Cory's own Oct 15 dip; makes White's contest a choice White steers. Flag: Penitents and Misers together win it every round.

## Whole color after the picks
Round I: Allowance, Pocket Money, Exchange. Round II: Farewell, Estate Sale. Round III: Cash In. Members change: Spendthrifts, Misers (full rate), Penitents in for Brokers, Misers, Old Money.
A great game: round I, Pocket Money and Allowance for white, a cheap On Recruit member of any color; round II, Estate Sale or Downsizers cast it out for white, Penitents or Spendthrifts dip you under the table for the +6 and first pick; round III, first at Cash In, or hold for Misers and take the +10. Rivals: block Cash In, steal white, buy Misers first, stay low themselves.
What still pays followers: Allowance's recruit bonus, Exchange, Farewell, Cash In. In rounds II and III those are now a choice (cash now or stay low), not a trap.
