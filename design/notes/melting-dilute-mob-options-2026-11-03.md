# Melting Pot, Dilute, Mob: options (Nov 4)

For Cory's playtest notes, Nov 3 8:01pm: "Melting Pot is worded strangely. I still don't like Dilute. Mob is probably just too strong needs rework?"
Every number below is Claude's fill-in. Nothing here is built.
Example table for the played turns: 4 players. Ava plays Red, Ben (you) plays Yellow, Cal plays Magenta, Dee plays Blue. So the resources in the game are red, yellow, magenta and blue.

Live text of the cards these touch:
- **Melting Pot** (Yellow, round II): "+1 per color among your members, in colors you choose. +2 yellow."
- **Dilute** (Yellow, round I): "+2 yellow, then change up to 3 of rivals' resources one for one."
- **Big Dilute** (Yellow, round II): "+4 yellow, then change up to 6 of rivals' resources one for one."
- **Harvest** (Yellow, round I): "+3 in round I, +4 in round II, +5 in round III, in colors you choose."
- **Leaders' Tax** (Yellow, round I): "+1 yellow. Choose a color: each rival with more of it than you gives you 1 of it."
- **Makeover** (Yellow, round III): "+4 yellow, then rearrange your resources into any mix of colors."
- **Gather** (Red, round I): "+2 red in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III."
- **Matchmaker** (Yellow member, 4 yellow + 2 any, worth 1): "Whenever you recruit a member of a color none of your members have, +2 in colors you choose and +1 follower."
- **Tourists** (Yellow member, 4 yellow + 3 any, worth 1): "On Recruit: +1 follower per color you hold. Round Start: +1 follower per color you hold."
- **Mob** (Red member, 8 red + 4 any = 12, worth 4, counts as 4): "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have."
- **Patriarch** (Red member, 4 red + 2 any = 6, worth 1): "Game End: +1 follower per 2 members you have."
- **Triplets** (Red, 2 red + 1 any, worth 3, counts as 3): "Worth 3 followers."
- **Deprogrammers** (Red, 2 red + 1 any, worth 4, counts as 4): "Whenever you take a rival's member out of their cult, they lose 1 follower."
- **Poach** (Magenta, round III): "Recruit a rival's member you choose, for free. +1 magenta."
- **Accomplice** (Magenta member): "On Recruit: steal 3 resources."

Why Mob, from the Nov 3 bot games (Normal and Hard bots, 4 players, 10-card deck): red cults hold about 10 member cards that count as 21 members, and Mob pays about +10 at game end. Cults holding both Patriarch and Mob won 64 to 87% of games, where 25% is even. Your 34-member, +41 game-end playtest win is the same plan. Cheap cards that count as several members (Triplets, Deprogrammers) push up both counts.

## 1. Melting Pot: wording only (same rule)

The rule stays the same: count how many different colors your members have, gain that many resources in any colors, and gain 2 yellow.

- A. "+2 yellow, then +1 in a color you choose for each color among your members."
- B. "+2 yellow. Count the different colors of your members, and gain that many in colors you choose." **[pick]**
- C. "+2 yellow, plus 1 of any color for each color your members come in."

Why B: the live text puts "in colors you choose" after a count, so it reads like "+1 per color, in that color". B says the count first and the reward second, and puts the fixed +2 yellow at the front, the way Makeover and Dilute do. One played turn: you have Gardener (yellow), Triplets (red) and Regular (blue), so 3 colors. You get 2 yellow and 3 in colors you choose.

## 2. Dilute replacement (Yellow round I), with Big Dilute's round II version to match

Yellow, in Cory's words: "rewards going broad, i.e. having a combination of colors and being flexible. it also allows you to translate currencies." Also from Oct 30: "this is more currency manipulation that it is treating different members." None of these options changes rivals' resources.

### D1. New Blood **[pick]**
Prints (round I): "+2 yellow. This turn, if you recruit a member of a color none of your members have, +2 followers in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III."
Big version (round II, in Big Dilute's place): "+4 yellow. This turn, if you recruit a member of a color none of your members have, +5 followers."
Played: round I, Ben's second turn. Ben has Gardener (yellow) and holds 3 yellow and 2 red, and Triplets (2 red + 1 any) is in the shop. He takes New Blood (5 yellow, 2 red), recruits Triplets with 2 red and 1 yellow, and gets +2 followers. If he takes Harvest instead, he gets +3 in colors he chooses and no followers. With Leaders' Tax he gets +1 yellow and at most 3 of one color. So New Blood comes out about 1 ahead when Ben recruits a new color, and behind when he doesn't. In round III it gives +2 yellow and +4 followers, against Harvest's +5 in colors you choose and Red's Initiation (a free member costing 8 or less). It stays good for as long as the shop still has a color you lack.
Choices: none on the card. The choice is in the shop: you go for the color you lack instead of the best card.
Who feels it: a rival who sees the only blue member in the shop and recruits it first to deny you. With Matchmaker you get +2 in colors you choose and 1 more follower on the same recruit.
Intuitive: one sentence. The words are already on Gather and Matchmaker.
Fun: you're pushed to recruit the one color you lack, and rivals can see which one it is.
Interacts: with every other color's members (you need theirs), and with the shop race.
Powerful: up to +4 followers on a single recruit late in the game.
Engine or counter: engine. It pairs with Matchmaker, and with Melting Pot (each new color is +1 there later).
Its color: rewards members of different colors, the direction you gave on Oct 30. Risk, in one line: it repeats Matchmaker's trigger, so it isn't a fully new way in.

### D2. Full Set
Prints (round I): "+1 yellow. If you hold every color on the wheel, +3 followers in round I, +4 in round II, +5 in round III."
Big version (round II): "+3 yellow. If you hold every color on the wheel, +7 followers."
Played: round I. Ben holds 2 yellow, 1 red and 1 blue, and no magenta. He takes Harvest first (+3, magenta among them). On his next turn he takes Full Set: +1 yellow and +3 followers. If Cal's Accomplice ("On Recruit: steal 3 resources") takes Ben's only magenta first, Full Set gives him just 1 yellow. The best other space for him that turn is Harvest again (+3 in colors he chooses), so Full Set is about 1 ahead, and in round III +5 followers against +5 resources.
Choices: none on the card. The tension is keeping one of each color instead of spending it on a member.
Who feels it: rivals get a reason to block. Magenta steals and Leaders' Tax can knock a color you hold only 1 of down to 0.
Intuitive: the table can see every color on the wheel and every pile.
Fun: a set the whole table can see, and rivals can break it.
Interacts: implicitly, with Magenta's steals and Yellow's own Leaders' Tax.
Powerful: followers with no spending, and more each round.
Engine or counter: engine, with Harvest, Gardener ("+1 of each color you have none of") and Caravan.
Its color: going broad by holding every color. Risk, in one line: it reads only your own pile, and full sets were the old Yellow contest that nobody chased.

### D3. Wild Yellow
Prints (round I): "+2 yellow in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. This turn, your yellow pays for any color."
Big version (round II): "+5 yellow. This turn, your yellow pays for any color."
Played: round II. Ben holds 7 yellow, saved for the yellow contest, and Mentor & Protégés (6 red + 3 any) is in the shop. He takes Wild Yellow (10 yellow) and pays all 9 in yellow. That leaves 1 yellow, so he probably loses the yellow contest. Harvest would give him +4 in colors he chooses, and he still couldn't afford the card.
Choice: Ben's own, between holding yellow for the contest and spending it as any color now. Both sides are real.
Who feels it: the rival who was sure Ben couldn't afford that member, and whoever was second in the yellow contest.
Intuitive: one sentence, "this turn" as on Gather.
Fun: a sudden big recruit nobody saw coming.
Interacts: with the yellow contest and the shop.
Powerful: your whole yellow pile becomes any color.
Engine or counter: engine, with Leaders' Tax, Melting Pot and Dyer's yellow.
Its color: translating currencies at the moment you pay. Risk, in one line: it is close to a smaller Makeover, which you've refused for round I before, and close to "Haggle" ("too focused on member costs").

## 3. Mob rework (it stays a red go-deep payoff, and stops paying for going wide)

All three keep Mob at 12 (8 red + 4 any), worth 4, counting as 4 members.

### M1. Mob, per rival you out-red **[pick]**
Prints: "Round End: +2 followers per rival with fewer red members than you."
Played: round II. Ava (Red) has Triplets, Greeters and Recruiter, which count as 6 red members. Dee has 2 red members and Ben and Cal have none. Ava recruits Mob, and it counts as 4, so she has 10. At the end of round II she gets +6 (three rivals have fewer), and +6 again at the end of round III (game end): +12 plus Mob's worth of 4, so 16 for 12. Today, if Ava ends on 18 red members, Mob gives +18 plus 4, and going wider keeps adding. Under M1, going wider adds nothing once she out-reds every rival. The best other buy at that price is Mentor & Protégés (6 red + 3 any, worth 3: "On Recruit: recruit the cheapest member in the shop.").
Choices: none on the card. Rivals choose whether to buy red members (Triplets and Deprogrammers are cheap and count as 3 and 4) to tie Ava and cut her +2.
Who feels it: every rival, every round end, as a sting. A second red player at the table turns it into a race. Poach can take Mob itself.
Intuitive: one sentence, and the table counts red members.
Fun: a visible +2 per rival per round, and a reason to grab red members.
Interacts: with Red's Promote and Demote, Body Snatchers and Deprogrammers, and with Magenta's Poach.
Powerful: about 12 a game, paid twice, so you feel it during the game and not only at the end.
Engine or counter: a go-deep payoff with a ceiling. It counters the go-wide runaway.
Its color: Red moves members, and the mob is whoever has the most bodies. It's a new way in: it pays for out-numbering rivals rather than for raw size.
With Patriarch: Mob still counts as 4 members, so it adds +2 to Patriarch. But the two stop multiplying: a 34-member red cult gets at most 12 from Mob, where today it gets about 20 or more. Patriarch alone becomes the go-wide payoff. If wide cults still win, Patriarch is the next card to look at. Nothing is proposed for it here.

### M2. Mob raids at game end
Prints: "Game End: take a member costing 4 or less from each rival with fewer red members than you."
Played: game end, same table. Ava has 10 red members, and every rival has fewer. She takes Deprogrammers (worth 4) from Dee, Crew (worth 2) from Cal, and Regular (worth 1) from Ben. She gains 7 followers' worth of members and her rivals lose 7, and her Patriarch counts 3 more members (+1 or +2). Today, Mob gives her +10 or more.
Choice: Ava picks which member to take from each rival, and she'll always take the highest worth costing 4 or less. That's a pick, not a dilemma.
Who feels it: every rival, and they see it coming all game. That's a reason to hold red members, or to keep a cheap card you don't mind losing. Martyrs ("Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost.") answers it.
Intuitive: one sentence, but the last moment of the game gets busy (whose Game End goes first).
Fun: the closing swing the whole table watches.
Interacts: directly with every rival's cult, and with Martyrs and Deprogrammers.
Powerful: a swing of up to about 12 followers with a 4-player table.
Engine or counter: a payoff for going deep, and a counter to cults full of cheap bodies.
Its color: Red moves members. A mob takes people.
With Patriarch: the taken members count, so Patriarch adds about +1 or +2. It still stops Mob growing with width.
Risk, in one line: the victim loses members at game end, which may read as harsh. It's also a Game End effect that moves members, and nothing else does that.

### M3. Mob, capped
Prints: "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have, at most 10."
Played: game end. Ava has 18 red members. Today she gets +18. Under M3 she gets +10, plus Mob's worth of 4. Mob counts 4 itself, so 6 more red members fill the cap.
Choices: none.
Who feels it: nobody new. It plays exactly as today, smaller.
Intuitive: the live sentence plus three words.
Fun: same as today.
Interacts: no more than today.
Powerful: up to 14 for 12, a bit under today's average.
Engine or counter: still a go-deep payoff, with a ceiling.
Its color: same as today. Nothing new.
With Patriarch: the pair's Mob side stops at 10. Patriarch still grows with width.
This is the smallest change, for if you'd rather keep Mob as it plays now.
