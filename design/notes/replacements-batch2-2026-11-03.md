# Replacement batch 2 (8 cards), bot games only

Built on the six principles Cory said yes to (Nov 3). A second Claude wrote 18, cut to 10; the foreman cut two more: **Time Thieves** (takes a rival's minion for the round, against the rule that a skip never costs a minion) and **Mint** ("Whenever a White space gives you followers, gain half as much white": the space-buff shape Cory refused). Prices are fill-ins, set a little high, including the +1 every member pays.

**1. Red. Body Snatchers** replaces **Triplets** ("Worth 3 followers.").
Prints: [4 red + 2 any, worth 2] "Round Start: swap one of your members with a rival's member of the same cost."
Played: round I, 4 players, you're second in turn order with 5 red and 2 blue after **Gather** ("+2 red in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower (+2 in round II, +3 in round III)."). The best other card in the row is **Busker** ([2 green + 2 any] "On Recruit: +2 green. Round Start: +2 green."), about 6 green over the game. You take Body Snatchers. When round II starts, you swap your **Headhunter** (cost 6; you used its once-per-game swap in round I) for Ana's **Gossip** (cost 6, "Whenever a player’s turn is skipped, +1 follower."). When round III starts, you swap your **Cutpurse** (cost 3) for Ben's **Accomplice** (cost 3, "On Recruit: steal 3 resources."). Its On Recruit goes off for you, because the swap is your own recruit, as with **Promote**. That's two fresh cards for two spent ones, plus a steal of 3 (Claude's reading: a swap by your own Red card counts as your recruit).
Intuitive: one swap, same price on both sides, which anyone can check by looking at the two cards.
Fun: at every round start a rival loses the card they were building around and gets your spent one back.
Interacts: Red's **Loyalists** ("Your other members can’t be swapped.") is the wall against it. **Deprogrammers** ("Whenever you take a rival’s member out of their cult, they lose 1 follower.") makes each swap sting more. Every color's members with a once-per-game ability, which are worth taking before they're used.
Powerful: two free trades a game, chosen from everything every rival owns.
Engine or counter: engine with **Martyrs** ("Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost.": swap it away for its cost in followers and still get a card back) and with any On Recruit card a rival holds. It's also a quiet counter to a rival's single key card.
Its color: Red swaps members. This is a new way in: a swap that comes every round without spending a space.
Risk (Claude): when no rival holds a card at one of your costs, it does nothing that round.


**2. Green. Mirror Image** replaces **Wallflower** ("Whenever you copy an action, double the resources it gives you.").
Prints: [6 green + 3 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: choose a rival and a color; gain that color until you hold as much of it as they do."
Played: round III, 4 players. Cleo holds 14 white, saved for **Cash In** ("Turn all your white into followers. +1 white.") and the White contest. You hold 2 white. You use Mirror Image and gain 12 white. Now you tie her for White, so she gets +5 instead of +10, and the 12 white also buys 10 followers with your recruit. The best other buy when you recruited it in round II was **Fan Club** ([3 green + 3 any, worth 3] "On Recruit: +1 green per Green space with anyone’s minion."), about 4 green and 3 followers. Mirror Image is worth about 10 to 17.
Intuitive: "match their pile" is one look at one rival's pile.
Fun: whoever saved the biggest pile watches you copy it, and their contest lead is gone.
Interacts: with every hoard at the table: White's Cash In pile, Silver's piles from **Toast** ("+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III."), and any color held for its contest. **Agents** ("Rivals can’t copy your actions.") doesn't stop it, since a pile isn't an action.
Powerful: a relative amount, often 8 to 15 resources in round III.
Engine or counter: a counter to hoarding; an engine with Cash In if you copy someone's white.
Its color: Green copies. This is a new way in: copying a rival's pile, not an action.


**3. Magenta. Skimmers** replaces **Desperado** ("While you have the fewest followers (ties count), your steals take 2 more resources.").
Prints: [4 magenta + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival buys 10 followers, steal 3 of them."
Played: round III, 4 players. Ben has 11 resources and buys the 10-follower card: he keeps 7, you get 3. Ana does the same two turns later: +3 more. Rivals buy the 10 card about 2 or 3 times in a game, mostly in round III, so Skimmers gives 6 to 9 followers without a minion. The best other buy in the round II row is **Kingpin** ([2 magenta + 2 any] "Game End: +1 follower per magenta member you have."), usually 2 or 3.
Intuitive: "steal 3 of them" uses the follower cards and the word steal, both already on the table.
Fun: every big follower buy pays you, in front of everyone.
Interacts: White's and Blue's late follower buying, and **Regular** ([2 blue + 1 any] "Whenever you buy followers, +1 blue."). **Bodyguards** ("Rivals can’t steal from you.") blocks it, and **Snitch** ("Whenever a rival steals from you, they lose 1 follower.") costs you 1 each time.
Powerful: 3 followers each time, with no turn spent.
Engine or counter: a counter to the buy-followers plan; engine with **Robin Hood** ("Whenever you steal from the player with the most followers, +1 follower.") when the leader buys.
Its color: Magenta steals resources and members. This is a new way in: stealing followers as they're bought.


**4. Blue. Doorbusters** replaces **Shopaholics** ("You can recruit twice each turn.").
Prints: [5 blue + 3 any, worth 2] "Round Start: recruit once, before anyone places."
Played: round I, 4 players. You buy Doorbusters for 8 over **Proselytizers** ([4 red + 4 any] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower."). When round II starts, the fresh 7-card row is dealt, and before anyone places you recruit **Night Owls** ([7 purple + 1 any] "From round II: after your last minion, +1 minion, placed after everyone else’s."), the card two rivals were saving for. When round III starts you buy 10 followers. That's two extra recruits, each the first pick of a new row.
Intuitive: one extra recruit, at a time everyone can see.
Fun: the best card in each new row can be gone before the first minion goes down.
Interacts: with every color's best round II and round III members. It feeds **Proselytizers** and **Connoisseurs** ("Whenever you recruit a blue member, +1 blue."). Rivals answer by buying it first, or with Red's **Promote** ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red.").
Powerful: two extra recruits, the thing that's scarce late, with first choice each time.
Engine or counter: engine for any recruit-count card; it also undercuts a rival who plans around the next row.
Its color: Blue recruits more. This is a new way in: recruiting before the round begins, from the fresh row.


**5. Yellow. Night Market** replaces **Ringleader** ("On Recruit: change 1 of each rival's resources one for one. +1 follower per rival changed.").
Prints: [4 yellow + 2 any, worth 1] "Round End: before contests are scored, swap up to 3 of your resources for other colors, one for one."
Played: round II end, 4 players. You hold 7 blue and 2 purple. Ben holds 4 blue and Ana holds 4 purple. Before scoring, you swap 3 blue for purple: you tie Ben in Blue (+3 each) and win Purple with 5 (+6). That's +9 instead of +6 for Blue alone, and Ana loses her +6. In round III the same swap moves +10 contests. The best other buy in the row is **Tourists** ([4 yellow + 3 any] "On Recruit: +1 follower per color you hold. Round Start: +1 follower per color you hold.").
Intuitive: the same "swap up to 3 for other colors" as **Trade Seeds** ("+2 yellow, then swap up to 3 of your resources for other colors."), done just before scoring.
Fun: no contest is safe until scoring is over, and the table watches you move resources at the last moment.
Interacts: every color's contest. It's the round-end answer to **Tinker** ("Round Start: swap up to 3 of your resources one for one."), and **Harvest** ("+3 in round I, +4 in round II, +5 in round III, in colors you choose.") feeds it.
Powerful: it can turn one contest into two, three times a game.
Engine or counter: a counter to anyone leading a contest by 1 or 2; an engine for a broad pile.
Its color: Yellow goes broad and translates colors. This is a new way in: translating at the moment the contests are counted.


**6. Silver. Fundraisers** replaces **Debutantes** ("On Use, once per game: double your silver. Each rival +2 silver.").
Prints: [6 silver + 3 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per game: each rival +2 silver, then +1 follower per 2 silver rivals hold in all."
Played: round III, 4 players. After your **Toast** ("+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III."), rivals hold 5, 3 and 4 silver. Fundraisers gives each +2 (7, 5 and 6 = 18), and you gain 9 followers. The best other buy in the round II row is **High Society** ([5 silver + 3 any, worth 2] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more silver than you.").
Intuitive: give, then count all the silver rivals hold, two to a follower.
Fun: rivals can spend their silver down first (for example on **Polish**: "Turn 2 of your silver into 4 of any colors."), so they watch your hand.
Interacts: Silver's gifts (Toast, **Party Gifts**: "Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver you give."), **Caterer** ("Whenever silver is gifted, +1 silver."). Magenta's Crime Wave on silver lowers your count.
Powerful: about 8 to 10 followers in round III.
Engine or counter: engine with every silver gift you've given.
Its color: Silver gives, and the giving comes back. This is a new way in: the gifts become your score all at once.


**7. Silver. Hush Money** replaces **Smooth Talkers** ("Your silver pays for any color.").
Prints: [4 silver + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival steals from you, you may give them 2 silver instead."
Played: round II, 4 players. Cleo takes **Crime Wave** ("Steal half of one rival's pile of one color, rounded down. +1 magenta.") on your 8 blue. That would take 4 and lose you Blue to Ben's 6. You give her 2 silver instead, keep 8 blue and win Blue (+6). When **Help Yourself** ("Steal 3 resources in round I, 4 in round II, 5 in round III, split among rivals as you choose.") takes just 1 from you, you let it go. The best other buy in the row is **Caterer** ([3 silver + 2 any] "Whenever silver is gifted, +1 silver.").
Intuitive: pay them off; the choice is yours each time.
Fun: the thief gets something, but never the thing they came for.
Interacts: every Magenta steal, including the "steal it" side of **Shake Down** ("Choose a rival. They choose one of their members costing 5 or less, then pay you its cost or you steal it. +1 magenta."), where 2 silver saves a member. Caterer pays on the gift.
Powerful: it turns a 4-to-7 resource Crime Wave into a 2-silver payment.
Engine or counter: a counter to Magenta, softer than **Bodyguards** ("Rivals can’t steal from you.").
Its color: Silver gives. This is a new way in: giving as a defense.
You choose: let a small steal through, or pay 2 silver for a big one. Each side wins sometimes.


**8. Grey. Spoilsports** replaces **Scouts** ("On Use, once per round: send any shop members to the bottom of the pile, then refill.").
Prints: [6 any, worth 1] "Round End: choose a contest you don't lead; nobody wins it this round."
Played: round II end, 4 players. Ana leads White with 9 and is 2 followers ahead of you. You lead Blue. You spoil White: Ana gets nothing instead of +6, and you're ahead. In round III you spoil a +10. The best other buy in the row is **Cheerleader** ([4 any] "Whenever you win a contest, +1 follower.").
Intuitive: one choice at scoring; the rim of the spoiled contest pays nobody.
Fun: anyone leading a contest has to wonder whether you'll spoil theirs, and the table watches you pick.
Interacts: every color's contest, **Tiebreaker** ("You win every contest tie you’re in."), and the hold-or-spend part of the game.
Powerful: takes 4, 6 and 10 followers from the rivals you're chasing.
Engine or counter: a counter to the leader and to hoarding.
Its color: grey has no color; it plays with every color's contest.
You choose: which rival to hurt. That depends on who you're chasing, so there's no fixed best pick.

## Cut earlier (8)
- **Red. Press Gang** for Recruit Bonus, "Once per round, when a rival recruits a second member in one turn, recruit the cheapest shop member for free.": strong, but it means counting each rival's recruits within a turn, and Red already has Body Snatchers here.
- **Green. Backup Dancers** for Muse, "On Recruit and Round Start: do the Round Start of one rival's member.": does nothing at a table where no rival holds a Round Start card.
- **Magenta. Grand Larceny** for Double Agent, "On Use, once per game: steal half of each rival's pile of one color, rounded down.": just a bigger Crime Wave, so not a new way into Magenta.
- **Blue. Impulse Buyers** for Window Shopper, "Whenever you send shop members to the bottom of the pile, you may recruit one of the members that refill the row, paying its cost.": no rival feels it, and with Trendsetters it repeats until you run out of resources.
- **Purple. Queue Jumpers** for Timekeepers, "Round Start: you may swap places in this round's turn order with a rival.": the leader uses it to take the trailing player's first seat, which undoes the catch-up rule.
- **White. Vault Keepers** for Recluse, "Rivals can't steal or change your white.": a narrow Bodyguards nobody reacts to. Recluse was also one Cory loved.
- **Yellow. Shape-Shifters** for Gardener, "Whenever a rival steals your resources, you choose which colors they take.": the victim picks a color they hold 1 of, so Crime Wave steals nothing.
- **Grey. Bookies** for Party Crasher, "On Recruit and Round Start: put your marker on a contest. Round End: +4 followers if you win or share it, −2 if you don't.": you just name the contest you already lead, so it mostly pays the leader.
