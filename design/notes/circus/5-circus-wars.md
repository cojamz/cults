# Pass 5: start from what really happened, the Circus Wars

Cory: "4 different entities are vying for different types of acts? how?" and who performs is unclear.

The fix is a premise where rival shows really did fight over the same things. That happened: the **American circus wars of the 1880s**. Barnum, Forepaugh and others raced to the same towns, poached each other's stars, pasted over each other's posters, and printed smear sheets about each other. In the end one show bought out the rest.

- **Who you are:** the owner of a travelling circus. Rival shows are on the same route this season.
- **What you're trying to do:** pack your tent. Every town has only so many people, and every rival wants them.
- **What winning makes you:** the show that buys out the others, "the Greatest Show on Earth."
- **Rounds:** three stops on the route, each bigger than the last (a small town, a city, then the big city).
- **Minions = advance men.** They aren't performers. They go into town ahead of the show and grab what's there: the best poster walls, the fairground lot, the newspaper, the mayor's favor. One spot each, first come first served. That's why only one minion fits a space.
- **Members = your acts and crew.** The lion tamer, the strongman, the press agent. They're the ones who perform, and their abilities are their tricks. This answers who performs.
- **Followers = your crowd** (tickets sold). A member counts 1 crowd because a star draws their own fans.
- **Colors = the kinds of dirty tricks and business a show runs, not act types:**
  - Red recruits and swaps members → **poaching stars** from rival shows.
  - Magenta steals → **billposting raids**: paste over their posters and steal their crowd.
  - Purple bends turns → **racing to town first** (or delaying a rival's train).
  - Silver gives gifts that pay back → **free passes** to the mayor and the press.
  - White turns things into crowd → **the box office**.
- **Contests** = winning the town's talk in that department: whoever has the most posters up, the most stars, and so on.

Still open after the premise: what each resource is (my lean: something you can hold, like poster stock, cash and contracts) and what the board looks like.
