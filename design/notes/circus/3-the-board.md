# Pass 3: why are the actions where they are?

Cory: the tent-rings analog doesn't land; why would actions sit in rings?

What a space has to explain: (a) you send one person there, (b) only one person can take it per round, (c) you come back with something, (d) more spaces open each round, (e) each color owns a slice.

## A. The lot map (a bullseye of the circus grounds)
- The board is the circus lot from above: the Big Top in the middle, the midway around it, the town at the edge.
- A space is a **job at a spot**: put up posters in town, run the shooting gallery on the midway, feed the cats behind the tent. One hand per job per night, and the job pays out.
- Rings open outside-in because a run starts in town drumming up business, and only later do you get work in the tent itself.
- A color's slice is its **department's patch** of the lot: the menagerie, clown alley, the ticket wagon.
- Weak spot: spaces near each other are just neighbours, so the circle shape adds nothing.

## B. The night's program (the wheel is the show's running order)
- The wheel is the bill for the night: each space is one **number in the show** (the knife act, the clown car, the trapeze). Placing a hand = you run that number tonight; only one troupe can run it.
- Rings: new numbers get added to the bill each night (opening night is short; closing night is the full show).
- A color's slice is one **kind of act** on the bill.
- Weak spot: "the knife act gives you 2 red" needs a reason (the crowd's reaction? the tips?).

## C. Drop the wheel: a row of wagons
- The circus is a parade of wagons, one per color (cat wagon, clown wagon, ticket wagon…). Each wagon has slots that are jobs; more open each night.
- Most literal and tabletop-friendly, but you lose the wheel, which is the game's look.

Lean: **A**, because "send a hand to a job" is the most natural worker-placement story, and a lot from above is a picture anyone reads.
