# Creditor: replacing "Game End: each rival loses 1 follower per 3 silver they hold."

Cory, Nov 4: nobody holds spare silver at game end; they buy followers with it. So the new text has to act while silver is in play.
Slot: Silver, round I member, 3 silver + 2 any, worth 2 followers. It counts as 1 member, so the name is singular: **Creditor**.

## 1. Creditor [pick]
"On Use, once per round: a rival you choose gives you 2 followers, then gains 3 silver."
- One played turn, round II, 4 players: Ana leads with 31 followers. You use Creditor on Ana, so you gain 2 followers and Ana loses 2. Ana gains 3 silver, which buys her about 3 followers later, so she ends about 1 ahead. You end 2 ahead, and over the three rounds that is 6. A Toast in round I gives +3 silver, and every rival gets +1.
- The choice: you choose the rival. Usually that is the leader. If the leader holds the most silver, you can pick someone else so they don't win the silver contest. Either way it is a real call.
- Who feels it: the rival you pick loses 2 followers on the spot, which stings, and the 3 silver softens it. Players holding **High Society** ("Game End: +2 followers per rival with more silver than you.") or **Hostesses** ("On Recruit and Round Start: give each rival 1 silver. +1 follower per rival who then holds more silver than you.") want rivals rich in silver, and this card does that.
- Not refused: it has the same shape as **Potluck** ("+2 silver. Each rival gives you 1 resource of their choice, then gains 2 silver."), which Cory liked. No ledger entry gives followers for silver this way.
- Principles: intuitive (one sentence, done at once); fun (you choose your target); interacts (a rival, and the silver-rich members); feels powerful (it takes followers straight from the leader); builds an engine (silver for High Society and Hostesses); new way into the theme (Silver gives, but here the gift is the repayment).

## 2. Creditor
"Whenever a rival recruits paying with silver, +1 follower."
- One played turn, round II: you took **Toast** ("+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III."), so each rival got +2 silver. Ben then buys the 5-follower card with 3 silver and 2 blue, so you gain 1 follower. Each recruit paid with silver earns you 1, so a game gives you about 6 to 10.
- The choice: each rival can avoid paying silver, but then the silver sits unused, and only the silver contest pays for holding it. Most of the time they pay and you earn.
- Who feels it: every rival, a little, on every buy. **Party Gifts** ("Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver you give.") and **Largesse** ("Give rivals up to 10 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +1 follower per silver you give.") pay you twice: once when you give the silver and again when rivals spend it.
- Not refused: Collector ("Whenever a rival recruits paying silver, +1 silver.") had no verdict. This version pays followers instead.
- Principles: intuitive; fun (each gift pays you back later); interacts (every rival's buys); feels powerful (it grows with every silver you give); builds an engine (with Toast, Party Gifts and Largesse); new way into the theme (what you give comes back to you).

Cut on paper: "Round End: each rival with more silver than you gives you 1 of it" (taking resources is Magenta's verb). An IOU loan repaid at game end (it needs a piece the game doesn't have).
