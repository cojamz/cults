# Replacement batch 3 (8 cards), bot games only

Built on the six principles Cory said yes to (Nov 3), from a draft of 16. Cut: **Cutthroats** (it sits close to the "member that upgrades a shared space" shape Cory refused), **Changelings** (Barterers' swap, much bigger), **Hecklers** (the same skip as Hold Up and Bouncers, aimed at the leader: not a new way in, and the sting is mild), **Ascetics** (nobody at the table reacts, and it repeats Recluse), **All-Nighters** (a bigger Crunch Time that takes three of the four round III centers), **Cat Burglars** (too close to Racketeers), **Shadows** (it needs Purple at the table) and **Dye Works** (it gives its owner little and removes Yellow's only counter). **Pacers** takes the eighth seat, replacing **Shift Crew**. Prices are fill-ins, set a little high, including the +1 every member pays. A member whose cost before that +1 is 3 or less joins in round I; the rest join in round II.

**1. White. Evictors** replaces **Hoarder** ("On Use, once per game: turn all your resources into white.").
Prints: [5 white + 3 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: cast out a rival's member to the bottom of the pile. You and that rival each gain white equal to its cost."
Played: round III, 4 players. Ana's engine is **Proselytizers** ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.", cost 8), with about 5 recruits left in her game. You cast it out. You gain 8 white, which becomes 8 followers through **Cash In** ("Turn all your white into followers. +1 white.") or wins the White contest (+10). Ana loses its 2 worth and about 5 future followers, and gets 8 white that only turns into followers if she spends round III recruits on it, the scarce thing. The best other buy in the round II row is **Shopkeeper** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 white. Round Start: +2 white."), about 4 to 6 white. Evictors is about +8 white for you and about a 7-follower hit to Ana, so roughly +2 to +4 white over Shopkeeper plus the hit.
Intuitive: one sentence. Cast out and the bottom of the pile are White's own words, from **Farewell** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain followers equal to its cost. +1 white.") and **Estate Sale** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain white equal to its cost. +1 white.").
Fun: you choose whose card goes and which one. A pricey card pays both of you more; a cheap key card pays the rival little. The table watches you pick.
Interacts: with every rival's engine card. Casting out a rival's **Martyrs** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 3] "Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost.") gives them its cost in followers as well, so you avoid it. With **Patroness** ([6 blue + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever shop members go to the bottom of the pile, +1 follower.") and **Scavengers** (batch 1: [4 blue + 2 any] "Whenever a rival sends shop members to the bottom of the pile, you may recruit one of them, paying its cost.") on the other side.
Powerful: about 6 to 10 white from one use, and a rival's best card gone.
Engine or counter: counter to a rival's single key card. Engine with Cash In and the White contest.
Its color: White casts out its own members for followers or white. This is a new way in: casting out a rival's.
Risk (Claude): close to Blue's **Doormen** ([3 blue + 5 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per round: send a rival's member to the bottom of the pile. Give them a shop member; it can cost up to 2 less, or any amount more."), but the rival gets white back instead of a replacement member. Used in round II on a key card, it is a hard sting (Oct 15: losing a member before round III should stay mild). If that's too hard, cap it at "costing 6 or less".
Build (Claude's reading): any rival's member can be cast out; it isn't a steal or a swap, so Bodyguards and Loyalists don't stop it. Its cost is its printed price. Martyrs pays its owner as it leaves.


**2. Purple. Interrupters** replaces **Courier** ("Whenever you take two turns in a row, +1 of each other color.").
Prints: [4 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival takes two turns in a row, you may skip the second."
Played: round II, 4 players. Turn order is you, Cleo, Ana, Ben, so Ben places twice in a row where the order turns around. You skip his second turn: play goes on to Ana, and Ben places that minion later in the round, after the spaces he wanted are gone. Later Cleo takes **Crunch Time** ("+2 purple. Your next two turns come right after this one.") to take **Promote** ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red.") and **Recruit Twice** ("Recruit twice this turn. +3 blue.") back to back. You skip her first one, and Ben takes Recruit Twice before she gets there. This happens 2 to 4 times a round. With **Gossip** ([4 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a player's turn is skipped, +1 follower.") each skip is also +1 follower. The best other buy in the row is **Closer** ([3 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "When you place the last minion of the round: +4 purple."), about 4 purple a round when you land it. Interrupters costs a rival a good space 2 or 3 times a round (about 3 to 5 followers' worth each time), and pays you about 6 followers a game alongside Gossip.
Intuitive: everyone can see when a player goes twice in a row. A skip is already a word on the cards.
Fun: the player at the turnaround and the Crunch Time player both watch you decide.
Interacts: Purple's **Right Away** ("+1 purple (+2 in round II, +3 in round III). Your next turn comes right after this one."), Crunch Time, and **Hot Streak** ([3 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: +3 followers per turn in a row you've taken just before this one."), whose streak you can break. **Early Birds** ([3 purple, worth 2] "Your turns can't be skipped.") is the wall.
Powerful: you decide where 2 to 4 rival turns land every round, with no minion spent.
Engine or counter: counter to Purple's two-in-a-row plan, and quietly to whoever sits at the turnaround, often the leader, who goes last from round II on. Engine with Gossip.
Its color: Purple bends turns. This is a new way in: bending rivals' turns as they happen, not only your own.
You choose: skip or not. A skip sometimes helps them: a rival with Closer wants to place the round's last minion. So it is a real choice.
Risk (Claude): the turnaround makes it fire often. If it's too strong, "once per round" fixes that.
Build (Claude's reading): the skip works like Hold Up's: that minion places after the round's other turns, Early Birds stops it, and Gossip pays. It's offered only while another player still has a turn left that round (with none, a skip would move nothing). Two players holding it are asked in seat order.


**3. Magenta. Kidnappers** replaces **Kingpin** ("Game End: +1 follower per magenta member you have.").
Prints: [4 magenta + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit and Round Start: steal the cheapest member of the rival with the most members."
Played: round II, 4 players. Ben (Blue) has 6 members. When you recruit Kidnappers, you take his cheapest, **Cutpurse** ([2 magenta + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources. Round Start: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources."), and from now on its Round Start steals for you. When round III starts, Ana leads with 8 members, and you take her **Recruit Bonus** ([2 red + 1 any, worth 2] "Your On Recruits also give +1 of that member's color."). That's two cards worth 3 followers between them, plus their abilities, and the two biggest cults are each one card smaller. The best other buy in the row is **Extortionist** ([5 magenta + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: steal 2 resources from the rival with the most resources. Round Start: steal 2 resources from the rival with the most resources."), about 4 resources. Kidnappers is about +3 to +5 for you and −3 to −5 for the rivals hit.
Intuitive: "the most members" is a count everyone can see, and "cheapest" is printed on every card.
Fun: the biggest cult feels it every round. It's a mild sting, since the cheapest card goes.
Interacts: **Bodyguards** ([6 magenta + 2 any, worth 2] "Rivals can't steal from you.") blocks it, giving a card bots rarely take a job. **Turncoats** (batch 1: [3 red + 1 any] "Whenever a rival takes one of your members into their cult, recruit a shop member costing up to its cost, for free.") answers it. **Deprogrammers** ([2 red + 1 any, worth 4] "Whenever you take a rival's member out of their cult, they lose 1 follower.") adds a sting. It also helps the "fewest members" side of White's **Recluse** ([3 white + 1 any] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you.").
Powerful: two free members a game, chosen by a count, not by a space.
Engine or counter: counter to the recruit-a-lot plan (Blue, Red). Engine with Deprogrammers.
Its color: Magenta steals resources and members. This is a new way in: stealing members from the biggest cult on a clock.
Risk (Claude): if the cheapest card is **Old Maid** ([5 any, worth 1] "Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers."), you take it and lose 5. That's funny once, but the table should know.
Build (Claude's reading): "the most members" counts printed member counts (HR counts as 4). A tie for the most members, or for the cheapest, is your pick. If the rival with the most members can't be stolen from (Bodyguards, Lie Low), nothing is taken. The stolen member counts as your recruit, so its On Recruit goes off for you, as with Heist.


**4. Blue. Completists** replaces **Patroness** ("Whenever shop members go to the bottom of the pile, +1 follower.").
Prints: [6 blue + 3 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per game: recruit every shop member of one color, paying their costs."
Played: round III, 4 players, 8 members face up. Three are red: Proselytizers ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.", 8), **Recruit Bonus** ([2 red + 1 any, worth 2] "Your On Recruits also give +1 of that member's color.", 3) and **Loyalists** ([3 red + 2 any, worth 3] "Your other members can't be swapped.", 5). You pay 16 and take all three, on top of your turn's normal recruit. With **Mob** ([8 red + 4 any, worth 4] "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have.") that's +3 more, and **HR** ([10 any, worth 4] "Counts as 4 members. Game End: +2 followers per color you have 3 members of.") pays +2 for the set. That's 7 worth, +3 to +5 from scoring, and Proselytizers' later recruits: about 12. Spending the same 16 on followers gives 10 followers and needs two recruits. The best other buy in the round II row is **Snobs** ([4 blue + 2 any, worth 2] "Game End: +1 follower per blue member you have."), about 4. Difference: about +5 to +8 when one color stacks up in the row.
Intuitive: one sentence. "One color" is printed on every card.
Fun: whoever was saving for one of those cards loses it in one move. A rival can clear that color first with **Out of Style** ("Send every shop member of one color to the bottom of the pile, then refill. +1 blue per member sent. +1 blue.").
Interacts: every color's members, every "per [color] member" scorer (Mob, Snobs, Kingpin, Chronicler, Timekeepers), HR, and **Connoisseurs** ([2 blue + 2 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit a blue member, +1 blue.").
Powerful: two or three extra recruits in round III, when the recruit is scarce.
Engine or counter: engine for color scorers and recruit counters. It also counters a rival waiting on one color.
Its color: Blue works the shop and recruits more. This is a new way in: buying out a color.
Risk (Claude): a color with one card in the row makes it a plain recruit, so the owner waits for a stack. Claude's reading, like Walk-In: these recruits don't use up your turn's recruit.
Build (Claude's reading): the members are the ones face up when you use it, not the ones the row refills with. Each is paid at its price the usual way. One you can't pay for when its turn comes stays in the shop. Grey members have no color.


**5. Green. Impersonators** replaces **Muse** ("Whenever a rival copies an action, +1 green.").
Prints: [4 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit and Round Start: choose a rival's member. Until you choose again, this member has its ability."
Played: round II, 4 players. You recruit it and choose Ben's Proselytizers ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower."). Your 3 remaining round II recruits give +3. When round III starts you switch to Cleo's **Mob** ([8 red + 4 any, worth 4] "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have.") with your 4 red members, for +4 at game end, or you keep Proselytizers for 5 recruits, for +5. That's about 8 followers. The best other buy in the row is **Busker** ([2 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 green. Round Start: +2 green."), about 4 green. Difference: about +4 to +6, more when a rival holds a strong engine card.
Intuitive: lay your card next to the one you're copying. It reads like **Doppelganger** ([3 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: this member becomes a copy of another of your members.") pointed at a rival.
Fun: the rival sees their best card working for you too. It's a light sting: nobody loses anything.
Interacts: every rival member, quietly, the way copying should. **Agents** ([2 green + 1 any, worth 2] "Rivals can't copy your actions.") doesn't stop it, since it copies a member, not an action.
Powerful: each round, the best engine card at the table is yours too.
Engine or counter: engine. It copies whatever fits your cult: Mob for a red cult, Snobs for a blue one.
Its color: Green copies actions, its own On Recruits (**Greatest Hits**: "Copy the On Recruit of each of your members, in any order. +1 green.") and its own members (Doppelganger). This is a new way in: copying a rival's member.
Risk (Claude): copying an On Recruit does nothing (nothing is being recruited). Copying an "On Use, once per game" card raises the question of whether choosing again gives a fresh use. Claude's reading: no, Impersonators gets one use a game.
Build (Claude's reading): it gets the chosen card's ability only, never its worth or member count. At round start the ability it holds does its own Round Start first, then you choose again, and the new card's Round Start waits for the next round. It gets one once-per-game use a game, and one once-per-round use a round, across every card it copies. Another Impersonators, or a Doppelganger or Impostors that isn't copying anything, has no ability to lend, so it isn't offered.


**6. Red. Missionaries** replaces **Patriarch** ("Game End: +1 follower per 2 members you have.").
Prints: [6 red + 3 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per game: give one of your members to each rival, then recruit a shop member costing up to 2 more for each one given, for free."
Played: round III, 4 players. You give your used Cutpurse ([2 magenta + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources. Round Start: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources.", 3) to Ben, Recruit Bonus ([2 red + 1 any, worth 2] "Your On Recruits also give +1 of that member's color.", 3) to Ana, and Busker ([2 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 green. Round Start: +2 green.", 4) to Cleo. Then you recruit three shop members costing up to 5, 5 and 6 for free, such as Loyalists ([3 red + 2 any, worth 3] "Your other members can't be swapped.") and two worth 2 each. You gave away 4 worth and took 7, plus three On Recruits, three recruits in round III and no space used. Each rival gets a free card. The best other buy in the row is **Proselytizers** ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower."), about +4 to +5 for the rest of the game. Missionaries is about +6 to +10.
Intuitive: give one to each rival, take one better for each. It's Promote's rule ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red."), done three times at once.
Fun: every rival gets a gift, and you choose who gets what. Your **Old Maid** ([5 any, worth 1] "Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers.") goes to the leader, who loses 5.
Interacts: Red's **Martyrs** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 3] "Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost.") given away pays you its cost. **Mob** ([8 red + 4 any, worth 4] "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have.") counts the new cards. The extra card hurts a rival holding White's **Recluse** ([3 white + 1 any] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you.").
Powerful: three free upgrades in one turn.
Engine or counter: engine, built around spent once-a-game cards and Martyrs.
Its color: Red moves members. This is a new way in: sending many of yours out at once.
Risk (Claude): Red is already the strongest color in bot games (+18 over an even share in the Nov 3 run), so this is priced high. Each rival's gift is real: a worth-2 card is +2 to them.
Build (Claude's reading): rivals get a member in seat order, starting on your left, as long as you have members; any member can go, Missionaries itself too. A member you give arrives in their cult; it isn't their recruit, so Old Maid's −5 lands on them and Martyrs pays you. Then you get one free recruit per member given, in any order, each costing up to 2 more than one of the members given. A recruit nothing in the shop fits is lost.


**7. Silver. Creditors** replaces **Collector** ("Whenever a rival recruits paying silver, +1 silver.").
Prints: [4 silver + 2 any, worth 2] "Game End: each rival loses 1 follower per 3 silver they hold."
Played: game end, 4 players. Your **Toast** ("+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III.") and **Largesse** ("Give rivals up to 10 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +1 follower per silver you give.") left Ben with 9 silver, Ana with 7 and Cleo with 4. Ben held his 9 to win the round III Silver contest (+10). They lose 3, 2 and 1. The best other buy in the round II row is **High Society** ([5 silver + 3 any, worth 2] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more silver than you."), about +4 to +6 for you. Creditors takes about 4 to 6 from three rivals, which moves you further in a 4-player race. Holding both, the same silver pays you twice.
Intuitive: one count at the end, by 3s.
Fun: every gift you hand out now has a price at the end. The Silver contest leader has to weigh holding for +10 against losing followers after scoring.
Interacts: all of Silver's giving, and **Party Gifts** ("Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver you give.") in particular. **Hostesses** (batch 1: [4 silver + 2 any] "On Recruit and Round Start: give each rival 1 silver. +1 follower per rival who then holds more silver than you.") pushes the same way.
Powerful: a hit on every rival at once, sized by how generous you were.
Engine or counter: engine with every gift you give, and with High Society.
Its color: Silver gives gifts that pay you back. This is a new way in: a gift that costs the person who got it.
You choose: nothing. The rivals choose to spend silver in their follower buys or keep it for the contest, and both happen at real tables.
Risk (Claude): followers can be bought with any colors, so careful rivals spend silver first. It bites hardest on the Silver contest leader and on whoever ends with leftovers.
Build (Claude's reading): it's counted after the other Game End cards, and nobody goes below 0 followers. It isn't a steal, so nothing blocks it.


**8. Purple. Pacers** replaces **Shift Crew** ("Worth 4 followers.").
Prints: [6 purple + 3 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per round: move one of your minions to an open space and do that action."
Played: round III, 4 players. Your first minion took **Gather** ("+2 red in round I, +3 in round II, +4 in round III. This turn, if you recruit a member, +1 follower (+2 in round II, +3 in round III)."). Late in the round, Recruit Twice ("Recruit twice this turn. +3 blue.") is still open. You move that minion there and recruit twice more. Gather opens again, and Cleo takes it. That's one more action each round with no minion spent, worth about 3 to 6 by round III, or about 8 a game. The best other buy in the row is **Night Owls** ([7 purple + 1 any, worth 2] "From round II: after your last minion, +1 minion, placed after everyone else's."): one more placement each round and its recruit, about 10. Pacers is about the same or a little less, and it hands a space back to the table.
Intuitive: pick up a minion and put it down somewhere else.
Fun: you choose which space to give back. A space you free is a gift to the next player.
Interacts: every space on the board. With **Echo** ("Copy the previous action. +1 green.") the moved minion's new action can be copied.
Powerful: an extra action every round.
Engine or counter: engine with any space that pays more late.
Its color: Purple bends turns. This is a new way in: using one minion twice.
Risk (Claude): there are only 4 spare spaces in rounds II and III, so late in a round the open spaces are the weak ones. That keeps it in check.
Build (Claude's reading): any of your minions on the board can move, one that joined a rival's space too. The old space's action stays done and that space opens. The move comes with no recruit: it's an action, not a turn.
