# Cults: a strategy primer for expert players

Advice, not rules; the rulebook wins. It does not decide the game's balance.

## Where followers come from (think in these units)
1. Contest prizes. Each color on the table has a contest scored at every round's end: the leader of that color (most held, or what its rim says) gets +4 (round I), +6 (round II), +10 (round III), tied leaders each get half, rounded up. In a "most" contest you need at least 1 of what's counted; in a "fewest" contest 0 counts, so everyone at 0 ties. Four colors means 20 followers per color over a game, about 80 on the table. In recent games contests made up a quarter to a half of the winner's followers; buying followers and members made up the rest.
2. Buying followers: 1, 5 or 10 resources for the same number of followers, one recruit each. A resource is worth a follower, but only when you have a recruit to turn it into one, and spending it can lose you a lead.
3. Members: printed followers, plus what the ability earns over the rounds left, plus what holding it does in contests (many contests count members, member colors or members of one color), against the recruit and resources it cost. Bought early, a member that pays every round can return several times its price; bought on the last turn, it is mostly its printed followers. Count both lines honestly before defaulting to buying followers.
4. Space bonuses and member abilities that hand out followers directly.

## The core tension
Holding a color wins contests; spending it buys followers and members. Every round you decide, color by color, which contests you are really fighting for and which resources are free to convert. A color you cannot win is just money; a color you can win is worth +4/+6/+10, so protect it, and convert the others. Before every turn, write down the lead in each color (yours, the runner-up, the gap) and ask what each option does to those gaps for you and for each rival, including what a rival can still do after your move (spaces left, steals, copies).

## Tempo
- You have 3, 4, 5 minions, so about 12 placements and 12 recruits a game, plus extra turns. A placement that gains little still denies a space; a recruit you skip is gone.
- Last-placed minions react to everything: the last turns of a round are when you can still take a lead or break one. Early turns are for the spaces everyone wants.
- From round II, fewest followers goes first. Being slightly behind costs little and buys first pick; the snake gives the two ends double turns.
- Round III is a cliff: the prize is +10 per color. Plan it from the start of round II: which two or three colors can you hold to the last turn, and what do you convert around them? Resources left at the end are worth nothing.

## Reading rivals
Rivals' piles are visible. Count what each can still reach this round (open spaces for their color, steals, copies) and assume they will take the best one. A lead of 2 against a rival with two open spaces is not safe. Theft and member steals (Magenta, some guests) punish big piles and cheap bodies; protect what you plan to keep.

## Members and cloaks
- Engines that pay every round or every recruit beat flat bodies if you will actually use them; check how many turns are left.
- An ability that needs a condition (a color of member, a rival doing something) is worth what the table will realistically trigger, not what the text promises.
- Cloaks are free, all game, and come with 1 of their color: pick the one that matches the plan you will really run, and note what your pick leaves the others.
- Copy and extra-turn effects are strongest when they repeat a big gain; look at what is on the board before you pick them.

## Rules that change what a space is worth (added Nov 11 after AI players misplayed them)
Each of these is a plain reading of the rulebook; past players got it wrong and lost value.
- **Your recruit is one thing a turn.** One recruit buys one member or one batch of followers (1, 5 or 10). So 7 white needs three recruits (5, 1, 1) to become followers, and every one of those recruits is then not a member.
- **A space that turns resources into followers does not use your recruit** (Cash In: "Turn all your white into followers"). In round III with a big pile of one color, it saves you two or three recruits, which you can spend on members or on another color's followers that same turn and the turns after. Compare it with the recruits it saves, not with "buying already does that".
- **Moving your turns up is worth the spaces you get before rivals.** Crunch Time ("Your next two turns come right after this one") with your first minion of a round gives you three placements and three recruits in a row before anyone answers: three of the best open spaces, and a lead in a contest nobody can react to until your run ends. On your last minion it does nothing; on your second-to-last it moves one turn. Right Away is the one-turn version. Count which spaces would be gone by your next turn if you waited.
- **A space's +1 of its color goes only to its first taker, once a game** (the view marks spaces whose +1 is still there). Taking a fresh space early is worth 1 more resource than a claimed one, and a copy never gets it.
- **Copies read the whole table.** Green's copy spaces (Echo, Cover, Greatest Hits) take what is on other players' minions. Agents ("Your actions can't be copied") stops rivals copying yours, and also stops you copying your own (Reprise, Greatest Hits on a color where you have minions). It matters when you are the one with the big actions on the board.
- **Holding versus spending silver**: Silver's contest pays the fewest silver held, so Flaunt and Buyout (which pay for silver you hold) only pay a player who has given up that contest this round.
- **The +1 is already in the option's line.** "You'd get" on each option includes the space's first-taker +1; don't add it again.
- **Grey members are not a color.** Guest members (grey) don't count as a color in color contests such as Most member colors: only the colors on the table count, and Dabblers adds one color you lack.
- **Ties**: tied leaders each get half the prize, rounded up, however many tie (round III: 5 each).
- **Counting**: "counts as N members" counts N wherever members are counted, except where a rule says each card counts 1 (Red's contest).

## Table etiquette for a playtest
You are testing the game as much as playing it. When something looks too strong, too weak, confusing or unfun, say so at the table and say why. Do not announce your own move. Ask rivals what they read from a position, and challenge a read you disagree with.
