# Replacement batch 6 (7 cards), bot games only

Built on the six principles Cory said yes to (Nov 3), from a draft of 12 (the draft kept 8). Cut before the build: **Gatekeepers** (a member tied to one space, which Cory refused Nov 3). Cut in the draft: **Wedding Planners** (kingmaking, a Party Gifts at round end paid from your own pile, close to Pay It Forward), **Night Sale** (Estate Sale every round for free, not a new way in), **Last Rites** (a bigger Farewell that no one at the table feels), **Social Butterflies** (small, the shape of Muse). Social Climbers takes the Debutantes slot: with REPLACE6 it replaces Debutantes, and batch 2's Fundraisers too when REPLACE2 is on. Dabblers, Debt Collectors, Toastmaster and Heiresses stay. Prices are fill-ins, set a little high, including the +1 every member pays. A member whose cost before that +1 is 3 or less joins in round I; the rest join in round II. Built behind REPLACE6=1 (bot games only).

**1. Silver. Social Climbers** replaces **Debutantes** ("On Use, once per game: double your silver. Each rival +2 silver."), taking the slot from batch 2's **Fundraisers** ([6 silver + 3 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per game: each rival +2 silver, then +1 follower per 2 silver rivals hold in all.").
Prints: [6 silver + 3 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: gain followers equal to the silver of the rival who holds the most."
Played: round III, 4 players. Your **Toast** ("+3 silver in round I, +6 in round II, +9 in round III. Each rival +1 silver in round I, +2 in round II, +3 in round III.") and Ben's own Toast leave Ben holding 12 silver, saved for the Silver contest (+10). You use Social Climbers: +12 followers, and Ben keeps all 12 silver. The best other buy in the round III row is **High Society** ([5 silver + 3 any, worth 2] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more silver than you."), usually +4 to +6 at game end. Social Climbers is about +12 plus 1 worth, against about +6 plus 2: roughly +5 to +7 better, and it gives rivals nothing (Fundraisers gave them 6 silver for about 9 followers).
Their choice: Ben can spend his silver down first, for example on **Polish** ("Turn 2 of your silver into 4 of any colors.") or **Sweet Talk** ("+3 silver. Then turn as many of your silver as you want into other colors."), but then he risks the +10 Silver contest. Hold for the contest and pay you, or spend and lose the contest: both sides win sometimes.
Intuitive: one count of one rival's pile, the same as **Mirror Image** (batch 2: "On Use, once per game: choose a rival and a color; gain that color until you hold as much of it as they do.") reads.
Fun: the Silver leader watches you cash in their pile, and everyone you gift silver to is now feeding you.
Interacts: every silver gift at the table: Toast, **Party Gifts** ("Give rivals up to 3 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +2 followers per silver you give."), **Party Favors** ("+2 silver. Each rival gives you 1 resource of their choice, then gains 2 silver."), **Hostesses** (batch 1: "On Recruit and Round Start: give each rival 1 silver. +1 follower per rival who then holds more silver than you."). Magenta's **Crime Wave** ("Steal half of one rival's pile of one color, rounded down. +1 magenta.") on the big silver pile lowers your take.
Powerful: a relative amount, about 8 to 14 followers in round III, with no minion spent.
Engine or counter: engine with every gift you give; Hostesses and High Society want the same rich rivals.
Its color: Silver gives. This is a new way in: a gift you collect all at once, on the card. It is Cory's High Tide ("Each rival +1 silver, then gain 1 follower per silver the rival with the most holds", "1 is quite good") as a member.
Risk (Claude): **Largesse** ("Give rivals up to 10 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +1 follower per silver you give.") all to one rival, then Social Climbers, is about +10 and +15 in one turn, though that rival gets 10 silver and likely the +10 contest. If that's too much, "half the silver" is the fix.
Build (Claude's reading): On Use, once per game, as Fundraisers was; reads the single biggest silver pile among rivals at that moment (a tie is the same number); the rival keeps the silver.


**2. Silver. Godparents** replaces **Hangers-On** ("Whenever a rival gives you something, +1 more of it.").
Prints: [4 silver + 1 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: give one of your members to a rival. Gain followers equal to its cost."
Played: round III, 4 players. You used **Go-Getter** ([5 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: +1 minion, placed right after this turn.", cost 7) in round II. You give it to Cleo, who is furthest behind: +7 followers for you; Cleo gets its 1 worth and a used card. Or you give **Old Maid** ([5 any, worth 1] "Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers.", cost 5) to Ana, the leader: you +5, Ana −5. The best other buy in the round II row is **Philanthropists** ([4 silver + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: give rivals up to 4 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +1 follower per silver you give."), +4 followers once. Godparents is +5 to +8, or a 10-follower swing with Old Maid: about +1 to +6 better.
You choose: which member and which rival. A pricey card pays more but helps whoever gets it; a spent card helps them little. Who to help depends on who you're chasing.
Intuitive: give a card, get its price in followers, in one sentence.
Fun: a rival gets a gift they didn't ask for, sometimes a poisoned one, and the table watches who you trust.
Interacts: **Martyrs** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 3] "Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost.") pays you its cost twice. **Recluse** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 1] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you.") loses 2 when you hand its holder a member. Red's **Promote** ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red.") gives members away too, for a different reason.
Powerful: 5 to 8 followers, and it turns a spent once-per-game card into points.
Engine or counter: engine with once-per-game members and Martyrs; a counter to Recluse.
Its color: Silver gives silver. This is a new way in: giving members.
Risk (Claude): close to White's **Farewell** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain followers equal to its cost. +1 white.") on a member; the difference is that a rival gets the card. The Oct 22 silver giver was flagged for the same closeness.
Build (Claude's reading): On Use, once per game: one choice, a member and a rival together. Any of your members, Godparents itself included (it goes used). The engine's member move, so Martyrs pays as it leaves and Turncoats fires for you; not a steal or a swap, so Bodyguards and Loyalists don't stop it. A used card stays used. Cost is the card's printed cost.


**3. White. Successors** replaces **Cashier** ("On Recruit: turn all your white into followers.").
Prints: [4 white + 1 any, worth 1] "Whenever you cast out one of your members, recruit a shop member costing up to its cost, for free."
Played: round III, 4 players. You used Go-Getter (cost 7) in round II. You take Farewell: cast out Go-Getter, +7 followers, and Successors recruits **Fan Club** ([3 green + 3 any, worth 3, counts as 3] "On Recruit: +1 green per Green space with anyone's minion.", cost 6) for free, +3 worth and about 3 green, on top of your own recruit this turn. The best other buy in the round II row is **Shopkeeper** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 white. Round Start: +2 white."), about 6 white a game. Successors gives about 2 free members a game at 5 to 7 each, about 12: roughly +6 better.
Intuitive: cast one out, a fresh one takes its seat. "Cast out" is already on Farewell and Estate Sale.
Fun: a cast-out turn becomes two recruits, and the row loses its best card on your turn.
Interacts: White's **Farewell** and **Estate Sale** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain white equal to its cost. +1 white."), every color's once-per-game members, and Martyrs (cast it out for its cost, Farewell's cost, and a free member).
Powerful: a free recruit, the thing that's scarce late, each time.
Engine or counter: engine: once-per-game member, use it, cast it out, replace it. Cory's Nov 1 White loop, built into one card.
Its color: White cycles members into followers. This is a new way in: cycling them into new members.
Risk (Claude): Farewell and Estate Sale both in round II and III could be 3 or 4 free members; the same "free member up to its cost" as **Turncoats** (batch 1: "Whenever a rival takes one of your members into their cult, recruit a shop member costing up to its cost, for free."), but on your own cast-outs.
Build (Claude's reading): any cast-out of your own member that you make (Farewell, Estate Sale, Appraiser, and the one a rival's Inquisitors has you choose); a rival casting out your member (Evictors) isn't you casting out. The free recruit is from the shop, up to the cast-out card's printed cost, and counts as your recruit.


**4. White. Inquisitors** replaces **Raiders** ("On Use, once per game: steal 4 followers from a rival. Pay them 2 white.").
Prints: [4 white + 1 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: each rival casts out one of their members. +2 followers per member cast out."
Played: round II, 4 players. Ana holds Proselytizers ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.") and **Busker** ([2 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 green. Round Start: +2 green."): she casts out Busker. Ben holds only **Volunteer** ([1 red + 0 any, worth 1] "Worth 1 follower."): out it goes. Cleo casts out her used Go-Getter. You +6 followers; Ana loses Busker's 2 green a round and 1 worth, Ben and Cleo 1 worth each. The best other buy is Shopkeeper, about 6 white. Inquisitors is +6 for you and about −2 to −4 to each rival: roughly +3 to +5 better.
Their choice: each rival picks which card to lose: a cheap one, a used one, or one whose ability they no longer need. Every rival's answer is different.
Intuitive: everyone casts out one, you're paid per card.
Fun: three rivals each make a small, visible sacrifice on your turn.
Interacts: every rival's cult. A rival holding Martyrs casts it out and gains its cost, so Martyrs answers it. A rival holding **Recluse** ("Game End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you.") loses a little, since everyone else shrinks too.
Powerful: about 6 followers and a hit to three cults at once.
Engine or counter: a counter to big cults, and an engine with Successors (if you cast out too) and Pallbearers (below).
Its color: White casts out its own members. This is a new way in: making rivals cast out theirs.
Risk (Claude): close to **Evictors** (batch 3: "On Use, once per game: cast out a rival's member to the bottom of the pile. You and that rival each gain white equal to its cost."), but here each rival chooses their own card and nobody gets white. Losing a member before round III is meant to be a mild sting; the rival's own choice keeps it mild.
Build (Claude's reading): On Use, once per game; each rival with a member, in seat order, chooses which of theirs to cast out to the bottom of the pile. It counts as that rival's own cast-out, so their Martyrs and Successors answer it and your Pallbearers pays for it. +2 followers per member cast out. Not a steal, so Bodyguards doesn't stop it.


**5. White. Pallbearers** replaces **Old Money** ("Game End: +1 follower per white member you have.").
Prints: [4 white + 1 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival casts out a member, gain followers equal to half its cost, rounded up."
Played: round II, 4 players. Ben takes Farewell and casts out his used Go-Getter (cost 7): Ben +7, you +4. Cleo takes Estate Sale on Fan Club (cost 6): you +3. In round III two more cast-outs pay about +7. The best other buy in the row is Shopkeeper, about 6 white. Pallbearers is about 10 to 14 followers a game at 4 players: roughly +4 to +8 better.
Their choice: a rival weighing Farewell or Estate Sale now hands you half; they still cast out (they get the full cost), but a small cast-out may stop being worth it.
Intuitive: one trigger, half its cost, words from Farewell.
Fun: every White space a rival takes pays you, in front of everyone.
Interacts: Farewell, Estate Sale, Evictors, Inquisitors, and anyone cycling once-per-game cards.
Powerful: 3 to 4 followers a fire, with no minion spent.
Engine or counter: a quiet counter to White's cycling; engine with Inquisitors (three cast-outs at once pay you about 6 more).
Its color: White casts out. This is a new way in: profiting from everyone else's cast-outs.
Risk (Claude): with few White players it fires rarely; at 2 players about half as often.
Build (Claude's reading): any rival's cast-out of a member to the bottom of the pile (Farewell, Estate Sale, Evictors, Inquisitors); half its printed cost, rounded up.


**6. Grey. Sore Losers** replaces **HR** ("Counts as 4 members. Game End: +2 followers per color you have 3 members of.").
Prints: [5 any, worth 1] "Round End: if you win no contest, steal 3 followers from the rival who wins the most."
Played: round II end, 4 players. You spent everything on **Recruit Twice** ("Recruit twice this turn. +3 blue.") and lead nothing. Ana wins Blue and Silver (+12). You steal 3 from her. Round III, again: +3. The best other buy is Con Men, 5 resources once. Sore Losers is about 6 to 9 followers a game, each a 6-follower swing with the leader: about +2 to +5 better.
You choose: hold to win a contest, or spend everything and steal 3. In round I (+4) and round II (+6) the steal is close to the prize; in round III the +10 usually wins.
Intuitive: lose every contest, take 3 from the biggest winner.
Fun: the player sweeping contests pays the one who sat out.
Interacts: every contest, **Spoilsports** (batch 2: "Round End: choose a contest you don't lead; nobody wins it this round.") and **Tiebreaker** ("You win every contest tie you're in."). **Bodyguards** ("Rivals can't steal from you.") blocks it.
Powerful: a 6-follower swing per round with the leader.
Engine or counter: a counter to hoarding and contest sweeps.
Its color: grey plays with every color's contest.
Risk (Claude): close to **Grudge** (batch 1: "On Recruit: choose a rival. Game End: steal 4 followers from them if they have more than you."); here the target is set by the contests each round. A shared contest counts as winning it (Claude's reading); ties for most: you choose.
Build (Claude's reading): read right after the contests are scored. A shared contest counts as a win; rivals tied for the most wins (at least 1) are your choice. 3 loose followers (bought, won, gained, never members' worth) through the shared steal, so Bodyguards blocks it, as Raiders takes.


**7. Grey. Peddlers** replaces **Alchemists** ("All your resources count as any when you recruit a member.").
Prints: [5 any, worth 1] "On Recruit and Round Start: put the top member of the pile beside this card. Rivals may recruit members there, paying you instead of the bank."
Played: round III start, 4 players. The top card of the pile is **Night Owls** ([7 purple + 1 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "From round II: after your last minion, +1 minion, placed after everyone else's."). It sits by your seat as an extra shop card. Ben recruits it, paying 8: you get his 7 purple and 1 blue. That's 8 resources (8 followers at par) and maybe the Purple contest. The best other buy in the row is Con Men, 5 resources once. Peddlers is 1 or 2 sales of 5 to 8: about +1 to +7 better.
Their choice: buy the card beside you and pay you, or buy from the row. Real when the stall card is good.
Intuitive: you run a stall; buyers pay you.
Fun: one more card on offer each round, and buying it feeds a rival.
Interacts: **Peek** ("Look at the top 4 of the pile and put them back in any order. This turn your recruit may be one of them. +2 blue in round I, +4 in round II, +6 in round III.") sets the top of the pile, for you or against you; whatever colors a buyer pays you move your contests.
Powerful: a rival's whole member cost lands in your pile.
Engine or counter: engine on rivals' recruits; quiet counter with Peek.
Its color: grey: no color, bends where the shop is.
Risk (Claude): unsold cards pile up beside you; if that clutters, "if none is there". Engine work: a new shop zone, the heaviest build in the batch.
Build (Claude's reading): the stall is a list beside the card: one card added on recruit and at every round start while the pile has cards, never refilled from the shop. Rivals see it as extra shop cards when they recruit (paid recruits only) and pay the full price to you, in the colors they pay. Unsold cards stay. If Peddlers leaves its owner, its stall stays unsold (Claude's reading).
