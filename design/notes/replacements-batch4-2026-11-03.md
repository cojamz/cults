# Replacement batch 4 (7 cards), bot games only

Built on the six principles Cory said yes to (Nov 3), from a draft of 13 (the draft kept 8). Cut: **Boomerangs** (one of the draft's 8 keeps; cut before the build), **Pied Pipers** (Kidnappers' clock, and it rewards staying small), **Encore Fans** (Encore on a member; nobody reacts), **Afterparty** (small, Echo at round end, withdrawn Oct 28), **Grave Robbers** (Scavengers' shape; the bottom card is unclear after the row clears) and **Pawn Shop** (nobody feels it; cash-in-a-card, refused Oct 20). Recruit Bonus stays. Prices are fill-ins, set a little high, including the +1 every member pays. A member whose cost before that +1 is 3 or less joins in round I; the rest join in round II. Built behind REPLACE4=1 (bot games only).

**1. Green. Copycats** replaces **Roadie** ("On Recruit: +1 green per green member you have. Round Start: +1 green per green member you have.").
Prints: [3 green + 1 any, worth 1] "Round End: in each contest where you hold exactly 1 less than the winner, you tie for it."
Played: round II end, 4 players. Ben leads Blue with 7 and you hold 6; Ana leads Green with 5 and you hold 4. You tie both: +3 and +3, since a tie splits the +6 (rounded up). Ben and Ana get 3 each instead of 6. Round I is worth about +2 a tie and round III about +5, so about +8 to +12 a game, and the leaders lose as much. The best other buy in the round I row is Roadie ([2 green + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +1 green per green member you have. Round Start: +1 green per green member you have."), about 4 to 6 green over the game.
Intuitive: one count at scoring, one less than the winner.
Fun: every narrow lead is at risk. The leader has to hold one more, and everyone can see whether you're one short.
Interacts: every color's contest and the hold-or-spend part of the game. **Night Market** (batch 2: [4 yellow + 2 any] "Round End: before contests are scored, swap up to 3 of your resources for other colors, one for one.") lines up "1 less" on purpose. **Spoilsports** (batch 2: [6 any] "Round End: choose a contest you don't lead; nobody wins it this round.") can cancel the one you tie. **Tiebreaker** ([4 any] "You win every contest tie you're in.", 15-card deck only) turns each tie into a win.
Powerful: a share of one or two contests a round without winning them.
Engine or counter: a counter to holding just enough to win. Engine with Night Market and Tiebreaker.
Its color: Green copies. This is a new way in: copying the winner's pile size at scoring.
Risk (Claude): close to **Mirror Image** (batch 2: [6 green + 3 any] "On Use, once per game: choose a rival and a color; gain that color until you hold as much of it as they do."), which also matches a rival's pile, but Copycats doesn't change any pile and fires every round. Build reading: you need at least 1 of that color.
Build (Claude's reading): a holder whose count is exactly the winner's count minus 1, and at least 1, becomes one of that contest's winners, so the prize splits as any tie's. A spoiled contest (Spoilsports) has no winner to tie. Tiebreaker then decides a tie Copycats makes, as any tie.


**2. Blue. Outbidders** replaces **Window Dressers** ("On Recruit: send 3 shop members to the bottom of the pile, then refill.").
Prints: [3 blue + 1 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival recruits a shop member, you may pay its cost plus 2 to recruit it instead. They get back what they paid and recruit again."
Played: round II, 4 players. Ben saved 8 for **Night Owls** ([7 purple + 1 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "From round II: after your last minion, +1 minion, placed after everyone else's.") and recruits it. You pay 10 and take it. Ben gets his 8 back and recruits 5 followers instead. You get the card that was going to win Ben the round, as an extra recruit outside your turn. Once or twice a round, worth about +3 to +6 each, less the 2 extra. The best other buy in the round I row is Window Dressers ([3 blue + 1 any, worth 3, counts as 3] "On Recruit: send 3 shop members to the bottom of the pile, then refill."), worth 3; its owners lose more often than not (Nov 3 run).
Intuitive: an outbid at the shop, which every table understands.
Fun: every big buy at the table can be taken out from under the buyer, and they watch you decide whether it's worth 2 more.
Interacts: every color's best members, and the hold-or-spend part of the game (holding makes you a threat at the shop). **Proselytizers** ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.") pays on each outbid.
Powerful: an extra recruit, of the best card on the table, when you choose.
Engine or counter: a counter to saving up for one card; engine for anything that pays per recruit.
Its color: Blue works the shop and recruits more. This is a new way in: outbidding rivals at the shop.
The rival chooses: what to recruit, knowing you might pay 2 more for it, or something you can't afford. You choose: is this card worth 2 more than its price. Both sides are live.
Risk (Claude): a rich owner can outbid several times a round. If that's too much, "once per round" fixes it. Being outbid may feel bad, though the rival still recruits.
Build (Claude's reading): it is asked in the recruit step (a turn's recruit, Spree's, Doorbusters', Late Shoppers'): a shop member a rival chooses and pays for there. Free recruits from a space or a card aren't asked ("they get back what they paid and recruit again" reads as a bought recruit). Holders are asked in seat order from the buyer's left; the buyer spends nothing until all of them pass. "Its cost" is the holder's own price for it (their discounts count), paid the usual way, plus 2 from the piles they hold most of. Outbid, the buyer has the same recruit step again.


**3. Green. Opening Act** replaces **Tribute Band** ("On Use, once per game: choose a rival. Do every action they have a minion on this round.").
Prints: [7 green + 3 any, worth 2] "Whenever a rival places the first minion of a round, you do that action too."
Played: round III, 4 players. Cleo has the fewest followers, so she places first and takes Poach ("Recruit a rival's member you choose, for free. +1 magenta."). You do it too and take Ana's **Proselytizers** ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.") with 5 recruits left, about +7. In round II the opener took Promote ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red.") and you upgraded a member, about +3. Over a game, about +10 to +14. The best other buy in the round II row is **Fan Club** ([3 green + 3 any, worth 3, counts as 3] "On Recruit: +1 green per Green space with anyone's minion."), about 3 worth and 3 green. Tribute Band, at 14, is the card bots never use (dead in the Nov 3 run).
Intuitive: "you do that action too", on the first placement of a round, which everyone watches.
Fun: the first player picks the round's best space knowing you get it too. Sometimes they take a worse one to avoid sharing.
Interacts: every color's best space, and the turn order (fewest followers goes first from round II). **Agents** ([2 green + 1 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "Rivals can't copy your actions.") stops it. **Bait** ([2 green + 1 any] "Whenever anyone copies one of your actions, they −1 follower and you +1 follower.") punishes it. The Green cloak **Impressionist** ("Whenever any player copies an action, +2 of its color.") pays.
Powerful: the best space of each round, three times a game, with no minion.
Engine or counter: engine with Impressionist and anything that pays on copying. A counter to the player who would get the best space alone.
Its color: Green copies. This is a new way in: copying the round's opening move as it happens.
The first player chooses: their best space, shared with you, or a weaker one you can't use as well. Both happen.
Risk (Claude): from round II the first player is the one with the fewest followers, so the catch-up player's first pick is the one you share. When you are the one with the fewest followers, it does nothing.
Build (Claude's reading): "the first minion of a round" is the round's first one placed on the wheel. The copy is made after that space's own choices and before its taker's recruit step, as a Green copy: Agents stops it, Bait and Impressionist answer it, and a space that took or traded is done again.


**4. Blue. Hawkers** replaces **Connoisseurs** ("Whenever you recruit a blue member, +1 blue.").
Prints: [5 blue + 3 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per round: choose a shop member. Starting on your left, each rival may recruit it, paying its cost plus 2 to you. If none does, you recruit it for free."
Played: round I, 4 players: you offer **Busker** ([2 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 green. Round Start: +2 green.", cost 4). Rivals are holding their 4 or 5 for contests, so nobody pays 6, and you take it free. Round II: you offer Night Owls (8); Ben pays 10 (8 to the bank, 2 to you) and gets it as an extra recruit. Round III: you offer **Mob** ([8 red + 4 any, worth 4] "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have.", 12); nobody has 14, and it's yours. About +10 to +14 a game. The best other buy in the round I row is Connoisseurs ([2 blue + 2 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "Whenever you recruit a blue member, +1 blue."), about 2 worth and 2 or 3 blue.
Intuitive: a street seller's offer: take it at a markup, or I keep it.
Fun: each rival in turn decides whether to pay you or let you have it, with the whole table watching.
Interacts: every shop member and every rival's pile; a rich rival denies you, a broke one can't. It's Blue's answer to Red's **Recruit a Member** ("Recruit a member in the shop costing 8 or less, for free. +1 red.").
Powerful: a free member a round, or 2 resources and a rival spending their pile on your terms.
Engine or counter: engine with recruit counters; a counter to rivals holding their piles for contests (to stop you, they spend).
Its color: Blue works the shop. This is a new way in: selling the shop's cards to rivals.
You choose: a card you want and they can't afford, or one they'll overpay for. Each rival chooses: pay 2 extra for an extra recruit, or let you have it free. Both sides win sometimes.
Risk (Claude): when rivals are short, it's a free member every round, which beats Red's round III space three times a game. If it's too strong, "costing 6 or less". It joins in round II (Connoisseurs joined in round I).
Build (Claude's reading): each rival in seat order from your left is offered it if they can pay; its cost goes to the bank as any recruit's, and 2 more (from the piles they hold most of) to you (the draft's own reading of "paying its cost plus 2 to you"). Their recruit is theirs (its On Recruit fires) and doesn't use their turn's recruit.


**5. Blue. Late Shoppers** replaces **Window Shopper** ("On Recruit and Round Start: send 2 shop members to the bottom of the pile. +1 of each one's color.").
Prints: [5 blue + 3 any, worth 1] "Round End: after contests are scored, recruit once."
Played: round II end, 4 players. You held 8 blue all round and win Blue (+6) over Ben's 7. After scoring, you spend 7 of it on **Snobs** ([4 blue + 2 any, worth 2, counts as 2] "Game End: +1 follower per blue member you have.") before the row goes to the bottom. At game end, after the round III contests, you buy 10 followers with the 12 white that just won White (+10). The same resources score a contest and then buy: about +5 to +10 a round. The best other buy in the round II row is Window Shopper ([3 blue + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit and Round Start: send 2 shop members to the bottom of the pile. +1 of each one's color."), about 4 to 6 resources over the game.
Intuitive: one more recruit, at a time everyone sees.
Fun: you hold your pile all round and still spend it. Rivals racing you for a contest know you'll keep holding.
Interacts: every color's contest; **Racketeers** (batch 1: [4 magenta + 2 any] "Whenever a rival wins a contest, steal 2 of that color from them.") and Magenta's **Crime Wave** ("Steal half of one rival's pile of one color, rounded down. +1 magenta.") want the pile you're holding; Spoilsports (batch 2) can take the contest away.
Powerful: it bends the hold-or-spend rule for one player, three times a game.
Engine or counter: engine with any plan built on holding a pile (White's Cash In, Silver's piles, Snobs).
Its color: Blue recruits more. This is a new way in: recruiting with what you held for the contest.
Risk (Claude): close to **Doorbusters** (batch 2: [5 blue + 3 any] "Round Start: recruit once, before anyone places."), another extra recruit between rounds; the new part is spending after scoring. The round III recruit comes after the last contests and before Game End cards are counted (Claude's reading).
Build (Claude's reading): after each round's contests, one recruit (a member or followers) per holder, in that round's turn order, before the row goes to the bottom; in round III, before the Game End cards count. It doesn't use a turn's recruit.


**6. Red. Defectors** replaces **Proselytizers** ("Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.").
Prints: [6 red + 3 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: swap all your red members, this one too, for all of one rival's red members."
Played: round III, 4 players. Your only red member is Defectors. Ana has gone deep in red: **Mob** ([8 red + 4 any, worth 4] "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have."), **Panel** ([5 red + 2 any, worth 3] "On Recruit: promote or demote another of your members.") and **Deprogrammers** ([2 red + 1 any, worth 4] "Whenever you take a rival's member out of their cult, they lose 1 follower."). You use Defectors: her three come to you, Defectors goes to her. You gain 11 worth and Mob's +3 at game end, and give up 1. Ana loses 11 worth and her Mob count, and gets back 1. The best other buy in the round II row when you recruited it was Proselytizers ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower."), about +4 to +5 over the rest of the game. Defectors is about +13 against a deep red cult, and +1 to +3 when nobody has more than one red member.
Intuitive: one sentence, and "red members" is a count anyone can see on the table.
Fun: whoever builds a red stack watches for it all game; the moment it's used, two cults change places.
Interacts: Red's members and the go-deep card Mob; **Loyalists** ([3 red + 2 any, worth 3] "Your other members can't be swapped.") is the wall (only Loyalists itself can go); **Turncoats** (batch 1: [3 red + 1 any] "Whenever a rival takes one of your members into their cult, recruit a shop member costing up to its cost, for free.") pays the victim back with free recruits; Deprogrammers, if you hold it, makes the victim lose 1 follower per member taken.
Powerful: three or four members in one move, chosen by you, with no space spent.
Engine or counter: a counter to going deep in red (Mob, a Red cult built on Promote and Demote). Engine with Deprogrammers.
Its color: Red moves members. This is a new way in: moving a whole color of them at once.
Risk (Claude): the swing against a deep red cult is large (about 25 followers between two players). Used in round II it takes three members from one player before round III, which is more than the mild sting Cory set for members lost early (Oct 15). Red is already the strongest color in bot games (+18 over an even share, Nov 3 run).
Build (Claude's reading): red members are the members of the Red color (Drifters set to red too; Dabblers isn't one). Any rival may be chosen. Members that can't be swapped stay where they are (a rival's Loyalists: only Loyalists itself moves; your own Loyalists holds your others), and Bodyguards doesn't stop it, as with Red's swaps. The members you get are your recruits (their On Recruit fires), as with Promote; the ones you give are arrivals for the rival.


**7. Red. Tithe Takers** replaces **Mentor & Protégés** ("On Recruit: recruit the cheapest member in the shop.").
Prints: [4 red + 2 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per game: each rival gives you one of their members."
Played: round III, 4 players. Ben gives you **Busker** ([2 green + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +2 green. Round Start: +2 green."), long since paid off. Ana gives **Martyrs** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 3] "Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost."): she gains 4 followers and you get a card worth 3. Cleo gives **Cutpurse** ([2 magenta + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources. Round Start: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources."). You gain 5 worth and 3 members; each rival is one card down. The best other buy in the round II row was Mentor & Protégés ([6 red + 3 any, worth 3, counts as 3] "On Recruit: recruit the cheapest member in the shop."), about 3 worth plus a cheap member, about +5. Tithe Takers is about +5 to +7, plus a small sting to each rival.
Intuitive: one sentence; each rival hands over a card.
Fun: every rival has a choice to make in front of the table, and some choices backfire on you. A rival holding **Old Maid** ([5 any, worth 1] "Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers.") gives it to you, and you lose 5.
Interacts: every rival's cult. Martyrs pays the giver; Turncoats (batch 1) gives the giver a free recruit; Mob ([8 red + 4 any, worth 4] "Game End: +1 follower per red member you have.") makes rivals avoid handing you red. White's **Recluse** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 1] "Game End: +2 followers per rival with more members than you.") gets better for the givers.
Powerful: three members in one move in round III.
Engine or counter: engine with member counts (**HR**: [10 any, worth 4] "Counts as 4 members. Game End: +2 followers per color you have 3 members of."), and with Mob when a careless rival gives you red.
Its color: Red moves members. This is a new way in: rivals choose what moves.
The rivals choose: which member to give. The cheapest card isn't always the right one, because a red card helps your Mob, Martyrs pays them to give it, and Old Maid hurts you. So it's a real choice for each of them.
Risk (Claude): rivals give their weakest card, so the gain is mostly worth 1 or 2 per card. It may read as weak.
Build (Claude's reading): each rival with a member, in seat order from your left, chooses one to give. It is a gift, not a steal or a swap, so Bodyguards and Loyalists don't stop it. Your card takes it, so the giver's Turncoats answers it and your Deprogrammers counts it; it is an arrival for you (Old Maid's −5 lands on you), not your recruit.
