# Replacement batch 5 (6 cards), bot games only

Built on the six principles Cory said yes to (Nov 3), from a draft of 14 (the draft kept 8). Cut before the build: **Night Painters** (it replaces Drifters, a card Cory kept, and adds to the rival-resource changing he complained about) and **Understudies** (out-of-turn play, which he refused). Cut in the draft: **Dyers** (a smaller Dilute, gives the victim yellow), **Paint Bombs** (a counter with no gain; bots pass on defense cards), **Souvenir Sellers** (no rival feels it), **Fall Guys** (a bigger Bodyguards that picks a victim), **Muggers** (small fixed steals, the shape of an earlier Extortionist pitch) and **Alarm Clocks** (weak, and Early Birds stays per Cory). Second Fiddles takes the Dyer slot: with REPLACE5 it replaces Dyer, or batch 1's Barterers when REPLACE1 is on too. Closer and Drifters stay. Prices are fill-ins, set a little high, including the +1 every member pays. A member whose cost before that +1 is 3 or less joins in round I; the rest join in round II. Built behind REPLACE5=1 (bot games only).

**1. Yellow. Second Fiddles** replaces **Barterers** in the Dyer slot ("On Recruit and Round Start: swap up to 3 of your resources with one rival's, one for one."; the slot's first card was **Dyer**: "On Recruit: +1 yellow per color you hold. Round Start: +1 yellow per color you hold.").
Prints: [5 yellow + 3 any, worth 1] "Round End: +2 followers per contest you come second in."
Played: round II end, 4 players, table Red, Blue, Yellow, Purple. You hold 3 red, 4 blue, 6 yellow, 2 purple. Ana holds 5 red, Ben 6 blue and 4 yellow, Cleo 4 purple; nobody else is above you in those colors. You win Yellow (+6) and come second in Red, Blue and Purple: +6 followers from Second Fiddles. Round III is the same count again (the +2 doesn't grow), so bought in round II it pays about 10 to 12. The best other buy in the round II row is **Tourists** ([4 yellow + 3 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: +1 follower per color you hold. Round Start: +1 follower per color you hold."), +4 on recruit and +4 at round III's start: 8. Difference: about +2 to +4, and it keeps paying a broad pile that doesn't win.
Intuitive: one sentence; "second" is a count the table already makes at every rim.
Fun: the player sitting second in three contests is plain to see, and a rival in third can push 1 or 2 past them to take it away.
Interacts: every color's contest, implicitly: it reads rivals' piles without naming a rival. **Night Market** (batch 2: [4 yellow + 2 any] "Round End: before contests are scored, swap up to 3 of your resources for other colors, one for one.") and **Trade Seeds** ("+2 yellow, then swap up to 3 of your resources for other colors.") set it up; **Crime Wave** ("Steal half of one rival's pile of one color, rounded down. +1 magenta.") aimed at you drops you to third.
Powerful: about 4 to 6 followers a round, relative to how broad you are, with no minion spent.
Engine or counter: engine with Yellow's broad gains: **Harvest** ("+3 in round I, +4 in round II, +5 in round III, in colors you choose.") and **Caravan** ([5 yellow + 4 any, worth 3] "Worth 3 followers. On Recruit: +1 of each color you hold. Round Start: +1 of each color you hold.").
Its color: Yellow rewards going broad. This is a new way in: being good, not best, at everything pays.
Risk (Claude): Cory said Oct 30 "contests are a separate color"; this is a Yellow member that reads contests, not Yellow as the contest color, but he may see it that way. At 2 players every contest you lose but hold some of is "second", so it pays more there. The contest change Cory has in hand (a different contest per color) still works with it: second is second whatever the rim counts.
Build (Claude's reading): scored right after the contests. Second is the biggest count below the winners' count (below the top count in a contest nobody wins, such as one Spoilsports spoiled); ties for second each pay; you need at least 1 of what's counted. A Copycats holder who ties for a contest is one of its winners, not second.


**2. Magenta. Second-Story Men** replaces **Sticky Fingers** ("Your steals take 1 more resource.").
Prints: [4 magenta + 3 any, worth 1] "Round End: before contests are scored, steal 2 resources from one rival."
Played: round II end, 4 players, table Red, Blue, Magenta, Purple. Ben holds 6 blue to your 5. You steal 2 blue from him: you hold 7, he holds 4, and you win Blue (+6) instead of Ben (+6). In round III the same steal flips a +10. Bought in round II, it is 4 resources, and when a contest is within 3, about +6 and +10 to you and the same off the rival you hit. The best other buy in the round II row is **Extortionist** ([5 magenta + 2 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: steal 2 resources from the rival with the most resources. Round Start: steal 2 resources from the rival with the most resources."), the same 2 resources a fire, but after scoring and aimed by a count, not by you: about 4 resources and no contest moved.
Intuitive: one steal, at a moment everyone sees.
Fun: every narrow contest leader watches you at round end, and you choose whose lead to break.
Interacts: every color's contest. **Lie Low** ("Steal 1. Rivals can't steal from you this round. +1 magenta.") taken that round and **Bodyguards** (above) block it; **Hush Money** (batch 2: [4 silver + 2 any] "Whenever a rival steals from you, you may give them 2 silver instead.") lets the victim keep the contest for 2 silver (a real choice: 2 silver against a +6); the victim's **Fence** ("Whenever a rival steals from you, +2 magenta.") pays them.
Powerful: a 2-resource steal that moves a +6 or +10 prize.
Engine or counter: counter to holding for a contest; with **Racketeers** (batch 1: [4 magenta + 2 any] "Whenever a rival wins a contest, steal 2 of that color from them.") you rob the contest both before and after.
Its color: Magenta steals from piles. This is a new way in: stealing at the moment the piles are counted.
Risk (Claude): **Spoilsports** (above) also strikes one contest at round end, from grey; the two together make round end busy. Sticky Fingers leaves, so nothing in Magenta's 10 adds to a steal any more.
Build (Claude's reading): asked after the round's last placement and before anything is scored, in this round's turn order (as Spoilsports and Night Market are): first the rival, then which 2 of theirs. It goes through the shared steal, so Bodyguards, Lie Low and Hush Money apply and Desperado adds; a stolen resource keeps its color.


**3. Magenta. Jackals** replaces **Shoplifter** ("Whenever you recruit, steal 1 resource.").
Prints: [4 magenta + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival steals resources, steal 1 of them from that rival."
Played: round II, 4 players, Magenta at the table. Ana takes **Crime Wave** (above) on Ben's 8 blue and takes 4: you steal 1 of that blue from Ana. Cleo takes **Help Yourself** ("Steal 3 resources in round I, 4 in round II, 5 in round III, split among rivals as you choose."), 2 from Ben and 2 from you: you steal 1 back from her. Ana's **Cutpurse** ([2 magenta + 1 any, worth 1] "On Recruit: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources. Round Start: steal 1 resource from the rival with the most resources.") steals 1 at round start: you take it. Rivals steal about 8 to 12 times in a 4-player game with Magenta at the table, so Jackals gives 8 to 12 resources with no minion spent. The best other buy in the round II row is **Extortionist** (above), about 4. Difference: about +4 to +8, and every thief comes away 1 short.
Intuitive: one sentence; "steal" is already the word.
Fun: every theft at the table pays you a cut, in front of the thief.
Interacts: every steal space and steal member, Magenta's own included; **Bodyguards** on the thief puts them out of reach; **Racketeers** and **Skimmers** (batch 2: [4 magenta + 2 any] "Whenever a rival buys 10 followers, steal 3 of them.") steal too, so Jackals takes a cut of those.
Powerful: a resource every time anyone else steals.
Engine or counter: counter to the thieves, quiet: the more a table steals, the more Jackals takes. Engine with Magenta's contest (stolen magenta is held magenta).
Its color: Magenta steals. This is a new way in: stealing from the thieves.
Risk (Claude): a third "tax on what rivals do" in Magenta after Racketeers and Skimmers. Two Jackals could feed each other; Claude's reading: a steal Jackals makes doesn't set off another Jackals.
Build (Claude's reading): once per steal (a steal that takes from several rivals counts once, as Fence pays once), of resources only (a member, or Skimmers' followers, isn't one); any rival's steal, from you too. The 1 is of a color the thief took: the one they took most of that they still hold. A steal Jackals makes doesn't set off another Jackals. Bodyguards or Lie Low on the thief blocks it.


**4. Magenta. Vultures** replaces **Double Agent** ("On Recruit: give this member to a rival and recruit one of theirs.").
Prints: [5 magenta + 3 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival's member goes to the shop or the bottom of the pile, you may steal it."
Played: round II, 4 players, table Red, Blue, White, Magenta. Dee takes **Farewell** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain followers equal to its cost. +1 white.") and casts out **Proselytizers** ([4 red + 4 any, worth 2] "Whenever you recruit, +1 follower.", cost 8) for 8 followers. You steal it on its way down: it joins you, worth 2, and pays +1 for each of your next 6 or so recruits. Later Ana takes **Promote** ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red.") and gives her used Cutpurse (above) to the shop: you steal it, and its Round Start steals for you now. With White and Red at the table, about 2 to 4 members a game. The best other buy in the round II row is **Extortionist** (above), about 4 resources. Vultures is about 2 to 3 free members, worth 1 to 3 each plus their abilities: about +5 to +10.
Intuitive: one sentence; the card is going away anyway, you take it.
Fun: casting out a strong card now hands it to you, so Dee picks what to throw away with you in mind.
Interacts: Red's **Promote** and **Demote** (the same, "costing up to 2 less"), White's Farewell and **Estate Sale** ("Cast out one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain white equal to its cost. +1 white."), Blue's **Doormen** ([3 blue + 5 any, worth 2] "On Use, once per round: send a rival's member to the bottom of the pile. Give them a shop member; it can cost up to 2 less, or any amount more."), batch 3's **Evictors**. **Martyrs** ([3 white + 1 any, worth 3] "Whenever this member leaves your cult, gain followers equal to its cost.") still pays the rival who lets it go. **Old Maid** ([5 any, worth 1] "Whenever this member joins your cult, −5 followers.") you let go by.
Powerful: free members, chosen from what the table discards.
Engine or counter: counter to White's and Red's casting out and trading down; engine with **Recruiter** ([5 red + 1 any, worth 1] "Whenever you recruit a member, +1 red.") and Proselytizers, since each steal is a recruit.
Its color: Magenta steals members (Shake Down, Poach). This is a new way in: stealing the members rivals throw away.
You choose: take it or let it go. A card with a spent once-per-game ability or Old Maid you leave.
Risk (Claude): close in shape to **Scavengers** (batch 1: [4 blue + 2 any] "Whenever a rival sends shop members to the bottom of the pile, you may recruit one of them, paying its cost."), which bots took 18% of the time; this one is free and reads rivals' own members, not the shop row. It needs White, Red or Blue at the table to fire.
Build (Claude's reading): a rival's member leaving their cult for the shop or the bottom of the pile (Promote and Demote to the shop, Farewell, Estate Sale, Doormen, Evictors). It is your recruit (its On Recruit fires), as a stolen member is; a rival's Bodyguards or Lie Low blocks it; the rival keeps what their space paid them; the row going to the bottom between rounds doesn't count (those are shop members).


**5. Purple. Tagalongs** replaces **Bouncers** ("On Recruit: the next two rivals' turns are skipped.").
Prints: [6 purple + 3 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival gets +1 minion, you get +1 minion too."
Played: round III, 4 players, 5 minions each and 4 spare spaces. Ana takes **Crowd In** ("+3 purple. +1 minion this round."): you get +1 minion too, placed after everyone else's. Ben's **Night Owls** ([7 purple + 1 any, worth 2] "From round II: after your last minion, +1 minion, placed after everyone else's.") gives him one in rounds II and III: you get one each time. Cleo's **Go-Getter** ([5 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: +1 minion, placed right after this turn.") gives you another. A minion is a space plus a recruit, about 4 to 8 in round III. Over a game: 2 to 4 extra minions, about +10 to +20. The best other buy in the round II row is Night Owls itself, about 10.
Intuitive: one sentence; "+1 minion" is already on the cards.
Fun: whoever takes Crowd In or uses Night Owls hands you a minion, in front of everyone.
Interacts: Purple's Crowd In, Night Owls, Go-Getter, and grey's **Extra Hand** ("On Use, once per game: +1 minion this round."). The Crowd In player now weighs giving you one, or you take Crowd In yourself.
Powerful: extra minions, the biggest thing Purple gives, with no space spent.
Engine or counter: counter to Night Owls, the strongest Purple card in bot games (owners win 34 to 46%); engine with Full House (below).
Its color: Purple bends turns and adds minions. This is a new way in: getting a minion when a rival does.
Risk (Claude): two Tagalongs would answer each other forever; Claude's reading: a minion from Tagalongs doesn't set off another Tagalongs. The rule that a new minion needs a free space still holds, so only 4 extras fit a round.
Build (Claude's reading): every +1 minion a rival gets (Crowd In, Night Owls, Go-Getter, Extra Hand, and Purple's other extra minions) gives you one, placed after everyone else's, while the board has a spare space for it (so none in round I). A minion from Tagalongs doesn't set off another Tagalongs.


**6. Purple. Full House** replaces **Timekeepers** ("Game end: +1 follower per purple member you have.").
Prints: [4 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "Round End: if you have more minions on the wheel than every rival, +1 follower per minion you have there."
Played: round II, 4 players. You own **Night Owls** (above): you have 5 minions on the wheel, rivals 4: +5 followers. Round III: you have 6, but Ana took **Crowd In** (above) and also has 6, so nothing; with Tagalongs you'd have 7 and get +7. Bought in round II with one +1 minion card: about +5 to +12. The best other buy in the round II row is **Closer** ([3 purple + 2 any, worth 1] "When you place the last minion of the round: +4 purple."), about 4 purple a round when you land it.
Intuitive: count the minions on the wheel at round end; everyone can see them.
Fun: the round III Crowd In becomes a fight: whoever takes it ties you, so you take it or watch who does.
Interacts: Crowd In, Night Owls, Go-Getter, Extra Hand, and Tagalongs on either side.
Powerful: 5 to 7 followers a round.
Engine or counter: engine for Purple's +1 minion cards.
Its color: Purple adds minions. This is a new way in: scoring the extra minions against the table.
Risk (Claude): dead without a +1 minion source, the same gap that left Scavengers on the shelf. Round I never pays (no +1 minion before round II).
Build (Claude's reading): counted at round end before the contests: every minion of yours on a space, one joining a rival's space too. A minion that sits out because no space is open isn't on the wheel.
