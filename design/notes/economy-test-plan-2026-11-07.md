# Finding the fun economy: plan for your OK (Nov 7)

Nothing in the live game changes until you pick.

## What we'd turn (numbers only, no new rules)
1. **Member cost**: the total price, and how much of it has to be the member's own color versus any color.
2. **Cloaks**: the 1 resource you get when you pick a cloak, and the cloaks whose ability pays resources.
3. **Board resources**: the "+N" of its color each space gives (for example **Tryout**, "Use the On Recruit effect of a shop member; it stays in the shop. +2 red.").
4. **Round I deck limit**: the most a member that can show up in round I costs (today 5).
5. **Cards that name a cost** move with the price, so they reach the same members (for example **Initiation**, "Recruit a member in the shop costing 10 or less, for free. +1 red.").

Left alone unless you say: the follower price (1 resource for 1 follower), contest prizes (+4, +6, +10), minions, shop size.

## How we'd judge "fun" (draft, for you to fix)
- **A. You can follow every cult.** Each player ends with about 6 member cards or fewer. (Your Nov 4 game: you had 14 cards, and your seat showed 24 members.)
- **B. A recruit is an event.** A member costs about a turn and a half of income, so you plan for it instead of buying one every turn.
- **C. Real choices every turn.** Rare turns where you can afford anything you want, and rare turns where you can afford nothing.
- **D. Resources matter.** Little is left over at round end, and a steal or a gift is a real share of a member's price.
- **E. More than one way to win.** At least 1 game in 5 won with 5 or fewer members; buying followers and building members both win.
- **F. Races stay close.** The player in last place after round II still wins sometimes.
- **G. It still feels big.** Final followers don't collapse, and each color's tricks still happen (Red's free recruits, Magenta's steals).

## The steps
1. Build the dials (today; bot games only, the live game stays as it is).
2. Try about 40 mixes of the dials in Hard bot games, all on the same deals, and score each mix on A to G. You get one phone page: the best 6, and what a round looks like at the table with each.
3. For the best 3, I write out one played round in plain words, and add each as a setting in the menu ("Economy: A, B, C") so you can play them with friends.
4. You play them and tell me how each felt; we tighten around the one you like and go again.

Bots buy about half as many members as people do, so their counts show which way a dial pushes; your games decide.
