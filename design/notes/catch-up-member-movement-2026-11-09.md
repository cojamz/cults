# Catch-up and member movement (Nov 9)

Cory, Nov 9: "random note that im playing a Blue Silver Green game and theres absolutley no member movement. Feels hard to catch up to a guy who quickly got 5 or 6 cards. This is on test with richer economy btw"

## What the bot games say

Hard bots, 3 players, 10-card decks, By color contests. 60 games per line unless noted (so each number can be off by about 10 points). Chance alone would give the leader 1 game in 3.

| Table | Leader on members after round II wins | Members changing hands per game | Winners holding 5 or fewer members |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue Silver Green, test cards, Richer (120 games) | 45% | 1.7 | 8% |
| Blue Silver Green, test cards, normal prices (120 games) | 49% | 2.4 | 1% |
| Blue Silver Green, live cards, Richer | 47% | 1.8 | 10% |
| Red Magenta White, test cards, Richer | 29% | 15.7 | 48% |
| Gold Purple Yellow, test cards, Richer | 40% | 0.5 | 12% |

- Gold Purple Yellow is the same story: members change hands about once every two games.
- In Blue Silver Green, whoever has the most members after round II wins about half the time. With Red, Magenta and White on the table it's under a third, because members change hands about 16 times a game there.
- The two or so moves a game in Blue Silver Green are almost all Silver gifts: **Guest of Honor** ("On Use, once per round: give a rival one of your members. +4 followers.") and **Godparent** ("On Use, once per game: give one of your members to a rival. Gain its cost in followers."). The giver picks what to give, so they hand over a cheap card. Nothing at that table takes a member from anyone except **Repossessors** in the test cards ("On Use, once per game: put a rival's member costing up to 8 into the shop.") and the grey **Anarchist** ("On Use, once per round: swap any 2 members that cost the same, in any cult or the shop.").
- Blue's contest is "most members", so at this table the member leader also collects the Blue prize every round. That is a runaway built into the colors.
- Richer: not the cause in bot games. The member leader after round II wins 45% with Richer and 49% at normal prices, the same within the noise. Richer makes cults smaller early (the biggest cult after round I holds about 4.5 members instead of 5.5), so a lead of 2 members feels bigger, but it doesn't win more often.

## Where catch-up lives today, by color

- **Red** moves members: **Promote** ("+3 red. Swap any member for one costing up to 3 more, from another cult or the shop."), **Demote** (the same, "up to 3 less"), **Body Snatchers**, **Headhunter**.
- **Magenta** takes them: **Poach** ("Recruit a rival's member you choose, for free. +1 magenta."), **Fixer**.
- **White** discards its own: **Farewell**, **Estate Sale**, **Downsizers**.
- **Silver** gives its own away (above). It slows the giver, not the leader.
- **Blue** works the shop and the discard pile. Only **Repossessors** (test cards) touches a rival's cult.
- **Green** copies. It never moves a member.
- **Grey** (in every game): **Anarchist**, **Sore Loser** ("Round End: if you win no contest, steal 5 followers from the rival who wins the most.").
- Every game: from round II, fewest followers places first.

So a table without Red, Magenta or White has almost no way to take a member off the leader.

## Ideas, each played through

Fill-in names and numbers are Claude's.

### 1. Everyone takes from the right (grey member) [pick]
Cory's own seed ("everybody pass a member left", Nov 8), turned so the taker chooses.
Prints: **Gift Swappers** [8 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: every player, you too, takes one member they choose from the player on their right."
- Why the taker chooses: if each player gave their own, everyone would hand over their cheapest card and nothing would change.
- Played: round II, 3 players. Ann has 6 members (a **Patroness** costing 12 under Richer, "Whenever shop members go to the discard pile, +1 follower.", among them), Ben has 3, you have 3. You use it. Ben is on Ann's left, so Ben takes Patroness. Ann takes the best of yours, and you take Ben's best. Ann loses her best card and gets back a weaker one.
- Who feels it: the whole table, the biggest cult most. The owner picks the moment, so everyone watches for it.
- Grey, so it can show up in any game, including ones with no Red, Magenta or White (Cory, Nov 1: counterplay goes in the grey deck).
- Not refused: Musical Chairs (a Red III swap with each rival) was "a little aggressive"; Cory put "everybody pass a member left" at the top of the ideas pile.

- Intuitive: one sentence, "the player on their right" is the only new idea. Fun: everyone gets a card and loses one, and the owner times it. Interacts: every cult at once, the biggest most. Powerful: a swing of up to a 10-cost card for each player. Engine or counter: a counter to a fast-growing cult. Its color: grey, which holds the counterplay every table gets.

### 2. Blue sends a big cult's member into the shop (Blue round II space)
Prints: **Foreclose** "+2 blue. Put a member of a rival with more members than you into the shop."
- Played: round II, Ann has 6 members and leads Blue's most-members contest. You have 3. You take Foreclose and put her 7-cost card in the shop, then you can recruit it with this turn's recruit. If you don't, the next player can.
- Serves Blue's own contest (most members) and Blue's verb (the shop). Only a player behind on members can aim it, and only at someone ahead: a count the table can see.
- Who feels it: the big cult loses a card, and the whole table sees a good card hit the shop.
- Close to **Repossessors** (test cards), but on a space, so it is there every Blue game instead of once if dealt.

- Intuitive: one sentence with a count everyone can see. Fun: the leader loses a good card to the shop and anyone can grab it. Interacts: Blue's contest and every rival cult. Powerful: a member gone from the leader plus a strong card offered to you. Engine or counter: a counter. Its color: Blue works the shop, now with a rival's card.

### 3. Everybody discards a member (grey member or White)
Cory's own seed (Nov 8: "you discard a member to make everybody else have to discard a member").
Prints: **Purge** [6 any, worth 1] "On Use, once per game: discard one of your members. Each rival with more members than you discards one too."
- Each player picks what they discard, so the leader drops their cheapest card. It is a mild sting, but it cuts their count for Blue's contest and feeds Blue's discard pile.
- Played: round II, you have 3, Ann 6, Ben 5. You discard a 3-cost card; Ann and Ben each discard one. You lose 1, the two ahead lose 1 each.
- Weaker than 1 and 2 in a 3-player game (one for one against each rival).

- Intuitive: one sentence. Fun: a mild sting for everyone ahead. Interacts: every bigger cult and Blue's discard pile. Powerful: weak in a 3-player game. Engine or counter: a counter. Its color: grey (or White, which gets rid of members).

### 4. Silver generosity lets you recruit (Cory's top of the Silver pile, Nov 8)
Noted, not pitched here: it helps whoever takes it recruit, not the player behind, so it doesn't answer this note.

## Cut while playing them through
- "Everyone gives a member to the player on their left": the giver always hands over their cheapest card, so nothing changes.
- Anything keyed to who leads a contest right now (verdicts 30).
- A catch-up rule such as "fewest members recruits first": placing first from round II already goes to fewest followers, and a rule change helps every table, not this one.
