# ~50 replacements: principles (second try) and 3 examples

For Cory's yes before anything is built. Second version, Nov 3 11:30am, after his no on the first ("if you spend a turn to buff your crime wave, you've wasted a turn and somebody else is going to take crime wave"). A skeptic read every example; two were cut (one copied Bouncers, one only worked when Old Maid was in the row). Prices are Claude's fill-ins.

## The principles
Cory's three, then the checks that come with them.
1. **Intuitive and easy to understand.** One sentence, in words already on the cards. A new player knows what it does after one read, and nobody has to track anything.
2. **Fun to play.** Someone at the table reacts to it: a sting, a gift, or a choice they have to make.
3. **Interacts with its color, with other colors, or both.** It cares what rivals do or hold, so players watch each other.
4. **Worth the turn you spend on it.** It pays when you recruit it, or from things that happen anyway; never only if you later win one particular space a rival can take first.
5. **Every choice has two live sides**, played out for the chooser.
6. **Replace what fails 1 to 3**, plus the cards you've named for rework (Encore, the White revisit). The cards the bots never want are where we look first, not a verdict. Cards you love stay.
7. **Tested in batches of about 10** on the new balance system, built as bot-test switches so the game stays as it is. Results in plain words: what the bots take, whether owners win more, which ones nobody wants. You pick what ships.

## Three examples

**1. Red. Turncoats** replaces **Volunteer** ("Worth 1 follower.").
Prints: [3 red + 1 any, worth 2] "Whenever a rival takes one of your members into their cult, recruit a shop member costing up to its cost, for free."
Played: round III, 4 players. A rival takes **Poach** ("Recruit a rival's member you choose, for free. +1 magenta.") and picks your Panel (cost 7). You recruit any shop member costing 7 or less for free, on top of your own recruit. If they take Turncoats itself, you get nothing back. Members you give away yourself (your own Promote or Demote) don't count.
Both sides: the thief picks whom to rob. Taking from you now hands you a free member, so they look elsewhere first, unless your card is worth it (or they take Turncoats).
Intuitive: one sentence, a counter like **Snitch** ("Whenever a rival steals from you, they lose 1 follower.").
Fun: the thief has to think twice, and when they go ahead the whole table sees you get a free card.
Interacts: with Red's **Promote** ("Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red.") and **Demote** (the same, "costing up to 2 less"), and Magenta's **Shake Down** ("Choose a rival. They choose one of their members costing 5 or less, then pay you its cost or you steal it. +1 magenta.") and **Poach**.
Worth the turn: worth 2 for 4, and it protects every member you own from the moment you recruit it.

**2. Blue. Scavengers** replaces **Critic** ("Whenever a rival recruits a blue member, +1 follower.").
Prints: [4 blue + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival sends shop members to the bottom of the pile, you may recruit one of them, paying its cost."
Played: round II, 4 players. A rival takes **Out of Style** ("Send every shop member of one color to the bottom of the pile, then refill. +1 blue per member sent. +1 blue.") on purple, sending Night Owls and Bouncers down. You pay 8 and recruit Night Owls on their turn, without using your own recruit. The row clearing between rounds doesn't count, since no rival sends it.
Both sides: the rival who digs through the row now hands you one card if you can pay, so they weigh the dig against what you might grab. You weigh the card against holding your resources for a contest.
Intuitive: "the bottom of the pile" is already Blue's words.
Fun: digging stops being free, and the cards going down become one player's second chance.
Interacts: with Blue's **Out of Style**, **Bury** ("Send a shop member to the bottom of the pile, then refill. Gain blue equal to half its cost, rounded down. +1 blue.") and **Savings** ("...Before you recruit, you may send one shop member to the bottom of the pile, then refill."), **Blue Cloak (Bargain Bin)** ("Whenever members go to the bottom of the pile, +1 blue.") and **Patroness** ("Whenever shop members go to the bottom of the pile, +1 follower."), which pay off the same digging.
Worth the turn: it pays whenever a rival digs, several times a round on most tables with Blue.

**3. Magenta. Tax Collectors** replaces **Crew** ("Worth 2 followers.").
Prints: [4 magenta + 2 any, worth 1] "Whenever a rival wins a contest, steal 2 of that color from them."
Played: round I end, 4 players. Ben wins Blue holding 6 and Cleo ties Silver with 3. You steal 2 blue from Ben and 2 silver from Cleo, right as round II starts. Round III's contests come at game end, so it pays in rounds I and II: about 8 to 12 resources a game.
Both sides: a rival holding for a contest still holds (the +4 or +6 is worth more than 2), but now a narrow win costs them, and **Bodyguards** ("Rivals can't steal from you.") blocks it.
Intuitive: one sentence; "steal" and "wins a contest" are words players already use.
Fun: every contest winner pays a tax the table can see coming.
Interacts: with every color's contest, the hold-or-spend part of the game, and with **Magenta Cloak (Fence)** ("Whenever a rival steals from you, +2 magenta.") on the other side.
Worth the turn: it works at every round end without placing a minion.
