# ~50 replacements: the principles, and 6 examples

For Cory's yes before anything is built. Written Nov 3 from the critic's review, last night's bot games and every card's live text. Prices and numbers are Claude's fill-ins.

## The problem they answer
You and the critic agreed: every turn is "take the biggest number", and going deep in a color doesn't pay more than spreading out. In last night's games the strongest bots faced a close call between two spaces on only 2 to 4% of their placements, and a winner's members came from about 3 colors.
The root: a space gives everyone the same thing, and most members are a flat gain. So a card is worth the same to every player, and nothing you own makes the next turn different for you.

## The principles
1. **Worth more to whoever built for it.** Each new card reads something you already own: your members of its color, the color's spaces you take, your place in the turn order. Two players should value the same card differently.
2. **A color's members make that color's spaces better, for their owner only.** This is the main tool. Going deep pays because your Red spaces now do more for you than for a rival, so the wheel itself rewards focus. No added bonus or multiplier on top.
3. **Hooks, not totals.** Each card names a verb another card makes (a swap, a steal, the Goes Last token, a copy, a gift, a recruit), so cards combine into an engine.
4. **Replace dead weight first.** Start with the cards the bots never want or that lose for their owner (Volunteer, Crew, Early Birds, Muse, Critic, Gift Wrapper, Line Cutters, Scouts, Dyer, Gardener, Window Shopper, Recruit Bonus, Loyalists, Party Crasher) and cards you've named for rework (Encore, the White revisit). The outliers (Night Owls, the cloaks) get number fixes, not replacements.
5. **Every choice has two live sides**, played out for the chooser. A rival feels each card: a sting, a gift or a reason to block.
6. **Tabletop clean.** One sentence; nothing to remember; spaces finish when taken.
7. **Big and simple beats clever.** No small "+1 when..." triggers, no plain bodies, no flat resource trickles (they make "biggest number" worse).
8. **Spread across the game**: about 5 per color plus a few grey, roughly 40 members, a few spaces, a few cloaks.
9. **Tested in batches of about 10.** Each is built as a bot-test switch, so the game stays as it is, and run on the new balance system. For each card the report says how often the bots take it, whether its owners win more, and whether it moves the close-call and focus numbers. You pick what ships.

## Six examples (each upgrades its color's own space)

**1. Red. Talent Scouts** replaces **Volunteer** ("Worth 1 follower.", 1 red).
Prints: [2 red + 1 any, worth 1] "Your Promote and Demote reach members costing up to 4 more or 4 less."
**Promote** is "Give one of your members to a rival or the shop, and recruit one of theirs costing up to 2 more. +3 red."
Round II, 4 players: you hold Triplets (cost 3, "Worth 3 followers."). Promote normally reaches a 5. With Talent Scouts you give Triplets and take Panel (cost 7, worth 3, "On Recruit: promote or demote another of your members.") from the shop or a rival, and Panel's own swap also reaches 4. Your best other space is Gather, +3 red.
Who feels it: a rival's 7-cost member is now in reach, so they have a reason to block Promote.

**2. Magenta. Lookouts** replaces **Crew** ("Worth 2 followers.").
Prints: [3 magenta + 1 any, worth 2] "Your Crime Wave steals from two rivals."
**Crime Wave** is "Steal half of one rival's pile of one color, rounded down. +1 magenta."
Round III, 4 players: two rivals hold 8 and 6 blue. Crime Wave alone takes 4; with Lookouts it takes 4 + 3 = 7, which can swing the +10 blue contest. Your best other space is Help Yourself, steal 5.
Who feels it: two players lose half a pile, so a big pile gets more dangerous to hold.

**3. Purple. Night Shift** replaces **Early Birds** ("Your turns can't be skipped.", your idea from Oct 22).
Prints: [3 purple + 2 any, worth 2] "Whenever you take the Goes Last token, +2 purple per minion on it."
**Last Call** is "+1 purple. +1 follower and +1 purple per minion you have left. Take the Goes Last token and put your other minions on it."
Round II, 4 players: you take Last Call with 3 minions still in hand after it: 3 followers and 4 purple, then those 3 go on the token, +6 more purple. That's 10 purple toward the +6 contest, against Crunch Time's +2 purple.
Who feels it: the purple pile becomes a Crime Wave target, and you play your last 3 minions on scraps.

**4. Green. Session Players** replaces **Muse** ("Whenever a rival copies an action, +1 green.").
Prints: [4 green + 3 any, worth 1] "Your Echo copies the previous two actions, in any order."
**Echo** is "Copy the previous action. +1 green."
Round II, 4 players: the two turns before yours were Crime Wave (takes 4 blue) and Promote. Echo with Session Players does both. Your best other space is Cover, one copy.
Who feels it: the two players just copied; **Agents** ("Rivals can't copy your actions.") becomes worth buying against you.

**5. Blue. Personal Shoppers** replaces **Critic** ("Whenever a rival recruits a blue member, +1 follower.").
Prints: [4 blue + 2 any, worth 1] "Your Recruit Twice recruits three times."
**Recruit Twice** is "Recruit twice this turn. +3 blue."
Round III, 4 players, 15 resources: Recruit Twice with Personal Shoppers gives three recruits, like a second **Spree** ("Recruit 3 times this turn. +5 blue."). A rival who also wants a recruit-more space now has two to fight you for.
Who feels it: the shop row empties faster in front of everyone. It stacks with **Shopaholics** ("You can recruit twice each turn."), which the bots will check.

**6. Silver. Benefactors** replaces **Gift Wrapper** ("Whenever you give rivals silver, +1 follower.").
Prints: [5 silver + 3 any, worth 2] "Your Largesse gives up to 15."
**Largesse** is "Give rivals up to 10 silver from the bank, split as you choose. +1 follower per silver you give."
Round III, 4 players: giving 15 gives you 15 followers instead of 10, but rivals gain 15 silver right before the +10 silver contest. Give it all to one rival and they likely win that contest; spread it and nobody jumps ahead. Both sides are live: more followers for you, or less help to the leader.
Who feels it: every rival, as a gift, and the silver leader as a threat.

Grey members have no spaces to upgrade, so their replacements (starting with **Line Cutters**, "You go first every round.") follow principles 1 and 3 instead, and come in the first batch. White and Yellow examples come with batch 1 too: White's cast-out spaces and Yellow's rearranging need care so they don't feed the loops the last balance list flagged.
