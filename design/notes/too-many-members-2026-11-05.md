# Too many members: what drives it, the levers, and a recommendation

Nov 5. Cory: "with people buying so many members each game, I almost feel like we need to either increase the costs by 1, or decrease the resources everything gives (or steals)." This page is analysis only. Nothing in the game has changed.

## What the numbers say

Your playtest last night (3 players): you ended with 14 member cards, and your seat showed **24 members**. Jerbear's seat showed 29. Bot 3 won with 85 followers and fewer cards than either of you. It held its colors for the contests (+23) and used Patriarch twice (+22).

Normal bots on today's deck (4 players, 240 games): about 6 member cards per cult at game end, and the seat shows about 11. They pass on recruiting about 3.5 times a game to hold resources for the contests. People don't pass, so they end up near 14 cards.

There are two reasons the number gets so high.

1. **One turn's income is about one member's price, every round.** For each turn:

   | Round | You gain (space, steals, gifts) | Average member bought costs |
   |---|---|---|
   | I | 3.5 | 2.9 |
   | II | 5.7 | 5.4 |
   | III | 6.5 | 5.5 |

   So a player who wants a member every turn can afford one. Spending the same resources on followers is worse while you hold less than 5. With 4 resources, the follower buy gets you 1 follower and uses up your recruit. A member gets you 1 to 3 followers plus its ability. The five round I spaces that give followers when you recruit a member (Gather, Stash, Allowance, Splurge, New Blood) push the same way.
2. **The seat count is about 1.8 times the cards.** 67 of the 224 members in the 2.0 colors count as 2 to 4 members, for example Triplets 3, Shift Crew 4, Deprogrammers 4, Loyalists 3. Your 14 cards showed as 24. Mentor counting as 1 and Ghost as 0 (already decided) trim a little of this.

Free recruits are not the cause. Walk-In, Initiation, Housewarming, Poach and the swaps add only about half a member per cult per game in bot games.

## The two levers, tried

Normal bots, the same 120 tables each, 4 players, 10-card deck. These are bots. The direction is reliable. The size is rough, because people buy more members than bots do.

| | Today | Every member +1 cost | Every space gives 1 less (stand-in, below) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Member cards per cult | 5.7 | 4.5 (−21%) | 4.2 (−26%) |
| Members the seat shows | 10.3 | 8.4 | 7.8 |
| Members bought per game | 5.4 | 4.2 | 3.9 |
| Follower buys per game | 4.5 | 5.2 | 4.3 |
| Turns with no recruit, per game | 3.5 | 3.7 | 4.8 |
| Round I income per turn | 3.5 | 3.5 | 2.1 |
| Average final followers | 74 | 70 | 61 |
| Winners with 5 or fewer members | 20% | 29% | 35% |

How "every space gives 1 less" was faked: each space's first gain was cut by 1. Steals are mostly not cut in this run, so a full version would cut a little deeper.

**+1 cost.** About one member fewer per cult. Most of that buy goes to followers instead, so turns still feel used. Spaces, steals, contests and follower prices all stay the same. It's one number on every card. On a printed card it's a new cost.
- Knock-on: anything that recruits for free (Walk-In, Initiation, Housewarming, Poach, the swaps) is worth 1 more. Red does most of this and was already the strongest color in the last Hard run. Watch Red.
- Knock-on: the cheap round I members go from 2 to 3 (Accomplice, Recruit Bonus), so round I buys get a little rarer.
- History: this exact lever went in on Oct 27. The count crept back as more members that count as several and more recruit bonuses went in. A price change alone won't hold if the deck keeps drifting that way.

**Less from spaces and steals.** It cuts a little more, but it costs more.
- Round I gets thin. A space gives about 1 resource on top of its +1. Income drops 40% and turns with no recruit go up by more than one a game. That is the bad feel: a turn where you can't do anything.
- Contests are scored on held colors, so they become 1 or 2 tiles and more ties. Steals (Help Yourself, Lie Low) hurt less, so Magenta loses its bite.
- Saving up to 5 or 10 resources for a follower buy gets slower. That pushes toward members, which partly cancels the lever.
- It touches every space's text, about 48 of them, plus members tuned to the current income.

**Neither one gets a 14-card player to about 8.** Both cut about a fifth to a quarter, so 14 cards becomes about 11. Halving it would take +2 to +3 on every cost, or halving income. Either would break the contests and follower buys.

## Other levers, smaller or for later (Claude's, not pitched as cards)
- **The number on the seat.** If 24 is the part that feels crazy, the count rule is the lever (worth N followers counts as N members, Cory Nov 1). The seat could show cards and followers separately. Or fewer members could count as several.
- **The round I recruit bonuses.** Gather, Stash, Allowance, Splurge and New Blood pay you for recruiting a member. If they also paid for a follower buy, a member would stop being the default buy in round I.
- **Holding pays more.** Bigger contest prizes make passing a recruit worth it. Bots already pass 3.5 times a game for this, and raising the prizes on Oct 27 moved members only about 6%.

## Recommendation
1. **Every member +1 cost** (the +1 wild every member already pays becomes +2). Same cut as less income, without the empty turns, the tiny contests or rewriting every space.
2. Before choosing it, tell me which number is too high: the cards in front of you (14) or the count on your seat (24). +1 cost shrinks both by about a fifth. Only the count rule fixes the gap between them.
3. Then one quick bot check on the live deck, and your next playtest decides.
