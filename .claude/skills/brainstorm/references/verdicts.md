# What Cory said yes and no to, and why

Calibration for the filter step. Each entry is a real pitch with his words. The full log is design/pitch-ledger.md (newest first, with tags); add new lessons here only when a verdict shows a pattern this file doesn't already name.

## What his yeses have in common

Read these as the target. Almost every space he approved is **one plain verb aimed at a real piece other than your own pile**, and it **creates a choice someone can feel**:

- Echo, "Copy the previous action": "absolutely perfect". One verb; it's only as good as what was just played, so it reads the table and scales on its own.
- Promote, "Swap a member with a shop member costing up to 2 more": a free upgrade, and it can target a rival.
- Shake Down, "That rival pays you its cost, or you steal it": the victim makes the choice. Both outcomes hurt a little.
- Poach, "Take any rival's member": "Aggression is intentional". Round III power.
- Recruit Twice / Spree: more recruits, the thing that's scarce late. "I like Buy Twice."
- Churn, "send 3 shop members to the bottom, then refill": a verb on the shared row.
- Crunch Time, "Take two turns after this one": "Crunch Time is great." Moves two turns, not one.
- Last Call, "+2 followers per minion you have left. Skip your turns until everyone else is out of minions": "YES now you're cooking". Position traded for followers by a count everyone can see.
- Crowd In, "you can place on taken spaces this round" (his idea): bends the one rule everyone feels.
- Hold Up, "a rival you pick skips their next turn": a mild sting on timing.
- Rising Tide, "Each rival +2 silver, then gain half the silver of the rival with the most": "I like this one a lot". A gift that sets up your own take.
- White round II, "Send one of your members to the bottom of the pile, gain white equal to its cost": "3 is good improves on mine". A real sacrifice (a member) for fuel toward the round III cash-in.
- White round I, "+1 follower and +1 white × round": accepted once its totals matched its Magenta sibling.

Members he loved: Squatter ("really great"), Recluse ("I love this one a lot"), Doppelganger, rule-benders ("good set!"), steady income and scorers ("they all kind of feel weak… except for scoring ones or passive income"), counters (Snitch: "I like the counter").

## The failure modes, most damaging first

Each one has drawn a no. Run every idea past all of them.

1. **Misreading a piece of the game.** "This turn your follower recruit has no limit" → "what does that even mean? the follower recruits are the cards in the shop. are you aware of how the game works?… the follower groups you can buy are set and a specific part of the design." Check: name the exact card, space or rule the idea touches, as it appears on screen. If the idea needs a piece that doesn't exist, drop it.
2. **A smaller copy of a stronger space, or net zero.** Purple round II "take one turn after this one" → "a smaller crunch time….. do you understand this game at all?" Check: write the placement accounting; never pitch a weaker version of a color's round III action for an earlier round.
3. **Pile arithmetic dressed as a verb.** Yellow round III "+2 of every other color" → "doesn't feel very good"; "swap any resources one for one" and "raise one color to match your biggest" → "both terrible"; White round I "turn up to 2 × round of your resources into white", "+1 white per member (at most 3)", "white equal to the cheapest shop member's cost" → "uhg i don't like these". Check: if the whole effect is "your pile changes by a formula", it needs a strong reason. One plain scaling gain per color is the exception.
4. **Access or information without power.** Blue round II "pull a pile card into the shop" → "so weak. shit like this makes me feel like you dont understand the game at all"; "only you can recruit a marked shop member" → "promote and ransom are just straight up way better". Check: round II and III must change what someone owns or how much they recruit.
5. **Doesn't scale, or scales too well.** Blue round I first drafts → "kinda don't scale". Yellow "do one round I action of each other color" → "they get stronger in round III. let's nix this idea." Check: run it in round I and in round III.
6. **Weaker than its sibling.** "+1 follower × round" → "way worse than magenta one". Check: compare totals with the same ring's reworked spaces.
7. **Wrong exchange math.** Claude pushed "2 followers per white"; Cory: "2 is way too many… man simple strategy shit that you can't wrap your head around". Check: the follower card's three limits (1 for 1, 10 per card, one recruit a turn) before touching a rate.
8. **Too narrow: variations on one idea.** Green round I "copy the next action / your last action / the first action" → "not thinking broadly enough… think about how the quadrant plays with the others". Silver/Sweet Talk/Hold Court replacements → "need better pitches". Check: the 2 or 3 you send must differ in which piece they touch.
9. **Things he has ruled out.** Spaces that charge you ("no board action should require spending"), rescue clauses ("taking a space you can't use is on you"), out-of-turn minion placement, win-more, cost reductions (dropped for now), Release, anything a real table must remember, losing a member before round III as more than a mild sting, scoring on member counts or variety, Claude- or agent-built decks pitched as finished ("mostly sucked", "those magenta changes look awful").
10. **Loose words about what a card does.** Purple, Oct 22: "Crunch time doesn't 'do it twice', you need to be more precise with your language." Crunch Time reads "Take two turns after this one": it moves two of your turns up, it doesn't repeat anything. Check: describe every card, yours and the live ones you compare against, by what its printed text makes happen, step by step; never by a shorthand ("doubles", "twice", "basically X") that a player at the table would dispute.
11. **An action with an effect that lasts.** Calibration pitch, Oct 22, White round I "+1 white. This round, whenever a rival buys followers, +1 white" → "no that's more of a member effect. it's difficult to keep track of at the table. i don't like actions with ongoing effects (i don't think we have any except for can't be stolen from)". Check: a space does its whole effect the moment it's taken. "Whenever…", "for the rest of this round" and "at round end" belong on members, where the card sits in front of you as the reminder.
12. **Strong enough but inelegant, or off-theme.** White, Oct 22: "the strength of your ideas is ok, they just lack an elegance and a connection to the core theme of the color… maybe say the theme of the color as you understand it". Check: state the color's theme in one line; each idea should sit comfortably in the color as one clean mechanic. A mechanic plus a bonus fails. But don't over-index: "it doesn't need to be super incestual" (Cory, same day).
13. **Absurd best case, or complicated for no reason.** White members, Oct 23: "+1 follower whenever you recruit followers" → "Is this a joke? Like, this would be so strong"; a "when recruited: gain followers" with a formula → "complicated for no reason". Check: play the best case (built around, round III, every turn it can fire); count how often a trigger fires per game. A card that needs explaining to do little is cut.
14. **Too strong by combination or timing.** A contest idea Cory liked ("gain the prize of a contest you lead now") was "a little too strong in round 3" and is held for a future contests color (design/future-colors.md). "Getting blue from cards going to the bottom seems too strong between rounds"; Bloom "+6 early is crazy"; Yellow round III with Green copying. Check: copies, round III, and between-round refreshes.

15. **Small-ball gains, and rewordings of a refused one.** Yellow, Oct 30: "+1 of another color, more each round" (Mix), "turn 1 yellow into 2 of other colors" (Plant), "+1 yellow per other color you hold" (Bloom) → "these are all so weak and uninteresting. wtf"; then choice-based small gains (Market Day, Keep Up) → "still fucking awful small ball gains. nothing interesting or related to the color goal". Check: hitting his numbers is the floor, not the idea. A space that only adds a few resources by a formula, even with a choice of whom, is small ball.
16. **Not the color's goal.** Yellow, Oct 30: reading the shop's colored parts and members of a color in every cult → "isnt really aligned with yello… doesnt make sense"; Yellow as the contests color → "no, contests are a separate color"; County Fair (recruit one member of each color paying only colored parts) → "i hate that round III… too focused on member costs". His Yellow: "rewards going broad, i.e. having a combination of colors and being flexible. it also allows you to translate currencies (kind of like launderer)." Check: Gate 2 of SKILL.md.
17. **What he said yes to that morning:** his own simplification, "+3 or +4 yellow and the launderers effect as round 3 action" ("I think that's right"), and Harvest as "+3/+4/+5 in colors you choose" (fuel spaces get the color's flavor). He liked Rainmaker ("raise each of your colors to match your yellow") and Square Up ("+1 of each color you hold, for each color you hold"); "every color you hold the fewest of goes up to your second-fewest" was "way too strong". Simple and big beat clever.

15b. **A rival's choice with a dominated side.** Magenta, Oct 31: "Each rival chooses: give you 1 resource, or you steal 2 from them" → "nobody would ever choose not to give one. really basic strategic thing that makes me have no confidence in you." Check: for every "they choose" or "you choose", play the chooser's side; if one option is always better for them, it isn't a choice, and the card is just the better option.

18. **Anything that reads as "what came back to you".** Silver, Oct 31: Return the Favor ("Each rival gives you 1 of their silver in round I, 2 in round II, 3 in round III") → "the exact fucking kind of thing i dont want players to have to keep track of", though the card needs no memory: pitched as "the silver you handed out comes back", it read as a ledger. Also out the same night: Sponsor (a bigger Toast aimed at one rival), Commission (fire a rival's On Use), Small Talk (a smaller Sweet Talk). Check: a round I slot beside a fuel space and a converter must touch a different piece, and nothing in its text or pitch should sound like counting past events.

## How he wants it delivered

- About 5 ideas per open slot, in one message, one line each. He hates one-at-a-time pitches: "why are you doing these so piecemeal?" (Oct 22). He still decides one slot at a time.
- Quote the card text as it would print. He reads the card, not your description of it.
- Say what changes for a player in one line, in game words.
- Your numbers are fill-ins; label them. He changes numbers by feel.
- He often answers with his own idea. His answer replaces yours unless he says "also"; build his exactly, and if his has a gap, ask about the gap, don't redesign it.

19. **A member that upgrades one shared space.** Nov 3, replacement principles ("your Crime Wave steals from two rivals", "your Echo copies two", "your Largesse gives up to 15") → "those principles are terrible. if you spend a turn to buff your crime wave, you've wasted a turn and somebody else is going to take crime wave… keeping track of who gets bonus what on which square is not great." Check: a member must pay off without winning one particular contested space, and must not leave per-space bonuses for the table to track. His own bar for every card: intuitive and easy to understand, fun to play, interacts with its color, other colors, or both.

20. **A card that needs another color in the game.** Nov 7, on the color map: Flatterers ("Whenever a rival copies one of your actions…") and Pickpockets ("Whenever a rival's turn is skipped…") → "you can never rely directly on the mechanics of another color being available… that thing becomes useful in the games where green is not, and that just feels like really, really bad design". Also: "the mechanics should synergize, but not overlap". Check: the card must work in a game without any one other color. Tie colors through shared pieces (resources, members, shop, pile, turn order, followers), never by naming another color's verb.

21. **Inventing a token.** Nov 7: "tokens are a thing that need to come from me. And the last turn token is that right now. I don't want you… willy-nilly throwing tokens in the game." Check: use only pieces already in the game (the Goes Last token is the only token). A new token is Cory's call, raised as its own question only when it clearly connects colors.

22. **Pile draws, randomness, hidden cards.** Nov 7, said many times: "i don't like drawing from the top of the pile. i don't like randomness. In general the game should be all public information." Check: every card a player gains or reads is face up and chosen; nothing comes off the top of the pile or by chance. presend bounces it.

23. **Members that get cheaper.** Nov 7, on "recruits cost 1 less per member of that color you hold": "i don't like members getting 'cheaper' - it's hard to track in a live game." Check: no running discounts on member prices; a printed price stays the price.

24. **Ties that aren't intuitive.** Nov 7, Red × Blue: crossed ties (a contest tie broken by another color's count) → "no, ties need to be intuitive." Check: a tie rule is the one a table guesses without the rulebook (split, or the plain obvious one); never reach into a second color to break it.

25. **Generic mechanics sold as color interactions.** Nov 7, Red × Blue third list: sell a member back to the shop, lend a member until round end, buy a rival's member by paying its cost, swap only with a bigger cult, the shop in color rows → "these aren't really blue and red interactions. they already inherently interact." Fourth list (Blue recruits twice of one color, Red swaps a whole color, a Blue clear paid in the deepest group's color) → "bad… not understanding the exercise". Check: an interaction is a shared moment both colors cause, paid off by a card naming neither (his Martyrs note: "when a member leaves your cult… it interacts with red. Inherently"). More of what both already do is restating; a new shop or cult rule is not a tie. Build from contests and verbs, not today's cards. references/color-interactions.md.

26. **Card review 2, refused in full.** Nov 8: "these ideas suck. they don't feel original or elegant at alll." 19 replacements and 11 fixes across every color, defaults built from small tweaks and stand-ins. Check: don't resurface any of its defaults (list in color-interactions.md section 6); after two nos on lists, redo from the color's contest and his starred cards, one color at a time, instead of another list.

27. **A tie that only fires when other colors are in the game.** Nov 7, batch 1, Temps ("Whenever one of your members leaves your cult on your turn, recruit an extra time this turn.") → "will there always be a way for members to leave? just anarchists? we can't assume". Check: a game may hold none of the colors that cause your moment; the card brings its own way to make it happen (an On Use that does it), and the other colors are a bonus. His fix keeps the payoff and adds the cause: "On Use, once per round: discard one of your members, then +4 of any colors and recruit an extra time this turn." (A first read-back dropped the extra recruit; "no AND it gives the extra recruit": a fix adds to the idea, it doesn't replace its payoff.)

28. **Giving any colors is interactive.** Nov 7: "anything that can give you colors of another resource is generally interactive." Resources of any color feed whatever the other colors in the game want, so "+N of any colors" is a natural payoff for a tie. Paying the exact colored part of a card ("gain its color cost") is "too complicated".

29. **The color's own job and contest.** Nov 7, batch 1: Silver "Give a rival a shop member costing up to 5, for free. Gain followers equal to its cost." → "doesn't do anything for the silver competition… white coded for no reason… removing a member for followers is what reads as white." Check: each card should serve its own color's contest or verb; trading a member away for followers is White's shape, whatever color prints it.

30. **No cards built on who leads a contest right now.** Nov 7: "Steal 2 resources from each rival who leads a contest." → "what if it's all ties?… you should avoid basing things off current contest leaders". Check: don't key a card to current contest leads; it's confusing and ties muddle it. Names say what they are (he asked what "Tall Poppies" meant).

31. **Green copying On Recruits: lean away for now** (Nov 7: "I don't love green copying on recruit we might move away from that eventually… lean away for now").

32. **Moving another color's piece needs a reason inside its own color.** Nov 7: Ushers "On Use, once per round: give the Goes Last token to a rival. +2 followers." → "interesting. but… not good execution… kind of a counter to purple, or maybe it lets you take it back? on the right track". Check: a card that hands the Goes Last token (or any color's signature piece) away should still serve that color's fight: a way to take it back, or a cost to whoever holds it.

33. **A sting that scales with no ceiling feels too bad.** Nov 7, batch 2: Protection Racket "Choose a rival. Steal 1 resource per member they have." → "good but might feel too bad". Check: play the victim's side at the top of the range (round III, a big cult); give a steal a cap or a gentle rate.

34. **Play the sequence literally, step by step.** Nov 7: Ushers "give the Goes Last token to a rival… After that rival places their next minion, you take it." → "what you're saying makes no sense. how could you delay them and then take it?" Check: write out who holds what after each placement; if the steps contradict (the token's whole effect is that its holder goes last), cut it. His seed from it: a Purple card that makes all of one player's minions go last.

35. **The cause must happen often enough.** Nov 7: Last Rites "Whenever you discard one of your members, first use its On Use, even if it's used up." → "how much white discard is there? idk that it's reliable." Check: count how many times per game its trigger happens with the color's live cards; a payoff on a rare moment is a dead card.

36. **Don't pitch into a slot he's happy with.** Nov 7: Movers (a Red round II space) → "I like Promote and Demote so red round II not needed". Check: before pitching a space, check which spaces he has starred or kept; new ideas go where he wants change, or as members.

37. **Simple beats close.** Nov 7: Open House "Each rival may recruit once now, in turn order. +2 followers per rival who does." → "close to interesting. A little complicated for my liking". Check: count the steps a table does (each rival decides, in order, then a count); more than two steps reads as complicated.

38. **Good kernels go to testing, not to refinement.** Nov 7: "don't just try and refine the good ideas until i approve the list. just use the good ideas for the broader testing." A kernel he calls good goes to the testing pool with a few variations; the next batch is new ideas, not polish of the last one.

39. **Giving a rival a minion feels terrible.** Nov 7, batch 3: "Give a rival one of your minions for this round. +5 followers." → "I don't like this at all. getting a minion is so strong, really feels bad at 4 player table". Check: never give a minion to a rival; an extra placement is among the strongest things in the game, and it lands on one rival at a 4-player table. Cory narrowed it (Nov 7): "it's more, never give away a minion to a rival. other things potentially can." Giving rivals resources, members or the token can still work.

40. **Never subtract followers a table can't take away.** Nov 7: "Give up to 3 of your followers to rivals… +3 white per follower given." → "avoid situation that makes negative members happen (esp when most early followers are from members - how would we subtract at a live table?)". Check: early followers mostly sit printed on members; a card that gives away or loses followers needs followers the player can actually hand over. Prefer other costs. Also: a setup for a round III cash-in "feels strange as round II action", and the numbers were low.

41. **A count that collapses as the table fills.** Nov 7: Yellow "Round End: +1 follower per color among your members that no rival has a member of." → "this gets so weak so fast. good consideration for skill because of how bad this is." Check: play the count in round III, when every cult holds 5 to 8 members; a payoff that needs nobody else to have something dies as the game goes on. Prefer counts that grow with the game.

42. **Liked: a capped tax on a common moment** (Nov 7: "I like this one!"): Magenta "Whenever a rival recruits a member costing 7 or more, steal 2 resources from them." His note: "this would have to scale with member costs". Check: a threshold tied to member prices breaks when prices move; tie it to something relative, or flag it for repricing.

43. **Spaces give their color.** Nov 7, on his own seed as a Purple space: "should give some purple". Every space gives at least 1 of its color, and a space whose effect isn't a gain should still give a real amount of its color.
