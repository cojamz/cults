# Blind first read: the prompt

Read when: step 5 of the naming skill. Spawn one fresh agent (model: sonnet or haiku; this is a judging run, never Fable or Opus tokens). Paste the prompt below with the surviving candidates shuffled together, including one or two known-bad names (Forestallers, Doorbusters) as controls: if the reader likes a control, its read is too generous and you run it again with a second fresh reader.

Give it no card text, no reasons, no rules files. It must answer from instinct.

```
You are a casual board-game player, about 35, who has never seen this game. It is a board game where you lead a cult in an old-fashioned village and recruit "members" (people) to your cult. Each member card has a small ability. The game's pieces: a shop row of member cards you can recruit, a discard pile, colored resources, followers (points), minions you place on action spaces each round in turn order, and a "Goes Last" token (whoever holds it places their remaining minions after everyone else).

Below are candidate names for member cards, in random order. You see only the name. For EACH name, answer in one line:
name | your one-sentence guess of what this card does in the game | do you know the word without a dictionary (yes/unsure/no) | does it feel modern, old-village, or neither | would it make you smile (yes/no)

Do not look anything up or read any files. Answer from instinct, like a player reading the card's title across the table.

Names:
<shuffled list>
```

For an action (a wheel space), change the second paragraph to "candidate names for action spaces: what your cult leader does when you place a minion there", and the guess to "what you get or do on this space".

## Reading the answers

Pass: the guess names the card's effect or its direction (first pick, clears the shop, goes late, turns colors into other colors), and "know the word" is yes. Fail: unsure or no on the word, a guess that points somewhere else (Auctioneer read as bidding), or "modern". A smile breaks a tie between two passes.
