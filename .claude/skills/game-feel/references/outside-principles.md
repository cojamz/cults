# Outside game-design principles (for the anti-local-minima step)

Walk this list every third use. For each, one line: does Cults keep it, break it, or is there a lens we lack? These come from published design writing and widely shared practice; they are prompts, not rules, and Cory's taste wins.

1. **A game is a series of interesting decisions** (Sid Meier). Interesting = no option always best, consequences visible, enough information to plan. Check: S1. Does any phase (round I buys, the round III scramble) reduce to one obvious move?
2. **Positive feedback loops need brakes** (Marc LeBlanc, feedback systems). Rich-get-richer is fine early if something pulls the leader back late. In Cults the brakes are turn order (fewest followers first), theft and contests. Check: S5. Is there a loop with no brake (contest sweep → resources → next contest)?
3. **Blocking is the worker-placement soul** (Caylus, Agricola, Lords of Waterdeep). Spaces must be wanted by more than one player for blocking to matter. Check: are the best spaces taken first by most seats, and do some go untaken often? A space nobody fights over is just a private action.
4. **Every strategy needs a counter, and the counter needs a counter** (David Sirlin, "Yomi" layers; rock-paper-scissors balance). Check: S4. Name the counter for each top card; is there a counter to the counter?
5. **Avoid kingmaking** (a trailing player decides who wins). Gifts and "a rival you choose" cards (Silver) can hand the game to one player. Check: when the target is chosen, does the chooser always pick the same seat?
6. **Keep the game state readable; hidden info should be a choice, not a burden** (Reiner Knizia's elegance; Friedemann Friese's "the rules should fit on the board"). Check: F3, F4.
7. **Variance should come from players, not dice** (worker placement norm). Cults' randomness is the shop row and the deck. Check: does a lucky shop decide games (one round I card carrying the winner)? Compare winners' first-round buys.
8. **Each role in the game should have its own fantasy** (Mark Rosewater, color pie). Each Cults color owns a verb. Check: is any color's best play really another color's verb?
9. **Make the strong choice feel good and the weak choice feel bad only when it's the player's fault** (Richard Garfield: luck vs skill; "fail-forward"). Check: traps (S3) and dead turns (probe). Taking a space that gives nothing should be your mistake, never the card's.
10. **Snowball the end, not the start** (Cascadia, Wingspan pacing: engines pay off late). Check: F2. Round III should be the biggest; a round I card that decides the game is wrong-ended.
11. **Downtime kills online play** (BoardGameArena practice). Check: decisions per turn, number of prompts in a turn, how long a rival's turn feels. A card that adds a prompt to every turn costs feel even if balanced.
12. **The player should always know how they could win from here** (Teuber/Catan "everybody stays in"). Check: S5 plus the score display: can someone behind name the turn that catches them up?
13. **Elegance: one rule, many consequences** (Knizia, Feld). Check: does a card add a rule that only it uses? Fewer, stronger hooks beat many small ones (Cory: small "+1 when…" triggers read as weak).
14. **Balance by cost curve, then by playtest** (Magic's mana curve; Dominion's price bands). Check: are there holes or crowds in the 2 to 11 cost range per color? A color with five cards at 9+ starves the round I shop.

When a principle keeps flagging something none of the lenses caught, add a lens to lenses.md and log it.
